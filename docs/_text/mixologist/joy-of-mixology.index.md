# Index: *The Joy of Mixology*, revised edition (Gary Regan, 2018)

A bartender's craft manual by one of the revival's teachers: a long history of mixed drinks, the bartender's trade and manner, balance and technique, Regan's **drink families**, and about 200 recipes, many with a short note on where the drink came from or whose recipe it is.

- **Text:** `joy-of-mixology.md`, OCR (macOS Vision, 2026-09-26) because the PDF's own text layer is broken into fragments. It's clean, but check any figure or name on the page before citing it.
- **Cite:** `Joy of Mixology, pdf N` (the PDF has no printed page labels).
- **Tier:** craft reference (Tomás), and secondary history (Hester). For drink origins it's an experienced practitioner's account. Confirm attributions in the Oxford Companion or *Imbibe!* before they become anchors; the recipe credits ("adapted from a recipe by…") are Regan's first-hand record of modern drinks.
- **Search:** `library.py search "Cable Car" --book joy` · `library.py page joy 261`

## Why it matters to the studio
- **Tomás:** Regan's families are another lens on the *Codex*'s six roots (Simple, Enhanced, New Orleans, International and Sparkling Sours; French-Italian; Duos and Trios; Highballs; Snappers; Hot Drinks; Juleps; Punches; Orphans). His "Theory of Mixology" (balance, individual taste) and "Craft" (every technique, from rimming to flaming and infusions) are plain-language method sources for the ritual.
- **Hester:** the history chapter (pdf 17–91) runs from the first cocktails to the revival. The recipe notes name modern creators and bars.
- **Wren:** "Do You Have What It Takes?" (pdf 92–124) covers the bartender's manner: mindful bartending, intuition, thoughtful communication, and reading a guest. It's close to the professional empathy Robin wants from Ryu.
