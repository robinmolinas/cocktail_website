# H1 "Who is this cocktail for?": calmer Playfair (2026-10-06)

On H1, the question and the six lens words feel "almost too much… very sharp, very premium-looking", and "a bit intense compared to the calm honesty" of the two cold-open lines. He wants the same family, calmer, more honest and intimate. Not a new font.

## What the screenshots show (the root cause)

1. **The cold-open lines are already Playfair Display italic 400**, at 24px with 1.625 leading (`font-playfair italic sm:text-2xl leading-relaxed`). So the calm Robin hears in them doesn't come from a different face. It comes from (a) a long sentence instead of a short question, (b) 24px instead of 27px, and (c) where they sit: centred, on the darker brown band. The H1 question sits at `top: 12.5%`, on the frame's palest amber, where Display's needle hairlines and tight, condensed italic are at their most visible.
2. **The sharpness is Playfair Display's design, not just its size.** The opsz ladder (`shots/opsz-ladder.png`) runs Display against variable Playfair at opsz 12 to 120, weights 400 and 450. Even variable Playfair at opsz 120 is wider, lower in contrast and less condensed than Display. Down at opsz 12 to 18 it turns sturdy and wide, close to Georgia, and loses the Dionysus elegance. **opsz 32 to 48 is the sweet spot:** it keeps the ball terminals and the italic swing, the hairlines thicken, and the "fashion headline" edge goes.
3. Moving the lens words from 500 to 400 earlier today ("current") took the boldness off but not the sharpness. They are still the Display cut.

## Notes, honestly

- **before / current:** premium, crisp and fashion-y. The 400 lens weight helped, but the question still has the sharpest hairlines on the page, on the palest band. This is what Robin is reacting to.
- **soft (opsz 28 / 16):** clearly calmer and still legible. The question reads like a literary italic rather than a magazine headline. Costs: the words at opsz 16 run wide and bookish, a little Georgia, and a touch thin over the amber. It is the most "honest" variant, but loses some of the Dionysus magic.
- **upright:** the roman lens words read like UI labels or hint text. They are honest but flat, and they break the Italic Voice Rule (choices are *spoken*). Not recommended.
- **whisper:** only the question's size changes, and it is still the Display cut, so it is still sharp, just smaller. It also puts Display (question) and text-cut (words) together on one screen, which reads as two voices. It shows that size alone doesn't solve it.
- **murmur (own, recommended):** the middle of the optical axis. The question keeps Playfair's swing and ball terminals at production size, but loses the condensed, needle-hairline drama. It sits with the cold-open voice instead of over it. The words at opsz 24 are calm and still elegant. Legibility on the pale band is equal to or better than Display, because the hairlines are thicker. A first pass also shrank the question to `clamp(1.35rem, 2.8vmin, 1.7rem)`. That was calmer, but it read *quieter than the cold-open line itself* on the bright band, so I reverted it. Production sizes are kept, so the change is the face alone.
- **Phone wrapping:** "The night version of me" breaks as "The night / version of me" in every variant at 1440, 390 and 375×667. It never goes to three lines. One side effect: the text cuts set wider, and at 390 "The me I used to be" fits on **one** line, against "The me I / used to be" in Display. It sits inside the rim with room to spare and avoids the stranded "I", so I'd call it neutral to positive. At 375×667 it is two lines in every variant.

## Recommendation: murmur

The production change is the face and the optical size only. No size, layout or colour changes.

**Option A: H1 only** (what Robin flagged; the smallest change to review). No TSX change is needed if `:has()` is acceptable (Safari 15.4+, Chrome 105+, Firefox 121+):

```css
/* H1: the question and its lenses in Playfair's mid optical size: same family,
   lower contrast, calmer than Display's hairlines (Robin, 2026-10-06) */
.hold-head:has(+ .sphere-cluster.is-lens) .hold-q {
  font-family: var(--font-text);
  font-variation-settings: 'opsz' 40;
}
.sphere-cluster.is-lens .sphere-word {
  font-family: var(--font-text);
  font-variation-settings: 'opsz' 24;
  font-weight: 400; line-height: 1.28; letter-spacing: 0.005em; /* was 0.01em */
}
```

(If `:has()` isn't wanted, add a class in TheDepths: `<h2 className="hold-q is-lens-q">`, then use `.hold-q.is-lens-q { … }`.)

**Option B: the whole journey voice**, for consistency. Add tokens and change the two shared rules:

```css
:root { --font-voice: var(--font-text); --voice-opsz: 40; --word-opsz: 24; }
.hold-q      { font-family: var(--font-voice); font-variation-settings: 'opsz' var(--voice-opsz); }
.sphere-word { font-family: var(--font-voice); font-variation-settings: 'opsz' var(--word-opsz); }
```

**The trade-off.** With A, H2 onward ("Which colour feels like you?" and the rest) goes straight back to Display, so the voice sharpens again one screen after Robin asked for calm, and DESIGN.md's single "voice = Display italic" rule gains an exception. B keeps one voice everywhere and is the more coherent end state. But B also changes things this test never covered: the base `.sphere-word` weight of 500 (H4 pairs, H5, H6), the large H4 `.ember` words, and `.depth-input` if it follows the voice token. The cold-open `.depth-line` uses `font-playfair`, so B leaves it alone unless that class is re-pointed. DESIGN.md §3 would need rewording too: "speaks in Playfair italic at opsz 40".
**Suggested path:** ship A now for Robin's sign-off on H1. If he likes it, do B as a second pass with a quick shot of H4, H5 and H6 at weight 500 versus 400 in the text cut. The cold-open lines can stay Display, because they already read calm. Do not use `font-optical-sizing: auto` alone in place of the fixed opsz: it would pick opsz ≈ 27 for the question and ≈ 17 for the words, which is the softer, wider, more bookish "soft" look.
