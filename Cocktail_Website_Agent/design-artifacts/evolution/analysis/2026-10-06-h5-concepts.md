# H5 · The Resonance — concept options (6 October 2026)

Written for Robin's review. Today's H5 is two rounds of a sphere cluster: tap, it lights, it fizzes. Robin's verdict: it has nice elements but feels basic and classic, a click with a link to it. The brief for the replacement:

- **Show the two directions.** Being drawn toward something, and people coming to you, should be *seen*, not labelled.
- **"You" is a circle,** not the glass. The figure and the glass belong to the reveal and must not be spent early.
- **"You" stays the same object** through both rounds and the transition between them.
- **Unchanged from the agreement with the matching owner:** up to three choices per round, reversible. Answers are stored in vocabulary order, so motion, distance and tap order are never captured.
- **Touch first.** DESIGN.md allows only one drag, H3's mote. A drag can only be offered *on top of* a tap that does the same thing.

The test: would a jury remember it the next morning? It must do something only this product can do, and it has to be made of the product's own material, which is liquid.

## 0 · A world of your own (Robin's reorder, prototyped; now recommended)

Robin, after seeing A, kept what worked and reversed the order. Words coming *into* her "just made sense, it's part of you", but A's reaching neck felt gamified. He asked to ask what people come to you for **first**, so she constitutes herself, and then let her spread toward what attracts her. His themes: gravity, planets, integration.

**The idea:** two forces, in their natural order.
- **Gravity first.** What people come to you for falls into you, and that is what makes you a body.
- **Light second.** Once you are whole, your light goes out to what you are drawn to.

Mass comes in and light goes out, so the two questions become one physical story: a self forming, then shining.

| Beat | What happens |
|---|---|
| Birth | Her H2 seed opens into a bubble of her own colour: a thin lit skin, an empty centre. "You" is a circle, not the glass. |
| Round 1 · *What do people often come to you for?* | The words are already in her pull, on a very slow orbit. A touch lets a word's orbit give way: it curves in, gathering speed, meets her skin with a ripple and stays inside her as written light. Each word fills her a little. Touch it inside her to let it go. A word can also be carried to her (needs Robin's one-drag override; tap stays primary). |
| The turn | Unchosen words drift off. She kindles once, and her first light crosses the dark. The farther words appear as it reaches them; they were always there, but she could not see them before she shone. |
| Round 2 · *What are you most drawn toward right now?* | A touch sends part of her light out on a curve, like matter streaming between two stars, and it settles around the word as a ring. Her skin leans very slightly toward it while it goes. Nothing stays drawn between them, so this is not a node graph; the ring is the only mark. Touch again and the ring unwinds home. |
| End | Her world: a lit body holding what others find in her, with ringed bodies she is drawn to turning slowly at their distance. Then the camera rises to H6. |

**Why it is stronger than A:**
- **The order is the arc.** You become someone before you reach for anything.
- **Each round uses one force,** and the two forces are opposite, so nothing needs the words "in" or "out".
- **The rings are planets** without a star-chart: no lines, no labels, no positions that read as scores.
- **She never stops being the same object.** The words inside her stay written there through round 2.

**Data:** unchanged. soughtFor and drawnToward stay ≤3 each, in vocabulary order. Round order doesn't change the shape. The 12-word list (Q12) now sits in round 2, where the far ring has more room on desktop; phones use 3×4 and fit at 375×667.

**Production notes:**
- It is plain canvas 2D: two layers so each ring passes behind and in front of its word's glass, and a sprite for light motes. No new dependency.
- A WebGL version of her body is optional polish. See [the component audit](2026-10-06-h5-component-sources.md) for React Bits / Paper Shaders orb techniques worth borrowing.
- Still Water: no orbit or drift; words arrive and rings form by fading in.

## A · Surface Tension (prototyped; superseded by §0 after Robin's review)

**She is a single drop of her own colour**, the seed she chose at H2. It's a circle of liquid, not glass, and not the figure.

- **What draws you.** Touch a word and the drop *reaches* for it. A neck of her liquid stretches across the dark, touches the word's glass, then thins at the middle and lets go. A bead of her stays inside that word. She gives a little of herself to what she's drawn to, and the drop is visibly a little smaller for it. Touch the word again and the bead flows home.
- **The turn.** On Continue, her beads flow back and she is whole again. Each one leaves a mote circling her, so what drew her stays in her orbit. The drop never changes or moves; only the direction of everything around it reverses.
- **What people come to you for.** Now the words *come to her*. Touched, a word's glass drifts in, meets her surface and is drawn inside. It stays written within her, held as light. Touch it inside her to let it go.
- **The end state** is a quiet portrait: a drop holding what others take from her, circled by what she reaches for.

**Why it can win.**
- **The physics is the meaning.** Reaching out costs a drop part of itself; taking in fills it. Nobody needs the words "outward" or "inward".
- **It's cocktail-native.** Surface tension, coalescence and a liquid neck pinching off are the true physics of a pour, so it's not a star chart and not a node graph.
- **It hasn't been seen** as a questionnaire mechanic.
- **It shows emotional cost and accumulation** with no UI chrome: no counters, no ticks.

**Prototype:** [`../prototypes/h5-surface-tension/`](../prototypes/h5-surface-tension/index.html) (served at http://localhost:5190/h5-surface-tension/; `?seed=%23c8102e` etc. to try her colour; `?motion=still` for reduced motion).
- **How it's built:** a canvas metaball field. Each reach is a row of liquid samples sized so the neck reads as one continuous surface. A soft highlight and a rim light make it read as a droplet.
- **Measured:** ~50fps at 1440px on CPU, and 120fps on a phone-sized canvas. For production I'd move the field to a small WebGL shader, which is the same look at almost no cost.
- **Screenshots:** `st-*-{1440,390}.png` in the prototype folder.

**Open points:**
- Should the neck's surface show a hint of refraction of the footage behind it? That's possible in WebGL.
- On phones the words sit in three rows beneath her, so every reach is a short one. That passes the thumb test, but the ring layout on desktop is more beautiful.
- The motes left in orbit in round 2 are small. They could brighten as she takes words in.

## B · The Reflection

**The same circle, seen from two sides of the surface.**
- **Round 1** happens above a meniscus line. Her drop rests on the surface of the drink, and the words of what draws her sit in the air above.
- **The turn:** the camera does not move, but the surface rises through the frame and the world flips to below the waterline. She is now her own reflection.
- **Round 2's words** appear in the reflection world. What people come to you for is how you look from the other side, how others see you.

**Why it's interesting:** the metaphor of a perceived social role is exact. The transition is a single, unforgettable beat.

**Risks:**
- It's harder to make legible: reflected type has to stay readable, so the words would be upright in a mirrored world.
- The surface-crossing is a large visual event in a calm stretch of the journey.
- It needs care so it doesn't read as an image flip.

**Best use:** as the *turn* inside A, if A's flow-home feels too quiet.

## C · The Lean

**Desire moves the camera.** The words float in the dark.
- **Round 1:** each word she chooses pulls the frozen frame a hair toward it, a composited 1–2% lean and drift, as if her gaze draws the world.
- **Round 2:** the world is still, and the words travel to her centre.

**Why it's interesting:** it's cinematic and uses the footage as material.

**Risks:**
- Composited motion on the paused frame was tried at H3 (the breath micro-zoom) before the "no per-question camera movement" lock. It would need Robin's explicit exception.
- The lean could also be read as intensity, which we've agreed not to imply.

## D · Gravity Well

**She is a small sun; the words are bodies at a distance.**
- **Round 1:** choosing a word bends her path, and the drop drifts and settles at the point its three choices pull it to.
- **Round 2:** the words fall into orbit around her.

**Why it's interesting:** you literally see "being drawn".

**Risks:**
- The drop's final position looks like a weighting (a score), which is the misreading we've agreed to avoid.
- It's closer to star-chart grammar, which was rejected for H5 in rev 1.
