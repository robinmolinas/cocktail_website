# Pour — I'll Do It · The Have A Go Hero (regular-guy-hero)

## Cocktail

- **name:** I'll Do It
- **tagline:** You say yes before you know how.
- **glassware:** rocks glass, one large cube
- **contains:** `[]`

**recipe**

| amount | item | note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 45 ml | Hudson Do The Rye Thing New York straight rye (recommended) | the rye line those amateurs began, sold today under this name; or any straight rye, ideally one distilled in New York State |
| 15 ml | American apple brandy | bottled-in-bond (50%) if you can find it; any American apple brandy of 40% or more works |
| 30 ml | sweet vermouth | a red Italian-style vermouth |
| ½ tsp | demerara syrup | equal parts demerara sugar and hot water, stirred until clear, then cooled |
| 2 dashes | Angostura bitters | |
| 1 | strip of orange peel | for the top |

**method**
1. Put a large ice cube in the rocks glass to chill it while you mix.
2. Pour the rye, apple brandy, vermouth, syrup and bitters into a mixing glass or a jar, and fill it with ice.
3. Stir for about 30 seconds, until the outside of the glass is very cold.
4. Strain it over the large cube.
5. Squeeze the orange peel over the top, skin side down, so its oils fall on the drink, then drop it in.

**closingLine:** *Make it before you've practised, with someone watching. Ask for a hand before you need one.*

## Reading

**epigraph**
*The rye this drink calls for was first made in a barn, by amateurs.*

**whoYouAre**
Someone says it in the pub, on the doorstep or in the group chat: somebody really ought to do something about it. The broken bench, the club with no treasurer, the school play nobody will run. Everyone nods. Before you can stop yourself, you've said "I'll do it", and only later, doing the washing-up, do you realise you've no idea how.

Nobody hears about the evenings: the videos, the forums, the cousin who might know. You're out of your depth most of the way through, and you know it. The words you dread are "who asked you?", and worse, the doubters turning out to be right. You go anyway, partly because if you thought about it any longer, you wouldn't. This is what it gives. The thing everyone was waiting for somebody to fix gets fixed. And because you so plainly need a hand, people who'd never have volunteered end up holding the ladder. It counts for more than you know: what was everyone's complaint becomes something they did together.

**yours**

1. For about twenty-five years, Ralph Erenzo had camped on climbing trips to New York's Shawangunk Ridge with no toilets and no running water. So in 2001 he bought an old mill near the ridge to give climbers a proper campground. The neighbours objected. One of them, as Erenzo remembers it, told him the aim was to stall until his money ran out.

2. When the campground stalled, Erenzo asked the town's zoning officer what he could do with the land, and heard that a winery counts as farming. So Erenzo and Brian Lee, a broadcast engineer, set out to make spirits instead. Neither had any idea how. Lee joked that the only fermenting he'd ever done was cinnamon buns with his children. By Erenzo's count it took about two and a half years, from knowing nothing, to switching the still on and making alcohol. He said later that he worked from instructions he found online. And right at the start, Erenzo had asked his son, Gable, too: "I'm building a distillery – I need your help!"

3. Their first bottles held a vodka made from New York apples. Later came their whiskeys, among them a rye from New York grain. By 2015 the state counted more than a hundred craft distilleries, and its agriculture commissioner called Tuthilltown a model for producers across the state. I like to think the best thing Erenzo made there wasn't in a bottle. It was that call for help: what he was about to try, and that he couldn't do it alone, in a single sentence, long before he knew how. You always say the first half. It's the second half you swallow.

4. The cocktail I've made for you is a Manhattan, one of the great classics: rye stirred with sweet vermouth (a red, wine-based aperitif with herbs) and bitters, then poured over a single large ice cube, and an orange peel pinched over it so its oils land on the drink. It's built for Hudson's rye, sold today as Do The Rye Thing, the line those amateurs began, if you can find it. Beside the rye there's a little apple brandy, as a nod to the apples they started with. It isn't their vodka. It's their first go, kept next to the thing they got good at, and one of my favourite cocktail books calls rye with apple brandy magical. It comes out deep and warm, a little sweet, and brighter for the apple.

5. So, when "I'll do it" next comes out of your mouth, finish the sentence: "and I'll need a hand." Say it at the start, while you still don't know how, and say who you'd like. You worry it'll show how out of your depth you are. It will, a little. It's also the reason they'll come. The job gets done sooner, nobody waits for the experts, and the thing you started ends up belonging to everyone who helped. I hope plenty of them say yes.
