# Pour — Don't Look at Me · The Straight Man (jester-ruler)

## Cocktail

- **name:** Don't Look at Me
- **tagline:** Your straight face brings out everyone else's funniest side.
- **glassware:** large plain tumbler (about 350 ml), three ice cubes
- **contains:** `[]`

**recipe**

| amount | item | note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 50 ml | straight rye whiskey | ideally about 50%; 43% at the least |
| half | a fresh lime | half a normal-sized lime, squeezed; the squeezed half goes in too |
| 105 ml | Apollinaris sparkling mineral water | recommended, plain (unflavoured), cold; or any plain sparkling mineral water with a noticeable mineral taste |
| 3 | ice cubes | ordinary cubes, not crushed |

**method**
1. Squeeze half a lime into a large tumbler, then drop the squeezed half in after the juice.
2. Add three ice cubes, then the rye.
3. Top up with the cold mineral water, poured gently so it keeps its bubbles. Stir once.
4. No sugar, and nothing on top.

**closingLine:** *Lime first, no sugar, not a word. Next time, let them catch you laughing.*

## Reading

**epigraph**
*Named after a regular, mixed by the bartender. The regular denied being its author.*

**whoYouAre**
Everyone around you thinks the funny one is your friend: the one who waves both arms through every story, gets the order wrong on purpose and starts the singing. You're sitting beside them, saying very little. You let them run, then one quiet word, or just a look, and the whole table goes. They get the laugh. It comes off you. Most of them take you for the serious one, and you go along with it, since that's part of the act.

How much you're enjoying it is the bit you hide best. You'd never say it, but you need them to be ridiculous. Put you in a room where everyone behaves and you're bored within the hour. People think you put up with the loudest person at the table. You picked them. Your fear is being taken at face value, as the grown-up who doesn't get the joke. You get it before anyone. And that's a gift to everyone near you: next to you, they're at their funniest, because someone steady is there to play it against.

**yours**

1. By the end of 1889, at a Washington bar everyone called Shoo's, a new drink had caught on: whiskey, half a lime, soda water and ice, and no sugar at all. It carried the name of a regular, a lobbyist known as Colonel Joe Rickey, who, it's said, showed bartenders how to make it wherever he went. Mixing it was the head bartender, George Williamson, who worked there for years and stayed with the drink a long time.

2. Then the comedy starts. In 1893 Rickey told a New York paper that he wasn't the drink's author at all, and at least three of his own accounts of how it began were printed in his lifetime. A later writer named Williamson as the real inventor. Even the lime has its stories: in Rickey's own, the switch to lime came from one of the bartenders; in another, a stranger handed Williamson the limes. Whether or not Rickey drank it himself, his friends did, and ordered it by his name. And in 1895, when he wrote his recipe out by hand for a newspaper, the lime was in it.

3. George Williamson, I like to think, loved it, and heard each new version of how the drink started, said nothing, and poured it again. The colonel had the name and the stories. The bartender had the drink. It doesn't work without both of them, and the steady one knows that better than anyone. So do you. You don't want the name on it. You want the act to work, and the act works because of you.

4. The cocktail I've made for you is a Rickey, a renowned classic, made with whiskey, the way it caught on at Shoo's: long, very cold and bone dry. Each partner puts something in. From the bar, a straight rye, because Shoo's sold its own and it often went into this drink. From the colonel, his water: he reportedly preferred Apollinaris, a sparkling mineral water, and I like to think it makes the lime taste a little brighter. The half lime belongs to both of them, whoever started it. The water is there as a nod to him, and the rye to the bar where Williamson poured, because I couldn't make it with only one of them.

5. You give everyone their best moment, and the one person nobody's watching is you. Once in a while, let it show. Let the loudest person at the table see you laugh. They've been playing to you from the start, and your laugh is the one they wanted most. The act survives it. They'll know you were having fun all along, and you get to have it with them, out in the open.
