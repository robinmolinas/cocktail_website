# Pour — Hear Me Out · The Devil's Advocate (outlaw-sage)

## Cocktail

- **name:** Hear Me Out
- **tagline:** You'll argue the other side. Sometimes it's even yours.
- **glassware:** an old-fashioned glass, fresh ice
- **contains:** `["egg-white", "nuts"]`

**recipe**

| amount | item | note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 45 ml (1½ oz) | amaretto | an Italian amaretto |
| 22.5 ml (¾ oz) | cask-strength bourbon | bottled at or near barrel strength; 55% or stronger |
| 22.5 ml (¾ oz) | fresh lemon juice | |
| ½ tsp (2.5 ml) | sherry vinegar | the Spanish kind (vinagre de Jerez); measure it, don't guess |
| 1 tbsp (15 ml) | egg white, lightly whipped | from a fresh egg; whip the white, then measure a spoonful |
| 1 | strip of lemon peel | over the top |

**method**
1. Fill the glass with fresh ice and leave it to chill.
2. Separate a fresh egg. Whip the white with a fork for about 20 seconds, until it's loose and frothy, then measure one tablespoon. You won't need the rest.
3. Pour the amaretto, bourbon, lemon juice and sherry vinegar into a shaker. Add the spoonful of egg white last.
4. Fill the shaker with ice, close it, and shake hard for about 15 seconds.
5. Strain into the glass, over the fresh ice. A thin white foam will settle on top.
6. Squeeze the lemon peel over the foam, shiny side down, so its oils fall on the drink, then lay it on top.

**closingLine:** *Measure the vinegar, and be ready to defend it. Next time, let the view you defend be one you hold.*

## Reading

**epigraph**
*One bartender made the case for this drink by admitting what was wrong with it.*

**whoYouAre**
Someone says it's settled, and something in you sits up. It isn't that you think they're wrong. Often you think they're right. But nobody has argued with it yet, and an idea nobody has argued with hasn't been tested. It's only been agreed with. So you take the other side.

You'd hardly ever admit how often you agree, or how glad you'd be to lose to a good answer. "Only playing devil's advocate" keeps your own view safe, where nobody can argue it down. The sigh is what you dread, the "there they go again": once your questions stop counting, you're stuck with decisions nobody was allowed to think about.

Nobody says thank you in the moment. They should. An idea that's survived you can survive almost anything, and when the real objection comes, from a boss, say, the others already have the answer, because you made them find it.

**yours**

1. In the 1980s the Amaretto Sour was a standard. An importer had launched it in 1974 as two parts of the sweet Italian liqueur to one of lemon juice, but by then most bartenders reached for bought sour mix instead, and the drink tasted of little but the liqueur.

2. Then, in February 2012, a Portland bartender, Jeffrey Morgenthaler, posted on his website that he made the best Amaretto Sour in the world. It opens: "No, really. I'm serious." And before he made his case, he gave the other side its due. Other Amaretto Sours were too sweet, he wrote, and amaretto on its own is weak. "It needs help." His help was cask-strength bourbon. Some readers teased him, and one asked whether it was still an Amaretto Sour at all.

3. That's the move I recognise in you. He never pretended the old drink was good. He granted the point, then showed what the drink could be once it was answered. Two years later he tried the move from the other end, with a post called "Hot Toddies suck—long live the Hot Toddy", and a better way to keep one hot. And by 2021, the Oxford drinks companion's own entry on this drink was his. I like to think the teasing pleased him: it meant someone was arguing back.

4. The cocktail I've made for you is an Amaretto Sour, a classic again, built on his fix: an Italian amaretto, cask-strength bourbon for backbone, fresh lemon, and lightly whipped egg white, a spoonful of it, shaken and strained over fresh ice, with lemon oil over its thin white foam. Then I argued with him, as you would. He said the others were too sweet, so I took him further than he went: no syrup at all, a little less lemon to match, and the amaretto doing all the sweetening. And there's sherry vinegar in it, half a teaspoon, an ingredient I'd have to defend in most bars, in honour of the side nobody takes. Vinegar is one of the best partners nuts have in the kitchen, and here it keeps the sweetness honest.

5. So here's my side, and I mean it. Keep testing everything: people are better for it, even the ones who sigh. But now and then, argue for something you actually love, and say plainly that it's what you think. No escape hatch, no "just for the sake of argument". Let them push back on you for a change. You may find your own ideas survive you too, and everyone else finally gets to argue with the real you.
