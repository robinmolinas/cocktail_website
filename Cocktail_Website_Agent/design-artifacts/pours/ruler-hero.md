# Pour — A Place Kept · The Noble Ruler (ruler-hero)

## Cocktail

- **name:** A Place Kept
- **tagline:** When you say there's room for everyone, there is.
- **glassware:** small stemmed wine glass (about 250 ml), chilled, no ice in the glass
- **contains:** `[]`

**recipe**

| amount | item | note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 60 ml | Jim Beam bourbon (white label, 40% as of about 2021) | recommended: the whisky that took his own name. Or any Kentucky straight bourbon of 40% or stronger |
| 22.5 ml | fresh lemon juice | |
| 20 ml | simple syrup | equal parts sugar and water, stirred until clear |
| 15 ml (1 tbsp) | dry red wine | a young, dry, juicy red such as a Cabernet Sauvignon. Floated on top, never shaken in |

**method**
1. Chill the glass in the freezer, or fill it with ice water while you mix.
2. Put the bourbon, lemon juice and simple syrup in a shaker. Fill it with ice.
3. Shake hard for about 12 seconds, until the shaker is frosty on the outside.
4. Empty the glass. Strain the drink into it, through a small sieve as well, so no chips of ice get in. Let it settle for a few seconds.
5. Hold a spoon upside down just above the drink, and pour the red wine slowly onto the back of it, so the wine spreads out and rests on top as a thin red layer. Don't stir. No garnish.

**closingLine:** *Float the red so it shows. Once in a while, so should the cost.*

## Reading

**epigraph**
*Red wine floats on this sour. In 1883, a bartender said wine made his sours better.*

**whoYouAre**
When you tell people there's a place for them, they stop worrying. They can, because you never do. You keep your word the way other people keep a house: every day, mostly unseen, and without asking whether it suits you this week.

You said there'd be a place for all of them. You never said what it cost you. Something of yours goes missing along the way, a plan, a weekend, the thing you'd hoped to put your name to, and you act as if it were the obvious thing to do. Partly because it is. Partly because you're afraid that if they saw you mind, they'd ask themselves whether the promise still holds. It does. Because of you, the ones who rely on you get to stop worrying about tomorrow, and most people go a whole life without that.

**yours**

1. Old Tub was a good name for a bourbon. Through the late 1800s it made something of a name for itself, made by Jim Beam's father and grandfather, and in 1892 Jim took over running it, with his brother-in-law. Then Prohibition shut everything down. When it ended, late in 1933, Jim was sixty-nine.

2. He gathered the family: his brother Park, Park's sons Carl and Earl, and his own son, T. Jeremiah. The family still remembers him telling them it was time they all went back to work. By the next August, building work had begun, and about four months after that the stills were running. Along the way he found out that the right to the Old Tub name had been lost. He minded. The word used for it is chagrin, which is the polite word for a wince. The whiskey went out carrying his own name instead. You've almost certainly seen it.

3. After he retired in 1944, his nephew Carl and his son T. Jeremiah ran it, and last I read, the family's descendants still have a big say in how it's made. We don't know what Jim told them about the name, but I like to think he said very little: there was work to do, and he'd promised them a place in it. I recognise that in you. You keep the place you promised, and when something of yours is lost along the way, you carry it quietly and go on.

4. The cocktail I've made for you is a New York Sour, a renowned classic. Mine is bourbon, fresh lemon and sugar, shaken cold and poured without ice, sharp and clean. I'd make it with Jim Beam, since it carries the name he ended up with, though any Kentucky straight bourbon will do. Then one thing goes over the top: a float of dry red wine, a thin red band above the tawny sour. Only the wine shows, and it brings a little dry pucker to the bright sour. Back in 1883, a bartender in Chicago told a reporter the wine made the drink look good and taste better. I put it there for the one thing in this story that stung.

5. One request, then. When keeping a promise costs you something, say so, once, to someone you kept the place for. The promise won't look any weaker. It'll look exactly like what it is: something you care about enough to pay for. And they'll finally get the chance to thank you, which you so rarely allow.

### Image brief

- **Glass and drink:** a small stemmed wine glass (about 250 ml), chilled, with a light frost on the bowl. The drink is a tawny sour, a little hazy from the shaken lemon, with a thin, sharp layer of crimson red wine resting on top. "Crimson float upon the tawny drink" is Oxford's own description (pdf 1387). A faint fine foam sits under the red. No garnish, no ice in the glass.
- **Props (3–5):** a plain bar spoon lying beside the glass, its back still wet with a trace of red (the float). A half lemon, cut side up, on a small wooden board. An uncorked bottle of red wine with no label, standing behind and to one side. An unlabelled bottle of amber whiskey is fine, but never a legible label, a brand or a name.
- **One impossible detail:** the red layer stays perfectly level and knife-sharp even where a drop has run down the outside of the glass, and it catches the light like a band of gold leaf at its edge.
- **Setting and palette:** a wide, calm, symmetrical composition, seen from slightly below eye level, in warm golden light. Antique gold (Pantone 871 C), the drink's tawny amber, the single crimson band and pale stone. A long, plain wooden bar or workbench, with a row of identical hooks behind it, every one in use (each person has a place). Never dark behind anything.
- **Must not appear:** a crown, throne, crest, statue, round table, knight or cape (archetype puns). A legible label, a barrel with a name stencilled on it, a still. A punch bowl or several glasses (*Four Shares*'s ground). Egg-white foam, ice in the glass, a cherry, smoke, people.

### Names considered

*A Place Kept* (Wren's pick; Tomás and Hester agree), over *Back to Work* (Tomás's second, leans on the family's remembered line), *Kept On* and *Minded*; *The Old Name* struck (gives the reveal away).

1. **A Place Kept** (pick, Wren's, and I agree): it praises the person, not the drink, and it's what they do: they keep the place they promised. Said across a bar, "a Place Kept, please" works. It shares no word with the tagline ("room for everyone"). The registry has no "Place" or "Kept" in a name.
2. **Back to Work**: duty as love, said plainly, from the family's memory of Repeal (paraphrased, never quoted). It sits opposite *Off Duty* (the Rescuer), and it leans on an attributed line. Second choice.
3. **Kept On**: the family kept on under a new name, and so does the person. It's quieter than the pick, and close to it.
4. **The Old Name**: points at the cost. It gives the reveal away before the reading does, so I'd strike it.
