# Pour — Already There · The Clairvoyant (magician-sage)

## Cocktail

- **name:** Already There
- **tagline:** You see who someone could become in the little things they do today.
- **glassware:** small tulip-shaped grappa glass with an open rim (about 150 ml), chilled, no ice
- **contains:** `[]`

**recipe**

| amount | item | note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 50 ml | Nonino single-varietal grappa (monovitigno), recommended | young, not wood-aged, made from one named grape; or any young, not wood-aged single-varietal grappa, 40% or stronger |
| 20 ml | blanc vermouth with fig leaf, homemade (below) | French blanc vermouth (Dolin Blanc style): pale, sweet and herbal |
| 5 ml (1 teaspoon) | simple syrup | equal parts sugar and water, stirred until clear |
| | *for the fig-leaf vermouth:* 1 medium fresh fig leaf, 200 ml blanc vermouth | tear the leaf into three or four pieces, leave it in the vermouth at room temperature for about an hour, then lift it out. Keep the vermouth in the fridge. Fig-leaf sap can irritate skin: rinse your hands after tearing it |

**method**
1. Steep the fig leaf in the blanc vermouth at room temperature. Taste it after half an hour, and lift the pieces out by about an hour. The vermouth keeps in the fridge; this makes enough for ten drinks.
2. Put the tulip glass in the freezer to chill.
3. Pour the grappa, the fig-leaf vermouth and the syrup into a mixing glass or a jar. Fill it with ice and stir for about fifteen seconds.
4. Strain into the chilled glass. No garnish: the grape is what you should smell first.

**closingLine:** *Steep the leaf and leave the figs. When you see where someone's heading, give them your reasons too.*

## Reading

**epigraph**
*The grappa here comes from one grape variety. Grappa made that way began in Friuli.*

**whoYouAre**
There's someone in your life that everyone has quietly made up their mind about. You haven't. You noticed something once: the evening they stayed behind to fix a thing nobody would ever have checked. Since then you've talked about them as if their turn is coming. Usually it comes. And when people ask how you knew, you can't really say.

That's the part nobody hears. Your reasons are so small that, said out loud, they'd sound like nothing, so you hand over the conclusion and keep the reasons. It's also why you can seem a little mysterious. Underneath sits a worry you seldom admit: that what you saw in someone was only what you hoped to see, and they went ahead because you said so.

And here's your gift to whoever is near you. In your eyes, nobody is ever finished. People grow into the place you kept for them, and plenty never find out who kept it. Most people only get that kind of faith from someone who loves them. You hand it out on far less.

**yours**

1. Grappa is the spirit Italy distils from the wine press's leftovers, once the juice has run off. Italians have made it out of thrift since the 1700s or earlier. With spirits like it, the custom was to bury the pressed grapes until the wine was made, and only then put them in the still. Outside Italy, people laughed at it for years as rough "moonshine" made from the "dregs".

2. In 1973, Giannola and Benito Nonino, of Friuli in Italy's north-east, did something small and new. They distilled the leftovers of one grape, the picolit, on their own, and used them only fresh and lightly pressed: the first grappa from a single grape. In Giannola's words, she and Benito "selected the best Friulian winemakers" to get it. One early doubter, she says, came round to be one of their strongest supporters. And six years before, the family says, the first of their test bottlings had their working printed on its label: the year, how much they'd distilled, how many litres came out, the bottle's number.

3. Nothing was rescued. Grappa was already being made. They only looked at it differently, one grape at a time, and I like to think Giannola could already smell what was in the picolit that nobody had kept apart long enough to find. Abroad, the laughing went on for nearly twenty more years. And not everything went the way the new grappa promised: most grappa is still drunk in Italy, and the traditional style has come back. I recognise you in that. Not right about everything, but right about the part that mattered, years before most people could see it.

4. The cocktail I've made for you is a Martini, that renowned stirred classic, with Nonino's grappa from a single grape in place of the gin. It's stirred with a pale, sweet French vermouth that has spent an hour with a fresh fig leaf. A fig tree is grown mainly for its fruit, and yet its leaves are highly fragrant. I chose it as a nod to the Noninos looking twice at something everyone already used. There's nothing on top: the grape reaches you first.

5. When you next see where someone's heading, show them what you saw, not just where you think they'll end up, the way the Noninos, as the family tells it, printed their working on the label: the evening they stayed behind, the small thing they fixed. Then they aren't carrying your hope. They're holding your reasons, and they can grow into them at their own pace. When they get there, they'll know they did it themselves. And you won't be the only one who can see it anymore.
