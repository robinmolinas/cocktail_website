## Conflicts and fences
| ID | Note |
|---|---|
| C1 | **Yield:** Oxford (F13) says the Persian was bred for *greater* juice yield than the key lime; Heugel (F8) says key limes' yield "is really high". Possibly per price or per weight, but no page says so. Never state that key limes yield more. |
| C2 | **Count:** the PUNCH subtitle says "six spirits experts", the body "five experts" (six names appear, one a gin brand). Give no count. |
| C3 | **Not on any page read:** Anvil's opening year; the full name "Anvil Bar & Refuge"; whether Heugel still runs it today. Newest library word on him is c. 2014. Any present tense about him is dated. |
| C4 | **Other people on the page stay unnamed (Wren):** the lemon-slice line and the lemon quinine fix are Toby Cecchini's; the no-price-rise line is Toby Maloney's. No lemon drink is Heugel's beyond F7. |
| C5 | **Point 5 (Wren's test):** both library mentions show growth (owner of "many" / "a slew" of bars) and call him "prominent". That fails "no promotion story" to the letter. What the page does show: in 2014 he was already "Bartender & Owner" and still handed the trade's trick to readers. That is our reading, not his motive. |

## Extension, round 3 (2026-10-01): the Rickey, for Tomás's drink
Sources: **S7** Oxford Companion, RICKEY (pdf 1661-1664). **S8** Wondrich, *Imbibe!*, pdf 117-119. **S9** Regan, *The Joy of Mixology*, pdf 376 (library copy is the revised edition: "Copyright © 2003, 2018", pdf 6; the quoted text is read in the 2018 edition only). **S10** *Cocktail Codex*, printed p. 115 ("The Balance: Citrus Juice").

| ID | Fact (our words) | Source | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | The classic Rickey is spirit, lime, soda and ice, with no sugar (Oxford: "No sugar is added to the classic Rickey", occasionally a scant amount in variations). Traditionally bourbon, rye or gin; half a lime squeezed and the shell dropped in. | S7 pdf 1661 | Our drink drops no shell: our method, not the classic's. |
| R2 | Wondrich's description of the drink as first made: the juice of half a lime, "or a whole one if the limes were small". | S8 pdf 118 | Wondrich's narration, undated, no source given for this line. The oldest amount on any page I read, and it counts limes by size. |
| R3 | The *Brooklyn Eagle*'s Washington man (1892, via Wondrich) on how it's made: lime juice squeezed into a goblet of crushed ice, then whiskey or gin "to suit the taste", then soda. The "[half]" before "a lime" is Wondrich's bracket. | S8 pdf 118-119 | So the 1892 amount isn't certain. |
| R4 | Rickey's own handwritten 1895 recipe (Syracuse paper) gives "LIME JUICE" with no amount. | S8 pdf 118; S7 pdf 1662 | |
| R5 | Wondrich: "Half a normal-size lime is plenty"; he quotes the *Philadelphia Times* (1895) calling cracked ice an abomination in any drink not taken through a straw, and says to use a cube or three. | S8 pdf 119 | **Conflicts with Regan's crushed ice** (S9). Tomás's call; never "the traditional crushed ice". |
| R6 | Regan: the secret of the Rickey is the amount of lime juice; many bartenders serve only a highball with one wedge squeezed. Recipe: ½ oz lime, 1½ oz bourbon, rye or Old Tom gin, 3-4 oz soda, built in a wine glass of crushed ice. He adds that he isn't a fan of the drink. | S9 | Regan's opinion, 2018 text. |
| R7 | *Codex*: lemons and limes "tend to be fairly consistent", but taste the juice anyway; and as a rule of thumb, with many exceptions, the authors pair lemon with aged spirits (bourbon, scotch, cognac) and lime with unaged. | S10 p. 115 | A rule of thumb, not a law: keep "many exceptions" if it's ever said. |

**Fenced (never in guest text):** Rickey was a lobbyist and Shoomaker's a politicians' bar (politics: Wren's Out) · his sugar "heats the blood" line (a health claim; Wondrich calls it "probably bunk") · his death in 1903 (S8 pdf 118) · lemon before lime in the Rickey: Oxford (pdf 1663) says Rickey added lemon and the bar switched to lime, which he disliked, while Wondrich has lime from the start. **Conflict: keep both out.**

| ID | Conflict |
|---|---|
| C6 | **Size isn't quality.** Heugel says packer's cases are cheaper because the sizes vary (F5), and, separately, that 2014's limes were poor in quality (F7). No page says the odd-sized limes are as good as, or better than, the sorted ones. Never "judged on quality, not looks" as his. |
