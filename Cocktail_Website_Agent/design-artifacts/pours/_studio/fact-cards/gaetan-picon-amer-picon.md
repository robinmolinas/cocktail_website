# Fact card: Gaëtan Picon and Amer Picon

Written 2026-09-29 by Hester, for caregiver-magician (The Healer).

- F1. Gaëtan Picon, 1809–1882; born in Genoa under temporary French control; raised in Marseille; apprenticed in distilleries in the south of France. Oxford PICON OR AMER PICON, pdf 1509.
- F2. Joined the Armée d'Afrique in 1830, which "executed the French colonization of Algeria" (Oxford's wording). pdf 1509.
- F3. Caught a tropical fever there, like many French soldiers, and was treated with quinine-infused wine and other bitters, "as was traditional". pdf 1509.
- F4. By Oxford's account, this "prompted him in 1837" to try to develop a better-tasting, more effective tonic (the cause is Oxford's; the motive isn't on record in his words; "more effective" is a health claim, not for the reading). pdf 1509.
- F5. 1840 formula: bitter and sweet orange peels plus gentian, quinquina, rhubarb. He left the army and opened a small distillery in Philippeville (modern Skikda). pdf 1509.
- F6. "Amer Africain" consumed in quantity across French Algeria; two more distilleries. pdf 1509.
- F7. First international medal: London Great Exhibition 1862. pdf 1509.
- F8. Reached France after the Franco-Prussian war, brought by soldiers from Algeria; Marseille distillery and renamed Amer Picon in 1872 (trademarked 1872, created 1840: Oxford APOTHECARY pdf 123). pdf 1509.
- F9. 1909: four distilleries in France, about three million oranges a year, all from Algeria. pdf 1509.
- F10. Proof: 40% at first → 38% (1920s) → 27% (1970s) → 18% (1989). Owned by Diageo; Picon Bière (21%) for mixing with beer. pdf 1509–1510.
- F11. Brooklyn (Grohusko, 1908): 5 ml Amer Picon; the modern bottle is "altered" and unavailable in America. Oxford BROOKLYN COCKTAIL pdf 360–361.
- F12. Modern Amer Picon is "less bitter and flavorful"; Bigallet China-China Amer is "crafted with the classic Amer Picon recipe in mind" (*Codex* p. 87); CioCiaro is a substitute (*Codex* p. 260). LI p. 136 table: Amer Picon 15% ABV, 20 g/100 ml sugar.
- F13 (secondary). Picon Punch: Amer Picon, 1 tsp grenadine, soda, lemon peel, 1/2–1 oz brandy; it originated in Basque American communities and travelled east to the Basque Country. S. Rosemann, Smithsonian Center for Folklife and Cultural Heritage blog, 2016-08-31.
- C1. Unverified: Picon Punch in a San Francisco boardinghouse, c. 1899, and "Italian" or "1837 creation" from web summaries. Not used.
- F13 (verified 2026-09-29): Picon Punch method per the Smithsonian page: fill an old-fashioned glass with ice; Amer Picon and grenadine; soda water; "float brandy and lemon peel on top".
- C2. Current strength conflicts: Oxford 18% (since 1989, pdf 1510) vs *Liquid Intelligence* p. 136's table at 15.0% ABV, 20 g/100 ml sugar. Don't state a current figure in a reading.
