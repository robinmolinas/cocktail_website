# Fact card: Alfred Barnard and *The Whisky Distilleries of the United Kingdom*

Verified 2026-10-02 by Hester for sage-ruler (The Scientist). Library = primary; every line read on the page in the same session. Quotes are under 15 words; paraphrase for any reading.

## Facts
| id | fact | source | tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | Barnard (1837–1918), an English journalist, "is regarded as the first 'whisky writer'". Keep "regarded as". | Oxford BARNARD, ALFRED, pdf 211 | primary |
| F2 | In 1885 he was commissioned by *Harper's Weekly Gazette*, a London journal "devoted to the interests of the wine, spirit, and brewing trade", to tour all of the whisky distilleries. The commission is the aim; no page counts the distilleries he reached. | pdf 211 | primary |
| F3 | The reports were gathered as *The Whisky Distilleries of the United Kingdom*, "published two years later" (1887). Other entries date it 1887 too (pdf 402, 1079, 2153, 2177). | pdf 211 | primary |
| F4 | The tour took in Ireland: in 1887 he found 28 distilleries working in Ireland, only five with Coffey stills (pdf 2153); 18 in what is now the republic, 10 in Northern Ireland (pdf 1079). Oxford's "in Britain" (pdf 211) and "of Great Britain" (pdf 1884) are loose; the title says United Kingdom. Say "Britain and Ireland". | pdf 2153, 1079 | primary |
| F5 | The detail: his writing is notable "for its precision in detailing the minutiae of much of the equipment within each of the distilleries". Note "much of", not all. | pdf 211 | primary |
| F6 | The gap: "the virtual absence of any mention of casks". This is Oxford's (Dave Broom's) characterisation of the book; the book itself has not been read here. Paraphrase: "his book hardly mentions the casks". Never "never mentions", never "missed". | pdf 211 | primary (single source; primary text unread) |
| F7 | Still counted from: later entries measure change against his 1887 figures (pdf 1079: 18 → 3, 10 → 2; pdf 2153); his still-house illustrations are captioned "as Alfred Barnard found it in the 1880s" (pdf 2150 Power's, Dublin; pdf 2168 Bowmore); "landmark survey" (pdf 2177); "the earliest and most accurate account" and "the template for numerous books" (pdf 211); reprinted 1987 (pdf 212). | pdf 211, 212, 1079, 2150, 2153, 2168, 2177 | primary |
| F8 | Warmth: "persnickety love of detail" beside "a gregarious man of not inconsiderable humor"; he "peppers his accounts with evocative descriptions of his journey"; the book reads as "a fascinating travelogue". | pdf 211 | primary |
| F9 | Not independent: "ultimately a jobbing freelance journalist" who also wrote smaller publications for individual distilleries and blending houses. | pdf 211 | primary |
| F10 | Later: 1889–1891, a second tour, *The Noted Breweries of Britain and Ireland* (beer: never the drink). | pdf 211 | primary |

## Conflicts (keep out of any reading)
- C1 **Why the casks are missing.** Oxford BARNARD (pdf 211) reads the absence as casks being thought "unimportant" then. Oxford PAXARETE (pdf 1483, same author) says nineteenth-century producers "greatly prized" ex-sherry casks, and paxarete-treated casks became "standard practice" from the late century. So no reason is told. "We don't know why" is honest; no mystery, no secret.
- C2 **Year of the book.** Text and four entries: 1887. Oxford's bibliography (pdf 212) cites an 1889 edition. Use 1887; never call 1889 the first.
- C3 **Scope word.** "Britain" (pdf 211, 1884) vs Irish counts (pdf 1079, 2153) and the title. Use "Britain and Ireland" or the title.

## Guards
- G1 Never pair the gap with paxarete or "distillers already prized casks" in guest text: it turns an absence into a miss.
- G2 Never "everyone trusts it" or "the most careful record anyone made": the page's level is "earliest and most accurate" (attributed) and "later writers count from his figures".
- G3 Leave out (Wren r1): Caol Ila, Lagavulin, the blending houses, Ireland's nationalist movement, Michael Jackson (pdf 1091 compares his 1990 catalogue to Barnard's: out), and distillery names from F9.
- G4 "Scientist" is not his label.
