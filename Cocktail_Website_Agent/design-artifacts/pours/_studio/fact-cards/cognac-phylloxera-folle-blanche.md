## Conflicts and guards

- **C1 Who found Cognac's rootstock.** Oxford names nobody. The Handbook credits Munson (with Jaeger) for European wine in general and never mentions Cognac, the Charentes or chalk. Tell it as Oxford does: a rootstock found in Texas after more than ten years. Munson can stand as one of the men who collected wild American vines and sent rootstock to France, with Jaeger named. Never "Munson saved Cognac", never "the man who saved French wine".
- **C2 "His hybrid rootstock".** Only the Handbook says the French asked for hybrids he had developed; O1 shows him selling wild-species seed. Don't say what kind: "rootstock" or "vines".
- **C3 Why Folle Blanche went.** pdf 545: unsuited to grafting because its tight bunches rotted. pdf 139: fragile and hard to graft. Say "didn't do well grafted; its bunches grew so tight they rotted". Never "rejected the graft".
- **C4 Not lost.** Folle Blanche is about 5% of Armagnac, still allowed in cognac, and a parent of baco blanc. Never "lost", "died out", "gone", "couldn't be saved" as plain fact. It gave way **in Cognac**.
- **C5 The honour's name.** "A French honour", 1888, Denison.
- **C6 The louse's American origin** rests on the Handbook alone here (Oxford pdf 2071 only says European vines died of it in colonial America). Dossier only.
- **C7 Phylloxera's year conflicts inside Oxford:** ABSINTHE (pdf 49) says the mid-to-late 1850s; ARMAGNAC (pdf 137) has it reach Gascony in 1870; the Handbook says found in 1868. No year for its arrival in the reading.
- **C8 The size of the save.** Cognac was never restored to its 1870s size (F4–F5). Say Cognac is still made, and still France's biggest single-product vineyard; never "restored".

## Round 3 additions (2026-10-04, library only; web budget spent)
- **G1 Bottles.** No library page names a single-varietal Folle Blanche spirit, and blanche Armagnac (pdf 138, a category since 2005) has no grapes on the page. A generic young Armagnac is mostly Ugni Blanc and baco (55% and 35% of the wine distilled, pdf 139), so it isn't "Folle Blanche" by default. Single-varietal Folle Blanche armagnacs are memory-grade only: never name one from this card.
- **G2 "Picquepoult".** Oxford gives it as a Gascon name for Folle Blanche (pdf 139); Noilly Prat's "picpoul" (pdf 1395) is a Languedoc wine grape. No page says whether they are the same variety. Never print "picquepoult" or "picpoul" in a recipe or reading: say Folle Blanche.
- **G3 Gros Plant du Pays Nantais:** not in the library ("no hits"). Unsourced.
- **G4 Verjus is taken as a spark:** *Are You Quite Sure?* (sage-innocent) swaps the citrus for verjus in a grape-brandy drink in a coupe, epigraph "The sour comes from the grape itself". *Codex* p. 91 (Sonoma: verjus, honey, Calvados) and p. 187 (Four to the Floor) use it too.
- **G5 "Acid doesn't distil"** is an absolute no library page supports: Oxford names volatile acids that do carry over (FERMENTATION pdf 765–766). Keep the physics out of the reading unless Tomás sources it for tartaric acid specifically.
