# Fact card: Floc de Gascogne (and baco blanc)

Hester, 2026-10-01, for outlaw-innocent (Tomás's Floc syrup). Robin's note this session: keep web research lean.

| # | Fact | Source | Tier | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | A mistelle: grape juice "in its first stage of fermentation" mixed with Armagnac; white or rosé. Typically 2/3 to 3/4 juice, 1/3 to 1/4 Armagnac (≥52%). Name coined 1954, from Occitan "Lou Floc", "bouquet of flowers". AOC 1990. | Oxford, FLOC DE GASCOGNE (pdf 818) | primary | |
| F2 | **Same estate is the law, not just "often":** the Armagnac used "provient de la même exploitation que les moûts". | Cahier des charges AOC Floc de Gascogne, Mutage §2 (INAO text, copy hosted by sommelier-academy.de, 2019) | primary (law), copy via third party | Oxford says "often coming from the same property". Say "from the same estate". |
| F3 | **Strength conflict:** cahier: 16–18% at bottling. Oxford: "some 18–20 percent". | Cahier §2, §4c; Oxford pdf 818 | conflict | Use the law: 16–18%. Tomás's maths should run at 17%. |
| F4 | **No legal sugar figure for the finished Floc.** The only sugar rule: must at ≥170 g/L when the spirit goes in; musts may have started fermenting; no enrichment, no earlier-harvest must. | Cahier §2a | primary | Any g/L figure for Floc is unsourced or ours. A search summary gave "≤3 g/L". That line is about wines in the 2026 INAO Document Unique, not about Floc's residual sugar. |
| F5 | **Makeability abroad unproven.** About a third is sold at the cellar door, mostly locally, and another third in French shops. Of the French mistelles, Pineau des Charentes is "the only one widely known abroad". | Oxford pdf 818; MISTELLE pdf 1315 | primary | No export or retail page was fetched. |
| F6 | "Increasingly viewed as a cocktail enhancer", mostly drunk on the rocks; served at 4.5–7 °C. | Oxford pdf 819 | primary | |
| B1 | Baco blanc (baco 22A): the only hybrid grape allowed in the French AOC system (folle blanche × Noah); about 35% of the wine distilled for Armagnac. | Oxford, ARMAGNAC (pdf 139) | primary | |
| B2 | The INAO had planned in 1992 to have all baco vines grubbed up by 2010. An AOC decree let it stay. The BNIA called baco "the only grape variety unique to Armagnac". | *Harpers*, "Baco to the future" (undated page; c. 2005–2010) | secondary (trade press) | **On the page:** a ruling after lobbying. No refusal, no named person, no price paid. Fails Wren's points 1 and 3. |
