## Conflicts and fences (keep out of any reading)
- C1 **"Sir".** Wikipedia's (unfootnoted) article titles Alexander I "Sir Alexander Walker"; Oxford gives "Sir" only to the son (pdf 667). Never "Sir" for the letter-writer.
- C2 **Trademark year and the square bottle.** Oxford: "by the mid-1860s", bottles round and square. Wikipedia: 1867, square bottle late 1870s. No year, and never credit him with designing the bottle or label.
- C3 **Recipient of the 1885 letter: unknown.** Never "to his son", "to a partner", "to a friend".
- C4 **The disdain is undated and belongs to "the Walker family".** The nickname runs through the 1880s and 1890s, past Alexander's death (1889). Never say he heard it, minded it, or fought it.
- C5 **Spelling and referent.** The nickname is "Johnny Walker"; the 1908 figure and today's brand are "Johnnie Walker". The page doesn't say the nickname was applied to the whisky (rather than the firm or the founder), or that the brand was "renamed". The page doesn't say who coined it either: never "the public gave it" (my own r2 shorthand, struck r3). Safe: "a nickname, 'Johnny Walker', stepped in"; "today the label carries that nickname, spelled 'Johnnie'" (Stevenson persuaded the business "otherwise", and the 1908 figure is "Johnnie Walker", pdf 1112).
- C6 **The doubling is his impression.** "It seems … I feel sometimes": keep both. The measured figures are fourfold over 1865–1880, then "exploded". Never "it doubled every year".
- C7 **After 1889 is not his.** Cardhu (1893), the 1906 range, the 1908 figure, the 1924 merger: "the company", never "he" or "his".
- C8 **No grain whisky or Coffey still** (the Alchemist's, `aeneas-coffey.md`; the 1908 commission is *Either Way*'s, `whisky-commission-1908.md`). F14's "plus grain whisky" stays a definition, never a story.

- C9 **pdf 52's "aggregate fruit" botany is also in *The Other Berry*** (creator-jester), but only in its anchors and name notes; its guest text never tells it (grep, r3). So "many tiny fruits carried as one" may stand. Never "not really a berry" or "the other berry" (that pour's angle and name).

## Our readings (never facts)
- R1 The runaway "took the founder's name": Wren's reading of F9 + F10 (the gap left by no advertising was filled by a nickname the family felt slighted the founder).
- R2 The 1886 company as the "second head" he wished for: inference from F5 + F6, a year apart. Signpost it ("I like to think…"); the page gives no cause.
- R3 The letter's tone (rueful, wry, funny): ours. The page only quotes it.
- R4 "The joy survived": true of the business (F7, F8, F12); his feelings after 1885 are not on record.
- R5 He wasn't new at anything in 1885: he had made the whisky a brand two decades before (F2–F4). "Starting before you're ready" belongs to the guest, never to him.
