# Fact card: Bat Masterson and "Colonel" Dick Plunkett, Waldorf-Astoria café, 22 June 1906

Hester, 2026-10-01, for ruler-caregiver (The Caring Leader), checking Wren's r1 lead. **Verdict: the calm-host version is a legend; the 1906 press contradicts it.** Kept as a card so it is never re-researched or told.

Tiers: 1906 newspapers read as page OCR via the YPL newspapers.com API (primary, OCR quality uneven); DeArment's two biographies and DeMattos via archive.org full-text OCR highlights (secondary, lending-only, highlights may be cut off); *Joy* (Regan) secondary relay of Crockett.

## Facts
| ID | Fact | Source |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | Regan's version: Masterson "once emptied the bar by grabbing Colonel Dick Plunkett, a fellow U.S. marshal, by the throat and accusing him of 'talking bad' about him". "The situation came to nothing, though; Plunkett remained calm and offered Masterson a seat, a drink, and the chance to talk things over." No source given on the page beyond the chapter's use of Crockett. | *Joy* pdf 32 |
| F2 | Date: the evening of 22 June 1906, in the Waldorf-Astoria café. | *New-York Tribune* 23 Jun 1906 p. 1; DeArment, *Gunfighter in Gotham* (2013), OCR highlight ("On the evening of June 22") |
| F3 | Tribune: Masterson "grabbed the colonel by the coat" and, with "Let's go outside", started to drag him out. Plunkett "was inclined to be submissive". His companion, E. Dinklesheet, struck Masterson and was then floored; the house detective, Smith, intervened and Masterson left; "the colonel and his friend were taken out" by another door. | *New-York Tribune* 23 Jun 1906 p. 1 |
| F4 | Evening World: Plunkett had "proceeded to berate" Masterson to a Judge E. F. Colburn, calling him a coward and saying he'd been "chased out of Denver". When Masterson came in, he "tried to pull the Colonel from [his] chair, but he seemed glued to" it; Masterson knocked down Plunkett's friend ("Dinkelspiel" in this paper); in the panic, Plunkett "suddenly remembered he had an engagement elsewhere". | *The Evening World* 23 Jun 1906 p. 5 (OCR garbled in places) |
| F5 | DeArment: "A man calling himself Colonel Dick Plunkett of Tombstone and Creede" arrived in June 1906 with a Texas editor; Plunkett "loudly proclaimed" Masterson "a phony, a fraud, and a fake". Elsewhere: Richard D. Plunkett, whom Bat had known as an officer at Creede. | DeArment, *Bat Masterson: The Man and the Legend* (1979); *Gunfighter in Gotham* (2013), OCR highlights |
| F6 | Masterson was then a U.S. (deputy) marshal (the Tribune: "United States marshal"). Nothing read makes Plunkett a U.S. marshal in 1906. | Tribune 23 Jun 1906 |

## Cautions
| ID | Caution |
|---|---|
| C1 | **Never tell the seat, the drink and the talk.** No 1906 account read has them; the man was the provoker (F4, F5), submissive or "glued" to his chair, and left in the panic. Regan's line is a later softening (perhaps Crockett's 1931 telling, not read: lending-only and not found). |
| C2 | "Fellow U.S. marshal" (F1) is unsupported (F6). |
| C3 | Guns ("ugly piece of artillery", the hand in the pocket) and a man knocked down: out of guest text in any case. |
