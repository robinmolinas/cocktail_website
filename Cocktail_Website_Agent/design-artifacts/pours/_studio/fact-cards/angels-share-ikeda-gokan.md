# Angel's Share (New York), Shinichi Ikeda, Shingo Gokan — and shochu

Card by Hester, 2026-10-04, for lover-magician (The Charmer). Read on the page this session.

## Sources
- **[PR]** Simonson, *A Proper Drink* (2016), ch. IV "Angels in the East Village", printed pp. 47–50 (pdf 61–64). Reliable secondary; quotes on the record. Interview dates from the source list: Gokan 17 Oct 2014 (p. 331), Ikeda 13 Feb 2015 (p. 332).
- **[OX]** Oxford Companion, SHOCHU (pdf 1784–1786). Primary (library).

## Facts (page's own words in quotes, under 15 words)
| ID | Fact | Source |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | Simonson's judgement: no 1990s New York opening was "more important to the future of the cocktail revival". Scope: the future of the revival, not "the best bar". | PR p. 47 |
| F2 | Same sentence: so "sui generis and willfully isolated" that the movement it helped inspire largely eclipsed and forgot it. Simonson's words, not anyone's stated motive. | PR p. 47 |
| F3 | Second floor, behind a door at the back of a Japanese restaurant, East Village. | PR p. 47 |
| F4 | The owner (unnamed on these pages) wanted a Tokyo-style bar: "small, intimate, and formal", emphasis on perfect drinks and service. | PR p. 47 |
| F5 | "It opened without notice in 1994." Simonson's narration; no one is quoted choosing it. | PR p. 48 |
| F6 | Ikeda got behind the bar two years later (so 1996). He had come from Japan, where he'd been a bartender, to play music. | PR p. 48 |
| F7 | Ikeda's own account: "they didn't know how to run the bar"; one day the owner told him to run it; "Somehow, he knew" Ikeda had tended bar in Japan. Interview 2015. | PR p. 48, 332 |
| F8 | He retrained the bartenders to stir Manhattans and Martinis as he'd learned in Japan, added classics, and originals "with Japanese ingredients like shochu and shiso leaf". **No shochu Martini, and no straining of shiso, is on the page.** | PR pp. 48–49 |
| F9 | No small talk ("partly because" many bartenders had limited English), no standing, no large parties. | PR p. 49 |
| F10 | Later: the owner didn't expand, none of its bartenders became stars, no cocktail book; "insular and inward-looking". | PR p. 49 |
| F11 | "But the refusal to reach out went in both directions." (Simonson) — the community didn't reach in either. Paraphrase in any reading. | PR p. 49 |
| F12 | Gokan, "arguably the best-known bartender" it produced, joined after moving from Tokyo in 2006; never felt part of the cocktail community. | PR pp. 49–50 |
| F13 | Gokan (interview 2014): there eight years; for the first six, no friends who were bartenders; "English is not my native language. We were always outside." "We" is the crew, not only him. | PR p. 50, 331 |
| S1 | Shochu: clear Japanese spirit, most often from sweet potato, barley or rice; koji-driven ferment. | OX pdf 1784 |
| S2 | Two camps: neutral multiply distilled (kōrui) and aromatic singly distilled pot-still honkaku; both "generally" 25% ABV. Max bottling 45% honkaku, 35% kōrui. | OX pdf 1784, 1786 |
| S3 | In the US much shochu is wrongly labelled "soju" (to sell on a beer-and-wine licence in California). Korean soju is a different spirit. | OX pdf 1784 |
| S4 | 1559 is the earliest explicit reference: graffiti two carpenters "apparently hid" under a shrine roof (Koriyama Hachiman, Kagoshima), complaining the priest never once gave them shochu. When it was uncovered is not on the page. | OX pdf 1784–1785 |

## Cautions
- C1: Petraske's visit, the olives on the side, the barback kicked (p. 49), Uyeda and "the way of the cocktail" (p. 48), Wondrich's "closed shop" are fenced (family plan, *Not Only the Way*).
- C2: No present tense about the bar: Proper is 2016; whether it's open today, and where, is unchecked. Gokan's later career isn't on these pages.
- C3: "Far from home", "handed him the bar", "the making of the place", "the card he hadn't played" are readings, not facts. Signpost them.
- C4: "No stars" sits beside "arguably the best-known" (F10/F12): never an absolute.
- C5: Don't gloss the bar's name ("what escapes the barrels" is *The First Guess*).
