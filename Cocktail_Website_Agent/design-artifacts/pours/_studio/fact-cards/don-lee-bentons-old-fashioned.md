# Fact card: Don Lee and the Benton's Old-Fashioned

Hester, 2026-10-01, for explorer-outlaw (*The Trailblazer*). Every row opened on the page this session. Tiers: *A Proper Drink* (Simonson; `proper`, printed pages) and Oxford = primary (library); PUNCH = secondary (trade press, named speakers quoted). ## Facts

| # | Fact | Source (tier) |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | Lee and John Deragon were "refugees from the tech world"; Lee ended up "as an IT administrator"; both eGullet regulars, met at Pegu Club to swap bottles. | Proper p. 165 |
| F2 | Lee rented a car and drove to New Jersey for bonded Laird's; four kinds of crème de violette when most bars had none; Friday cocktail parties at his Chinatown apartment ($5 entry). | Proper pp. 165–166 |
| F3 | The pair were Pegu's "favorite guinea pigs" for bartenders' experiments (Saunders sometimes disliked it). | Proper p. 165 |
| F4 | Six months into Death & Co, Phil Ward asked Lee and Deragon to take over the bar for a night (a staff party). Simonson's narration: "no civilians in New York" knew more about cocktails. Meehan came "to see the train wreck" (Deragon); then offered them work at PDT. | Proper pp. 268–269 |
| F5 | At first Meehan was only a consultant and put the bar "largely in the hands of" Lee and Deragon; they "were left to their own devices". PDT was on St. Mark's Place, next to Crif Dogs. | Proper pp. 269–270 |
| F6 | Monday nights (an industry off night) drew other bartenders: their own regulars were now bartending (Lee, paraphrased). | Proper p. 271 |
| F7 | Influenced by chefs Dufresne, Chang and Sam Mason, they tinkered with fat-washing and infusions; popcorn-infused white rum, butter-fat-washed. | Proper p. 271 |
| F8 | They once tried to carbonate cherries; the bottle blew up. People came in to see what the two were up to. | Proper p. 271 |
| F9 | Madrusan's "no stripes. He was a customer" is a **complaint** ("Nobody enjoyed being told what to do by somebody like Don"). | Proper p. 271 |
| F10 | Lee and Deragon did it "mainly for fun", for hourly wages and tips, with outside lives. Meehan returned full-time; "by-the-book" vs "loose-limbed". | Proper p. 272 |
| F11 | Freeman (WD-50) tested Lee on drinks that never reached the menu; fat-washing was "sidelined"; Freeman picked it up from Mason. | F12 | Lee "did not invent fat-washing spirits"; he invented "the drink that made fat-washing famous". | Proper p. 275 |
| F13 | Lee's own telling: Tien Ho, "a regular of ours at PDT", had bacon fat he didn't know what to do with. Freeman's versions were delicious but never on his menu; "So we just put it on the menu." (Lee's guess at Freeman's reasons: hassle and backlash; **a guess, stays out**.) | Proper p. 275 |
| F14 | Momofuku Ssäm Bar let Lee have its leftover bacon fat. | Proper p. 271 |
| F15 | Became PDT's best-known and best-selling cocktail; on menus beyond PDT; inspired fat-washing "with every meat and nut under the sun". | Proper p. 275 |
| F16 | Lee: when it wasn't good, it was nearly always because it wasn't made with Benton's. | Proper pp. 275–276 |
| F17 | Lee "is proud of the cocktail" and "a bit haunted by it": it kills him that it's the drink that "probably follows me to the grave". | Proper p. 276 |
| F18 | Recipe: 2 oz Benton's-fat-washed Four Roses, ¼ oz Grade B maple, 2 dashes Angostura, orange zest; rocks glass, one large cube, stirred. Wash: 1½ oz fat warmed, 750 ml bourbon, 4 h room temp, 2 h freezer, lift fat, strain through cloth. | Proper pp. 275–276 |
