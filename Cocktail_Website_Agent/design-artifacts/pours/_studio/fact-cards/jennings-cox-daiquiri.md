# Fact card: Jennings Cox and the first Daiquiri

Hester, 2026-10-04, for hero-sage (The Problem Solver). Every library line below was read on screen this session: Oxford DAIQUIRI (pdf 621, 623, 624, Wondrich), COCKTAIL (pdf 502), BACARDI (pdf 181–183); *Imbibe!* pdf 240–241; *Cocktail Codex* p. 106. Web 2 of 2 used: archive.org full-text search (OCR highlights only; labelled), one query for the Wolfe wording, one batch of six phrase queries (retellings only, nothing primary beyond Wolfe).

Channel, always carried: the scene of the first pitcher is **the family's account**: Cox's step-granddaughter Carmen Puig, telling it in 1970 to Time-Life cookbook author Linda Wolfe, relayed by Oxford. It is not Cox's own words. Motive lines (the worry) are fact only as "the family story goes".

## Conflicts

| id | conflict | ruling |
| --- | --- | --- |
| C1 | Year: Oxford 1896 (pdf 621); *Codex*'s legend 1898 (p. 106); archive.org retellings split 1896/1898. | No year in the reading. "In the 1890s" passes. |
| C2 | Who named it: Linthicum, via Oxford, says the San Carlos bartender in 1908; Woon (1928) says Cox himself; *Codex*'s legend has him naming it that night after the beach. | Never say who named it, or when. |
| C3 | "Twelve years" counts from the contested year (C1) and comes from accounts whose details vary (pdf 623). | "For years, the old newspaper accounts say, he just called it his rum sour." No number. |
| C4 | Setting: pdf 621 places only the limes (his garden); retellings say Santiago or a party. | "At home" at most, through the garden; no party, no Santiago. |
| C5 | "It was an immediate success": no speaker on pdf 621. | Paraphrase, attributed to the accounts. |

## Not on any page (signpost or keep out)
- The clock (how long the visitors waited), any hurry: not stated. Implied by "for some American visitors" only.
- Cox's pleasure in solving it: not on record.
- That the visitors never saw his worry: our reading ("I like to think").
- That he was proud, relieved or modest: nothing.

## Guards for the room
- G1 Never "by accident"; he improvised (F4). Oxford's "fluke" is its word, not ours.
- G2 "The family story goes" covers F4–F7 wherever restated (re-signpost per paragraph).
- G3 "Made, or at least supervised" (F2).
- G4 Lyman's group credit: one clause (F8).
- G5 Not "moves on the moment the riddle's done": he kept introducing it (F11).
- G6 Naming motif: *Just Knew* (explorer-magician) owns "You just never called it that" and a closing line "name one habit". *Before It Had a Name* is outlaw-ruler's title. A position about naming must not echo either.
- G7 Constante, Floridita, Hemingway, Kennedy, frozen Daiquiri, navy doctors: fenced (plan; `constante-ribalaigua-floridita.md`).
- G8 Bacardi Carta Blanca stays in the story; today's Bacardi isn't Cuban (F14, F15).
