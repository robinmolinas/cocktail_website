## Conflicts
- **C1 Dates (ruled 2026-09-30).** Oxford: office boy by twenty-one (so by 1813), desk clerk and bartender 1816, julep lesson 1816, retires 1836 "after twenty years behind the bar", back 1843, out 1848. Imbibe pdf 22: at the City Hotel from 1811, soon behind the bar, julep lesson 1817 (a Buffalo reporter in 1851), "a brief interlude" of retirement, at the bar until 1848, finally home in 1849. **Agree:** 1792, 1848 as the end at the bar, 1876, the numbered doors, one retirement and a return. **Disagree:** the start year (1811 vs 1813/1816), the julep year, whether the final move home was 1848 or 1849, and when he moved into the numbered house (Oxford: bought 1836; Imbibe: the 1849 home). **Ruling:** the reading may say he left the bar in 1848, that he retired once and came back when the hotel had failed (1843, Oxford only, fine to use), and nothing else by date. No "twenty years", no "forty years", no dated doors.
- **C3 Which recipes are "his".** Imbibe pdf 175 says the captain recorded "four of the Great One's recipes", the Cocktail among them. The captain's own page (p. 300) names Willard only for the Apple-toddy. So the Cocktail, Sling and Julep being Willard's is Wondrich's attribution; say "Wondrich credits them to Willard" or leave it. A sibling may cite the 1833 Cocktail as the captain's record without calling it Willard's.
- **C2 The julep lesson** (1816 vs 1817, "probably impossible to corroborate", Oxford). Legend-grade; out anyway (the Romantic's julep).

## Guards for any pour
- Never name Cato Alexander (the Pure Heart's lead). And **never call the captain "Alexander" alone**: in a family whose sibling tells Cato Alexander's story, the surname reads as Cato. Say "a British army captain".
- Death, "assumed he was no more", the grave: out (Wren).
- Ice pioneering and the julep: out (*Half a Rim*, the Romantic). A single "lump of ice" in his toddy is his recipe, not clear-ice craft.
