| F8 | Absinthe: neutral spirit flavoured with wormwood (*Artemisia absinthium*) and aniseed, other botanicals supporting. | F9 | The louche: French for cloudy, shadowy and, by extension, disreputable. Water pulls the anise oil out of solution and the drink turns opalescent. | F10 | The drip: ice-cold water added slowly, in a thin stream; slow is the point, letting the oils come out of solution more fully. About five parts water to one of absinthe, the ratio on old labels (Oxford's recipe: 30 ml absinthe, 150 ml water, in a glass of at least 240 ml). Drunk leisurely, like wine. Best glass: a stemmed water goblet or wine glass, which shows the opalescence, best in daylight. | Oxford, ABSINTHE DRIP (pdf 52); ABSINTHE (pdf 50) | primary | A gesture for the Checks. |
| F13 | Sweetening is optional. ## Legends / cautions
- **C1 The US ban year.** Oxford: 1912. *A Proper Drink*: "effectively banned… in 1915". **Contested: no year in the reading.**
- **C2 "Eiffel designed the distillery."** *A Proper Drink* p. 245 only (single secondary source). Keep out, or label as "the story goes".
- **C3 Hallucinations / thujone madness.** Myth (Oxford pdf 50–51). Never repeat it as fact, even as colour.
- **C4 "He ended the ban."** No (F7).
- **C5 His motive.** Justice on the record (F5). Love, devotion, "fell for": our reading, signposted.
- **C7 "Always said".** His injustice motive is on record once (2014 interview). "He says", not "he has always said".
- **C6 Living person.** Breaux is alive; nothing beyond what he said on the record.
