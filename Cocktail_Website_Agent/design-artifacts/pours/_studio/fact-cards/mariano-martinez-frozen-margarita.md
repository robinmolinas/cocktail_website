## Conflicts and limits

- **C1 Year.** 1971 (Oxford MARGARITA pdf 1237; NMAH; AP; *VinePair*, "May 11, 1971") vs 1973 (Oxford FROZEN DAIQUIRI pdf 854). Oxford contradicts itself: no year in the reading. "In the early 1970s" satisfies both.
- **C2 The Slurpee's own origin.** NMAH says the Slurpee machine was "invented in Dallas in 1960"; I can't square that with its usual history and have no trusted page on it. Keep the Slurpee's origin out; say only that the idea came from one at a 7-Eleven.
- **C3 Inspiration vs machine.** The Slurpee was the idea; the machine he adapted was a soft-serve ice cream machine (Sani-Serv). Never "he adapted a Slurpee machine".
- **C4 Who built it.** His own "we developed" (F15). One maker isn't the maker: "he adapted" is Oxford's and the museum's verb, fine; never "alone".

## Legends / out
- "Invented the frozen Margarita": no. The blender Margarita predates him (F3). He made the machine.
- "It improved the Margarita": no page says so; Oxford says the reverse. Never.
- Oxford's sniff (crowd pleaser, fruit flavours, the grain-alcohol scandal, "lazy bartending"): out.
