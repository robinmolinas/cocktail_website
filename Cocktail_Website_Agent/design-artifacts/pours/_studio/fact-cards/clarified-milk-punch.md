## Cautions
- Sibling motifs: clarity/cloudiness is already *Half a Rim* (clear ice) and *Rosetta* (the louche). A clarified drink would echo both.
- Contains dairy (washed out, but whey remains): not veto-free.

## Cautions added round 3
- **Which way the milk goes.** Arnold: add the liquor to the milk, not the other way round (LI p. 271). The old recipes do the opposite: Franklin puts boiling milk into the punch; Oxford's Rockett adaptation pours scalding milk into the cognac. "Punch into milk" is **Arnold's modern rule**, never "Rockett's gesture".
- **"Strain again through the same curds"** is Oxford's printed adaptation; not verified as Rockett's 1711 wording. The 18th-century instruction on record is Franklin's: run it through a jelly-bag "till it is clear" (LI p. 267).
- **"Keeps for years"** is a rounding. Oxford: clarification made "indefinite storage without refrigeration possible"; bottles were in Dickens's cellar at his death (no age given). Never claim it of *our* punch (tea, tamarind, ~16%); LI p. 267: milk-washed booze "doesn't go bad" but its whey loses foaming power in about a week.
- **LI p. 265** (for "more itself"): washing moderates the harshness, "leaving the rest of the tea flavor intact". But p. 266: proteins "also strip color and some flavors". "The milk leaves acid and sugar alone" is Tomás's inference.
