| F5 | Oxford calls him "a hungover chef at a rural airport". | Oxford pdf 1769 | primary | Tone only; Oxford's aside, no evidence given. Signpost if used. |
| F6 | He is credited: Oxford writes "Sheridan's 'Gaelic Coffee'", and revival bars make it "the way Sheridan did". | pdf 1076–1077 | primary | So "never credited" is false. Only the drink's *name* lacks his. |
| F7 | Original serve: footed glass goblet, 180 ml; sugar cubes; fresh cream. Oxford recipe: 2 sugar cubes, 45 ml Irish whisky, 75 ml hot coffee, 30 ml cream whipped to double. | pdf 1076–1077 | primary | The recipe is Oxford's modern spec, not attested as Sheridan's own measures. |
| F8 | Oxford's own definition of the drink allows either: hot coffee, "sugar or simple syrup", Irish whisky, whipped cream. So syrup is not outside the drink; what Oxford calls adulteration is syrup *replacing the original cubes*, alongside bigger glasses ("drowned in coffee"), canned cream and green colouring. | Oxford IRISH COFFEE pdf 1075; pdf 1076–1077 | primary | Added 2026-09-30 (rework). The adulteration list is specific; it is not a rule against every addition. |
| F9 | Regan licenses riffs: sweeten with a liqueur (Irish Mist "the natural choice"; Bénédictine also works) and omit the sugar if you do; "just be sure to let the customer know of your intentions"; or change the spirit and rename it (Highland Coffee for scotch). | Joy pdf 307 | primary | Added 2026-09-30. Advice, not Sheridan's practice. Bénédictine contains nutmeg (Oxford BÉNÉDICTINE pdf 256) → `nuts` under STUDIO-RULES 4. Irish Mist: whiskey liqueur with herbs and honey (Joy pdf 421); its formula is proprietary, classify on the safe side. |
| F10 | The *Codex* files Irish Coffee as a Flip variation (cream whipped to soft peaks and floated, not mixed in), and says Flip variations push the template by swapping the base, removing egg or dairy, or changing the sweetener. Its own spec: 60 ml heavy cream, 45 ml Jameson, 22 ml Demerara Gum Syrup, 90 ml coffee, in a warmed mug. | Codex pp. 266, 268 | primary | Added 2026-09-30. |
| F11 | Dead Rabbit's spec as Regan prints it: Clontarf Irish whiskey, rich demerara syrup, hot medium-blend coffee, freshly whipped heavy cream; Jillian Vose prefers 33–35% fat so it floats. | Joy pdf 307–308 | primary | Added 2026-09-30. So even the revival bar Oxford names (F4) uses syrup: "the way Sheridan did" is Oxford's phrase for the bar's pride, not a claim that its recipe matches his. |

## Conflicts
- C1: Joy (Regan) pdf 306 calls him "a bartender at Shannon Airport" who wanted a drink "that would appeal to American tourists". Oxford: chef, Foynes flying-boat station, for touring dignitaries, citing 1944–45 press. Oxford is closer to the evidence; use chef/Foynes with Oxford as source, but no reading may hinge on "not a bartender", and no motive (tourists, or warming anyone) as fact.
