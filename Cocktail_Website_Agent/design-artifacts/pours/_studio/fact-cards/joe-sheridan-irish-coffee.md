| # | Claim (our words) | Source | Tier | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| F1 | Created late 1944 or early 1945 at the Foynes flying-boat station by chef Joe Sheridan (1909–1962), asked to make something special to greet dignitaries touring the site. | Oxford IRISH COFFEE pdf 1075 | primary | Dublin Dolphin Hotel origin unsupported. |
| F2 | First called "Gaelic Coffee"; by 1947 offered to all travellers deplaning at Shannon. | pdf 1076 | primary | |

| F4 | Fame → syrup for sugar cubes, bigger glasses (original footed goblet 180 ml), canned cream; revival bars make it "the way Sheridan did". | pdf 1076–1077 | primary | |
| F5 | Oxford calls him "a hungover chef at a rural airport". | Oxford pdf 1769 | primary | Tone only; Oxford's aside, no evidence given. Signpost if used. |

| F7 | Original serve: footed glass goblet, 180 ml; sugar cubes; fresh cream. Oxford recipe: 2 sugar cubes, 45 ml Irish whisky, 75 ml hot coffee, 30 ml cream whipped to double. | pdf 1076–1077 | primary | The recipe is Oxford's modern spec, not attested as Sheridan's own measures. |

## Conflicts
- C1: Joy (Regan) pdf 306 calls him "a bartender at Shannon Airport" who wanted a drink "that would appeal to American tourists". Oxford: chef, Foynes flying-boat station, for touring dignitaries, citing 1944–45 press. Oxford is closer to the evidence; use chef/Foynes with Oxford as source, but no reading may hinge on "not a bartender", and no motive (tourists, or warming anyone) as fact.

## Struck in the room (caregiver-regular-guy, round 2)
- Hester r1: "for tired strangers arriving in the cold" — that is L1 in other clothes. On record: dignitaries (1944–45), then all travellers deplaning (by 1947).
- Hester r1: "hungover chef" as plain fact — Oxford's aside only.
- Wren r1: "the world put his name on the drink without crediting him" — contradicted by F6.
