# Fact card: Angelica (the botanical)
Verified by Hester, 2026-09-28, for caregiver-innocent (The Angel). Reuse; don't re-research or re-tell differently.

| # | Fact | Source | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | By the late 19th century gin makers had "a general consensus" on botanicals: juniper; "angelica, which boosts the juniper"; coriander for lemony notes; orris "to fix the other botanicals" | Oxford, GIN, pdf 894 | primary |
| F2 | Angelica root and seeds are among the nine botanicals of a widely cited 1895 Beefeater formula (Burrough's formula book) | Oxford, BEEFEATER, pdf 245 | primary |
| F3 | **By 1860** Plymouth redistilled with seven botanicals including angelica root (pdf 1538); today's Plymouth is "a bit more earthy, from the rooty angelica and orris" (pdf 1541). The count "seven" is dated 1860, not today. | Oxford, PLYMOUTH GIN, pdf 1538, 1541 | primary |
| F4 | Tanqueray "purportedly" uses four: juniper, coriander, angelica, licorice (bills are guarded secrets) | Oxford, TANQUERAY GORDON & CO., pdf 1986 | primary, **hedged**: say "reportedly" |
| F5 | Common gin botanicals include angelica | Cocktail Codex p. 66 | primary (names it only; describes nothing) |
| F6 | Bénédictine is made with 27 plants and spices including angelica, hyssop, saffron; honey, saffron and spices are added at the end; 40% ABV | Oxford, BÉNÉDICTINE, pdf 256 | primary |
| F7 | Angelica is listed among typical herbs of herbal liqueurs | Oxford, HERBAL LIQUEUR, pdf 998 | primary |
| F8 | Grown near London "for the use of its candied stems"; used in gin with juniper, in vermouth, and "notably Chartreuse" | Grieve, *A Modern Herbal* (1931), botanical.com | secondary (period book) |
| F10 | Taste on record: Oxford puts Plymouth's earthiness down to "the rooty angelica and orris" (jointly, one gin) | Oxford pdf 1541 | primary; scope: joint, one bottle |
| F9 | Flavor Matrix places angelica beside camomile, lemon balm, milk/cream, honey | Matrix pdf 45, 77, 81 | primary |

## Legends and cautions
| # | Item | Status |
|---|---|---|
| L1 | Name: "revealed in a dream by an angel to cure the plague"; or blooms on St Michael the Archangel's day; "The Root of the Holy Ghost" | **Legend**, as Grieve reports it (1931). Tell only as "the story goes". |
| C1 | Bénédictine's 1510 monk (Dom Bernardo Vincelli) | **Company claim**; Oxford: no document produced, no independent trace. Never state. |
| C2 | "Original recipe" / exact 27 | Oxford repeats the company's count; fine as Oxford's statement, never as "the monks' recipe". |
| C3 | "Boosts" is Oxford's verb. "Makes juniper more itself", "harmonises", "binds" are NOT in the library (trade/brand sites only). Our reading, signposted. |
| C4 | Gin trade sites say angelica is more intense than juniper and used in small amounts. Unverified (brand/trade copy). Library taste on record is F10 ("rooty", earthy, jointly with orris): don't call it "gentle" or "quiet". |
| C5 | 1665 College of Physicians plague remedies with angelica: lead only (EEBO text not reached). Not usable. |
