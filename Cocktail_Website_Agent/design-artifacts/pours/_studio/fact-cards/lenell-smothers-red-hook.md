# Fact card: LeNell Smothers and her Red Hook shop

Hester, 2026-10-04, for hero-outlaw (The Lovable Rogue). Every line below was read on the page this session (`library.py page proper 237–239 --printed`, p. 331 for interview dates; `search Underberg`). *A Proper Drink* (Simonson, 2016) is a reliable secondary; quotes from Smothers and Boelte are their words on the record, given to Simonson (Smothers: email interview, 9–14 Oct 2014; Boelte: 25 Sep 2014, per p. 331, which dates conversations, not individual quotes). Simonson's narration is his judgement and is attributed as such. Web: one search (Underberg packaging) and one failed curl of VinePair; **nothing from the web is carded as fact.**

## Facts

| id | fact | source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| F1 | Smothers opened her wine and liquor store in 2003, nearly on the Brooklyn waterfront, at the end of Van Brunt Street in Red Hook. Simonson calls the place remote and the shop isolated. | *Proper* p. 237 |
| F2 | Simonson's joke (his judgement, not a record of anyone's bet): if you wanted to hide a business from customers, you could hardly have picked a better spot. | *Proper* p. 237 |
| F3 | People, cocktail bartenders especially, came to the shop despite the location (Simonson: they "found LeNell's anyway"). | *Proper* p. 237 |
| F4 | Stock (Simonson's list): many bourbons, including brands not distributed in New York; rye at a time he describes as "when no one had rye"; Red Hook Rye, from barrels she chose herself on trips to Kentucky; Italian amari; Brazilian cachaça; Hendrick's and Aviation gins, then exotic. | *Proper* p. 237 |
| F5 | She "flouted local liquor laws" to carry Peychaud's and Underberg bitters, which were treated as food products, i.e. grocery-store stock. | *Proper* p. 237 |
| F6 | Simonson says she was the first to carry Bitter Truth bitters from Germany when they became available. Unscoped "first" (where? among whom?): attribute or leave out. | *Proper* p. 237 |
| F7 | Her own words on her motive: with her Southern background bourbon had to play a major role, and she "also knew how hard it was to find many items if you were into classic cocktails". | *Proper* p. 238 (Smothers, 2014 email) |
| F8 | Simonson: many items she stocked were readily available, but most liquor stores didn't bother with esoteric stock that didn't sell quickly. | *Proper* p. 238 |
| F9 | Her own words: friends in the business worried about buying big cases of Hennessy at a good price; she was "chasing down oddball cases of amaros, like Amaro Cora". | *Proper* p. 238 (Smothers) |
| F10 | The shop quickly became a destination; by the end of 2005 the *New York Times* had written it up several times. | *Proper* p. 238 |
| F11 | Simonson: the shop was instantly legendary among the booze-conscious, and so was its owner, "often as much for her cantankerous personality as for her inventory". "Fiery Alabamian" is also his phrase. | *Proper* p. 238 |
| F12 | Damon Boelte, on his first visit, saw the Red Hook Rye wrapped in tissue paper, wanted to see the bottle, started taking the paper off, and was yelled at by Smothers straight away ("What the hell do you think you're doing?!"). His account. | *Proper* p. 238 (Boelte) |
| F13 | In a parenthesis, Simonson: Smothers later hired Boelte, who went on to design the cocktail programmes at Prime Meats and Grand Army in Brooklyn. **The page does not link the yelling to the hiring**, and gives no date or reason for the hire. | *Proper* p. 238 |
| F14 | Simonson: being told off by the owner became "almost a rite of passage" for the store's devotees. The qualifier "almost" is part of the fact. | *Proper* p. 238 |
| F15 | The store lasted six years. When Red Hook's fortunes rose ("partly due to businesses like hers"), her landlord refused to renew the lease. No closing year on the page (2003 + 6 is our arithmetic). | *Proper* p. 239 |
| F16 | Simonson: by the time it shut, other New York stores had learned to carry what bartenders wanted; "You could buy rye anywhere." | *Proper* p. 239 |
| F17 | Boelte: he can tell anyone where he has worked as a bartender, but when he says he worked at LeNell's, the reaction is "Oh my God, you worked at LeNell's!" His words. | *Proper* p. 239 (Boelte) |
| F18 | Context for "no rye": Simonson's own next section says that around 2000 "there was little rye on the market". | *Proper* p. 239 |
| F19 | Underberg in the library: *Joy* names "Underberg bitters" as an example of a digestif, a drink "designed to aid digestion". Nothing else in the six books. | *Joy* pdf 419 |
| F20 | **Sorghum syrup** (for the drink): sorghum is a grass of the same subfamily as corn and sugar cane; pressing its stalks and boiling the juice makes syrup. The "current heartland … southern United States" line in the same entry is about sorghum **spirit** (labelled whisky), not the syrup. The grain itself "has a naturally bitter taste": the grain, not the syrup. | Oxford SORGHUM pdf 1834 |
| F21 | The *Flavor Matrix*'s sugar-syrup entry lists sorghum syrup among the subtypes ("made from juiced sorghum") and grain among the family's best pairings. Family-level pairing, not a sorghum-specific one. | *Matrix* pdf 240 |
| F22 | The **Red Hook** cocktail (rye, Punt e Mes, maraschino) was created by Vincenzo Errico at Milk & Honey in 2004 and named for the Brooklyn neighbourhood. Nothing links it to LeNell's. Milk & Honey is the Lover family's ground. | Oxford RED HOOK pdf 1623; *Proper* p. 114 |

## Counterweights and guards

| id | note |
| --- | --- |
| C1 | **Not a secret.** The *Times* wrote the shop up several times by end-2005 (F10). Never say the shop never announced itself or "never said it would"; that half of the mirror lives only on the guest's side. |
| C2 | **"Nobody else carried it" is false** (my own plan shorthand, struck): many items were readily available (F8), and Peychaud's and Underberg were grocery-store stock (F5). Say other liquor stores didn't bother. |
| C3 | **"Rye, when no one had rye"** is Simonson's narration and hyperbole; his own p. 239 says "little rye". Paraphrase as "when hardly anyone stocked rye"; never in quotation marks as hers. |
| C4 | **Red Hook Rye:** she chose the barrels; she didn't make the whiskey. "Gone"/"no longer sold" is not on the page. |
| C5 | **The lease is not her sacrifice** (F15): the landlord refused. "Partly" stays. One clause at most on impact (F16), and not as "the world caught up" (sibling ground). |
| C6 | **Money:** no page says she lost money. "Didn't move quickly" is said of such stock in general (F8). |
| C7 | **Dates of quotes:** p. 331 dates the conversations (Boelte 25 Sep 2014; Smothers email 9–14 Oct 2014), not each quote. Safe: "told a writer in 2014" for her; nothing finer. |
| C8 | **Living person:** no present-tense claim about her or Boelte. Oxford's bibliography cites a "Camacho, LeNell" (2013, LINCOLN COUNTY PROCESS pdf 1184); that this is the same person is unverified. Unused lead. |
