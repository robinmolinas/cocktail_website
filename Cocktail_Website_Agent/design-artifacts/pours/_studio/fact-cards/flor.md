# Fact card: Flor (the yeast film on young sherry)
Verified by Hester, 2026-09-30, for innocent-caregiver (The Big Sibling). Reuse; don't re-research or re-tell differently. Quotations copied from text on screen.

| # | Fact | Source | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | Sherry casks are not filled to the top (Codex: "four-fifths"; see C1). This lets a layer of yeast called flor develop on the young wines, "a protective barrier on top of the wine that prevents oxidation". | *Cocktail Codex* p. 245 | primary (craft) |
| F2 | The flor "feeds off of the sherry as it ages, consuming some components and contributing others". This is biological ageing; it makes the driest sherries, fino and manzanilla. | *Codex* p. 245 | primary (craft) |
| F3 | For amontillado and palo cortado, flor is only the first step; they then age in the presence of oxygen. A palo cortado came from a cask that "would suddenly lose its flor" early. | *Codex* pp. 245, 247 | primary (craft) |
| F4 | The film is formed "by specific indigenous ambient yeasts": nobody adds it. In Andalucía it starts "almost immediately after fermentation" (Luyten). | Consejo Regulador, sherry.wine "Ageing"; Luyten, *SherryNotes* 2013 | secondary (the DO's own body; named educator) |
| F5 | **Scope (corrected r4):** the Consejo's "This input of oxygen is rapidly consumed by the yeasts' breathing" is about oxygen **dissolved when the wine is moved between barrels** (solera transfers), which also stimulates the flor's regrowth; the wine "remains protected beneath" the film. General statement: Luyten, flor protects the wine from oxygen "while also consuming most of it". | sherry.wine "Ageing"; Luyten | secondary |
| F6 | **All sherry** must age at least two years (Pliego de Condiciones). Scope: all sherry, not fino in particular. | sherry.wine "Ageing" | secondary |
| F7 | A fino of five years' ageing was sampled from casks "with an intense white flor yeast development". In the study's cellar the veil was checked periodically, and casks losing vigour were replanted with flor from adjacent casks (that cellar's practice; not a rule). | Valcárcel-Muñoz, Guerrero-Chanivet, Rodríguez-Dodero, García-Moreno, Guillén-Sánchez, *Molecules* 27(2):365 (2022), doi 10.3390/molecules27020365 | secondary (peer-reviewed) |
| F8 | Acetaldehyde "comes mainly from the metabolic action" of the flor and gives fino its green-apple and almond aromas. The flor uses glycerol (down more than 90% in the oldest fino) and part of the alcohol. | *Molecules* 2022 | secondary (peer-reviewed) |
| F9 | Fino is fortified to about 15% (Codex p. 246); amontillado's re-fortification to at least 16% stops the film developing (*Molecules*); Luyten gives 17% for oloroso, and says amontillado follows when the flor dies off, naturally or by added alcohol. | *Codex* p. 246; *Molecules* 2022; Luyten | mixed; see C3 |
| F10 | Fino and manzanilla are delicate: their delicacy "can be lost among more powerful ingredients, such as spirits". Manzanilla (Sanlúcar de Barrameda) is noticeably briny. Fino "offers dryness and salinity". | *Codex* pp. 245–246 | primary (craft) |
| F11 | Winemakers report the flor thicker in spring and autumn; it's thicker on the coast (Sanlúcar, El Puerto) than inland in Jerez. An "Ivory coloured, wrinkled, waxy foam, up to two centimeters thick". | Luyten | secondary (reported) |
| F13 | Butts for flor ageing leave "two fists" of air at the top, and "the wood is permeable to oxygen". The flor protects the wine "by preventing direct contact between the liquid and the air contained within the butt". | sherry.wine "Ageing" | secondary |
| F14 | Biological ageing raises acetaldehydes, "responsible for the sharp sensation in the nose". | sherry.wine "Ageing" | secondary |
| F15 | Amontillado has "all the benefits of the flor (dry and yeasty)": the Codex's own attribution of dry and yeasty to flor. In cocktails fino and manzanilla add "subtle saltiness and creamy yeastiness". | *Codex* pp. 247, 246 | primary (craft) |
| F16 | Oxidised in air, the wine "becomes gradually darker" (oxidative ageing, the oloroso way): air makes a different sherry, it doesn't spoil sherry. | sherry.wine "Ageing"; Codex p. 245 | secondary / primary |
| F12 | The humid bodegas "nurture the flor that typifies fino and manzanilla". | Oxford, BRANDY DE JEREZ, pdf 346 (Oxford's only mention of flor) | primary |

## Conflicts, legends and guards
| # | Item | Status |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | Fill level: Codex four-fifths; Luyten four-fifths, "sometimes even less for a Manzanilla"; *Molecules* cellar 540 L in 600 L casks; sherry.wine 500 L ("30 arrobas") in butts of up to 600 L, "not usually filled to the top". | No fraction, and not "never": "aren't filled to the top". |
| C2 | How long fino ages under flor: no fixed figure on any page read. Web wiki figures ("4 to 7", "3 to 8 years") are unattributed. | "For years" only. |
| C3 | The strength that stops flor: 16% (*Molecules*) vs 17% (Luyten). | No number in the reading. |
| L1 | Flor "was seen as a defect" once (Luyten, no source given). | Legend-grade, and the Underdog's scorned-then-useful shape: out. |
| G1 | The flor's end (amontillado, palo cortado) is self-erasure: out of any Big Sibling reading. Fino only. | guard |
| G2 | Solera (criaderas y soleras, "the only viable method" for biological ageing, sherry.wine): A Brother's Care's ground, never named. | guard |
| G3 | "Green apple": the Innocent family's one apple is Thoreau's. Say almond; almond is a taste word, not the `nuts` veto. | guard |
| G4 | Walls, evaporation, "angels' share", "lives on what escapes": the Free Spirit's (Baudoinia) and the Lover family's ground. | guard |
| G6 | "Nobody adds it" is false as an absolute: *Molecules*' cellar replanted weak casks from neighbours (F7). Say "it forms by itself". | guard |
| G5 | "Bread" is Tio Pepe's note (Codex p. 246, a bottle), and "yeasty" is Jarana's and La Gitana's; only the almond link to flor is on the page (F8). Family vs bottle. | guard |
