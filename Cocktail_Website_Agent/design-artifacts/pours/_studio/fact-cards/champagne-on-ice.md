# Fact card: champagne and ice

Written 2026-10-01 by Hester for regular-guy-outlaw (Tomás's "ice in the sparkling wine").

## Library (primary)
| # | Fact | Source |
|---|---|---|
| I1 | Until the 1840s, champagne "was preferred sweet". "If too sweet, it was best served almost frozen." | Oxford, CHAMPAGNE COCKTAILS, pdf 427 |
| I2 | The first description of a Champagne Cocktail is from an American bar in Panama, 1855, "with ice and bitters-saturated sugar". Jerry Thomas published the recipe in 1862. | Oxford pdf 427 |
| I3 | As brut became fashionable, that "made additional ice less important". The posthumous 1887 Thomas edition reduced the ice. | Oxford pdf 427–428 |
| I4 | Arnold: "Unlike cocktails, sparkling wines don't taste best when chilled below refrigerato[r temperature]" (the text cuts off at the page break; the rest is unread). It's a counterweight to "colder is better" for the wine. | *Liquid Intelligence* p. 320 |
**Limit:** Oxford never states a rule against ice in champagne, and never dates one. "The rule is younger than the habit" is NOT on the page. What is: sweet champagne was once best served almost frozen, and the first Champagne Cocktail had ice.
