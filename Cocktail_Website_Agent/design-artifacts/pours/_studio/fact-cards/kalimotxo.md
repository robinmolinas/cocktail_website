| F11 | The Getxo town council's account, as reported by EFE (*Noticias de Gipuzkoa*, 12 August 2022): the San Nicolás del Puerto Viejo festival in Algorta (Getxo), 12 August 1972. The mix had existed in Spain since the 1920s but was a minority drink until Spain's first Coca-Cola factory (1953). Earlier names: Rioja Libre, Mochete, Tincola, Cuba Libre del Pobre, Cubata del obrero. The Antzarrak were running the festival that year. They had bought 2,000 litres of red wine that had turned ("picado"), perhaps from the heat, perhaps from the seller's trickery. A doctor they knew said it wasn't harmful, but it tasted very unpleasant. After several mixes they tried cola, found the amount that hid ("disimular") the taste, and settled on equal parts, so they could sell 4,000 litres. Someone suggested not saying what the drink was. The name spread through Euskadi and Navarre and was popular by the early 1980s, then spread to all of Spain. The RAE dictionary lists "calimocho". In 2022 the festival paid homage to the Antzarrak. Eight members are named. | noticiasdegipuzkoa.eus, read on the page | secondary (press agency, relaying the council) |

## How to hold "Rioja Libre" (room ruling, round 3)
- **R1, the name, held as fact (secondary):** "Rioja Libre" was one of the names for wine and cola in Spain before "Kalimotxo". Two sources give it: the council account (F11) lists it among the mix's names after 1953, and Oxford pdf 386 gives it as the drink's first name (Funky Project, legend-grade). Safe wording: "one of its older names". Not "its first name", and not "what the Antzarrak called it" (that part is Funky Project only).
- **R2, "a Cuba Libre made with wine":** Oxford CUBA LIBRE pdf 613, primary. As Coca-Cola spread, the drink was made "with local spirits and even wine", with a cross-reference to CALIMOTXO. Oxford itself files it as a wine Cuba Libre.
- **R3, the lime is "sometimes":** Oxford pdf 613 lists the Cuba Libre's ingredients as rum, cola, ice and "sometimes, lime juice". Its recipe (half a lime) and *Codex* p. 213 both use lime. Never "a Cuba Libre always gets lime" or "of course". *Codex* p. 213: lime peel is a key ingredient in most colas, and the juice draws that flavour out and cuts the sweetness. Never its "elevated".
- **R4, the name doesn't name the wine:** nothing on the page says the drinkers used Rioja. A young Rioja is our choice of style.
- **R5, "Libre" stays untranslated:** Oxford pdf 613 says "Cuba libre" ("Free Cuba") was the slogan of the forces opposed to *Spanish* rule. Telling it inside a Spanish drink puts a political wink and a defiance note in the reading. Out.
- **R6, precedent for citrus:** Schaap (F9) adds a squeeze of lemon herself. Her purists line is a hedge ("might consider"), not a recorded objection.

## Conflicts
- **C1, the name.** Oxford gives Kalimero + Motxo, or Kalimero's other nickname "Motz" (short). The council's account says someone remarked that *motxo* means "ugly" in Basque, about a friend "de aspecto poco agraciado" (not good-looking). Wikipedia has Kalimero + Motxongo. **Contested, and one version is a joke about a man's looks.** Say only "after a friend's nickname".
- **C2, the day.** The council gives 12 August 1972. Oxford gives only 1972. Use the year.
- **C3, the motive.** Every detailed account is a sales fix: bad wine had to be sold, and cola hid the taste (Miles: "to mask the flavor"; council: "disimular", 4,000 litres to sell, and a suggestion not to say what it was). "They made the wine good to drink" inverts the record. If the room tells the origin at all, it's "the story goes", and it can't be softened into delight.
