## Legends
- **L1** "He made the best Amaretto Sour in the world." His claim, in his title. It can be quoted as his boast, never stated as ours.
- **L2** A 2017 reader reported that a Disaronno hang tag printed an Amaretto Sour with cask-strength bourbon. One anonymous comment, no image: legend-grade. Never "the brand adopted his recipe".

## Conflicts
- **C1 Syrup.** Oxford pdf 97: 15 ml simple syrup. Post 2012: 5 ml of 2:1 syrup. Both are his. So no single "his recipe" for the sweetening: if the reading names measures, it names ours.
- **C2 Strength word.** "Cask-proof" (post) vs "cask-strength" (Oxford). Same idea; in a 2026 reply he puts the sweet spot at about 120 proof (60% abv). Tomás picks the bottle style.
- **C3 Origin vs fix.** "Going back to fresh lemon juice" (Oxford) means the 1974 original used lemon; Oxford doesn't say the 1974 juice was fresh in so many words. Say "back to lemon juice" or "fresh lemon again", never "the original recipe, fresh-squeezed".

## Guards
- G1 Never "his recipe" for our glass if Tomás changes anything (C1 already makes it ours on the syrup).
- G2 Nothing after 2021 about him in the reading, and no present tense about his bars (F15 is stale; F18 is unfetched).
- G3 The cost is a ribbing and the purist's question (F9). Never "resented", "mocked by the trade", "the trade turned on him".
- G4 Never "on the record" (owned by *Asked In*). "No, really. I'm serious." is clean.
- G5 Amaretto is `nuts`. Oxford pdf 94: amaretto is generally made from apricot kernels, not almonds, and some brands use almond cookies (Lazzaroni). Neither lifts the veto.
- G6 *explorer-innocent* (draft) already has him as a visitor in London, "by his own account" thinking the aged Manhattan tasted like a Manhattan. Don't echo that line or its barrels.
