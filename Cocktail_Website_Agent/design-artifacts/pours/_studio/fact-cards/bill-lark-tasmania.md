# Fact card: Bill and Lyn Lark, and the still-size law in Tasmania

Verified 2026-10-04 by Hester for innocent-magician (The Dreamer). Library = primary; web = secondary (named, dated). Source text saved: `work/innocent-magician/historian-src-spiritsbusiness-2022.txt` (curl + strip, read in full).

Sources: Oxford Companion, AUSTRALIA AND NEW ZEALAND (pdf 171–172) and WHISKY, SINGLE-MALT, GLOBAL (pdf 2173; `library.py` labels the page "UNDER THREE YEARS OLD. DUTY", cite by text). Melita Kiely, "The big interview: Bill Lark", *The Spirits Business*, 21 Nov 2022 (SB), secondary; Lark's own words are quoted in it, the rest is the journalist's narration (marked).

## Facts
| id | fact | source | tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | Whisky distilleries operated in Van Diemen's Land (now Tasmania) by the late 1820s and early 1830s, after Macquarie legalised distilling. Governor John Franklin outlawed distilling on the island in 1839. | pdf 171 | primary |
| F2 | From federation (1901), federal law required a licensed distiller to be able to exhaust at least 150 imperial gallons (680 litres) of wash an hour; so for most of the 20th century Australian distilling was the "sole province" of large industrial operations (Bundaberg, Château Tanunda, Corio). | pdf 172 | primary |
| F3 | After the 1970s trade reforms, cheaper imports came in, Australian consumers disdained local brands, and those brands competed on price rather than quality. An Industries Assistance Commission report says whisky production had effectively ceased by 1977. | pdf 172 | primary |
| F4 | Same book, other entry: Australia distilled its own whisky from 1791 until the 1980s, when the last two large distilleries (Victoria) closed. **Conflicts with F3's 1977: see C1.** | pdf 2173 | primary |
| F5 | The setting, in Lark's words: on a day out trout fishing with his father-in-law, the two of them were "sitting up in the highlands of Tasmania", reflecting that the island had (they thought) some of the best water in the world, good barley, and a climate that seemed fine for whisky, "yet no one was making it". It was a conversation ("We talked about this for a little while"), not one man's thought. The article dates the trip 1989 (see C5). | SB 2022 (Lark quoted; date is narration) | secondary |
| F6 | Then: "my wife turned up and she said, 'well, let's give it a go'." The first reply on record to the idea is Lyn's yes. | SB 2022 (Lark quoted) | secondary |
| F7 | Neither had any distilling experience: he was a land surveyor, Lyn a survey draftsman. | SB 2022 (narration) | secondary |
| F8 | The obstacle: "We were being stymied by regulation" (Lark). In his telling, federal politicians "helped us instantly amend the legislation" so that small-scale distilling could start again in Tasmania. | SB 2022 (Lark quoted) | secondary |
| F9 | Oxford's verbs: Lark "successfully lobbied" the government to ease the still-size requirements of the 1901 Distillation Act (pdf 172, "early 1990s"); in 1992 he "succeeded in persuading" it to rewrite the minimum-still-size rule, "thereby permitting small-scale distillation" (pdf 2173). The new Australian single malt industry starts there, in Tasmania, where most of the country's distilleries now are (pdf 2173). | pdf 172, 2173 | primary |
| F10 | "Lyn and I were the first ones to get a licence." (1992, the article.) The licence was the couple's. | SB 2022 (Lark quoted) | secondary |
| F11 | After the licence: "I was sitting at home thinking 'what the heck do we do now?'" Within two weeks, a phone call at 10 at night from John Grant of Glenfarclas (Scotland): his Hobart distributor had told him of the licence; he offered to help them make good whisky. "people wanted to help us" (Lark). | SB 2022 (Lark quoted, Grant relayed by Lark) | secondary |
| F12 | Grant's reason, as Lark remembers it: if someone's first experience of whisky is at your distillery, Grant would like it to be a good one, because then they'll go on to explore the wider world of whisky. Channel: Lark's memory of Grant's words, 30 years on. | SB 2022 | secondary |
| F13 | **He set out to help those who followed, on the record:** "As other producers came along, I thought I should help them as well", so that they could "go beyond being just a novelty" and become an industry. He ties this to Grant's words "ringing in my ear". He calls the others "good mates"; by 2000 there were six distilleries on the island (his recollection). His stated sentiment: "a rising tide lifts all boats" (an idiom; never as copy). | SB 2022 (Lark quoted) | secondary |
| F14 | Oxford: "several craft distillers soon followed in Lark's footsteps" (Australia-wide, gins, rums, vodkas as well as Tasmanian whisky). | pdf 172 | primary |
| F15 | First making: wash bought from Cascade Brewery; a copper pot still bought at a local auction (the price in the article is implausible, never used); the first licensed distillery was in their residential cul-de-sac; moved to Richmond in 1997; first commercial single malt 1998; both full-time in 2000. | SB 2022 (narration + Lark) | secondary |
| F16 | Tasmanian practice: distillers tend to use local brewing malt and often buy in wash; local peat has different microflora, so different aromatics; Australian wine and fortified-wine casks, often smaller than Scotland's. Lark himself "played with smaller casks" and fortified-wine casks (SB). | pdf 2173; SB 2022 | primary; secondary |
| F17 | Price: Australian craft spirits are "often significantly more expensive" than mass-produced or imported ones (economies of scale, tax). Supports a Makeable flag on a Tasmanian bottle. | pdf 172 | primary |
| F18 | Living person: in Nov 2022 he was the distillery's founder and global ambassador, and won *The Spirits Business* Lifetime Achievement award 2022. Date any present tense. | SB 2022 | secondary |

## Conflicts (keep out of any reading)
- C1 **When Australian whisky-making stopped:** "effectively ceased by 1977" (pdf 172, IAC report) vs "until the 1980s" (pdf 2173). State no date; say only that the big makers had stopped.
- C2 **Franklin's ban:** 1839 (Oxford pdf 171) vs 1838 and Lady Jane Franklin's influence (SB narration). Out anyway (Explorer family's Franklin ground; Wren's guard).
- C3 **The rule's number:** a wash capacity of 150 imperial gallons an hour (Oxford) vs "a minimum still size of 2,700 litres" (SB narration). No number; "an old rule about how big a still had to be".
- C4 **"First in N years":** SB narration says the first Australian distillery to make single malt spirit in 154 years; the plan cited "about 150 years" (*Whisky Magazine* 2022, not re-read this session). Pdf 2173 has large Victorian distilleries working into the 1980s, so the "Australian" claim is false as worded. Never a count.
- C5 **Year of the fishing trip:** 1989 in SB; the plan noted other sources differ. No year for the question.
- C6 "early 1990s" (pdf 172) vs 1992 (pdf 2173, SB): compatible; 1992 is safe for the law change and licence.
