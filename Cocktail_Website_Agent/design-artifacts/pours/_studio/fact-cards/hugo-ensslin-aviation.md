## Legends
- **Why Craddock left the violette out.** Not on record. Violette was fading from cocktail use by the 1930s (F11), so availability or taste are possible; carelessness is not shown. The reading can say the copy lost it, never why, and never that the violet "made it true" as fact (that is our reading).
- **"Named for the aviators of the First World War."** *Joy* (pdf 237) links it to WWI aviators; but the name is in print by 1911, before the war. Don't say it.
- **Any feeling of Ensslin's** (that he loved the ordinary, that he meant to preserve a moment). Nothing personal of his is on record (F5). Interpretation only, signposted.

## Conflicts
- **C1, dates.** Oxford: 1878–1929 (death sourced to the *Wilkes-Barre Evening News*, 2 Jan 1929). *Imbibe!* (pdf 241): 36 in 1916 (so ~1880), died 1930. No dates in the reading.
- **C2, his death.** He took his own life; the reason differs by account (Oxford: ill health or a failed relationship; *Imbibe!*: an ex-fiancée). **Never in guest text.** Dossier only, flagged.
- **C3, authorship.** See F5. Not "his drink", ever.
- **C4, his title.** *Imbibe!*: head bartender at the Wallick House; Oxford only puts him behind its bar. Safe: "a bartender at the Hotel Wallick in Times Square".
- **C5, the name.** Sky-blue colour (Wondrich's reading) vs. flying as the new sport of 1911 (Oxford, context only). Not a fact either way.

- **C6, what two dashes measured.** Ensslin wrote "2 dashes"; *Imbibe!* (pdf 242) converts that to about 1 tsp of violette (and 1½ tsp of maraschino). Tomás's spec treats a dash as a modern dash (2 dashes ≈ ⅓ tsp). Guest text may say "two dashes, as his book gives it", never that ⅓ tsp is what he poured.
- **C7, Haus Alpenz vs. Rothman & Winter.** The 2007 return was the importer Haus Alpenz, bringing in the Austrian Rothman & Winter brand (Oxford, pdf 601, 975). Not "Rothman & Winter brought it back".

## Foil (dossier only)
- **Stanley Clisby Arthur** (1881–1963): also a reporter and photographer by training, whose 1937 *Famous New Orleans Drinks* founded cocktail history, but who printed supposition as fact and even altered evidence (dropped "Stoughton's" to imply Peychaud's). Oxford, ARTHUR, STANLEY CLISBY (pdf 158). Two photographers in drink history: one retouched, one didn't.
