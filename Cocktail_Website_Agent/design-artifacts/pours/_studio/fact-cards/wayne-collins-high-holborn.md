# Wayne Collins, the fruit "Martini" and High Holborn

Verified 2026-10-01 by Hester for ruler-creator (The Perfectionist). Sources: *A Proper Drink* (Simonson 2016; reliable secondary; Collins interviewed 17 June 2014, p. 330); Oxford Companion COLLINS, WAYNE (pdf 557, Alice Lascelles; primary).

- F1. Born 1970 (Oxford). From Camden, North London. Began pulling pints at a relative's pub in Hoxton in the late 1980s (Proper p. 102).
- F2. "Hated the fruit Martini", wrote an article in *Class* bashing it (pp. 101–102). Undated, and no target drink named.
- F3. "I didn't like that it was called a Martini" (p. 102).
- F4. His cocktail schooling came from Lee Chappell. Collins's quote: "Why would anyone drink gin and vermouth? He'd say, 'You better start liking it. It's not about you, is it? It's about the guests.'" The asking voice is Collins's own, recalled; "He'd say" is habitual (p. 102).
- F5. Chappell ran the Long Island Iced Tea Shop, Covent Garden (bottled cocktails "at a time when no one else did"). Per Collins he was like Bryan Brown's character in *Cocktail* and knew 500 recipes (p. 102).
- F6. Mid-1990s, moving bar to bar: read old cocktail books and studied Dorelli (Savoy) and Calabrese (Lanesborough) (p. 102); "grabbed what he could find at book fairs" (p. 60).
- F7. 2000: chef David Cavalier hired him as bar manager at High Holborn (a forty-seat bar). Classic menu: Manhattans, Daisies, Punches; "drinks that I found in books that were not on lists around London" (p. 102).
- F8. Simonson: most London drinkers had then never been served a cocktail without ice; Collins stirred Sazeracs and served them in expensive glassware (p. 102).
- F9. Conigliaro: "it influenced a lot of people" (the bar). Reaburn: "An amazing bar to work in and a terrible restaurant" (p. 102).
- F10. Oxford: Mixxit training and education programme (developed for Maxxium UK), "driving up service standards and improving product knowledge in the industry". Undated.
- F11. The old Daisy: Oxford pdf 627 (definition; 1870s; first in print in Thomas 1876; "one of the progenitors of the Sidecar and the Margarita"). In Thomas's Daisies the cordial is "an accent, not as the main sweetener" (*Imbibe!* pdf 105–106), so by *Codex* p. 106 structure it's a sour. *Codex*'s "daisies include liqueur" is the modern kind. (fixed 2026-10-01, audit H1)
- F12. Oxford pdf 627: neither Daisy "has been prominent in the twenty-first-century cocktail renaissance".
- F13. *Proper* p. 39: *Class* magazine (publisher Simon Difford); Met Bar "all about fresh-fruit Martinis"; LAB late 1990s. Counterweight: fruit "Martinis" "kept the cocktail's name alive" (Simonson), and Difford argued the name made them serious. p. 98: "greeted like a revelation".
- C1. High Holborn "closed within a year"; the restaurant won a Michelin star but "didn't succeed the way the bar did" (pp. 102–103). Never blame the standard.
- C2. White Negroni: 2001 (Proper p. 106) vs 2000 (Codex p. 89). Conflict, unused.
- C3. Oxford calls him a "brand ambassador". The LAB/"serious drinkers" quote is p. 102 and the "bang!" quote p. 101.
