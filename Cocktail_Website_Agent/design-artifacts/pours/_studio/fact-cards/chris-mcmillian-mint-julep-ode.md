# Fact card: Chris McMillian and the julep ode

Library: Oxford McMILLIAN, CHRIS (Wayne Curtis) pdf 1273; ICE MALLET (Philip Greene) pdf 1064–1066; the julep entry (David Wondrich) pdf 1120–1122; *A Proper Drink* (Simonson) pp. 284–286, 331. ## The ode (web, period printing and anthologies)
| # | Claim (our words) | Source | Tier | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| W1 | **It's prose, not verse.** A long, lush prose tribute: first to old bourbon, then to the julep. Oxford's "Ode" is a fair word; "poem" is not. | *Morning Star* 1907 (djvu) | secondary (period printing via archive.org) | |
| W2 | **Printed by January 1907** (Wilmington *Morning Star*, 20 Jan 1907). That is a floor for the text, not its date of writing. Oxford: "late nineteenth-century". | *Morning Star* 1907; Oxford pdf 1273 | secondary; primary | Never give a year of writing. "More than a century before McMillian" is safe; "a century later" is a rounding (1907 to c. 2000 is about 93 years). |
| W3 | **Authorship:** the 1907 printing ends signed "M. B." (OCR; columns interleave, so the signature is on the bourbon passage, read as the same piece). The 1956 anthology *Bluegrass Cavalcade* (ed. Thomas D. Clark) prints it as "J. Soule Smith, Zenith of Man's Pleasure" (p. 269; Alvey 1992 cites pp. 270–71). | archive.org FTS highlights (contents page and text) | OCR highlight | **Conflict C2.** Say "Soule Smith's, as it's usually credited", or just "a Kentucky writer's". Smith's dates: **not found.** |
| W4 | **Title varies:** Oxford "Ode to the Mint Julep"; the anthology "Zenith of Man's Pleasure". Wording varies too: 1907 "The zenith of man's pleasure is reached in the julep"; later printings "Then comes the zenith of man's pleasure. Then comes the julep". | *Morning Star* 1907; FTS highlights (Harwell 1975, Clark 1956, Cobb 1929) | secondary / OCR highlight | Never quote a single "the" text. |
| W5 | The piece calls bourbon and mint "lovers", born of the same land and the same stream. | *Morning Star* 1907 | secondary | Our paraphrase for the room: "it says bourbon and mint were made for each other". The Lover echo is real; it's Wren's call whether to use it, never as a quotation. |
| W6 | **Its own method:** water from a cold spring mixed with sugar "till it seems like oil"; mint crushed with a spoon all round the inside of the glass, then thrown away ("it is a sacrifice"); the glass filled with cracked ice; bourbon poured in and left to cool; then the sugared water poured over; no spoon, "no stirring is allowed", let it stand; sprigs of mint round the brim so the drinker gets "a taste and odor at one draught"; sip it slowly. | *Morning Star* 1907 | secondary | The ode says **cracked** ice; the canvas bag and mallet are McMillian's (and the 1847 *Oxford Night Caps*'s, ICE MALLET pdf 1064), not the ode's. Never "the ode's recipe" for a drink we change. |
| W7 | Its closing advice: sip it and dream. | *Morning Star* 1907 | secondary | |
| W8 | It was read aloud long before McMillian: in Irvin S. Cobb's novel *Red Likker* (1929; serialised in *Cosmopolitan*, May 1929) a colonel recites it; the *Atlanta Constitution* (16 Jul 1933) has a man "reading" it out. | FTS highlights | OCR highlight | Counterweight: McMillian didn't rescue a forgotten text; he took his place in a long line of people saying it aloud. Never "forgotten" or "lost". |
| W9 | Carson's 1963 *Social History of Bourbon* (the book McMillian says he was reading, F6) has a chapter 18 titled "The Zenith of Man's Pleasure" (p. 207). | FTS highlight (contents page) | OCR highlight | Whether the chapter prints the ode isn't in the highlight. **That McMillian found the ode in Carson is inference**: "I like to think" at most. |

## Guards
| # | Guard |
| --- | --- |
| G1 | The ode's love lines are gendered and of their time ("like a woman's heart … when bruised", the mint "a virgin … soon to be married", "No maiden's kiss…"). None of them, quoted or paraphrased, ever reaches a pour. |
| G2 | Kentucky pride ("Kentucky's riflemen") and Oxford's note that the late julep became "a ritual of southern identity" with "plantation trappings" (pdf 1121): no silver-cup-and-gloves romance in the reading or the image. |
| G3 | No present tense for McMillian beyond 2020 (his last dated act on the page is the interview). The recital and the mallet are "he'd", at the Ritz. |
| G4 | Not "every word", not "by heart", not "a poem", not "forgotten", not "his recipe". |

## Conflicts
| # | Conflict |
| --- | --- |
| C1 | Oxford: "a fourth-generation bartender". *Proper* p. 284: he "seemed like a lifelong barkeep, but was not", had been in sales and needed a job in 1994 (his pregnant wife asked her boss to give him one, in catering). Not necessarily contradictory (family trade vs his own career), but tell neither "fourth-generation" nor "lifelong". "Came to bartending in his thirties" is supported by *Proper* (b. 1961; 1994). |
| C2 | Authorship of the ode: "M. B." (1907 printing) vs J. Soule Smith (1956 anthology; Oxford). Credit it as usually credited, softly. |
| C3 | The bar's name: Library Bar (Oxford, *Proper* p. 285) vs Library Lounge (ICE MALLET, *Proper* p. 284). |
