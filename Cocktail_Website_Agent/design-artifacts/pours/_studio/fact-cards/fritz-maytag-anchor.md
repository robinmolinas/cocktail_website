# Fact card: Fritz Maytag, Anchor Brewing and Anchor Distilling

Hester, 2026-10-01, for outlaw-ruler (*The Leading Edge*). Tiers: Oxford = primary (library); everything else secondary (web, fetched and read in the same session unless marked). Quotes under 15 words; paraphrase for the reading.

## Facts

| ID | Fact | Source | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | 1965: Maytag bought the "faltering" Anchor Brewery and made it "a craft-beer vanguard". | Oxford, ANCHOR DISTILLING COMPANY, pdf 103 | primary |
| F2 | 1993: he founded Anchor Distilling, "based in part on his long interest in rye whisky and early American spirits". | Oxford pdf 103; Abate, *SF Chronicle*, 27 Apr 2010 ("In 1993, Maytag founded a distillery"); oldpotrero.com ("since 1993") | primary + secondary |
| F3 | The rye: 100% malted rye, pot-distilled; the first was bottled after one year, following "Colonial-era practice"; sold as Old Potrero. Oxford: "the first modern microdistilled whisky to reach market", "an early—perhaps too early—shot across the bows" of the US whisky industry. **Attribute these to Oxford.** | Oxford pdf 103 | primary |
| F4 | Rye's later comeback began with Old Potrero and was "driven by the cocktail revolution". | Oxford pdf 2165 | primary |
| F5 | Production year conflict: Oxford, production began 1994; maker's page, first whiskey "went into the barrel in 1994"; Ross 2010 says he "formulated the two spirits in 1996"; Chronicle 2004 (Wren's read) gives first bottling 1996. **State no year for the rye; "in the 1990s" or "in 1993 he founded a distillery".** | as cited | conflict |
| F6 | The distillery was in a secret corner of the brewery (Yenne, *San Francisco Beer*, 2016, archive.org OCR highlight: "in a secret corner of"). Wren's 2004 Chronicle: secret from all but a few employees. | Yenne 2016 (OCR highlight, incomplete); Holbrook 2004 (Wren) | secondary |
| F7 | Inheritance, his words: he had "a nest egg" but "spent it all on the brewery" (to Scott Ostler, 2000, relayed by Abate 2010; "(from the Maytag inheritance)" is the reporter's insertion). | Abate, *SF Chronicle*, 27 Apr 2010 | secondary |
| F8 | Why he bought it, his words (2015): "just a fun project"; enthralled by a business with "character, and history". The beer "wasn't good... sort of sour"; he brought a microscope into the brewery. In the black in 1975: "It took 10 years." | Lucchesi, *SF Chronicle* Q&A, 12 Aug 2015 | secondary |
| F9 | His own words on what he built (2015): "I designed every square inch of this place." "What the world wants is a good story that's true." | Lucchesi 2015 | secondary |
| F10 | **Keeping it small (counterweight).** Burlingham, *Small Giants* (2016 ed., OCR highlight): in 1992 he "recognized what nobody had told him": his company didn't have to keep "growing ever bigger and more impersonal". Acitelli, *The Audacity of Hops* (2013, OCR highlight): "Maytag kept Anchor small." Maytag to *Stanford* magazine 1996, via Acitelli in *Alta* 27 Jul 2023: "We do not, emphatically do not, want to get too big." | as cited | secondary |
| F11 | 2010 sale: retired and sold Anchor Distilling (Oxford) and the brewery to Keith Greggor and Tony Foglio (Griffin Group); Maytag, 72, stayed on as chairman emeritus. | Oxford pdf 103; Abate 2010; Ross, *SF Chronicle*, 11 Aug 2010 | primary + secondary |
| F12 | **His own words on the sale:** "I plan to do whatever Keith says, to help them do well." (Ross 2010). Company statement in his name: Anchor "will be enjoyed in San Francisco for generations to come" (Abate 2010; a press statement, not an interview). | Ross 2010; Abate 2010 | secondary |
| F13 | Others on the sale: the motive is the **buyer's** guess, not his: Greggor, "I think he felt it was time to let somebody else help lead the way." Ken Grossman: "I didn't think at this point he was ready to move on." | Abate 2010 | secondary |
| F14 | After him: Sapporo bought Anchor in 2017; in July 2023 it ceased operations (company's statement cites the pandemic, inflation, competition). Thirteen years after his sale. | Alta 2023; Reason 2023 (Wren/plan); SF Chronicle 2023 | secondary |
| F15 | May 2024: Hamdi Ulukaya (Chobani founder) bought the brands, recipes and the Potrero Hill site through his family office, promising a revival. | American Craft Beer, 6 Jul 2026; SF Chronicle (Lander), 14 May 2026 | secondary |
| F16 | **The comeback is not done (as of 1 Oct 2026).** Federal approval for the old Jim Stitt labels (ACB, 6 Jul 2026: "the beer is not back on store shelves just yet"); activity at the site but "no beer is coming out" (SF Standard, 17 May 2026); an SF Standard opinion of 28 Sep 2026 says it has been left to "lie fallow ever since". | ACB 2026; SF Standard 2026 x2; Chronicle 2026 | secondary |
| F17 | Old Potrero today (oldpotrero.com, product pages read 2026-10-01): core range is **Straight Rye 6YO, 48.5% ABV** and **Toasted Barrel 6YO, 51.2% ABV** ("called initially Old Potrero 18th Century Style Whiskey"; 100% rye malt, copper pot still), both $64.99/700 ml; single barrels "ABV: Varies"; Hotaling's Bottled in Bond 13YO (sold out). The maker's BiB page names "the distillers at Hotaling & Co."; Old Potrero assets sit on hotalingandco.com. **The label itself not seen.** Neither core bottle is the one-year-old rye of 1994. | oldpotrero.com; hotalingandco.com | secondary (maker) |
| F18 | **The tsunami line** is the Chronicle's narration (Stett Holbrook, "Of grape and grain", *SF Chronicle*, 29 Jan 2004), about the 1970s beers: "The brewery produced the first drops of what would become a tsunami of artisan brews." Same paragraph: they reached the market when "factory beers like Budweiser and Pabst ruled". Bryson (Malt Advocate), same page: everybody outside the major brewers "owes him a debt". | Holbrook 2004 (fetched 2026-10-01, sfgate …2804774.php) | secondary |
| F19 | **Did he like the beer? Conflict.** Holbrook 2004: Anchor Steam was "his brew of choice" and he told Kuh how much he liked it. Ogle, *Ambitious Brew* (2006) p. 261 (OCR highlight): "Maytag, who rarely drank Anchor". **State neither.** His own later verdict (F8) is safe: the beer "wasn't good". | Holbrook 2004; Ogle 2006 | conflict |
| F20 | The secret, exactly: a distillery "in a remote corner of the brewery's warehouse"; "until the whiskey was aged" and he knew it was good, the enterprise was "a secret to all but a few employees"; the stills, behind a roll-top door, were called "the gadget". First bottling 1996 (Holbrook) vs production from 1994 (Oxford): F5 stands. | Holbrook 2004 | secondary |
| F21 | Oxford RYE-area entry (pdf 2165), scope: rye from large producers was "supplemented by a wave of new ryes from micro-distillers, beginning with" Old Potrero; growth "by the 2010s" was "driven by the cocktail revolution". The wave of small-distillery ryes began with his, not the whole revival. | Oxford pdf 2165 | primary |
| F22 | Quince, *Flavor Matrix* pdf 196: "rarely eaten raw" because of "hard, astringent flesh"; "a strong perfume"; high in pectin, "excellent jellies and jams". The "Best Pairings" list (citrus, brandy, bourbon…) belongs to the whole apple/pear/quince entry, not quince alone. | Matrix pdf 196 | primary |
| F23 | Membrillo labels (GFI Foods listings, read 2026-10-01): Ponce de León, ingredients "Quince 52%, sugar and lemon", "May Contain: Tree Nuts"; Mitica, "quince paste, sugar, lemon", "May Contain: None" (a distributor listing, not a nut-free certificate).
