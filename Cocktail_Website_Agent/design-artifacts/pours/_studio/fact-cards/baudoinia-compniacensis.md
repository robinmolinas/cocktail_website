# Fact card: *Baudoinia compniacensis* (the warehouse-wall fungus) and Antonin Baudoin

Hester, 2026-10-01, for innocent-explorer (The Free Spirit). Every row read on the page in this session. Quotes under 15 words; everything else paraphrase.

Sources:
- **[OX]** Oxford Companion, BAUDOINIA COMPNIACENSIS (pdf 238); ANGEL'S SHARE (pdf 110); MOLD (pdf 1347); CHAUFFE-VIN (pdf 444) and CUT (pdf 619) bibliographies. Primary (library).
- **[SC07]** Scott, Untereiner, Ewaze, Wong & Doyle, "*Baudoinia*, a new genus to accommodate *Torula compniacensis*", *Mycologia* 99(4): 592–601, July 2007 (Crossref; full text via Wayback copy of mycologia.org, 2015-09-30 snapshot). Primary: the paper that made the name.
- **[BNF]** BnF catalogue records: A. Baudoin, *Étude sur le nostoc des murs de Cognac*, Pons, impr. N. Texier, **1878**, 15 p. (ark:/12148/cb30066907z); A. Baudoin, *Les eaux-de-vie et la fabrication du cognac*, Paris, Baillière, 1893 (ark:/12148/cb30066906m). Primary (catalogue of the printed works).
- **[WI]** Adam Rogers, "Unraveling the Mystery of the Canadian Whiskey Fungus", *Wired* 19.06, 17 May 2011 (Kavli award; basis of his *Proof*, 2014, which Oxford cites). Secondary: named science journalist, interviews on the record.

## Facts
| # | Fact | Source | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | A black fungus grows on the outsides of buildings where spirits age in barrels, feeding on alcohol vapour lost from the casks. | OX pdf 238; SC07 abstract | primary |
| F2 | In **1872** the French pharmacist Antonin Baudoin brought a black, sooty growth on walls and roof tiles near Cognac distilleries to two botanists' attention. | SC07 intro | primary |
| F3 | Those botanists took it for an undescribed *Xenodochus* and informally suggested "*X. baudoinii*". | SC07 intro | primary |
| F4 | Baudoin himself printed a short study calling it a **nostoc** (a blue-green, algae-like organism): wrong. Title: *Étude sur le nostoc des murs de Cognac*, 1878, 15 pages. | BNF; SC07 ("incorrectly attributed … to … *Nostoc*"); WI | primary |
| F5 | In **1881** Richon and Petit described it as *Torula compniacensis*, in a note on "the cryptogamic plant of the walls of Cognac". | SC07 intro + refs | primary |
| F6 | *compniacensis* = "from Cognac" (Rogers: literally "the *Torula* from Cognac"). Corroborated by Richon & Petit's title (F5). | WI; SC07 refs | secondary + primary title |
| F7 | *Torula* was a broad catch-all genus: over 300 names put in it, nearly all since moved elsewhere. | SC07 intro (Crane 2001) | primary |
| F8 | After the 1950s the scientific literature had nothing on it; industry-commissioned studies on three continents kept naming the same ordinary, everywhere-fungi, which Scott calls "etiologically implausible". | SC07 intro | primary |
| F9 | Scott found the old name through trade literature and the web, and traced it with Stanley Hughes in Canada's national herbarium (Ottawa), where a roofing-tile sample matched. | SC07 intro; WI | primary + secondary |
| F10 | The 2007 paper showed it isn't a *Torula*, made a new genus, and named it "in honor of Antonin Baudoin" (Etymology line). The species name was kept. | SC07 taxonomy | primary |
| F11 | Why him: the team chose "the man who first brought the stuff to the attention of mycologists" (Rogers' account; Scott couldn't name it for himself). | WI | secondary |
| F12 | Its **closest** relatives in the 2007 analysis: *Friedmanniomyces*, rock-dwelling fungi "known only from the Antarctic"; next, weakly, *Trimmatostroma*. | SC07 discussion | primary |
| F13 | It is believed to use food other than alcohol; it's millions of years old and big ethanol production is recent, so "this must be the case". | OX pdf 238; WI (as a question) | primary + secondary |
| F14 | No simple formula predicts *Baudoinia* growth (Oxford's words are about growth, not about where). | OX pdf 238 | primary |
| F15 | A. Baudoin also wrote a book on cognac making (1893), cited twice in Oxford's cognac entries. | BNF; OX pdf 444, 619 | primary |
| F16 | Rogers: Baudoin was director of Cognac's agricultural and industrial chemistry laboratory. | WI | secondary |
| F17 | Scott's way into mycology: a first-day lecture about a fungus on peach pits where nobody knew how it spread; "I could go out there and look for stuff." | WI | secondary (his words via Rogers) |
| F18 | At Lakeshore (Ont.) the distillery had been told it was "normal environmental fungi"; Scott: "that's not what it is". | WI | secondary (quote via Rogers) |
| F19 | In 2015 the genus was split into five species by continent; *B. compniacensis* now means only the French one. | Scott et al., *Persoonia* 37 (2015), PMC5315285 (title/abstract; via Wikipedia lead, abstract not yet quoted here) | lead: confirm before use |

## Conflicts
| # | Conflict | Ruling |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | Renaming date: Oxford "the 1990s" vs the renaming paper itself, 2007. | The paper is the act; Oxford is wrong. Still, per the rule, the reading gives **no decade**: "more than a century later" is true on both. |
| C2 | Oxford "related only to a fungus that grows in Antarctica" vs SC07 "most closely related" to a genus (plural species), with *Trimmatostroma* next. | Never "only". Say "its nearest known relatives live on rock in Antarctica". |
| C3 | Baudoin's pamphlet: Wired "in 1872"; BnF 1878; SC07 places it after Chatin, "a few years later". | 1878 (BnF). Never "in 1872 he published". 1872 is only when he brought it to botanists. |
| C4 | Order of who named what: Wired has Roumeguère after Richon; SC07 has Roumeguère and Durieu first (1872). | Keep the order out beyond F4 → F5 → F10. |
| C5 | Oxford says Baudoin "first … identify the mold, originally called *Torula*". He didn't name it *Torula* (Richon & Petit did), and he thought it wasn't a fungus. | "First to raise it", never "first identified it" or "he called it". |

## Legends and guards
- L1: "It was mistaken for soot." Not on any page: the sources say it *looked* sooty (SC07) or as if chimney sweeps had been through (WI). Say "looked like soot", never "was taken for soot".
- G1: never "angels' share" (Lover family; sibling's ground); "the spirit that escapes the barrels".
- G2: Lakeshore complaints, Louisville lawsuit (OX), Hiram Walker's contract: out (clause 4, profit).
- G3: Night Light fence: no yeast, flor, film, veil, cover, protect, white; *Torula* never glossed as a yeast.
- G4: "improves the spirit" only as what some distillers believe (OX pdf 238, 1347).
- G5: Scott is living: anything present-tense about him is dated and secondary.
