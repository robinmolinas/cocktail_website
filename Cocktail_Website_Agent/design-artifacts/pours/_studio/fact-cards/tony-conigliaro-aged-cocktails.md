# Fact card: Tony Conigliaro's bottle-aged cocktails, and the barrels that followed

Our words throughout. Quotes under 15 words, all read on screen on 2026-10-01: *A Proper Drink* printed pp. 227-229 and 236 (pdf 241-243, 250); Oxford BARREL-AGED COCKTAILS (pdf 216-217, by David Wondrich; Conigliaro's quote there is from a personal communication, May 2016); Robert Simonson, "The Aged Cocktail's Barrel Zero", *PUNCH*, 19 June 2019 (an oral history; quotes are the named speakers' own, secondary; fetched with curl, full page read). Simonson is a reliable secondary: lines in quotation marks from Conigliaro are on the record, the rest is his narration. Note spelling: *Proper* prints "Morganthaler"; Oxford and *PUNCH* print **Morgenthaler** (use this).

## Conflicts and fences

| # | Issue | Ruling |
| --- | --- | --- |
| C1 | Six weeks (Oxford pdf 217; Tilden) vs seven weeks in *Proper*'s printed Barrel-Aged Negroni (p. 236). | Say "weeks" (or "about six weeks" for the Manhattan, Oxford's scope). |
| C2 | "Being an impatient American" and "took the idea to the bank" are Simonson's (p. 236), not Morgenthaler's. No source gives Morgenthaler's own reason for switching to barrels. | Both out (Wren r1). "Couldn't wait five years" is **our reading**: signposted ("I like to think…") or not said. |
| C3 | Motive: "to see how they evolved" is Simonson's narration; Conigliaro's own words (F8) are a hypothesis borrowed from wine. | The reading may say he wanted to see whether time could do for a cocktail what it does for wine (our words). Never "with no idea what he'd find". |
| C4 | What went round the world was barrel-aging (wood, weeks), which Morgenthaler adapted, not Conigliaro's glass (years). Oxford does date the revival's start to the 2009 visit (pdf 216). | Safe: "the idea spread", "it started with his bottle". Never "his method went round the world". |
| C5 | 2004 is printed only in *Proper* p. 236. | Consistent with Oxford (five-year-old in 2009) and *PUNCH*; usable, labelled *Proper*'s in the dossier. |

## Madeira, for Tomás's glass (added r3, 2026-10-01)

Read on screen 2026-10-01: the Madeira DO rules, Portaria n.º 38/2015 and n.º 39/2015, *Jornal Oficial da Região Autónoma da Madeira* I série n.º 28, 2.º supl., 13 Feb 2015 (joram.madeira.gov.pt, Portuguese original, curl + PDFKit; English translation hosted by madeirawijn.nl, same text); *Cocktail Codex* p. 251; Oxford ALDEHYDES pdf 88 and OXIDATION pdf 1458; Barbeito's technical sheet for its Rainwater 3 Anos (producer's spec sheet, secondary).

| # | Claim (our words) | Source | Tier | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| M1 | "Rainwater" is a legal designation of Madeira defined by colour (pale to gold) and sweetness (1.0 to 2.5 degrees Baumé), and it may carry an age of at most 10 years. The law sets **no minimum age and no oak time** for it. | Portaria 38/2015, art. 7 no. 3 b) (JORAM 13 Feb 2015) | primary (the law) | **Codex p. 251's "a minimum of three years in oak" is not the rule.** Never say it of rainwater as a category. |
| M2 | All DO Madeira is made one of two ways. Canteiro: at least 2 years in wood, not bottled before 3 years. Estufagem: kept in heated containers at no more than 50 °C for at least 3 months, and not sold before 31 October of the second year after the harvest. | Portaria 39/2015, arts. 9, 13 no. 3-4; Portaria 38/2015 art. 7 no. 3 a) | primary (the law) | So every Madeira waits roughly two years or more before sale, by law. Estufagem containers aren't required to be wood. |
| M3 | Barbeito's Rainwater 3 Anos (medium dry): estufagem, then 3 years in oak casks before blending and bottling; 18.27% abv; Baumé 1.5; total sugars 58 g/L; total acidity 6.46 (units not printed). | Barbeito technical sheet (vinhosbarbeito.com) | secondary (producer's sheet) | "Three years in oak" is true of **this bottle**, not the category. A recommended bottle or a label age statement is what can carry it. |
| M4 | Madeira is among the wines and spirits "marked by oxidation", with high levels of acetaldehyde and other aldehydes (nutty, bruised-apple aromas). | Oxford, ALDEHYDES pdf 88 | primary | Second page beyond the Codex for the oxidation. Oxford OXIDATION (pdf 1458) says that in most wines oxidation "would kill the freshness": Madeira is the deliberate exception, so never "oxidation is good for wine". |
| M5 | Rainwater: "a whisper of residual sugar"; lightens cocktails "without making them seem diluted". | Codex p. 251 | secondary (craft book) | Category lines. **"Bright, citrusy acidity" sits under the Broadbent bottle only**, not rainwater in general (the recommended-bottle trap). |
| M6 | Morgenthaler's first barrel-aged cocktail (a Manhattan) went into a one-gallon barrel that had held Madeira (F14). | Oxford pdf 217; *PUNCH* 2019 | primary + secondary | "The Madeira nods to his barrel" is **our symbolism**, fair when worded that way. Never "the Madeira his Manhattan aged in" as a taste claim, and never that he chose Madeira for the cocktail (it was seasoning the barrel for something else). |

## The El Presidente (added r4, 2026-10-01)

| # | Claim (our words) | Source | Tier | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| E1 | A Cuban cocktail of rum, vermouth and dashes of curaçao and grenadine, introduced in the mid-1910s; first in print in Havana in 1915 (John B. Escalante, *Manual del cantinero*). | Oxford, EL PRESIDENTE pdf 715 | primary | Inventor contested (Ribalaigua "quite possible"; a Chicago 1912 claim uncorroborated): **no inventor in the reading.** |
| E2 | It "rapidly achieved widespread popularity" at home and abroad, and was especially popular with Cubans themselves; in the 1930s "not far behind" the Sidecar. | Oxford pdf 715 | primary | Supports "a Cuban classic", "popular far beyond Havana". |
| E3 | Its popularity did not outlast the 1950s; recent years have seen "a cautious" revival. | Oxford pdf 716 | primary | **Fence: "renowned" overshoots** (and "a renowned classic" is already *Not Only the Way*'s and *Next One's Mine*'s phrase). |
| E4 | The original vermouth was a semi-sweet white vermouth de Chambéry; most published recipes said dry ("French"), leaving it "awkwardly balanced". Oxford's recipe: 45 ml each white Cuban rum and sweet white vermouth, 5 ml each orange curaçao and grenadine, stirred, strained, up; orange twist and cherry. | Oxford pdf 715-716 | primary | "White Cuban rum" is Oxford's noun. |
