## Legends

- **L1: "lost it all".** A 1976 wine-auction catalogue (*The Eighth Premiere National Auction of Rare Wines*, 27 May 1976, FTS OCR fragment) has a line ending "…Selwyns and lost them all before he died in 1949". The OCR doesn't show what "them" refers to (fortunes, presumably). It's a fragment, written 27 years later, and uncited. Oxford says only "looked to these interests for livelihood". **Out:** it's the ruin frame.
- **L3: the gin Red Snapper.** Dale DeGroff, quoted in O'Hara's *The Bloody Mary* (1999; FTS), says the St. Regis used gin in the Red Snapper because there wasn't any vodka. Gaige's printed recipe (F17) is vodka. Later gin versions exist, but the one Lauryssen gave Gaige is vodka. **For the reading: vodka.**
- **L2: the memoir as a source of fact.** Shaw: Gaige's memoir gave his birth year as 1881, but his draft card says 1882. Treat any memoir-only detail as his telling, not fact.

## Conflicts

- **C1: the guide's year.** Oxford (GAIGE pdf 861; WHITE RUSSIAN pdf 2186), the 1942 review (F5), the copyright page (F6) and Open Library all give **1941**. *Joy* gives **1945** everywhere (pdf 67, 72, 249, 266, 356, 358, 373; its bibliography, pdf 429). **Our reading (inference):** Regan used a later printing. Oxford's WHITE RUSSIAN also says "four years later" than 1936, which would be 1940: loose wording. **For the reading: 1941, or no year. Never 1945.**
- **C3: the St. Regis drink's year.** Oxford RED SNAPPER says served "since 1934" (pdf 1624). BLOODY MARY has a vodka bar in 1936 and a documented speciality "since at least 1941" (pdf 292), with no contemporary evidence before that. **No year for the St. Regis drink.**
- **C2: birth year** 1881 (memoir) vs 1882 (Oxford, draft card via Shaw, age 66 at death). No birth year in a reading.
