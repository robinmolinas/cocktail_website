# Fact card: Jim Beam at Repeal, the Old Tub name, and the family at the stills

Written by Hester, 2026-10-01, for ruler-hero (The Noble Ruler). Reuse these rows as they stand; don't re-tell them differently.
Sources: Oxford Companion, JIM BEAM (pdf 1105-1107, Lew Bryson; bibliography Pacult 2003, Regan 1995, Rothbaum 2015), primary (library). Chuck Cowdery, "What's in a Name? (Beam's Version)", *The Chuck Cowdery Blog*, May 2024 (chuckcowdery.blogspot.com/2024/05/whats-in-name-beams-version.html, read in full by curl), secondary (named bourbon historian).

## Facts

| ID | Fact | Source | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | James Beauregard Beam, born 1864, into a family making whisky since the 1790s. | Oxford pdf 1105 | primary |
| F2 | His father David M. Beam and grandfather David made bourbon "at their Old Tub distillery"; "the Old Tub name gained some distinction through the late 1800s". Cowdery: the distillery was Beam & Hart, near Bardstown, and its "main bourbon was called Old Tub". | Oxford pdf 1105; Cowdery 2024 | primary; secondary |
| F3 | Jim "took over the operation of Old Tub in 1892, along with his brother-in-law" (Oxford). Cowdery: brothers Jim and Park, with brother-in-law Albert Hart, "took over the operation". **Operation: neither page says who owned it.** | Oxford pdf 1105; Cowdery 2024 | primary; secondary |
| F4 | T. Jeremiah joined in 1913, about six years before Prohibition "shut everything down". | Oxford pdf 1105 | primary |
| F5 | Cowdery: during Prohibition "the brothers bought the property" at Clermont (it had been a distillery, and had a gravel quarry). | Cowdery 2024 | secondary |
| F6 | At Repeal, late 1933, "still spry at age sixty-nine", he "gathered his family" (brother Park, Park's sons Carl and Earl, and T. Jeremiah); "family tradition recalls him saying" a line to them about getting back to work (Oxford quotes it; the line is family memory). Cowdery: "with their sons, the Beam brothers rebuilt and reopened the distillery at Clermont". | Oxford pdf 1105; Cowdery 2024 | primary; secondary |
| F7 | Oxford: "By the following August" he had raised capital "from the Blum family of Chicago and their National Brokerage Co." and started construction; "the Beams would manage operations, the Blums would own the business". Distilling began 120 days later. | Oxford pdf 1105 | primary |
| F8 | Oxford: "To Jim's chagrin, he learned that he had lost the rights to the Old Tub name", so the whisky became a whiskey under his own name, "soon to become the familiar 'Jim Beam.'" Cowdery: "There were some problems with ownership of the Old Tub brand name, so Jim Beam became the name of the brand and company." | Oxford pdf 1105; Cowdery 2024 | primary; secondary |
| F9 | Oxford: when Jim retired in 1944, "his nephew Carl joined T. Jeremiah in running operations". | Oxford pdf 1105 | primary |
| F10 | Cowdery: "In the 1940s, the family sold the company to a trio of Chicago investors. The new owners kept the name, and the family." Jere ran the business side; Park's sons Earl and "Shucks" made the whiskey. Harry Blum, "by then sole owner", sold to American Tobacco (Oxford: 1967). | Cowdery 2024; Oxford pdf 1105 | secondary; primary |
| F11 | Oxford: Jim Beam remains "the best-selling bourbon"; the white label is "a four-year-old 80-proof whisky". (Oxford accessed sources in 2021.) | Oxford pdf 1105-1106 | primary, dated |
| F12 | Cowdery, 2024: "Beam descendants still have a large say in how those whiskeys are made." | Cowdery 2024 | secondary, dated |

## Cautions

| ID | Caution |
|---|---|
| C1 | **Ownership is contested.** Oxford has the Blums owning the business from 1934 (F7); Cowdery has the family selling it in the 1940s (F10). Keep who owned what, and when, out of guest text. Never "he gave up the ownership" or "he let them own it". |
| C2 | **The name was lost, not given.** Oxford: "he learned that he had lost the rights". Never "he gave up the name" or "he chose to". Why it was lost isn't on either page. |
| C3 | **Chagrin** is Oxford's word alone (Cowdery says only "some problems"). Uncontested, so usable as Oxford's account. |
| C4 | **Agency at Repeal:** Oxford has Jim gathering the family; Cowdery has "the Beam brothers" with their sons. Never "he alone"; "he brought them back" rests on Oxford. |
| C5 | The family-tradition line is gendered and in the first person plural: told only as family memory, paraphrased, never quoted in guest text. |
| C6 | Never "Colonel" (the label's title). Say "his own name". |
| C7 | Nobody's later path beyond F9-F10 is on these pages: no "all four stayed for good". |
| C8 | No motive for anything is on record (why he went back at 69, why he went on under his own name): "I like to think". "A mark he never sought" is not on either page. |
| C9 | Unchecked, from memory only, **stays out**: that the Old Tub name is back on a Beam bottle today. |
| C10 | Booker Noe, American Tobacco, Suntory, and Jacob Boehm's own name change stay out (Wren's guards). |
