# The Vesper

Fiction, owned as fiction. The drink is real; the man who orders it is a character. Our words below; quotations kept short.

## Facts

| # | claim (our words) | source + page | tier | notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| F1 | The Vesper is gin, vodka and the French aperitif wine Lillet (once sold as Kina Lillet). It's the first cocktail James Bond orders in the novel that introduced him, Fleming's *Casino Royale* (1953). | Oxford, VESPER (pdf 2099) | primary (library) | Own the fiction in one clause: "in Fleming's first novel". |
| F2 | His order gives the measures himself: three measures of Gordon's, one of vodka, half a measure of Kina Lillet; shake it very well until ice-cold; then a large thin slice of lemon peel. | Oxford, VESPER (pdf 2099), quoting the novel | primary (library) | Proportions 3 : 1 : ½. A "measure" = 20 ml is Tomás's conversion, ours. Gordon's to vodka is three to one (Oxford, TANQUERAY GORDON & CO., pdf 1986). |
| F3 | In the novel he first asks the barman for "a dry martini", then: "One. In a deep champagne goblet." The measures follow. | Fleming, *Casino Royale*, read as archive.org full-text OCR highlights of two printings: `casinoroyalejame0000flem` (2002) and `casinoroyale0000flem_i2v0` (2006), same words in both; fetched 2026-10-04 | primary text via OCR highlight (lending-only books) | Settles the glass: the deep champagne goblet is the novel's. Oxford's "chilled cocktail glass" (pdf 2100) belongs to Oxford's own modern recipe, as do *Joy*'s (pdf 402) and the *Codex*'s Nick & Nora (p. 71). "One." = a single drink, on the page. Chapter and page number not read. |
| F4 | The drink is presented as his own invention on the spot, and at first it has no name. Later he borrows the name of his colleague Vesper Lynd and christens it the Vesper. | Oxford, VESPER (pdf 2099) | primary (library) | "Later": no page here gives the scene's order beyond that. |
| F5 | After her death at the end of the book, he never drinks it again. | Oxford, VESPER (pdf 2099) | primary (library) | **Scope:** her death is "at the end of the book", so "never again" can only cover Fleming's later books. Corroborated in other words by *Shaken Not Stirred* (archive.org `shakennotstirred00mill`, OCR highlight, cut off): "the Vesper was never mentioned again (when Vesper died, the name seemed …". The rest of that sentence is unread: its explanation is not usable. Verb: Oxford's is "drinks", not "orders". See C1. |
| F6 | Fleming inscribed a copy of *Casino Royale* to his longtime friend Ivar Bryce (a fellow Jamaica resident): "For Ivar, who mixed the first Vesper." | Oxford, VESPER (pdf 2099) | primary (library) | Oxford also calls Bryce an OSS veteran: out of guest text (Wren: no war, family ground). |
| F7 | Bryce's own account (his 1975 memoir, relayed by Oxford): he remembers making the cocktail with Fleming's help, and the name, for the character and the drink, came from a drink of frozen rum with fruit and herbs served at a country house in Jamaica, which the butler announced with "Vespers are served". | Oxford, VESPER (pdf 2099), citing Bryce 1975 | primary (library) relaying a memoir | Oxford hedges twice: "a much more plausible story" and "apparently". Tell it as Bryce's memory. **The page names no fruit, herb, rum style or rum origin.** The frozen rum gave the *name*, not the Vesper's recipe. Memoir title is a Bond-adjacent phrase: never in guest text (Wren). |
| F8 | "Kina" (French for quinine) was dropped from Lillet's name in 1986. The reduction in its bitterness came in 1917, decades before Bryce and Fleming mixed with it (Londeix 1998, via Oxford). | Oxford, VESPER (pdf 2099–2100) | primary (library) | So never "the original can't be made". See C3 before saying "the same bottle". |
| F9 | Regan: Bond insisted his Martini be shaken, preferred Lillet to vermouth, and thought a grain vodka better than a potato one. The vodka softens the gin; a soft gin leaves the drink little character. | *Joy*, pdf 402 | secondary (library) | Regan's own recipe is 2 : ½ : ¼, shaken, cocktail glass. |
| F10 | The *Codex* files the Vesper as a classic Martini whose two core spirits are combined, with Lillet blanc in place of vermouth; the vodka stretches the gin's botanicals and lets the Lillet show. | *Cocktail Codex*, p. 71 (also pp. 65, 75) | secondary (library) | The *Codex* stirs it into a Nick & Nora: its method, not the novel's. |
| F11 | In the 2006 film, the spy shows his expertise by inventing the Vesper. | Oxford, ACTOR HEINZ RÜHMANN (pdf 782) | primary (library) | Films stay out of guest text entirely. |

## Legends

| # | legend | status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| L1 | The Vesper was created for Fleming at Dukes Bar, London, by Gilberto Preti. | **Debunked**: Preti started at Dukes almost twenty years after Fleming's death; the recipe was in print long before (Oxford pdf 2099; DUKES BAR pdf 689). Dossier only. |
| L2 | Today's Lillet can't make the original, because the quinine went in 1986. | **False rumour**: the bitterness change came in 1917 (Oxford pdf 2100). |
| L3 | "Shaken, not stirred." | A film line, not this order (Wren: never in guest text). The novel's order does say to shake it (F2). |

## Conflicts

| # | conflict | what it means for the reading |

| C1 | Oxford's "never drinks it again" vs the films. Wren remembers a post-death Vesper in the 2006 film (unverified); one unvetted OCR highlight (*Tales of Insomnia*) puts a Vesper in *Quantum of Solace* (2008). | C3 | *Codex* p. 74: the original Kina Lillet formula had "a bit more cinchona" than today's. Oxford pdf 2100: the bitterness reduction came in 1917. | Neither "the original can't be made" nor "the same Lillet Fleming had". Say "Lillet Blanc, once sold as Kina Lillet". |
| C4 | Who made the first Vesper: the novel presents it as Bond's on-the-spot invention; the inscription credits Bryce with mixing the first; Bryce recalls making it with Fleming's help. | The inscription is the fact; Bryce's account is his memory; the novel's is fiction. Don't merge them. |
| C5 | Oxford's own words for her ("lover", "double agent", "suicide"). | On the page, out of guest text (Wren). "After he lost her" is the ceiling. |
