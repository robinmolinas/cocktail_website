## Legends / cautions
- **C1 Was burnt sack set on fire?** Not established. A named blogger (Purdy, *Virtual Grub Street*, 2017) reads it as sack warmed with sugar, citing Dekker 1607 ("sacke and suger to be burnt"); one period line he quotes hints at flame. Not strong enough: never "flamed sherry in Shakespeare's day". "Hot sugared sherry" is closer, still hedged.
- **C2 The Shakespeare trap.** *Shakespeare in Love* is the persona's example, not a drink's history. Putting Shakespeare in the reading because of the card is the literal-example trap (same lesson as Dalí, creator-jester). Only if it mirrors the guest on its own.
