# Fact card: Stanislav Vadrna and Paparazzi's menu (the parcel from New Orleans)

Written by Hester, 2026-10-01, for innocent-hero (The Underdog). Every quote below was on screen in the same session. Tiers: *A Proper Drink* (Simonson 2016) = library, reliable secondary with his words on the record (interviews 5/6/15 and 5/11/15, p. 333). Tales of the Cocktail's own winners archive = the organiser's record (closest to primary for the award).

## Facts
| id | fact | source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| F1 | In 1994 he saw the Tom Cruise film *Cocktail*; "It changed my life" (his words). The film is older (1988): never "the new film". | *Proper* p. 206 |
| F2 | The next day he walked into the local pub he worked in, in the village of Zemné (Simonson: "two thousand-plus"), and asked the boss to buy him a cocktail shaker. **The page doesn't say the boss bought it.** Say "asked", never "got". | *Proper* p. 206 |
| F3 | Born in Trnava (Simonson spells it "Ternava"), then part of Czechoslovakia. Zemné is where he worked, not where he was from. | *Proper* p. 206 |
| F4 | In the late 1990s (a cocktail bar boom in Prague) he wanted to work at Tretter's New York Cocktail Bar, a formal place. He had long dreadlocks. "What about the hair?" asked the owner. He "trimmed it back a bit". | *Proper* p. 206 |
| F5 | "Every weekend, he took the train five hours to Prague to barback at Tretter's", then went back to Slovakia, where he ran a restaurant during the week. **No duration is given:** never "for years". | *Proper* p. 206 |
| F6 | 2002: started at Paparazzi (Bratislava, pp. 35, 134, 205) as a bartender, then head bartender, then bar manager; stayed until 2009. When he arrived it served Daiquiris, Margaritas, Piña Coladas and High Societys, after the Munich (Schumann) style. Never claim those as his. | *Proper* pp. 206–207 |
| F7 | "With every trip Vadrna took, Paparazzi's menu evolved." After a visit to London he threw out the bar's thick glassware and simple garnishes and brought in tropical fruits imported from Holland. | *Proper* pp. 206–207 |
| F8 | When not travelling he "haunted Internet cafés", saw photos of bartenders and translated articles from English to Slovak. **The page doesn't say the cafés fed the menu:** keep the study and the menu in separate clauses, or link them with "I like to think". | *Proper* p. 207 |
| F9 | On his first New York trip he walked into a bar he knew from the internet, "fishing for ideas to improve Paparazzi". | *Proper* p. 134 |
| F10 | Having read about the Spirited Awards online, "on a whim" he mailed the menu he created for Paparazzi to the founder of Tales of the Cocktail (name her never; Hero plan guard). "Many weeks earlier" than the parcel. The whim is the posting, never the win. | *Proper* p. 205 |
| F11 | At the first ceremony the Best Cocktail Menu went to Paparazzi. "Two months later, a package arrived in Bratislava". "He didn't know his bar had won until he opened his mail." Contents of the package: not stated (no trophy, no certificate). | *Proper* p. 205 |
| F12 | **The year is 2007, not Simonson's 2008.** Tales' own archive lists 2007: "Best Cocktail Menu · Papparazzi · Bratislava, Slovakia" (their spelling), and says the awards were established in 2007; 2008's Best Cocktail Menu was 1806 Bar, Melbourne. Difford's Guide's winners list agrees (2007 Papparazzi, 2008 1806 Bar). Simonson's own "first ceremony" fits 2007. | talesofthecocktail.org/spirited-awards-archive (fetched 2026-10-01); diffordsguide.com encyclopedia/1014 (fetched 2026-10-01); *Proper* p. 205 |
| F13 | Simonson's judgement: "the prize for obscure and improbable must go to Vadrna". Attribute it to Simonson; never "obscure" in our voice. | *Proper* p. 205 |
| F14 | Eben Klemm: "He was a man in the wilderness"; Simonson glosses it as a lone soul collecting whatever he could about New York and London cocktails. Klemm's phrase, attributed. | *Proper* p. 205 |

## Conflicts and fences
| id | note |
| --- | --- |
| C1 | **Year:** Simonson 2008 vs the organiser's archive 2007. Tell no year in the reading ("the first year of a new award" is safe and true in both). If a year is ever needed: 2007, sourced to Tales, with the conflict recorded. |
| C2 | **Trnava's size:** Simonson's "a city of four hundred thousand" is far off. Wikidata (Statistical Office of the Slovak Republic) gives 63,803 at the 2021 census and 63,184 at end-2025. Never state Trnava's population. |
| C3 | **Doubt on the page** is the owner's "What about the hair?" (doubt that he fitted a formal bar), not an accusation of cheating. The fear of being disbelieved stays the guest's. |
