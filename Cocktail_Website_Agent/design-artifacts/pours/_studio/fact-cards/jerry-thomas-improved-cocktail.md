# Fact card: Jerry Thomas and the Improved Cocktail

Our words throughout. Wondrich's judgements are his, labelled as such.

## Facts

| # | Claim (our words) | Source | Tier | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| F1 | Thomas's *How to Mix Drinks* (1862) is the first book with a section of recipes devoted to the Cocktail: thirteen of them. | *Imbibe!*, pdf 173; Oxford ARRACK PUNCH (pdf 155) calls it "pioneering" | primary | "First book with a cocktail section", not "first bar book". |
| F2 | In 1876 he and his publisher reprinted the book with a supplement of new drinks. In that edition Thomas adds three "Improved" Cocktails (brandy, gin, whiskey). | *Imbibe!*, pdf 17, 173, 179 | primary | His own standard, revised by him, 14 years on. |
| F3 | What changed: curaçao out, maraschino in; a dash of absinthe added (the "more important" change, Wondrich); Angostura allowed as an option. | *Imbibe!*, pdf 179–180 | primary | |
| F4 | "Bogart's" became "Boker's": the 1862 bitters name was a publishing or transcription error, fixed in 1876. | *Imbibe!*, pdf 179; Oxford BITTERS (pdf 274) | primary | A printer's slip, **not his mistake**. Never "he corrected his own error". |
| F5 | He kept the old ones: in 1876 the book was reprinted with a supplement of new drinks, so the 1862 recipes stayed and the Improved Cocktails were added beside them. | *Imbibe!*, pdf 17 ("reprinted his book with a supplement"), 173 ("adds"), 176 | primary | "Printed beside" / "stayed in the book" are sourced. "Kept them on purpose" is not. |
| F6 | Wondrich: the improvement is "a subtle one" (pdf 173); of the drink, "nothing wrong with it at all" (pdf 180). | *Imbibe!* | primary (judgement) | "Better" is Wondrich's opinion and the book's label, not a fact. |
| F7 | Wondrich: the Sazerac is originally "nothing more than" Thomas's Improved Whiskey Cocktail with the bitters juggled; the Sazerac "was the Improved Whiskey Cocktail". | *Imbibe!*, pdf 183–184 | primary (Wondrich's reading) | The revision outlived its author under another name. |
| F8 | Thomas "differed with himself" in 1862: his brandy Cocktail stirred and unstrained, his gin and whiskey ones shaken and strained. | *Imbibe!*, pdf 178 | primary | A master not bound by his own rules. |
| F9 | Wondrich suggests the Holland gin version with the maraschino replaced by an old-school orange curaçao, i.e. putting back the 1862 liqueur. | *Imbibe!*, pdf 180 | primary | Useful for Tomás: maraschino is nuts in our table. |
| F10 | Thomas died in 1885; the 1887 edition was a thorough revision by an unnamed expert bartender. | *Imbibe!*, pdf 17 | primary | 1887 is **not** his. |

| F11 | The 1876 serve (Wondrich's composite of Thomas): mixed in an ordinary bar-glass one-third full of shaved ice, **shaken** well, strained into a fancy cocktail glass, rim moistened with lemon; lemon rind twisted to express its oil. Amounts: 2 dashes Boker's (or Angostura), 3 dashes gum syrup, 2 dashes maraschino, 1 dash absinthe, 1 small wine-glass of spirit. | *Imbibe!*, pdf 180 | primary | "Composite" = Wondrich's reconstruction. Stirring is Wondrich's allowance ("be right and stir"), so a stirred drink is **ours**. |
| F12 | Dash sizes are Wondrich's modern readings: gum 3 dashes ≈ 1 tsp; maraschino 2 dashes ≈ ½ tsp; absinthe 1 dash ≈ ⅛ tsp; small wine-glass ≈ 2 oz. | *Imbibe!*, pdf 180 (brackets) | primary (editorial) | Never "Thomas's ⅛ teaspoon": the brackets are Wondrich's. |
| F13 | By 1876 the small cup-bottomed coupe had been adopted for Cocktails; in 1862 a small wineglass had to do. | *Imbibe!*, pdf 178 | primary | |
| F14 | Chartreuse reaches American cocktail books only in the 1880s (Harry Johnson, Kappeler: Bijou, Widow's Kiss). | Oxford CHARTREUSE (pdf 439) | primary | Thomas never used it. Ours: the next decade's new bottle in his slot. Owned as our choice. |
| F15 | Spirit: Thomas's recipe is for brandy, gin **or** whiskey, unspecified type; Wondrich recommends Holland gin, cognac and rye. | *Imbibe!*, pdf 179–180 | primary | "Rye" is Wondrich's and our pick, not Thomas's. |

| F16 | Oxford independently: apart from its way of adding the absinthe (rinsed round the glass and thrown out), the Sazerac was "virtually identical" to Thomas's 1876 Improved Whisky Cocktail. | Oxford, SAZERAC COCKTAIL (pdf 1753) | primary | A second source for F7, and the one to cite. |
| F17 | The Sazerac is tied to New Orleans: the Louisiana legislature declared it the city's official cocktail in 2008. It was first mentioned in print in 1899. | Oxford, SAZERAC COCKTAIL (pdf 1750, 1752) | primary | Its origin (Peychaud, 1850s) is contested and stays out. 1899 is after Thomas's death (1885): "it outlasted him" holds. |
| F18 | Absinthe was **not new** in 1876: a cocktail with absinthe is attested in 1843 and listed in an 1869 manual. What's sourced is "faddish in the 1870s and 1880s" and "everywhere". | *Imbibe!*, pdf 179–180 | primary | "The fashionable bottle of the day": yes. "New bottle": no (Hester's own round-2 slip). |
| F19 | Chartreuse today: made under the supervision of Carthusian monks, though most of the distillery staff are laypeople; the formula is known only to a handful of monks. | Oxford, CHARTREUSE (pdf 439) | primary | "Monks still make" overstates. Say "still made under Carthusian monks, to a recipe only a few of them know". |

## Not on record (stays out)
- **Why** he added the Improved Cocktails, or whether anyone asked. "When nobody was asking him to" is a motive/absence claim: signpost it ("I like to think…") or drop it. Imbibe pdf 17 says only that "he and his original publisher" reprinted with a supplement.

## Conflicts
- **C1** *Joy of Mixology* (pdf 36) calls the 1887 *Bar-Tender's Guide* Thomas's "second book". *Imbibe!* (pdf 17): posthumous, revised by someone else. Follow *Imbibe!*; never credit 1887 to him.

## Legends / keep out
- The 1883 *Tribune* bartender is "almost certainly" Thomas (Wondrich, pdf 179): inference only.
- Anything about Thomas's showmanship (the Blue Blazer): performed drink, and famous version. Not used.
