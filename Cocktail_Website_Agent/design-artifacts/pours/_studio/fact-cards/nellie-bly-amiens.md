# Fact card: Nellie Bly at Amiens

Source for every F row unless noted: Nellie Bly, *Nellie Bly's Book: Around the World in Seventy-Two Days* (New York: Pictorial Weeklies, 1890). Primary (her own account). Read in the transcription at *A Celebration of Women Writers*, University of Pennsylvania Digital Library (https://digital.library.upenn.edu/women/bly/world/world.html), fetched in full with curl and searched. The transcription gives chapters, not pages, so we cite chapters. Our words throughout. Every quotation was read on screen in this session and is under 15 words.

## Birthplace (for the rye lead)
- B1. Born Elizabeth Cochran, 5 May 1864, at Cochran's Mills, Pennsylvania. Sources: Ehrlich, *Nellie Bly* (1989), archive.org OCR highlight; Wikipedia (Burrell Township, PA; lead only). "Western Pennsylvania" isn't on a page read. Oxford RYE pdf 1715 makes rye western Pennsylvania's spirit "Beginning in the late colonial period", which is region and era, not her county. At most: "rye, long Pennsylvania's whiskey", owned as a coincidence.

## Guards (Wren's ruling, round 3)
The race, her speed, "first" and "around the world" are background, in one clause at most (*Nothing to It*, Explorer). The novel that inspired her is context only, never "inspired by" (the Muse's ground). The Vernes aren't the story: a household waiting on a platform is *Hoping You'd Come*'s, so the reading stays with the one arriving. Out: the rival (Elizabeth Bisland), her later life, her real name unless needed, "I can start this minute" (ch. I; *Off Duty*'s "already gone"), any exact date.
