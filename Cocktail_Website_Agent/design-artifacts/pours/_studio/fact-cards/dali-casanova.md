# Fact card: the Casanova in *Les Dîners de Gala*

**Status: secondary only.** The book itself is not in our library. Everything below comes from a named writer quoting it, cross-checked against the publisher.

## Facts

| # | Claim (our words) | Source | Tier | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| F1 | *Les Dîners de Gala* was first published in 1973: 136 recipes in 12 chapters, compiled and illustrated by Dalí, one chapter given to aphrodisiacs. Many of the recipes came from the chefs of Paris restaurants (Lasserre, La Tour d'Argent, Maxim's, Le Train Bleu). Taschen reprinted it in 2016. | Taschen, publisher's page (taschen.com, book 04639), fetched 2026-09-27 | secondary (publisher) | |
| F2 | The book prints a "Casanova cocktail": the juice of one orange, 1 tbsp Campari (listed as "bitters"), 1 tsp ginger, 4 tbsp brandy, 2 tbsp Vieille Cure (glossed as "old brandy"), a pinch of cayenne. | Maria Popova, *The Marginalian* (then *Brain Pickings*), 29 April 2014, quoting the book; fetched 2026-09-27 | secondary (named writer quoting the primary) | Frontier Mixology (2015) repeats it and credits Popova, so it isn't independent. |
| F3 | Method as printed: the pepper and ginger go in the bottom of the glass, then the Campari, then the two brandies; the glass goes in the fridge or freezer for thirty minutes, and the orange juice is stirred in at the end. | Popova 2014, quoting the book | secondary | |
| F4 | The book offers it as a pick-me-up for exhaustion, overwork, or "an excess of sobriety", and adds that it's a remedy you won't pull a face at. | Popova 2014, quoting the book | secondary | The "excess of sobriety" joke is the book's, on record. Quote ≤ 4 words. |

## Cautions
- **Not "Dalí's recipe" or "Dalí's invention."** It's printed in his book, and many of the book's recipes came from restaurant chefs. Who wrote the Casanova isn't on record. Say "a drink printed in Dalí's cookbook".
- **Which chapter?** The name suggests the aphrodisiac chapter, but we haven't seen that. Don't claim it.
- **Vieille Cure** is a herbal liqueur, not an "old brandy" (the gloss belongs to the book). Nothing in our library covers it, including whether it's still made. Tomás needs a stand-in and has to say so.
- **Library gap for Robin**, only if the room uses it: the Taschen reprint of *Les Dîners de Gala* (2016), so we can cite the page ourselves.
