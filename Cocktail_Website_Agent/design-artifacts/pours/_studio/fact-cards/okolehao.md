## Conflicts
- **C1 Did Hawaiians brew from ti before distilling?** Oxford pdf 1419: from the roasted ti "they brewed a mild, beer-like intoxicant". Alexander 1891 p. 87: "Neither fermented nor distilled liquors were used by the Hawaiians" (their drink was awa, kava). **So:** the underground roasting is safe (Oxford; Alexander p. 157 describes the baking), the traditional ti brew is not stated.
- **C2 When it came back.** Oxford: "Only since 2012"; Oxford's own bibliography lists Hoover, "Will New 'Okolehao be Your Cup of Ti?", *Honolulu Advertiser*, 2003. No year for the revival.
- **C3 Whaling pots in the 1790s.** "Whaling" rests on Oxford and Rowley; Alexander says only "procured from ships". I haven't checked when whalers reached Hawaii; until someone does, "pots off the ships" is the safe telling, and "whaling" is Oxford's.
- **C5 Who brought distilling.** Aloha State Daily 2026: "likely" introduced in the years after 1778, when British sailors brought distillation "with iron implements". Against Oxford/Alexander's Stevenson. Keeps him at "the man usually credited".
- **C4 The first legal distillery:** Oxford 1907 (Kona Okolehao Distillery) vs Rowley 1906 (E. H. Edwards imported a still). Out of guest text.
