## Conflicts

- **C1, who inherited.** Oxford GRAPPA (pdf 936): Giannola "had inherited the Nonino distillery". The house (H1): the distillery came down Benito's line and Giannola married in (1962). Never "inherited", and never say whose distillery it was by birth. Credit "Giannola and Benito Nonino" (house) or "Giannola Nonino and the house" (Oxford GRAPPA + POLI). Never Giannola alone as the only maker.
- **C2, the year.** Oxford: "early 1970s" (GRAPPA), "the 1970s" (POLI). House: 1 December 1973. No conflict, but the exact day is the house's: "in 1973" is safe; the day only with "the family says".
- **C3, the label (Wren's C7).** POLI's "selling … with varietal designations" belongs to the followers. The house calls the 1973 grappa "Picolit Cru Nonino" and the 1967 label gave year, amount, litres and bottle number. **No page I read shows the 1973 label naming the grape.** Say the house named it for its grape ("the family calls it…") at most; never "the label said picolit".
- **C4, "first".** First single-varietal **grappa** (Oxford twice, house). Never first single-variety pomace brandy (Alsace's gewürztraminer marc, pdf 1231), never "first quality grappa".
- **C5, the wait.** The early-1990s line is grappa's ridicule **outside Italy** (pdf 936), not a wait for the Noninos' own success. 1973 to "the early 1990s" is "nearly twenty years" at most (our arithmetic). Never "nobody believed her for twenty years", never "she waited twenty years".

- **C6, 1967 vs "first".** The house says its 1967 "Aquavitae Optima" was distilled from single-variety pomace (H2), yet both Oxford entries and the house itself call the 1973 picolit the first single-varietal grappa (the house calls 1967 "the first of a series of tests"). Never an absence before the 1970s ("no grappa did"); never call the 1967 bottling "single-grape" beside a "first" for 1973.
- **F12 (library, round 4):** Oxford BORTOLO NARDINI pdf 319: Italy's first grappa house, founded 1779 in Bassano; grappa "had been made in Italy previously"; Nardini marketed it as a quality spirit, not a poor farmer's way to squeeze every drop of alcohol from his harvest. Supports "since the 1700s or earlier" and the thrift for grappa itself; kills "always".

- **R2, "unaged" is wrong in law:** a young grappa (giovane/bianco) rests at least six months, usually in steel or glass (Oxford pdf 935; card `angelo-gaja-barbaresco.md` P2). Say "young, not wood-aged".
- **R3, fig leaves:** *Flavor Matrix* FIG pdf 120: trees grown mainly for the fruit; the "highly aromatic leaves are another valuable commodity", wrapped round food before grilling or roasting to give their flavour. So the leaf is used in cooking, and isn't a part nobody wants. **Fig sap irritating skin: no library page.** Kept as a labelled safety caution (unsourced), never as a fact in guest text beyond the recipe note.
- **R4, Codex p. 94** describes room-temperature infusions for ingredients "that infuse very quickly, generally an hour or less", tasted every couple of minutes. A description of quick ingredients, not a limit.
