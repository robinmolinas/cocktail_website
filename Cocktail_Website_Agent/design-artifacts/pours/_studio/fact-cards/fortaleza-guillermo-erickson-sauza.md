## Legends
- None told. Fortaleza = "strength of spirit" is Miller's gloss ("essentially means"), not a fact about why he chose it: out.

## Conflicts
- **C1 Who sold, and when.** Oxford: Francisco Javier sold half to Domecq, the rest passed at his death in 1990. Guillermo: "my grandfather" sold in 1976. Miller's narration: "His father Javier sold" in 1976, which contradicts both Oxford's lineage and his own words. Reading: no year of sale, no "his father"; "the family business was sold" or "passed to a Spanish company and on".
- **C2 Los Abuelos.** Oxford: renamed because of a rum's trademark. Miller: the name couldn't be used in the United States. Reading: "a rum's trademark forced another name"; never "everywhere" or "worldwide".
- **C3 Launch year.** Miller says 2002 (Mexico); the search summary said 2005 (not read on a page, not cited). Reading: no year, or "more than twenty years" dated to 2025 only via W3/W5.
- **C4 Volume.** Miller's 323,000 litres a year and "approximately 10,400 bottles" can't both be true. No numbers.
- **C5 Estate-grown.** Oxford's "100 percent estate-grown" (an aim) vs 2025's bought agave. Reading: "agave grown on the family's land" is true of part; never "every agave".

## Guards
- G1 "Barred", "forced": never "gave up", "walked away" or "refused the name". Who barred him isn't on the page.
- G2 No villains: diffusers answered a shortage (F4); Sauza has 100% agave lines (F3); the family itself sold.
- G3 "Ancient" for the mill type only (F7).
- G4 Not the only tahona (F8).
- G5 "Lost complexity", "best juice" and quality are his words; no quality fact in the bartender's voice.
- G6 His "no additives" line stays out (adulteration neighbours; it reads as a sneer).
- G7 The old distillery was called La Fortaleza (W2, his words) and the brand became Fortaleza (F6, Oxford). Neither says he named the brand after it: two facts side by side, or "I like to think".
- G8 Present tense rests on 2025: "as of 2025" in the dossier.
