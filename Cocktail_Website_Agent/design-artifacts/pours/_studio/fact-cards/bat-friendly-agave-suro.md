# Fact card: the agave flower, its bats, and David Suro-Piñera

Hester, 2026-09-30, for explorer-caregiver (*The Conservationist*). Tiers: library = primary; web = secondary, named. Web quotes were read through WebFetch extracts on 2026-09-30: re-open the page before any of them is quoted in a reading. Quotes here stay under 15 words.

## The plant (library, primary)

| # | Fact | Source |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | Agaves are monocarpic: each rosette flowers once, then dies. The flowering stalk is the *quiote*; it can grow up to 20 cm a day and reach 2–10 m. | Oxford, AGAVE pdf 74 |
| F2 | Pollen and nectar are produced at night; agaves are pollinated mostly by bats (also hawkmoths, hummingbirds, songbirds, bees, opossums). | Oxford, AGAVE pdf 74 |
| F3 | Agaves also clone themselves: the mother plant sends out offshoots (from about age 2–3, three or four a year), which farmers remove and replant. | Oxford, AGAVE pdf 74–75 |
| F4 | As tequila grew, producers "ceased using reproduction through the inflorescence": this removed a primary food of *Leptonycteris* bats, caused "a dramatic shift in their migratory paths", and let the crops' genetic diversity wane, leaving the plant open to disease. | Oxford, AGAVE pdf 77 |
| F5 | Blue agave takes five to nine years to reach harvestable size. | Oxford, TEQUILA pdf 2001 |
| F6 | Siembra Azul is "Produced by David Suro, a champion of ethical and quality tequila production"; clean, floral, works in stirred drinks too; "reasonably priced". Same page recommends Siete Leguas Blanco for stirred tequila drinks ("vegetal character"). | *Cocktail Codex* p. 164 |

## Conflicts and limits

- **C1 "Certified" vs "no official certification process".** batfriendly.org lists certifications by year; *PhillyVoice* (2023) says growers count as bat-friendly if they let 5% flower "though there is no official certification process". The reading says "let some flower", never "certified".
- **C2 Whose fields.** No page says Suro owns or farms agave fields. Siembra's tequilas are made with family distilleries (Siembra Alteño at La Alteña, *Mezcalistas* 2024). The ceiling is "one of his own tequilas" / "the agaves behind it", never "his fields".
- **C3 Conflict of interest, owned.** Siembra Valles Ancestral sits on the certified list of a programme Suro co-founded. True, and worth one honest clause in the dossier, never a gotcha in the reading.
- **C4 Motive.** His on-record reason joins the bats to the plant's own health (genetic strength). Never pure altruism, never "he did it for the bats" alone.
- **C5 The bats' fear is Oxford's migration shift**, not "they came back and found nothing". "Came back and the flowers were gone" is our reading and needs "I like to think".
- **C6 The delisting.** The lesser long-nosed bat came off the US endangered list in 2018 (plan row, USFWS; not re-opened this session). Many causes: never "tequila saved the bat".

| F21 | A second tequila-agave pollinator, the Mexican long-nosed bat (*Leptonycteris nivalis*), has been on the IUCN Red List as endangered since 1996; it migrates along a "nectar corridor" and pollinates agave. | Bat Conservation International, 15 Dec 2021 |

- **C7 Two bats.** Never "the bats recovered" or "the bats are back" in general: one species came off the US list, the other is still endangered. Name the lesser long-nosed bat or say nothing about recovery.
- **C8 The 7 Leguas asterisk** is unexplained on batfriendly.org (checked 2026-09-30). Never call Siete Leguas bat-friendly.
- **C9 "Capado"** (Tomás's term for cutting the young stalk) is on no page I opened. Not needed: F7–F8 state the cost without it. Keep the word out.
- **C10 EDF's blog** says farms letting 5% bloom "restore" feeding habitat: advocacy, causal. Out of the reading (C6).
