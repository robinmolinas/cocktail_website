## Conflicts, gaps and legends
- **C1 (my slip):** the family plan cites "pdf 1090–1091". The entry runs pdf 1091–1092. Cite pdf 1091 for the books and the tribute, pdf 1092 only for F12, F13 and H1.
- **C2 ("first"):** a search summary relays a 1996 interview in which he said he thought he was the first person to use the phrase "beer style". I couldn't open the page (fetch failed), so it is **not a fact here**. Lead only: Cornell, *Zythophile*, 23 Oct 2010. Say "introduced the idea" (Oxford), nothing stronger.
- **C3 ("for the first time"):** WHISKY BOOKS pdf 2177 credits the 1987 book with the first tasting notes for hundreds of whiskies. True to Oxford, but out by Wren's condition, and never "he invented tasting notes".
- **L1 (limb 4, the risk or the check):** no page I read records his doubt, a check, or any worry that styles would be misused. The same search summaries say later critics thought his styles hardened into rules ("not true to style"), which he had meant as description. Unread and unverified, so it is **not** on the card and never in guest text. Limb (4) stays a signposted "I like to think" (Wren's fallback). Library gap, if Robin wants (4) as fact: *The World Guide to Beer* (1977) itself, for its own introduction on what a "style" is.
