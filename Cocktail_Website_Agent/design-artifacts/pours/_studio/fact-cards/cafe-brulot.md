| F7 | Labour-intensive: most ingredients marinate for several hours first, then several intricate steps; DeGroff judges it worth the work. | Oxford pdf 381 | primary | The hours are this recipe's (C4). |
| F8 | Oxford's recipe: 3 cinnamon sticks, 1 whole lemon peel and 1 whole orange peel steep for several hours in 240 ml VS cognac and 120 ml kirsch. The spirits are warmed by a lower bowl of lit 151-proof rum until they catch; the flaming brandy is ladled over the studded peel several times. | Oxford pdf 381 | primary | |
| F9 | In that recipe the orange's oil cells spark and the cloves glow, which the recipe calls "a wonderful light show for your guests"; then the coffee is poured into the bowl to put the flame out, sweetened to taste with demerara syrup, served in demitasse cups. | Oxford pdf 381–382 | primary | The "for your guests" is DeGroff's recipe voice, not Alciatore's motive. |
| F10 | Commander's Palace's recipe (as adapted by Regan): lemon and orange each peeled in one strip; the lemon goes in the bowl with triple sec, brandy and cinnamon; the orange is studded with 4 cloves and held over on a fork; the flaming liquid is ladled over it; the coffee is poured in slowly to put out the flames; sweetened with the peeled orange's juice. ## Conflicts
| ID | Conflict | Rule for the reading |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | Lemon or orange spiral: Oxford's description (pdf 381) ladles the flame down a spiral-cut lemon peel; its own recipe pours it over a clove-studded orange spiral, the lemon only steeping. Commander's (*Joy* 262) and frenchquarter.com agree with the recipe. | "a long spiral of orange peel studded with cloves". Lemon only in the steep. Never "a lemon spiral". |
| C2 | Order: coffee last, putting out the flame, in Oxford's recipe (F9), Commander's (F10), Antoine's 1978 cookbook (F11) and Brennan's (F12). frenchquarter.com describes the coffee added to the brandy before the lighting (F13). | "In the classic recipe the coffee comes in at the end and puts the flame out." Never "always" or "everywhere". |
| C3 | Who and when: Oxford "ascribed" to Jules; frenchquarter.com "lays claim … sometime in the 1890s"; Tucker, *New Orleans Cuisine* (2009), OCR highlight, cut off: café brûlot is not on a menu used at Antoine's from 1910 [rest unread]. | "It's said" Jules Alciatore made it. No decade, no "invented". |
| C4 | The hours of steeping are Oxford's (DeGroff's) recipe only; Commander's has none; Antoine's practice unread. | "In the recipe I follow" / "one classic recipe", never "at Antoine's they steep it for hours". |

## Legends and out
- **L1, Prohibition cover:** frenchquarter.com says it was popular in Prohibition because coffee hid the liquor; Arnaud's "restaurant lore" likewise. Unsigned page plus lore: legend-grade, and a hidden truth is the Trickster's shape. Out.
- **L2, dimmed lights:** frenchquarter.com only ("in many cases"). Fire staging. Dossier only.
- **L3, devil cups:** a 1977 guidebook (FTS highlight) ties "Diabolique" to devil motifs on brûlot cups. Unread in full. Out.
