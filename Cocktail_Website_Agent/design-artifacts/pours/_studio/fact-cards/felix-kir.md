## Conflict inside Oxford (ruled 2026-09-28, round 3)
- C1: KIR (Monti, pdf 1133): 1904 "often claimed", a Dijon café, for mayor Barabant; recipe probably older; the name moved after Kir's election. VERMOUTH-CASSIS (Castellon, pdf 2097): invented 1904 at the Café Georges in **Beaune**; in the 1950s Kir, often in Paris, "made a habit of only drinking" it to promote it, and the press renamed it for him (cites Douarche 1957).
- Ruling: the year, the café, the city and the mechanism of the renaming are contested. Out as fact. Safe: the drink is older than the name (both entries); he didn't invent it (both); the name came after the war, while he was mayor (both: after 1945 / in the 1950s).
- The Paris-promotion account may be told only as one account, signposted ("one history says…"), and "to promote his region's growers" is a motive not on record from Kir himself: the bartender's reading, never fact.
- F12: aligoté and cassis are both local Burgundian products (VERMOUTH-CASSIS pdf 2097); Lagoute's first commercial cassis in the 1840s; phylloxera hit Burgundy in 1878 and growers turned to blackcurrants; French law: at least 400 g sugar per litre (CRÈME DE CASSIS pdf 597). Primary.

## Legends and unverified
- L1: "Kir invented the Kir." False per Oxford (F2).
- L2: "He helped 5,000 prisoners escape from the Longvic camp": widely repeated (Wikipedia, local press); not in Sycomore. Out unless an official/historian source is found.
- L3: The naming rights (Lejay-Lagoute 1951, later granted to others, court case): not verified. Out.
