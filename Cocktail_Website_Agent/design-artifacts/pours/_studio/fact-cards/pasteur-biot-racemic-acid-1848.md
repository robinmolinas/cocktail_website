## Legends and inferences
- **"Biot was his teacher."** Not on the page; Biot "constituted himself the sponsor". Never.
- **"He proved the great men wrong."** Mitscherlich missed the facets, an honest miss, and was later cordial (F17). Biot's acid really was neutral (F12): his statement wasn't wrong.
- **"My dear child … makes my heart beat"** is the common English of a line Pasteur himself retold. It's his memory, carried by his son-in-law and others: always attributed.
- **The year 1848.** The book doesn't date the test. It comes after his May 1848 paper and his return to Paris that summer, and before he left for Dijon (postponed to early November 1848). Biot "then seventy-four". 1848 is a strong inference from the sequence: "in 1848" is safe; no month.

## Conflicts
- C1 English translations differ ("My dear boy … touches my very heart" vs the literal "My dear child … makes my heart beat"). The French "enfant" settles it: our literal translation, labelled.
- C3 Names: the 1920 contents prints "J. J. Biot"; his name is Jean-Baptiste (Picard, *La vie et l'œuvre de Jean-Baptiste Biot*, 1927, title via archive.org). Mitscherlich's first name (Eilhard) is on no page I read: surname only.
- C2 Renan's 1882 version (highlight: "en serrant votre bras") shows no "mon cher enfant" in its cut-off highlight. Incomplete, not an absence.

## Guards (for the reading)
- G1 Health, hard (Wren): no medicine, germs, vaccines, milk, fermentation or "pasteurised". His mother's death in May 1848 stays out. Osler's introduction is a medical framing: not used. Guest text: "a young chemist in Paris".
- G2 Never claim which hand the tartaric acid in a jar of powder is: not on any page I read. Verjus's tartaric is the grapes' own (the right-hand form, F18) by inference from grapes; say "the grapes' own acid", never "only one form" (F19), never "the very one Biot read" (F18).
- G3 No "chirality", "stereochemistry", "founded a science", or later fame.
- G4 The doubt's reason (his newness) is the biographer's: attributed.
- G5 Biot never teacher; the story ends at the reading of the solution and the line (Wren), with F14–F15 at most one clause.
