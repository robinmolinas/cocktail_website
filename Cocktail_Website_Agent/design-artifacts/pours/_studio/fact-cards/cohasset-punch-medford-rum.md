# Fact card: the Cohasset Punch and Medford rum

Written by Hester, 2026-10-01, for regular-guy-lover (The One Next Door). Every quote below was on screen when written. Tiers: Oxford/Imbibe = library (primary for the studio); period guides and newspapers = primary text read via archive.org OCR; everything else secondary.

## Facts
| ID | Fact | Source | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | A mix of rum, sweet vermouth and lemon with a piece of preserved peach; a Chicago fixture from the 1890s until 1986. | Oxford, COHASSET PUNCH, pdf 549 (Wondrich) | library |
| F2 | Oxford's origin: in the 1890s Williams, who kept a saloon in Chicago's theatre district, was a guest of the comic actor William H. Crane at his summer house at Cohasset, on Massachusetts Bay. The party concluded New England made the best punches. Williams "was not satisfied with the verdict" and wired his partner Tom Newman for a new punch, shipped by the keg, express. | Oxford pdf 549, citing *Bonfort's Wine and Spirit Circular*, 10 Aug 1902, p. 305 (unread) and Fougner, *NY Sun*, 1938 | library |
| F3 | Newman's punch was "Medford Rum–based" and "rapidly won over the crowd"; then Chicago. | Oxford pdf 549 | library |
| F4 | Medford is a Massachusetts town "about five miles north of Boston"; rum made there from the early 18th century. | Oxford, RUM, MEDFORD, pdf 1699 (Curtis) | library |
| F5 | From the 1830s Daniel Lawrence advertised "Medford Rum"; the name "became associated with all New England rum"; trademarked 1871, "by then … in common usage". Lawrence's distillery, the last in town, ran until 1905, so it was still making Medford rum in the 1890s. | Oxford pdf 1699 | library |
| F6 | A period guide prints the Cohasset Punch with "1 jigger New England rum", vermouth, gum syrup, orange bitters, half a lemon's juice, stirred, served "with a preserved peach and its liquor". The same book titles its drinks "Medford or New England Rum Punch" and "…Rum Sour": the two names used as one. | Paul E. Lowe, *Drinks: How to Mix and How to Serve* (Stanley Paul, London; archive.org in.ernet.dli.2015.221478, djvu text; Toronto printing catalogued 1927 by McGill). DLI's "1889" date is unreliable: date it only as "an early-twentieth-century guide". | primary (OCR) |
| F7 | Medford rum was a standard American bar rum: called for from Harry Johnson's 1882 manual, and in Thomas (1887 ed.) and Kappeler (1895). | Oxford pdf 1699–1700 | library |
| F8 | Bottled from 1899 (national, "if modestly", by 1905); a recipe printed 1901; regulars agreed neither "did the drink true justice". At the bar: a brandy-soaked eighth of a peach, stirred "at length", and (one regular) a dash "from a brown bottle". | Oxford pdf 549 | library |
| F9 | In 1900 a "striking poster" for Cohasset punch was printed for Williams & Newman (design H. G. Maratta). | *Inland Printer*, Apr 1900, vol. 25 no. 1 (archive.org FTS highlight) | primary (OCR highlight) |
| F10 | 1916: the Ladner brothers took on the drink; after Repeal, a neon lighthouse sign, "Home of Cohasset Punch"; a fixture until 1986. | Oxford pdf 549–550 | library |
| F11 | In Aug 1934 a reader wrote to a *Tribune* column (letters addressed "Wake"; column name not checked) assuming the Cohasset "would be back" after Prohibition and asking where it was; the column's reply tells the Crane-house origin. In Dec 1934 another reader recalled 1890s bachelor dinners with "two bottles" of it. | *Chicago Daily Tribune*, 31 Aug 1934 and 6 Dec 1934 (archive.org djvu text) | primary (OCR) |

## Conflicts (keep the contested detail out)
| ID | Conflict | Sources |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | **Who made the first punch, and where.** Oxford: Newman made it in Chicago and shipped a keg. 1934 *Tribune* reply: Williams "once mixed the drink" for Crane's guests. Felten, *WSJ* 2008 (via Chicago Bar Project): "Gus Williams" created it at the party, with canned-peach syrup. | Oxford pdf 549; *Tribune* 31 Aug 1934; chibarproject.com Ladner Bros page |
| C2 | **Williams's first name:** Louis (Oxford), Gus (Felten 2008), Marshall (*Cohasset Mariner* 2005; *In Cod We Trust* 2015). Say "Williams". | as cited |
| C3 | **How the Ladners got it:** Oxford, Williams and Newman retired and sold in 1916; Kogan 1979 (via CBP), Newman's widow sold after Williams died; 1934 *Tribune*, the partners "became associated with" the Ladners. Say only "in 1916 it passed to the Ladner brothers". | Oxford pdf 549; CBP; *Tribune* 1934 |
| C4 | **Medford rum's reputation:** Oxford RUM pdf 1690 "villainous"; SANTA CRUZ RUM pdf 1732 "lower end of the American market"; pdf 2072 "poor reputation"; but pdf 1699 Lawrence's a "benchmark" brand and Imbibe pdf 47 "a byword for quality". Never call it cheap or fine. | as cited |

## Legends and inferences
- L1 Why Newman chose Medford rum: **not on record.** Never a joke, a wink or a choice "on purpose". F7 suggests it may simply have been the rum a Chicago bar poured.
- L2 Whether the Cohasset guests knew or recognised the rum: **not on record.** "Won over by what they already knew" is our reading only, signposted.
- L3 The brown-bottle dash: one regular's memory (Oxford's own framing).
