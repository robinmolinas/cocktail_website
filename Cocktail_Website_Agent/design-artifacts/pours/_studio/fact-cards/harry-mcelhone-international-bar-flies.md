# Fact card: Harry McElhone and the International Bar Flies

Our words throughout. Facts are free; the sources' sentences are not. Quotes below are dossier-length and stay out of any pour unless the room picks one.

**Sources read on the page, 2026-10-01:**
- Oxford Companion, MCELHONE, HENRY "HARRY" (pdf 1271–1272, Castellon and Wondrich) and HARRY'S NEW YORK BAR (pdf 972–973, Monti), both read in full. Primary (library).
- **The primary behind the story:** *Barflies and Cocktails* (Paris: Lecram Press, 1927), title page "by Harry and Wynn, with slight contributions from Arthur Moss". Scan and OCR text at archive.org, identifier `mc-elhone-harry-barflies-and-cocktails-1927` (`_djvu.txt` downloaded and read). ## Conflicts

- **C1. Who founded the IBF.** Oxford (pdf 1272) says he created it "with" McIntyre. The 1927 book says McIntyre's article set it off, Harry called the meeting, and the presidency was "foisted upon" McIntyre as the "unconscious inspirer". **For the reading:** never "co-founded with McIntyre". Say McIntyre's piece set it off and he was made its first president; Harry called the meeting and his bar was Trap 1.
- **C2. Who runs Harry's now.** pdf 1272: Franz-Arthur, the great-grandson. pdf 973: Duncan's widow and their son. The dates differ too: Andy from 1958 and Duncan from 1989 (pdf 973), against Andrew 1923–1996 and Duncan 1954–1998 (pdf 1272). **Say only "his family".**
- **C3. Spelling.** McElhone (Oxford; the 1927 book; *Proper*) against MacElhone (*Codex* p. 90; the family today). Use McElhone.
- **C4. Authorship of the book.** "By Harry and Wynn, with slight contributions from Arthur Moss". The birth chapter is signed by Moss, and the rules are unsigned. Say "his bar's book" or "his 1927 book". Never "Harry wrote" about the chapter or the rules.

## Legends and inferences

- **L1.** The Bloody Mary, Sidecar, White Lady and French 75 claims are "mostly bogus" and came after his death (pdf 973). The White Lady conflicts inside Oxford (see the family plan). Never.
- **L2.** Hemingway's twenty Whisky Sours: "said to" (pdf 972). Out.
- **L3.** The lions and panthers were stories his team spread (pdf 973). Tell them only as his stories.
- **L4.** The sugar-lump emblems with dead flies are Moss's joke, hedged on the page (F7).
- **L5.** "Harry invented the word barfly": no. In the book's story the word is McIntyre's, and it was an existing word. I haven't dated it, so claim nothing about its age.
- **Inference:** the joke of the name (flies, so their branches are traps) is our reading of an obvious pun. Nobody on the page explains it.

## Guards for any pour

- The 1927 book's other joke recipes include a blackface "Minstrel Cocktail" and Ku Klux Klan puns. Never quote the book beyond the lines carded here.
- Drinking-harm jokes (falling down, hangovers) stay out. The Wacky One's trap is nothing about drinking.
- The Monkey Gland (a medical graft) and the Scofflaw (Prohibition) stay out (family plan).
