# Fact card: Gary Regan and mindful bartending

Hester, 2026-10-01, for explorer-magician (The Spiritualist). Every line below was read on the page in the same session (library.py page). Tiers: Oxford Companion primary; *A Proper Drink* (Proper) reliable secondary, quotes on the record; *The Joy of Mixology* (Joy, revised edition 2018 per Oxford pdf 1635) is Regan's own account of himself, secondary. Joy cited by pdf page; Proper by printed page.

## Facts
| ID | Fact | Source |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | Born to pub owners in Lancashire; tended bar from fourteen at his parents' pub, the Prince Rupert. | Oxford REGAN, GARY pdf 1634 |
| F2 | Moved to New York "in the early 1970s"; job at Drake's Drum, an Upper East Side Irish bar. Joy gives 1973 in his own words ("In 1973, when I first started tending bar in New York"). No conflict; Joy's year is his. | Oxford pdf 1634; Joy pdf 119, 133 |
| F3 | He had left the pubs at home "in order to start a new life" in New York. | Joy pdf 119 |
| F4 | At Drake's Drum he was "nervous about interacting with people and having to be in control of the bar"; "after weeks of dreading work" he began to arrive "at least an hour before my shift began". | Joy pdf 119 |
| F5 | What he did in that hour: sat at a table, ordered dinner, "refused to let anyone join me", composed himself, then kept sitting alone, absorbing the bar's mood and "telling myself that I was going to have a great night". | Joy pdf 119 |
| F6 | Looking back: "I was, in effect, meditating and setting my intentions, though I wasn't really conscious of that at the time. I just knew it worked." | Joy pdf 119 |
| F7 | The result he reports: customers no longer intimidated him; he "gained their respect"; soon he loved being the centre of attention; customers still teased him "(and still do, which is how I like to be treated)". | Joy pdf 119 |
| F8 | The idea of applying mindfulness to bartending came to him "when I was attending mindfulness workshops"; he gave presentations to bartenders in Moscow, London, Paris and Beirut; "I don't purport to be a qualified teacher of mindfulness"; he is passing on what he's "been learning in recent years". | Joy pdf 115 |
| F9 | His advice to the reader (not his 1973 habit): five to ten minutes alone to set intentions for the night; eyes closed, attention on breath; think "I'm breathing in. I'm breathing out." | Joy pdf 119-120 |
| F10 | His own example of a wandering thought, written to the reader in second person ("you start fretting"): it's Thursday night and a named, insulted regular will be in later. Put it in a cloud, let it float off. It is an illustration, not a reported memory. | Joy pdf 120 |
| F11 | His own alternative, in the first person: alone in a bar setting up for the first shift of the day, "I've often found that playing the right playlist at the right volume can help center me. For me, loud music can be a form of meditation." Also: stillness "isn't the only way". | Joy pdf 120 (boxed note) |
| F12 | "don't ever take things too seriously. We're here to have fun and you can't be happy and so serious at the same time!"; "you will never achieve 100 percent enlightenment, but that's not the point"; "A little mindfulness is better than none at all". | Joy pdf 116 |
| F13 | Mindfulness is "associated with Buddhism, though atheists, agnostics, Christians, Hindus, Jews, and Muslims practice" it; "plenty of bartenders have been masters of it for years without even knowing it". | Joy pdf 115 |
| F14 | "Throughout his career, Regan underscored that a successful bar relies primarily on one variable: the happiness of its patrons." | Oxford pdf 1635 |
| F15 | Wrote *The Negroni* (Ten Speed, 2015). | Oxford pdf 1635 |
| F16 | Simonson: "a bon vivant eccentric… blissfully unconcerned with his reputation"; stirs Negronis with his finger on occasional shifts. | Proper p. 62 |
| F17 | "Throughout the 1970s and '80s, Regan bounced around New York from bar to bar". | Proper p. 63 |

## Cautions
| ID | Caution |
|---|---|
| C1 | **Kept for years is not on the page.** Nothing says he went on arriving an hour early after Drake's Drum (F17: he moved bar to bar). What lasted, on record, is the result (F7, "still do") and that decades later he taught a version of it (F8). Never "he did it every night for forty years". |
| C2 | **The naming is retrospective, not an event.** He doesn't say the workshops named his 1973 hour. On the page: he looks back and calls it "in effect, meditating" (F6), and the workshops gave him the idea for bartenders (F8). Write "years later he looked back and called it…", never "the workshops told him what he'd been doing". |
| C3 | **Advice ≠ his own practice.** The breath line and the five-to-ten minutes (F9) are instructions to the reader; his 1973 hour was dinner, sitting, the room's mood, and a sentence to himself. Any breath-counted method is labelled his advice, not his habit. |
| C4 | **The Thursday regular is an example (F10),** not a memory. Never "his mind drifted to the regular he dreaded". |
| C5 | Joy pdf 114's "I have always believed success behind the bar…" is an epigraph from someone else's memoir context (Morrill Cody's *Jimmie the Barman*, credited on pdf 115), not Regan's line. |
| C6 | "What is intuition? It is tuition from within" (pdf 118) is printed unattributed: never his coinage, and the "within" wordplay is out anyway. |
| C7 | Sibling motif: *Anyway* (jester-caregiver) has the tagline "You decide it's going to be a good night. Then you make it one." F5's last clause is that tagline in his words. One clause at most, never the recognition. |
| C8 | Owned overlap: the Wise Kid row (innocent-sage, unauthored) holds Joy pdf 133 (the month of watching, the Scotch Sours, the waitress). This pour keeps all of pdf 133 out. |
| C9 | Oxford's REGAN entry does not mention mindfulness; the mindful story rests on Joy alone (his own account). Death year (2019, pdf 1633) stays out (Innocent plan, family rule). |

## Round 3 additions (2026-10-01): the Negroni method
| ID | Fact | Source |
|---|---|---|
| F18 | Regan's Negroni in *Joy* (revised ed.): 1½ oz each Campari, sweet vermouth, gin (listed in that order), orange twist; "BUILD in any order in an ice-filled rocks glass. Add the garnish." "in any order" occurs once in the whole book, so it is not house boilerplate. | Joy pdf 351; library grep of joy-of-mixology.md |
| F19 | The same line is in the 2003 first edition: "BUILD in any order in an ice-filled rocks glass. Add the garnish." (Negroni, then filed under "Milanese drinks", pp. 308–309 per its index). So the instruction stood across both editions, 2003 and 2018. | archive.org full-text search, `joyofmixology0000rega` (lending-only; OCR highlight, secondary) |
| F20 | *Joy* pdf 351 points readers to his 2015 book *The Negroni: Drinking to La Dolce Vita* for the drink's history. | Joy pdf 351 |

| ID | Caution |
|---|---|
| C10 | **Not verified in *The Negroni* (2015).** The book is not in the library, not on archive.org (advanced search and full text: nothing), and no trusted page quotes its method. Library gap raised for Robin. A search summary relays a line about ratios from a "gaz regan Notion" book; not read, not usable. |

So "any order" is his Negroni in *Joy*, never "how he always made it". He also stirred Negronis with his finger on shifts (Proper p. 62). |
