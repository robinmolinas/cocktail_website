# Fact card: Angelo Gaja, Barbaresco, 1996 and 2013

Hester, 2026-10-01. The library has nothing on Gaja, Barbaresco or Nebbiolo (`library.py search`: no hits). Every row is web, so every row is **secondary**. Pages fetched with curl + strip in this session; quotes copied from the fetched text.

Sources:
- **WN 2006**: Kerin O'Keefe, "Barbaresco Breaks Rank", *The Wine News*, Oct/Nov 2006 (Wayback 2010). Trade press, named author (a Piedmont specialist). Carries Gaja's own words, quoted to her.
- **DEC 2019a**: Chris Mercer, "Gaja goes back to Barbaresco with single-vineyard wines", Decanter.com (last updated 6 Nov 2019). Trade press. Gaia Gaja quoted.
- **DEC 2019b**: Robin Lee, "Gaja single vineyard Barbaresco: All three 2013s tasted", Decanter.com (21 Feb 2019). Trade press, tasting piece.
- Wikipedia "Gaja (wine)" raw text: leads only.

| G7 | **What grew back (the wines):** from the 2013 vintage the three single vineyards (Costa Russi, Sorì Tildin, Sorì San Lorenzo) came back into the Barbaresco appellation, now 100% Nebbiolo. ## Legend-grade (not fetched)
- **Darmagi**: in 1978 a prime Nebbiolo site in Barbaresco was planted with Cabernet; the name, "what a pity", "is said to be" his father Giovanni's comment. Wikipedia cites the NYT (Prial, 30 Aug 1989), the *Oxford Companion to Wine* and the *Independent* (1993); none fetched (the NYT returned nothing). Wikipedia also has Gaja saying he planted it not from love of Cabernet but to persuade the world Italy could make great wine (Asimov, NYT blog 2007; fetched, the line not found). As a lead story it fails Wren's points 1 and 4 (he planted a grape he didn't claim to love, and nothing on a page shows the Nebbiolo going stale). Usable at most as a labelled "the story goes" clause.
