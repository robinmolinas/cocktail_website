Hester, 2026-10-01, for ruler-caregiver (The Caring Leader), candidate. Draft card: every line below is from text on screen this session.

Tiers: *This Must Be the Place: Memoirs of Jimmie the Barman (James Charters) as told to Morrill Cody* (Lee Furman, New York, 1937), archive.org `thismustbeplacem0000jame`, lending-only: **OCR highlights only** (full-text search), first-person memoir **through a ghost-writer** (carry the channel: "as he told it to Morrill Cody"). *Joy* pdf 114–115 prints his credo as an epigraph (library, secondary relay).

| F1 | Credo, printed by Regan as the epigraph to "Mindful Bartending": "I have always believed success behind the bar comes from an ability to understand the man or woman I am serving, to enter into his joys or woes, make him feel the need of me as a person rather than a servant." | F2 | He came to the Dingo "in 1924 as assistant barman", having worked on the Right Bank before. | memoir OCR highlight |
| F3 | He had boxed professionally ("short but strong, from my years in professional boxing"; first public fight at the Liverpool stadium). "I do not think I am like the usual barman; I even pride myself on my difference." | memoir OCR highlights |
| F4 | His job, as he lists it: called upon "not only to mix many drinks but also to settle fights, listen to intimate confidences, prevent jealousies, arrange parties, and crack jokes. I never worked so hard in all my life". | memoir OCR highlight |
| F5 | His rule: "Fights in a bar are to be avoided at all costs, of course. I can usually 'smell' a fight coming on, and then I do everything to prevent it. I try to divert the combatants by telling them jokes, or by giving them drinks on the house. Usually the free drink works. In the pleasure of receiving it they forget their quarrel and drink to each other, or both drink to me, or in some way forget to be angry." (Joined from three overlapping highlights; the joins overlap word for word.) | memoir OCR highlights |
| F6 | "There is nothing like a 'drink on the house' to make the client feel good." | memoir OCR highlight |

## Round 3 additions (2026-10-01): what he poured
| ID | Fact | Source |
|---|---|---|
| F11 | His own drink: "I must tell you of a cocktail I invented while I was at the Dingo that had a powerful effect on some of the Quarterites. It was made with [OCR '£8', most likely 2/6] cognac, 1/6 Pernod, 1/6 Amer-Picon, 1/6 Mandarin, and 1/6 sweet cherry brandy. This mixture was shaken thoroughly in a cocktail shaker and then either drunk straight or diluted with a little soda. Either way the taste is pleasant…" (cut off). No name for it in the highlights. | memoir OCR highlights, chapter "About Drinks", p. 83 |
| F12 | When a client bought him a drink, he kept stand-ins under the bar: "[…] bitters and plain water with ice, which looks just like whisky or brandy; another, black currant cordial in plain water, which looks like port; another, plain water with just a dash of bitters, which looks like 'gin and it.'" Asked if he'd "had mine", he would "raise the appropriate glass from under the bar, for I believe in always drinking what the client drinks!" | memoir OCR highlights |
| F13 | A scene (not Jimmie's own act): a man, T, came at Jimmie's friend Bob "with teeth clenched and fists closed. I thought there was going to be murder"; Bob "simply smiled"; T's fists unclenched; "'Have a drink,' said T. 'Make it a whisky and a Pernod, Jimmie.' They drank and they talked". The calm is Bob's (Bob McAlmon, per a nearby highlight), the order T's, the pour Jimmie's. | memoir OCR highlights |

| C7 | **F11's cognac fraction is OCR-garbled.** 2/6 is our inference (the sixths then sum to one), unconfirmed: the book is lending-only and can't be rendered. Say "cognac, with a sixth each of…" or "about a third cognac" only as inference. "Powerful effect" = strong; never a boast in guest text. Its seasonings touch siblings: Amer Picon is *Till Spring*'s card (Gaëtan Picon); Pernod is anise (*Rosetta*'s absinthe is a different bottle). Sweet cherry brandy: Tomás classifies (maraschino is `nuts` in the plan). It is shaken, not stirred. |
| C8 | **F12's water glasses are a kind deception** (Trickster ground, *No Accident*): if used, it's "kept a clear head while matching them glass for glass", never "fooled them". |
| C9 | **F13's calm belongs to Bob, not Jimmie.** Never give Jimmie that scene. |
