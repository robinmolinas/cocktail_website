## Facts

| F1 | At the Murphys' evenings the cocktails were what Gerald called "just the juice of a few flowers": a name for his cocktails in general. | G, para. on the terrace dinners |
| F2 | In a letter to Alexander Woollcott (15 Feb 1939, Beinecke) he listed a cocktail he called **a Bailey**, "invented by me as were a great many other good things". Ingredients: gin (Booth's House of Lords), grapefruit juice, fresh lime juice, fresh mint, ice ("a great deal"). **No measures and no sweetener given on G's page.** | G, text + note 4 |
| F3 | **The "Juice of A Few Flowers" card, as transcribed by Hester from G-img:** 1 oz. orange · 1 oz. grapefruit · ½ oz lime · ½ oz lemon · 1 oz. gin · "shake & pour into: moisten rim of glass w/ lemon and dip in coarse sugar". No mint, no named gin, no glass named. ("rim" is my reading of a word that could be misread as "rum"; the sense requires rim.) Written on notepaper printed "The Westbury, A Knott Hotel, Madison Ave. at 69th St., N.Y." (the Manhattan is on that sheet; the flowers card is a separate sheet in the photo). **Undated.** | G-img |

## Conflicts
- **C1 The father's bar tray.** G: he later said *Cocktail* was *not* a nostalgic recollection of his father's bar tray, though it may have been the starting point. W: the objects "were based on Murphy's memory of his father's bar accessories". Contested: keep the father out.
- **C2 Which drink is "the juice of a few flowers".** The plan (my error) gave the Bailey's ingredients to the flowers drink. G's text uses the phrase for his cocktails in general; G-img shows a separate card titled with it, with its own recipe (F3). Never give the flowers drink mint; never give the Bailey orange, lemon or a sugar rim.
