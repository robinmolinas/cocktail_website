# LAB (London Academy of Bartenders): the school that became a party bar

Hester, 2026-10-01, for ruler-jester (the Charismatic Leader). Primary: Robert Simonson, *A Proper Drink* pp. 97–101 (chapter X, "Rock Stars"; printed pages, re-read whole on screen this session), reliable secondary with participants on the record; Oxford Companion, ANKRAH, DOUGLAS (pdf 119, Alice Lascelles). Web, secondary: *The Spirits Business*, 17 Aug 2021 (curl + strip, read on screen). Quotations below are dossier-only; reading text must paraphrase.

## Facts
- F1. LAB "started as a school and became a bar" (p. 97, Simonson). Doug Ankrah and Alex Turner formed the idea of a school for the new kind of London bartender; they had worked with Richard Hargroves at London's first Planet Hollywood in the mid-1990s (p. 97).
- F2. The school opened in 1996, with Hargroves as a partner (p. 99). 1996 dates the **school**, not the bar. Three-week course, £600, ten students at a time, 10 a.m. to 4 p.m.; it taught service, product knowledge and sales more than cocktail-making; the last days were shifts at bars run by people the founders knew (p. 99).
- F3. Underfunded. Turner: "We were always one student away from not being able to run the following course" (p. 99). The founders "all ended up having to work bar jobs": bars at night, training in the morning (Turner, p. 99).
- F4. "Eventually, the school went bust" (p. 99). The name survived it (p. 99).
- F5. Two accounts of why the bar exists, both p. 99. Turner: the school "didn't work out, so we opened a bar instead". Simonson's narration: the bar "was intended as a living advertisement for the school". The page does **not** say whether the bar opened before or after the school folded. Order-neutral wording only ("the school didn't survive; the bar did").
- F6. The owners' choice: "They wanted a party bar, high volume and high energy" (p. 99, Simonson's narration of the bartender-owners), set against the later New York "temples to mixology" (Death & Co, PDT).
- F7. Ankrah: "Other bars are very, very passively boring … LAB was a bar for people who rock every night. And everyone came there." (p. 100). Undated interview; Simonson's book is 2016. Past tense only ("Ankrah said").
- F8. Turner helped with the training and the drinks list "but left before the bar opened" (p. 100). That left Ankrah and Hargroves; Ankrah brought in Jamie Terrell (three years at Dick's Bar at the Atlantic); Dre Masso joined a few months in (p. 100).
- F9. Terrell: "At the time, we wanted to make the best drinks in the world." He and Ankrah ("Doug and I") wrote five cocktail menus together, each of two hundred drinks (p. 100). **Never "a thousand drinks"**: the page doesn't say the menus didn't overlap.
- F10. The drinks were "flamboyant"; vodka and fruit at the base (Simonson, p. 100); Reed: the backbar "like a greengrocer" (p. 100); "mountains of ice" (Simonson, p. 100). Turner: everything came in "a massive fourteen-ounce glass of crushed ice" (p. 100; 14 US fl oz ≈ 414 ml), and the drinks were "lurid blue or green". Turner's word and Simonson's ("lurid", p. 97) stay out of guest text.
- F11. Simon Ford's memory (pp. 100–101): the bartenders "brought a chef approach", "putting straws in and sipping, then adding a bit more mint, a little more sugar, a dash of bitters, adjusting the drink"; "I'd never seen anything like it." The page gives no role for Ford here (pp. 108–112 make him Plymouth gin's ambassador later). "They" = the bartenders, never Ankrah alone, never "every drink".
- F12. Turner's joke: "order two drinks … a drink and a beer, so you've got something to drink while they're making your cocktail" (p. 101). Turner had left before the bar opened (F8); the page doesn't say he was a customer. Attribute to him; never "a regular".
- F13. "At first, nobody paid much attention" (p. 101). "The first few months of 1999 were slow." Then a good *Time Out London* review, and a famous regular's line was quoted; "That did it" covers **both** (p. 101). Guest text: "good press turned it"; never "one review saved it". The regular (the mayor) stays out.
- F14. Legacy (Simonson's verdict, p. 101): few LAB cocktails outlived its heyday; it established bartenders as celebrities, "not wage slaves"; and it helped knit together the growing London bartending community. Conigliaro: "Everybody in the industry used to be at LAB. If you wanted to get hired, or hire, or meet a rep, that's where you went." Gonzalez: "a bartenders' bar" (p. 101).
- F15. Oxford: LAB and Townhouse were famous in the late 1990s as breeding grounds for new bartending talent, thanks to long cocktail lists and "high standards of service" (pdf 119).
- F16. Douglas Ankrah died in August 2021; his family announced it on 16 August (*The Spirits Business*, 17 Aug 2021, secondary). Oxford's "(1970–)" predates it. No present tense about him; his death stays out of guest text unless Wren needs it.

## Conflicts and guards
- C1. **Jiggers contested** (p. 100): Terrell "insisted he always used jiggers"; patrons remember drinks built on the fly; Ford: "very much free pour". Never measure/free-pour/"by eye"/"on the fly" in guest text. Ford's straw-tasting (F11) can be used cut before "free pour": tasting and adjusting is not contested by the jiggers.
- C2. **Bar opening year**: not in *Proper*. *The Spirits Business* (2021) says Ankrah co-founded "the Soho-based London Academy of Bartending (Lab)" in 1999, which merges the 1996 school with the bar. No year for the bar in guest text.
- C3. **Founder count**: Oxford calls Ankrah "founder"; *Proper* gives three for the school (Ankrah, Turner, Hargroves) and Ankrah and Hargroves for the bar. Say "one of its founders" or name the partners.
- C4. **Out**: the Porn Star Martini; the building's former use; the district; the drinks' names; the mayor; "rock stars" as a label for the guest; Bradsell and the Atlantic as story (Explorer's). Not "London, late 1990s" or "In 1990s London" as an opening (*On Their Behalf*). "Not many know LAB began a school" stays out of the recognition (*For Good*).
- C5. Where the school taught is **not on these pages**: never "a school in Soho" (the bar was on Old Compton Street, p. 100; the location stays out anyway).
