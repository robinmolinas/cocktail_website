# Fact card: Spurrier, Gallagher and the Paris tasting, 1976

Hester, 2026-10-01, for outlaw-magician. The library has **nothing** on Spurrier or the tasting (`library.py search "Spurrier"` and `"Judgment of Paris"`: no hits). Every row below is web, so every row is **secondary**. Pages fetched with curl + strip in this session; quotes copied from the fetched text. Wikipedia used for leads only (its raw wikitext, to find its sources).

Sources fetched:
- **TIME 1976**: George M. Taber, "Modern Living: Judgment of Paris", 7 June 1976 (read by Wren r1; the only reporter present). Secondary, contemporary, eyewitness. Strongest page we have.
- **BW 2001**: Thane Peterson, "The Day California Wines Came of Age", *BusinessWeek*, 8 May 2001 (Wayback 2007 copy). Secondary, established press, 25 years on. This is Wikipedia's source for the ban.
- **SFC 2018**: Esther Mobley, "The hidden figures behind the Judgment of Paris", *San Francisco Chronicle*, 24 Oct 2018 (Wayback 2019). Secondary; carries Gallagher's own account, quoted.
- **WS 2026**: Mitch Frank, "An Eye-Opening Blind Tasting" / "The Judgment of Paris turns fifty", *Wine Spectator*, 1 May 2026. Secondary, trade press, named author.
- **CNN 2021**: Jacopo Prisco, 10 May 2021 (fetched in the plan, step 4). Secondary.
- **JR 2006**: Jancis Robinson, "Steven Spurrier – champion of French wines", 27 May 2006 (Wayback 2015). Secondary, named critic.
- **NG 2026**: Richard Ballantyne MW, "The Judgment of Paris: Fifty Years of the Wrong Story", Noble Grape (his own wine merchant's site), 7 Mar 2026. Secondary, commercial site, a re-analysis. Counterweight only.

## Facts

|---|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | The idea for the tasting was Patricia Gallagher's (Gastaud-Gallagher), Spurrier's American colleague at his wine school; "No one, least of all Spurrier, disputes this." | F3 | Gallagher and Spurrier both tasted and scored, but their scores were **not counted**; the official result was the nine French tasters'. | NG 2026 ("their scores were excluded from the count"); Wikipedia citing Taber 2005 pp. 218, 238, 276 (not read) | secondary | So "she was a judge" overstates: she tasted, uncounted.
