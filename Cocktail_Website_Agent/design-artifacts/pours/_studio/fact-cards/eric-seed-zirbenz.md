## Conflicts
- **C1 (start year):** Seed "jumped into the game, in 2004" (p. 240); Oxford founds Haus Alpenz in 2005; *PUNCH* brings Zirbenz to the US in 2005. Not a contradiction, but no year in guest text unless it is "by 2005".
- **C2 (maker):** a WebSearch summary named Purkhart (another Alpenz producer). The importer's catalogue and *PUNCH*, read in full, say Josef Hofer. The card holds Hofer.
- **C3 (where):** *Proper* "studying in Austria", Oxford "Vienna as a student". Compatible; Vienna is Oxford's.

## Guards
- **G1** Never say Seed was afraid (Wren). The only feeling of his on the record, "made me want to cry", is from LeNell's telling-off (p. 238): the Rogue row's, out entirely.
- **G2** He didn't go to Austria *for Zirbenz*. He went to find an example of the kind he'd met as a student, and Zirbenz is what he brought back (F3).
- **G3** "Struggled" always carries "but there were places where it did well" (F6). Never "failed", "nobody wanted it" or "no takers": the distributors weren't interested; Kenyon was.
- **G4** "Could afford only a pallet" is a limit, not a stake: never "everything he had", "his savings".
- **G5** "What they were missing" is our gloss. The page says "what they would be interested in carrying" (the store) and "what he should be importing or creating" (the bartenders). In guest text: "asked what they wanted" or the page's words.
- **G6** He mostly imported things that existed abroad (F9): never "revived", "brought back from the dead" or "lost recipe" for Seed. "Long absent from the American market" (Oxford) is fine.
- **G7** Credit the makers plainly: the Hofer family distillery, since 1797. Seed found it, bought it and sold it; he didn't make it.
- **G8** Out: crème de violette (*As It Was*), Old Tom and Wondrich/Ransom (*Instead*), navy-strength gin (sea ground), Jerry Thomas's Gin and Pine (in *PUNCH*; Thomas is *Beside the First*'s and *The Entertainer*'s), LeNell (Rogue). Velvet Falernum may sit in a list, but Batavia arrack and allspice dram are cleaner (Explorer's Genius reserves home-made falernum).
- **G9** Stay off the chapter title's words ("Bringing It All Home"; Wren, for *Brought Home*). Haus Alpenz in one clause at most (*As It Was* names it).
- **G10** Living person: any present tense is dated ("last I read, in 2016"; the import "as of 2026").
- **G11** "Mild-mannered" is Simonson's; "bookworm" is Kenyon's, said with admiration ("He has amazing things").
- **G13** Never "the way he sold it" or "the Alpine way" for a hot drink: the toddy is the importer's suggestion and our build (F20). "Sold as an après-ski tradition" is the page's passive: keep it.
- **G12** **Veto:** the maker's own description is a maceration of the young whole cones (F15), so the seeds (pine nuts) are in the steep. On the safe side that's `nuts` (STUDIO-RULES 4 names pine nuts). This is our inference from the method; I found no allergen statement from the maker.
