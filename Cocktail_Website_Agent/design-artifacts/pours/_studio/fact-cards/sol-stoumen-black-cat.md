## Facts
| id | fact | source |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | The Board of Equalization found he "kept and permitted his licensed premises to be used as a disorderly house" because persons "of known homosexual tendencies" met there; recommended "indefinite suspension". A second count: beer sold to a minor. | S1 |
| F2 | The court: no evidence of "any illegal or immoral conduct on the premises". Mere patronage isn't enough to suspend the licence (count one). | S1 |
| F3 | The court: members of the public "have a right to patronize a public restaurant and bar" while acting properly; the proprietor has no right to exclude them "except for good cause", and is liable in damages if he does (Civ. Code §§ 51, 52). | S1 |
| F4 | The trouble began in 1949 when Stoumen refused to sign a closed-shop agreement with the culinary workers' union; Reilly (Board chairman) warned him, then ordered a case built against the bar (Kamiya, relaying Agee). | S2 |
