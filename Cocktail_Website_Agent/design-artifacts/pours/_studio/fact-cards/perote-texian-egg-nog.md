# Fact card: the Perote egg-nog (21 April 1843) and "Tecolote"

Verified 2026-10-04 by Hester for jester-hero (The Wise Talker). Primary: Thomas Jefferson Green, *Journal of the Texian Expedition Against Mier* (New York: Harper, 1845), archive.org `journaloftexiane00gree`, djvu text read and grepped (copy: `work/jester-hero/historian-src-green-1845.txt`). Printed page numbers from the running heads. Library: *Imbibe!* pdf 127, 129–130 (Wondrich). Secondary: TSHA *Handbook of Texas*, "Green, Thomas Jefferson" (Robert Bruce Blake), curl 2026-10-04.

Quotes stay under 15 words and stay in the dossier. Paraphrase in the reading.

## Facts
| ID | Fact | Source |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | Green (1802–1863) was second in command on the Mier expedition, surrendered to Ampudia, was held at Perote and escaped. | TSHA |
| F2 | By early April 1843 the prisoners had been "five months from our homes". ('five months' is Green's 'we', the Mier party; the Bexar men had been held since September 1842. Hester 2026-10-06.) | Green p. 256 |
| F3 | They were chained; Green's irons were taken off on the hospital surgeon's order because his health had declined (cold quarters, poor food). | Green p. 258 |
| F4 | San Jacinto anniversary (21 April): Green's money was down "to the last extremity"; he spent his "last doubloon" on the celebration. | Green pp. 258–259 |
| F5 | Bought: seven gallons of "vino mascal", as many of ass's milk, thirty dozen eggs, a large loaf of sugar; all their cooking utensils and water jars used. **Bought, not smuggled.** | Green p. 259 |
| F6 | Colonel Fisher, Captain Reese and Lieutenant Clarke beat the eggs; the old sailing-master Lyon pounded the sugar while telling a yarn; Dan sang; Green stirred in the ingredients. | Green p. 259 |
| F7 | The men stood round the bowl in rags with chains on their ankles, which they called their "jewelry". | Green p. 259 (and p. 262) |
| F8 | "the juice of the agave inspired the soul … the ass's milk filled the stomach" (Green's own line, as the men's verdict on the drink). | Green p. 260; *Imbibe!* pdf 129 |
| F9 | The noise alarmed the guards; the men told the captain of the guard it was how their country celebrated its saints' days, and asked him not to disturb their worship; he said "Bueno" and turned to go. | Green p. 260 |
| F10 | Then they "gave the wink" to Trimble, who could mimic an owl (look, head-twist, whoop). He rolled his eyes, twisted his head round and whooped. The captain hesitated "whether to laugh or swear", said "Tecolote" (screech-owl) and moved on, to a "universal roar of laughter". | Green pp. 260–261 |
| F11 | Trimble "carried the soubriquet to his grave"; Green still calls him "Tecolote" on the escape night. | Green pp. 261, 315 |
| F12 | Escape night: Green and Ogden lay on the floor at the count; the plan was for one man to say they were very sick. "Tecolote" sang out "Here is two muy malo", "with an admirable mixture of confidence and sympathy"; the officer said "Bueno" and left. Green: "we … continued to crack our jokes even in this excitement." | Green pp. 314–315 |
| F13 | Same night they made the door sentinel good-natured with vino mascal passed through the grates "in an eggshell", nothing larger fitting. | Green p. 316 |
| F14 | Other days: a soldier smuggled brandy in three feet of beef gut curled in his cap and sold it "per suck"; a sergeant's wife smuggled it in a bladder. | Green p. 277 |
| F15 | Later life: planter (Florida; Esmeralda Plantation, NC); died 12 Dec 1863, by his son's account of heartbreak over the Confederacy's reverses. | TSHA |

## Counterweights (carry into the room)
| ID | Fact | Source |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | Seventeen Mier prisoners were shot at Salado on 25 March 1843 after drawing black beans (17 black, 159 white), four weeks before the egg-nog. | Green pp. 169–170, 283 |
| C2 | Captain Ewen Cameron was later shot on Santa Anna's order; the prisoners wept at the news. Green dates it "the 25th" (p. 376); the month is not on a page I read (standard histories: April 1843, unverified). | Green pp. 283–285, 376 |
| C3 | Trimble later died "for the want of bread", leaving a destitute family. | Green p. 261 |
| C4 | The toasts include "no more white flags" and an ethnic insult to Santa Anna's nation. Never in guest text. | Green p. 260 |
| C5 | The room at the bowl was two groups, and all hands were summoned (p. 259). (a) About fifty Bexar prisoners, taken when Woll's Mexican army seized San Antonio on 11 Sept 1842; some had fired on it before surrendering as prisoners of war (pp. 29–30, 235; App. I p. 447: sixty-seven taken; TSHA "Perote Prison"). (b) Green and fifteen Mier men (p. 243), from a Texan raiding force that crossed the Rio Grande, occupied Mier, levied supplies, took the alcalde and attacked the town, 23–26 Dec 1842 (pp. 74–82; TSHA "Mier Expedition": "the last of the raiding expeditions from Texas"). **Trimble was a Bexar prisoner** (App. I p. 448). Never "a raid into Mexico" for the room or for him. *(Corrected by Hester 2026-10-06; was: "The Mier men were a Texian raiding force taken after attacking the Mexican town of Mier (Dec 1842).")* | Green pp. 29–30, 74–82, 235, 243, 259, 447–448; TSHA (Nance; Connor) |

## Legends / corrections
- L1 *Imbibe!* pdf 129 compresses two things: Green's egg-nog ingredients were **purchased** (p. 259); the gut-in-the-cap smuggling was a Mexican soldier's brandy trade on banned days (p. 277), and in his cap, not "under their shakos". The bribe is not in Green's egg-nog pages. Never tell the gut tubes as part of the egg-nog.
- L2 "some 160" fellow prisoners (*Imbibe!* pdf 129): not on Green's egg-nog pages. Don't use a count.
- L3 Trimble's first name, unit and fate beyond C3 are not on any page read. Don't add them.
- L4 Hester's memory, unverified: Green's role in California's 1850 foreign miners' tax. Not on the TSHA page. Not to be told either way.
- Name guard: Thomas Jefferson Green, never "Tom Green" (a different Texan, a Confederate general).
