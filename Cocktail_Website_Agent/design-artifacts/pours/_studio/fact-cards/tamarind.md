# Fact card: tamarind

Our words throughout. Main source: El-Siddig, Gunasena, Prasad, Pushpakumara, Ramana, Vijayanand, Williams (2006), *Tamarind, Tamarindus indica* (Fruits for the Future 1, rev. ed.), Southampton Centre for Underutilised Crops. Fetched and read 2026-09-27 (PDF, blog.soton.ac.uk/fruits/files/2012/01/tamarind-monograph.pdf). Tier: **secondary** (a named-author scientific monograph that cites its evidence). Page refs are the monograph's printed pp. ~16–17, 40.

## Facts

| # | Claim (our words) | Source | Tier | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| F1 | The dried tamarind pulp sold commercially generally holds 8–18% tartaric acid and 25–45% reducing sugars (about 70% glucose, 30% fructose). | El-Siddig et al. 2006, text + Table 2.4 (per 100 g dry weight) | secondary | Table is on a **dry-weight** basis; a wet block with seeds and fibre will be lower. |
| F2 | About half of that tartaric acid is bound as potassium bitartrate (cream of tartar), less as calcium tartrate; young fruit has more free acid (up to 16%), readily drawn out with hot water. | El-Siddig et al. 2006, citing Lewis & Neelakantan 1964 | secondary | So the *sour-tasting* free acid is well below the total. |
| F3 | Acid and sugar vary widely by type: sour tamarind in Pakistan 8.4–12.4% acid; Thai samples 2.5–11.3%; Thai *sweet* tamarind as low as 2.0–3.2% with 39–48% sugar. | El-Siddig et al. 2006 (Hasan & Ijaz 1972; Feungchan et al.; Table 2.5) | secondary | **Buy sour tamarind, not sweet Thai.** |
| F4 | Tamarind's acid is mostly tartaric, unusual in plants in that quantity; as it ripens the acid stays while sugar rises, so it is known (their word) as at once the most acidic and the sweetest fruit. | El-Siddig et al. 2006, citing Lewis & Neelakantan 1964; Coronel 1991 | secondary | A reported reputation, not a measured ranking: "is known as", never "is the most". |
| F5 | A tamarind drink syrup at 20–24% pulp comes out at about 1.11% total acidity (as tartaric). | El-Siddig et al. 2006, p. ~40 (citing Tarber 1915; Bueso 1980) | secondary | A real-world check on dilution. |
| F6 | Tamarind is a *Flavor Matrix* surprise pairing for dairy (with date and potato chips). | *Flavor Matrix*, pdf 104 (Dairy) | primary | |
| F7 | Jerry Thomas's 1867 *American Barkeeper* has a Tamarind Punch: Brandy Punch with tamarind jelly in place of the lemon juice, Jamaica rum on top. | *Imbibe!*, pdf 77 | primary | A glass punch, not a milk punch. |
| F8 | Tamarind is among the acidic fruits used as bar acids. | Oxford, ACID PHOSPHATE (pdf 66) | primary | |

## Worked estimate for Tomás's tamarind water (100 g pulp to 250 ml hot water ≈ 350 g)
- Total tartaric if every gram came out: 8–18 g → **2.3–5.1%**; free (sour-tasting) acid roughly half of that (F2); less still after straining off seeds and fibre. Sugar: 25–45 g → **7–13 g per 100 g**, not 20.
- So 3–5% is the ceiling of the range, not its middle. The taste test before the milk stays the real guard.
- Arithmetic is mine (inference from F1–F3), not the monograph's.

## Cautions
- Morton, *Fruits of Warm Climates* (1987, Purdue NewCROP page) prints tartaric acid as "8–23.8 mg" per 100 g, almost certainly a unit slip for g. Don't cite that row.
