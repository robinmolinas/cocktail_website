# Fact card: Elmer T. Lee and Blanton's, the first single-barrel bourbon brand

Hester, 2026-09-30, for explorer-hero (*The Pioneer*). Every row opened on the page this session. Tiers: Oxford = primary (library); web = secondary, named. Quotes kept under 15 words; channel noted for each.

## Facts

| # | Fact | Source (tier) |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | Blanton's "became the first 'single barrel' bourbon brand when it was introduced in 1984." | Oxford, BLANTON'S pdf 283 (primary) |
| F2 | Genesis: two liquor executives, Ferdie Falk and Robert Baranaskas, bought the Albert B. Blanton Distillery (Frankfort, KY; Buffalo Trace since 1999) and decided to start their own business. | Oxford pdf 283 |
| F3 | Lee, "a longtime distillery employee who had worked under Albert Blanton", "guided the creation of the brand". | Oxford pdf 283 |
| F4 | Albert Blanton worked in the industry 50+ years from 1897, and was remembered for bottling whisky "from a single, particularly appealing barrel for personal use or as gifts to industry insiders", a practice "not uncommon among distillery executives of his era". The habit itself is **not dated**. | Oxford pdf 283 |
| F5 | Barrels for Blanton's were chosen from single barrels that had all aged in the same metal-clad warehouse "and fell within a specific flavor profile". | Oxford pdf 283 |
| F6 | It arrived "after a long slump in American whisky sales, when some market watchers began noticing an uptick in the popularity of niche offerings"; the higher-end market was big in Japan; later sold to Takara Shuzo. | Oxford pdf 283 |
| F7 | Distillers usually dump many barrels together "to ensure consistency"; single-barrel spirits "carry all the unique qualities and variations of an individual barrel". Bottlers "will often handwrite the barrel number, aging warehouse" etc. (general, not Blanton's-specific). In the 19th century most whiskies were single-barrel; "the modern practice, however, was pioneered in 1984 by Elmer T. Lee with the Blanton's brand." | Oxford, SINGLE BARREL pdf 1806 |
| F8 | US bourbon sales peaked in 1970 and fell for roughly thirty years; Lee was "one of the first people to realize" premium bourbon was the shift; Booker Noe "was also clandestinely working" on what became Booker's. | Oxford, WHISKY, BOURBON pdf 2143 |
| F9 | Lee "was not the first distiller to introduce a line of bourbon for the refined palate; others had tried it" and withdrew them "for lack of interest". | *Boston Globe*, 22 July 2013, obituary datelined New York (secondary; byline not shown on the page; Oxford cites Vitello's NYT obituary of 21 July 2013, paywalled) |
| F10 | "Mr. Lee and his staff selected their best bourbon whiskey", in decanters with horse-and-jockey stoppers, about $30 against $10–15. "The audience gathered slowly." Booker's "followed suit in 1988". | *Boston Globe*, same |
| F11 | Lee: hired 1949 as a maintenance engineer at the George T. Stagg Distillery (now Buffalo Trace), after an engineering degree; superintendent 1966; plant manager 1969; "master distiller" title early 1980s; spent his working life at the one plant. | *Boston Globe*, same; WhiskyCast (Buffalo Trace statement), 16 July 2013 |
| F12 | Lee's account: the owners came to him asking for a high-priced premium bourbon; "We tossed around several ideas"; the one that appealed was "what I had learned from Colonel Blanton" (Blanton tasting samples from a favourite warehouse and keeping the best barrel for himself); "That sounded to the new owners … like a good marketable idea." | Lee to Liza Weisstuch, "The way of a Master", *Whisky Magazine* issue 85, 22 January 2010 (secondary; his words through a reporter, interviewed a few weeks after his 90th birthday) |
| F13 | Lee on the name: "We properly named that bourbon after Colonel Blanton". | *Whisky Magazine*, same |
| F14 | Lee's understatement, on the record: "I had no idea it would become as worldwide as it is"; "We got the jump on the industry"; "the other distillers followed suit, you might say, three to four years after that." | *Whisky Magazine*, same |
| F15 | A single-barrel bourbon named Elmer T. Lee was released in 1986; he was then the only living distiller with a whiskey bearing his name. | WhiskyCast, 16 July 2013 (Buffalo Trace CEO Mark Brown's statement + WhiskyCast's text; secondary) |
| F16 | Asked (as he told it) whether they could name a bourbon after him: "Yes, provided you let me pick the bourbon that goes in it", "so I still do that" (at 90). WhiskyCast's 2006 interview: his only stipulation was to select the barrels; he tasted samples on Tuesdays "until recently" (2013). | *Whisky Magazine* 2010; WhiskyCast 2013 |
| F17 | On tasting barrels to the standard: "You could learn it, but you'd want to practice for a while." | *Whisky Magazine*, same |
| F18 | Lee had "a highball every night before dinner, around 6:00" (spirit not stated). | *Whisky Magazine*, same (reporter's narration) |

## Guards
- The private habit is Blanton's; Lee **learned** it and made it a brand (F4, F12). Never "Lee kept it private for years"; never "for decades" (undated).
- One maker isn't the maker: owners asked (F2, F12), "all of us" liked it, staff selected with him (F10).
- "Nothing to lose" (Globe) is the paper's judgement and cuts against "risked everything": neither goes in the reading.
- Blanton's barrels were chosen to a flavour profile (F5): "every barrel different" holds for single barrels in general (F7), fenced for Blanton's.
- Blanton's label marks (handwritten barrel/warehouse/dump date, Warehouse H) found only on brand, retailer and sponsored pages: **not carded**. Use F7's general sentence or nothing.
- War (B-29 radar bombardier/navigator: sources differ) stays out (Wren's trap).
- The brand-name point in the family plan ("carried his old boss's name, not his") is **false as a whole** after F15. Struck.

## Round 3 additions (2026-09-30): the drink side

| # | Fact | Source (tier) |
|---|---|---|
| F19 | Bourbon, by US regulation, must be at least 51 percent corn, "(most have substantially more)"; the thresholds date from 1938. | Oxford, CORN pdf 583; MASH BILL pdf 1255 (primary). Joy pdf 418 adds "no more than 79 percent" (secondary; not echoed by Oxford, so not used) |
| F20 | Blanton's Original Single Barrel: 93 proof (46.5% ABV); mash bill undisclosed. | Breaking Bourbon review, 2021 (named enthusiast site; secondary, weak tier) |
| F21 | Blanton's Straight From the Barrel: proof "varies by bottle depending on the barrel"; enters the barrel at 125 proof (62.5%); the reviewed bottle was 130 proof (65%); offered then only abroad and in some duty-free shops. | Breaking Bourbon, Jordan Moskal, 14 July 2016 (secondary; sample supplied by the company) |
| F22 | The labels of two reviewed bottles record the dump date, barrel number, "Warehouse H", rick number and bottle number (reviewers transcribing the bottle). "Handwritten" is **not** on this page. | Breaking Bourbon 2016 and 2021 |
| F23 | Reviewer's judgement: Blanton's "is able to replicate its pour so closely from one barrel to another"; "Blanton's has become so hard to find". | Breaking Bourbon 2021 (opinion, secondary) |
| F24 | A heavily aged whiskey tastes sweeter and brings more woody spice: back off the sweetener (about ½ tsp syrup as a start). | *Codex* p. 12 (Tomás's shelf) |

Guards (round 3): "typically 120–140 proof" was a search summary, not on the page: never used. The *Flavor Matrix* says caramel's flavour is "derived largely from Maillard reactions" (pdf 72); that chemistry is the book's and stays out of the copy (a cause is a claim). "Mostly corn" is true of every bourbon by law (F19), never "most of the flavour is corn".

## Round 4 additions (2026-09-30): the barrel

| # | Fact | Source (tier) |
|---|---|---|
| F25 | Bourbon must be aged in a new oak container, charred inside; "The charring of the wood helps to caramelize the sugars in the wood". | Oxford, WHISKY, BOURBON pdf 2140 (primary) |
| F26 | Heating a barrel breaks the wood's hemicellulose into simple sugars that brown, "which creates caramel and toffee notes"; lignin gives vanilla and smoky notes. US straight whiskies use new charred barrels, four char levels. | Oxford, CHARRING, TOASTING (AND RECHARRING) pdf 436–437 |
| F27 | In Kentucky's hot, relatively dry summers, bourbon "generally gets stronger as it ages", as water evaporates before alcohol. | Oxford pdf 2140 |
| F28 | Lee's account of Blanton's habit: he tasted samples from his favourite warehouse and picked "the best of the lot" for his personal use or entertaining. | *Whisky Magazine* 85, 2010 |

Guards (round 4): pdf 2140's proof sentence is garbled ("not more than 62.5 percent ABV when it comes off the still"); pdf 2175 gives the rule correctly (distilled ≤80%, barrel entry ≤62.5%). Never cite pdf 2140 for proof rules. The barrel caramelises the **wood's** sugars; the pan caramelises cane sugar: "the same thing done to sugar", never "tastes like the barrel" or "the barrel's own caramel".
