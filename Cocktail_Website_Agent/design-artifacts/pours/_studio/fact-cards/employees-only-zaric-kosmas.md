## Facts
- **F1** Early 2004: Zaric and Kosmas left Schiller's (Keith McNally's), taking the bar manager Billy Gilroy with them. Igor Hadzismajlovic (from Sarajevo, three years at Pravda) and Henry LaFargue (New Orleans, also Pravda) joined them. *Proper* p. 155.
- **F2** McNally didn't welcome the departure. Kosmas: "He was not happy with us." Simonson calls it a "mass exodus". p. 155. No reason for leaving is given on the page.
- **F3** Oxford: five partners founded it (Gilroy, Hadzismajlovic, Kosmas, LaFargue, Zaric), all from Pravda or Schiller's, both McNally bars. Opened 2004. pdf 721.
- **F4** Opened that autumn on Hudson Street, West Village, inside a former pastry shop. p. 155.
- **F5** Zaric wanted food: at Pravda he had seen customers leave when they got hungry. "Jay and I come from a European background", where eating and drinking went together (Simonson's gloss). p. 155. This is the motive for *food*; no page gives one for the soup.
- **F6** From the first day, homemade chicken soup went to anyone still at the bar at closing time. pp. 155–156. Oxford: a "famous chicken soup", free, each night at closing. pdf 721.
- **F7** Free pouring: Zaric didn't want jiggered drinks; Kosmas: "We weren't jigger pouring." pp. 155, 157. Oxford: other serious bars used jiggers; EO kept free-pouring. pdf 721.
- **F8** Oxford: unlike other cocktail bars it served food; drinks were largely frothy, fruity and shaken, and it didn't shun vodka as other New York bars then did. pdf 721.
- **F9** Staff loyalty (Simonson's narration): in an industry of job-hopping, "no one ever left EO"; several bartenders (Dev Johnson, Steve Schneider) have worked nowhere else. p. 156.
- **F10** Zaric: "very little turnover because they feel they had a hand in creating this", and not only bartenders: cocktail waitresses and busboys too. Also "they had no other option" (fenced) and a code, "if I've been helped, I'll help you." p. 156.
- **F11** Simonson: the customers picked up on that solidarity and contentment. p. 156.
- **F12** Among the cocktail elite it never got a reputation for the best drinks. pp. 156–157. They served gin Martinis, used coupes, played with infusions. p. 157.
- **F13** Zaric: "We're definitely not the best mixologists"; "We chose to play a different card in order to provide experience." p. 157.
- **F14** Kosmas: other cocktail people looked at them as if "They can't be really taking their drinks that seriously because people are having too much fun there." He called EO's role "the merry prankster". p. 157.
- **F15** A colleague (Katie Stipe) on the two: Zaric "such a philosopher, really deep and intense"; Kosmas "a happy, smiley, easy-going guy". Simonson: they combined those traits into one bar. p. 153.
- **F16** Zaric and Kosmas published *Speakeasy* (2010). Oxford pdf 721. Later: branches from 2016 (Oxford: Miami, Singapore; *Proper* p. 313: Austin, Miami Beach, Singapore announced).
- **F17** Oxford names curaçao in the Employees Only Manhattan as a house variation, adding "which was actually included in the original 1884 recipe". pdf 533. EO's measures are on no library page.

## Manhattan and curaçao (scope check, for Wren and Tomás)
- **M1** The Manhattan reached print in three 1884 books (Byron, Winter, Gibson). Oxford MANHATTAN pdf 1224. "Original" is itself contested there (rye or bourbon: "references to both").
- **M2** Winter's 1884 recipe has no curaçao: Peruvian bitters, gum, whiskey, vermouth, 1:1. *Imbibe!* pdf 194.
- **M3** Jerry Thomas's 1887 guide: "2 dashes of Curacoa or Maraschino", 1 oz rye, 2 oz vermouth, Boker's bitters. *Imbibe!* pdf 195; *Joy* pdf 330; archive.org FTS of the 1887 guide. Muckensturm (1906): dashes of curaçao, 2:1. *Joy* pdf 330.
- **M4** Byron's 1884 recipe: not read (FTS didn't find it). So pdf 533's "the original 1884 recipe" is Oxford's alone, in one parenthesis, and its own MANHATTAN entry gives three 1884 recipes.

## Checks and fences
- **C1** Never "the original recipe had curaçao". Sourced at most: "curaçao turns up in some of the Manhattan's earliest printed recipes" (M3, 1887), or Oxford's sentence attributed to Oxford. "Closer to the original than the serious bars" is out: no page says what the other bars' Manhattans held.
- **C2** Never "their recipe" / "the Employees Only Manhattan" for our glass. No EO spec in the library (gap: *Speakeasy*, 2010, only if Tomás wants their measures).
- **C3** "Walked out" is stronger than the page ("left", "mass exodus", McNally "not happy"). No protest and no reason are stated. Say they left and took the bar manager with them.
- **C4** Leaving a job isn't a rule broken on the page. The rules they broke on the record are the scene's: free pour instead of jiggers, food, vodka (F7, F8). Anything else is our reading.
- **C5** "Nobody left" is Simonson's absolute; Zaric's own words are "very little turnover". It's the staff, not the guests.
- **C6** Not two men alone: five partners (F3). Use "Zaric, Kosmas and their partners", or name the two as the ones the book quotes.
- **C7** Soup: motive for food only (F5): partly business (hungry customers left), partly background. Tell the soup by who was still there, never the night ending (*Curtain Call*'s).
- **C8** Fenced by Wren (r1): "outside of that universe", the Angel's Share comparison, "no other option", "cried all the way to the bank", "women came… men followed", the patrons line, "merry prankster" (and the chapter title), the barback promise, the façade. Also "party bar" as the point.
