## Batavia arrack and Seville orange (for Tomás's drink)

| # | Fact (our words) | Source | Tier | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | Batavia arrack is a pungent, intensely flavoured spirit from Indonesia made from sugar cane and rice, traded internationally since the 1600s. Overseas Chinese distillers made it from Indonesian ingredients, with Dutch money. | Oxford, ARRACK, BATAVIA (pdf 147) | primary | Wren: one plain clause at most on the colonial origin. |
| A2 | It was usually redistilled two or three times and is strong, over 50% ABV (as made in the 19th-century accounts). | Oxford, ARRACK, BATAVIA (pdf 149) | primary | A period figure; today's bottle strength is Tomás's to check on the label. |
| A3 | In London punch houses it sold at eight shillings a quart when the finest French brandy cost six. | O1 | Seville (sour, bitter) oranges are generally only available in winter and are high in pectin; in the 18th century sour-orange juice was thought the best citrus juice for punch (Oxford points to ASHLEY). ## Conflicts

- **C1, the shrub's certainty.** ASHLEY (pdf 162) rests it on "circumstantial evidence"; SHRUB (pdf 1794) states it flat ("the only kind he would use"). Tell it labelled ("by his own advertisements, it seems…"), never as his recipe.
- **C2, whose hands.** ASHLEY says only "mixed in front of the customer"; PUNCH (pdf 1328) corrects "He—or rather" to Mrs. Gaywood and the young women under her. Never Ashley mixing every cup.
- **C3, why in front of the customer.** Both pages give checking/assurance of quality as the reason; the speed came from the shrub (F14). Wren's r1 ruling ("mixing in front … only as part of the system's speed") gives it a cause the page doesn't: tell the mixing with no reason, or not at all (honesty and fair measure are *Fair Measure*'s and the Good Wizard's).
- **C4, stature.** ASHLEY hedges ("perhaps more than any other person"); PUNCH says flatly "the beginning of modern, mixological bartending". Keep the hedge.
- **C5, forty-five years.** The plan said the house "ran forty-five years". He kept it forty-five years; it ran to the mid-19th century (pdf 163).
- **C6, "first spirit on his price list"** (plan's drink direction): not on the page. Arrack is the first named and the dearest of the three (6s against 4s).
- **C7, "Catherine".** "Perhaps" on the page; never as fact.

## Legends and inferences

- **L1:** Oxford's "apparently" on his ambitions (cheese → tavern): an inference, no motive.
- **L2:** The punch "taking over" the name because customers chose it: our reading, signposted. The page is passive.
- **L3:** Why he came back to the bar: nowhere. A signposted "I like to think…" only (Wren's ruling).

## Guards

- **G1** Benjamin Franklin is *Overnight*'s; Boswell's "dissolute youths" out (Wren).
- **G2** No bowl, portion, cup-size or "for one" language (*Four Shares*). The ceiling zests came from bowls: image only.
- **G3** "Made before anyone asked" is *Not Only the Way*'s epigraph; "made days ahead … waits until someone's there" and "make the syrup before you know who it's for" are *Whoever Comes In*'s epigraph and closing line; "make it a day ahead" is *For Good*'s closing, "the day before" *By Design*'s. Tell the shrub as **bottled and cellared**, not as "made ahead/before anyone ordered".
- **G4** No whole-bar look (*Say So*); the house is the work behind the counter.
- **G5** No nutmeg (`nuts`), although shrub was served with "a scraping of nutmeg" (pdf 1793).
- **G6** Never "invented the bar", "the first bartender", "the first woman bartender".
- **G7** The 1750s: never "retired", "stepped away", "took it back", "couldn't let go" as fact; "back running the bar, with his son".
- **G8** No barrel-cellar close-up in the image (*A Brother's Care*'s solera; *Night Light* fenced cellars). The cellar is fine in words.
- **G9** Batavia arrack is named in *Someone Else's Map* (Meehan asked for it); a category is never "taken", no overlap in story.
