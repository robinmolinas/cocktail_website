# Batch jester: summary for Robin

The batch ran from 2026-09-30 to 2026-10-04: eleven pours, each in its own room with three fresh agents, from the family plan (`plans/jester.md`). It ran headless, so you weren't asked anything along the way, and disagreements the rooms couldn't settle are flagged below rather than decided. The round cap changed during the batch. Caregiver and creator ran 7 rounds; regular-guy, explorer and hero ran 6 or fewer; innocent through sage ran 4, with round 4 in four steps. The last three (outlaw, ruler, sage) ran side by side at your request.

Result: 9 drafts, 2 flagged. 6 of the 11 are veto-free.

## What the rooms found hard

- **Stories with no drink of their own.** The Jester's best stories are jokes, doors and verses, not recipes. In magician (a phone booth), sage (a poem) and lover (a timing game), the drink had to act out the story's shape: a bottle that looks ordinary, a dry drink with the hope underneath, a coffee someone else finishes. Each room tied the shape to the drink in one clause and no more.
- **Hester striking her own shorthand.** In outlaw, ruler and sage, her strongest round-4 point was her own earlier wording: "no banana liqueur in 1806", "two of the three stories", and time spans counted from an undated poem. Each was corrected as a quoted wording fix in step 3, so no room ran over.
- **Historical "he" against the guest.** Almost every reading tells a real man's story, so the pronoun warnings were constant. In outlaw, one "he" turned out to be an anonymous reader rather than a historical person; it couldn't be accepted, and it was rewritten.
- **Neighbours across families.** *Word Gets Round*'s Rickey, *Fine by Me*'s bar and the Spanish Coffee backup were all owned with written fences, never by dropping a classic. (Your rule: only the exact same cocktail is off-limits.)
- **Names.** With picks held until the vote, ten rooms settled in one vote (unanimous or 2–1). Only jester-ruler split 1–1–1.
