### Round 1

🪞 **Wren:** Hester, Tomás, here's who this is, and then I'm going back on something I said in the plan review. My full card is `_studio/work/ruler-caregiver/psychologist-persona-card.md`.

**The one true thing.** When it gets loud, they don't raise the stakes. They give the other person a hearing. Everyone's talking at once and someone is angry, maybe at them. They're the one person who could settle it with a word, and everyone knows it. Instead they ask a question, pull up a chair and let the angry one finish. Their decision comes afterwards, and it holds because everyone was heard. Their authority is on loan from the people who trust them, and they spend it by listening. **Hidden:** they often already know what they'd decide, and the waiting is the hardest part. The patience takes effort; it isn't their temperament. They know people check their face first, so they keep it steady for everyone else. **Fear:** chaos on their watch, and someone in their care paying for it. The private version is "I listened too long and it came apart anyway." **Gift, said out loud:** people feel safe enough to say the real thing, and even the angry one leaves heard and still welcome.

**Siblings and neighbours.** The Judge weighs both sides and gives a verdict. This person would rather nobody got hurt than be right. The Noble Ruler decides alone; this one decides after listening. The ground nearby is crowded, so these are traps: *Fair Measure* (the Nanny) owns "the sensible one" and "against the fun". *In One Piece* owns "raise your voice", the room settling and "nobody has to fight". *Before the Room* owns "calm" as its key word. *Off Duty* owns "you call, you hold this" and the "Everyone else…" tagline opener. "Let someone look after you" is worn out across the family, so it can't be the position. Gandhi is never named, there are no job labels, and nobody is made a saint.

**Story test.** (1) Someone with real power (standing, or the right to use force) in a moment of heat. (2) They answer with a hearing, not force. (3) It visibly costs them something. (4) Nobody is hurt, and the angry person stays welcome. Out: rescues, limits for the fun's sake, strength held back, verdicts, politics and war, and Petraske (owned elsewhere).

**Verdict on the plan's lead: I'm overturning Bilgray and withdrawing my own accept.** I re-read Oxford pdf 267 and 269 in full. Page 267 calls Bilgray's "one of the twin pillars" of Panama's "raunchy, boozy fun". His 1938 garden had "the same sort of risqué floor shows" as the rival bar. So the garden isn't a calm line held; it's the same trade next door, and telling only the honest-saloon half would be selective. The cost I accepted, being "too staid", is the Nanny's sting. "Kept the fun, just elsewhere" is Vernon's shape, and "quiet, never loud" is *In One Piece*'s tagline. Nobody is in his care and there's no moment of heat. Those missing parts are events, not motives, so a signpost can't supply them. The Met Bar backup stays where it is.
