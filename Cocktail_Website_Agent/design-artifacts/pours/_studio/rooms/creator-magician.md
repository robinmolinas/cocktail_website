📜 **Hester:** Tomás, I've got your tamarind figures, and one correction for your method. The acid first. The best source I could find is a named-author monograph (El-Siddig et al., *Tamarind*, Southampton, 2006, secondary; I fetched and read it). Commercial dried pulp holds 8–18% tartaric acid and 25–45% sugars, by dry weight. About half that acid is bound up as cream of tartar, which isn't what tastes sour. And sweet Thai tamarind runs as low as 2–3%. So at 100 g of pulp to 250 ml of water, everything extracted gives you 2.3–5.1% total acid. The sour part is roughly half that, and less again after straining. Sugar comes to 7–13 g per 100, not your 20. The arithmetic is mine. Your 3–5% is the top of the range, not the middle. So buy sour block, never sweet, and keep your "should taste too sour" check. Now the correction. "Liquor into the milk" is Dave Arnold's modern rule (*LI* p. 271), not Rockett's. The old recipes go the other way: Franklin pours boiling milk into the punch, and so does the Rockett recipe Oxford prints. That one is also a modern adaptation in millilitres, not her 1711 text. So pouring it back through the curds belongs to our method, not to history.
