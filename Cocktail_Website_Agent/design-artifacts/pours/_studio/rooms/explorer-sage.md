### Round 1

🪞 **Wren:** Hester, Tomás, here's the Genius. Full card: `work/explorer-sage/psychologist-persona-card.md`.

**The one true thing.** They hold one question for years, alongside a perfectly ordinary life, because they need to *understand* it. Not for the fun of not knowing (that's the Free Spirit's), not to fix a nuisance (the Innovator's), not for someone who's hurting (the Researcher's). What keeps it open is an answer that's true and explains nothing. They don't love the chase. They love the day they finally know, and then they give all of it away.

**Hidden:** how long it's been. There's a question they first asked years ago and still haven't put down, and they've stopped mentioning it because people think it should have been settled or dropped. They also get taken for a know-it-all, when really they just can't rest inside something they aren't allowed to understand. **Fear:** being stuck with an answer they can't get behind, for good (entrapment of the mind), and, more quietly, one day settling for it themselves. **Gift, said out loud once:** when they get there, they hand over the whole answer, plainly, so nobody else has to take it on trust. Things everyone had written off get seen properly.

### Round 2

📜 **Hester:** Wren, I've read the lead on the page and it holds. I'd keep the Zombie narrowing, with one correction first. Card: `fact-cards/jeff-berry-zombie.md` (F1–F18, H1–H4, C1–C4).

**Correction (C2).** In 1979 the question was "What's in this drink?" asked of *every* drink. Ray answered "Rum and fruit juice" "no matter what the drink" (Proper p. 258). Nothing on any page has him asking about the Zombie in 1979. What the page does say is that many recipes, "including the Zombie", were already lost "by the time Berry started asking" (p. 259). The Zombie itself is first on record as his question in 1998. So the reading can say he started asking at twenty-one and that by 1998 the Zombie was the one he still couldn't answer. It can't say he held the Zombie question from 1979.

**Your flag 1: the 1998 line.** It's Wayne Curtis quoting Berry (Oxford BERRY pdf 260), and it sits too close to *The First Guess*. Our paraphrase: "In 1998 he put it in print that the Zombie's recipe was still unknown." "Ten years and three books" is his own count, so it stays "he said", and nobody adds it to 1998.

**Your flag 2: the binder and the notebook (C1).** They come from two different authors. Curtis (pdf 260) calls the recipe from the self-published 1950 binder "an early recipe", and its author thanked Don the Beachcomber for his "generous assistance". Curtis never mentions the notebook. The notebook comes from the ZOMBIE entry, which Berry signed himself ("discovered by this writer in 2006", in headwaiter Richard Santiago's notebook, pdf 2229). He's the one who calls it "the original secret formula" that Beach "took to his grave". So the binder is never "the original", and the notebook is "what he calls the original". Neither page gives a clean order between the finds, so no "first… then at last". One more small fix: the microfilm is Proper pp. 260–261, not Oxford.

**Tomás, which recipe is on a page.** Oxford ZOMBIE pdf 2229 prints a full recipe straight after the notebook sentence, in Berry's own entry: 22.5 ml lime, 15 ml falernum, 45 ml each of gold Puerto Rican and gold Jamaican rum, 30 ml Lemon Hart 151 demerara, 1 tsp grenadine, 6 drops Pernod, a dash of Angostura, and 15 ml Don's Mix (two parts grapefruit to one part cinnamon syrup). It's blended no more than five seconds with 3/4 cup crushed ice, poured into a tall glass with ice to fill, and garnished with mint. The page doesn't say in words that this is the notebook's recipe, so the reading calls it "the Zombie as Berry printed it", never "Beach's own" or "the notebook's". The Codex Zombie Punch (pp. 126–127) is Death & Co's homage to Brian Miller's rework, shaken, with Velvet Falernum. It's modern, so don't let it pass as the recovered one.

**Worth the Trip.** There Berry is only the teller ("As Jeff Berry tells it", y2). Here he's the story. And our notebook line is also his own telling, so the reading carries his word ("he calls it") but never that "as he tells it" frame. We own it in the dossier in one clause.

**Haigh is free.** No pour file uses him, and the *In a Minute* room only weighed him as a backup. Your "least like you" is already on the page as Simonson's word: "Gregarious and bombastic, Haigh was the opposite of Berry" (p. 259). On his own, Berry got "rum and fruit juice". 🍸 **Tomás:** Wren, I take the Zombie, and the drink is the story. Berry's answer to "rum and fruit juice" was eleven named parts, so the glass should hold every one of them. I start from the recipe Berry printed in his own Oxford entry (ZOMBIE pdf 2229; Regan prints the same one, Joy pdf 409). That's three rums: gold Puerto Rican, gold Jamaican and Lemon Hart 151 Demerara (I'd name that one as recommended, or any 75% Demerara overproof). Then lime, falernum, Don's Mix written out as two parts grapefruit to one of cinnamon syrup, Angostura and a mint sprig.

Your three watch-outs. **Blender:** out. I shake it with ice and pour it over crushed ice in a tall Zombie glass, the way the Codex makes its own Zombie (p. 127). **Grenadine:** one plain teaspoon, never mentioned in the reading. If you'd rather drop it, 2.5 ml more falernum puts the sugar back. **Pernod:** six drops is a seasoning, not *Rosetta*'s bottle. Hester, the sources disagree on what "Pernod" means (Oxford just says "Pernod", the Codex says "Pernod Absinthe"). Tell me which, and I'll keep it or drop it.

**Falernum is the one I'd fight for.** Oxford (FALERNUM pdf 761) says the recipes vary: cloves, ginger, nutmeg, and "almonds are sometimes added". So a bought bottle is the one part you'd have to take on trust, and on the safe side it counts as nuts. Mine is made at home: aged rum steeped with lime zest and cloves, then sweetened. It has no almond and no nutmeg, and no ginger either, because our table classes ginger as spice. That keeps the drink veto-free, and it puts the person's gift in the glass: nothing in it anyone has to take on trust. I checked the *Matrix* for a spark (citrus, pdf 80). Its best pairings include ginger, which the veto rules out, and nothing else earned a place. So the drink is interesting because the code is broken in the method, not because of a twist.

**My numbers caught the original.** Berry's proportions come out at 21.0% strength, 4-5 g sugar per 100 ml and 0.56% acid. A shaken sour's range is 15-19.7%, 5-8.9 g and 0.76-0.94%, so it's out on strength and acid. That's my shake on paper, not his five-second blend, which waters it down more, but the acid shortfall holds either way. I keep his rums exactly (45/45/30 ml) and take the lime to 37.5 ml, Don's Mix to 30 and falernum to 22.5. Everything lands: 18.4%, 7.1 g, 0.81%, about 340 ml. So the parts are his, the proportions are mine, and I'll say so. It's a big drink, about 60 ml of pure alcohol (roughly six UK units, my arithmetic), and Checks will flag it.

Closing-line seed for you, Wren: label the falernum jar with everything in it. My sweep is in `work/explorer-sage/mixologist-r2-notes.md`. The v0 spec comes in round 3.
