# Batch hero: summary for Robin

The batch ran from 2026-09-30 to 2026-10-04: eleven pours, each in its own room with three fresh agents, from the family plan (`plans/hero.md`). It ran headless, two pours per sitting with a compact between, so you weren't asked anything along the way. The first rooms ran on the 7- and 6-round budgets; from hero-innocent on, every room had 4 rounds, with round 4 in four steps. **Ten pours are drafts and one is flagged.** Every pour lints with 0 errors, and every room closed with all three sign-offs. **Seven are veto-free** (the plan expected nine).

## Flags and edges first

**Flagged: your call**
- ***One Line* (hero-lover): balance reads OUT on sugar, by the room's choice.** It's a dry Martini, the Vesper exactly as the novel orders it (Gordon's, vodka, Lillet Blanc, lemon peel, shaken ice-cold), with a teaspoon of Jamaican rum poured in first as the spark. **Edges worth a look**
- ***Work It Out* (hero-innocent) is a meat drink:** DeGroff's Bullshot, with home-made beef broth. It's veto-free on the table, but a vegetarian can't have it (rule candidate below).
- ***Anyone Would Have*:** Marco Dionysos's surname coincides with the app's name. He chose it, and the reading owns it in one clause. The Innocent plan had set him aside for this reason.
- ***One Line*:** the story is fiction (Fleming's *Casino Royale*), and the reading says so once ("in the novel"). The deep champagne goblet rests on archive.org scans of two printings, because the library has no copy of the novel. "Never drinks it again" is scoped to Fleming's later books, and the 2006 film is left out.
- ***Hold the Shark*:** that the shark was Vic's own telling rests on Difford's alone. The Appleton 12 label figures are unsourced (covered by the ABV sweep).
- ***Day Job* (hero-magician):** Trader Vic, *Hold the Shark*'s man, is named once, as the reason the Floridita cocktail survived. The reading never says the recipe is his. Two changes from the printed drink: half a lime instead of a whole one (the whole lime is OUT), and the lime's own peel instead of orange.
- ***Dressed for It* (hero-ruler):** acid sits at the top of its band (0.141%, edge, justified). The Crusta is never called "the Sidecar's ancestor" (Oxford contradicts itself).
- ***Leave It With Me* (hero-sage):** the scene is the family's account (Puig to Wolfe, 1970), checked through archive.org scans. `serves: 6` was added to the cocktail block by hand, because the assembler has no such field.
- ***Next One's Mine* (hero-caregiver) and *The Long Answer* (hero-creator):** no spark (your call, 2026-09-30). Each room says why, and each drink is made interesting from the books instead.
- **Library gaps** the rooms named: Walker, *The Night Club Era* (who owned Gallant's inn, the time served); Powers, *Faces along the Bar*; *Frankly Speaking* (Vic's own telling); a published Underberg ingredient declaration; Fleming's *Casino Royale*.

## What the rooms found hard

- **One book feeding most rows.** *A Proper Drink* carried seven of the eleven stories. Hester's guard (vary the telling, date every living person's quotes) held, but she had to strike the plan's own shorthand more than once: "stock nobody else carried" and "when no one had rye" (outlaw), and "the one rule he kept for himself" (ruler).
- **Doubters and critics.** In hero-ruler the reading first asked the guest to find the fair point in a doubt the story proves wrong. Hester's audit separated the "terrible idea" people (wrong) from the critics of the label (partly right), and the closing line had to follow.
- **Closing-line crossings.** Twice (ruler, sage) Wren and Tomás moved towards each other's positions in the same step. The owner settled both in step 3 without an extra round. In hero-sage, Wren's sign-off was conditional on the line, and it landed.
- **Allergens of secret recipes.** Bénédictine and Underberg each cost a planned veto-free row, because no botanical list could be read. Hero ends at seven veto-free, against the plan's nine.
- **Sibling motifs.** With eleven readings close in theme, the rooms repeatedly found their best phrase already owned ("You just never called it that", "the other half", "every morning", "say so first"). Three stock 4-gram overlaps were accepted as warnings.
