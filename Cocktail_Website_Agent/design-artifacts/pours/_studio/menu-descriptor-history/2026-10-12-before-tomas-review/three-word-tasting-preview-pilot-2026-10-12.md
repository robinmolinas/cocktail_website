# Three-word tasting previews — five-pour pilot

Status: proposed for Robin's review. These descriptions are inferred from the authored recipes, methods, flavour checks and Tomás/Hester/Wren room discussions; they are not tasting results. No pour, recipe or application data has been changed.

Robin accepted the direction and requested the full collection. The expanded set is now in `cocktail-menu-descriptors-2026-10-12.md` and its structured JSON catalogue. The five revised pilot trios below are preserved in that set.

## Purpose

Give the guest a quick sense of the drink when it appears, without requiring them to understand its ingredients. Robin's photographed bar menus provide the reference: three short words separated by dots or dashes. Robin clarified that at least one word should feel evocative or inspiring, with examples such as vibrant, velvety, lovable and liberating. Suggested placement: immediately beneath the cocktail name.

## Editorial approach proposed for review

- Exactly three familiar words. Use two sensory anchors and one evocative word as a working pattern, with flexible ordering.
- Sensory anchors come from the served recipe or method: flavour, sweetness/bitterness/acidity, texture, bubbles or intensity.
- The evocative word gives the line feeling and character. It can draw on the drink's sensory impression, its gesture or the authored story; it is an editorial invitation, not a claim about alcohol's effects.
- Allow repeated words when the sensory trait is shared. These labels need to be accurate; they do not need the uniqueness of cocktail names or taglines.
- The five established the voice. Full-collection authoring is now requested; implementation in the experience remains separate from this wording review.
