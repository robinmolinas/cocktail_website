# Dionysus — three-word cocktail menu

132 draft descriptions · 12 October 2026

Each trio gives a quick sense of the sip and some of the drink’s character, in the spirit of Robin’s reference menus. Flavour, texture, intensity, mood and playful style words can mix naturally. The five revised pilot trios are retained.

The descriptions are inferred from the current recipes, methods, flavour checks and available Tomás/Hester/Wren discussions. The words of feeling draw on the authored story or the drink’s impression; they describe character rather than promise an effect from alcohol.

These are wording drafts for review. Cocktail recipes, approval statuses and the application have not been changed.

## Review notes

- The Other Berry leads with ripe red fruit and vanilla; its savoury note sits underneath, so the trio emphasises fruit and tang.
- A repeated word can describe a real shared trait. All 132 complete trios are distinct, without requiring every individual word to be unique.
- “Dry”, “Tart”, “Bitter” and “Bittersweet” describe different expectations. In particular, the port-and-tea drink keeps its sweetness; the still cider drink does not promise bubbles; unpeated whisky drinks do not acquire smoke from their persona.
- Texture words follow the service: egg or cream can give foam or creaminess; clarification and stirring can give silkiness without cream flavour.
- “Nutty” in A Brother’s Care describes oloroso’s aroma, rather than adding a nut ingredient or changing the declared allergens.
- At the Time gains its coffee flavour as the coffee block melts. Its first sips remain an Old-Fashioned.
- Is It Just Me is a two-glass serve: the fruit is in the apricot rye, and the malt is in the pilsner.
- Smoke strength, fruit intensity and subtle floral accents can vary with the named bottle or permitted substitute. The trios describe the intended recipe profile.
