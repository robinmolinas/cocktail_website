# Batch magician: summary for Robin

The batch ran from 2026-09-30 to 2026-10-04: ten pours, each in its own room with three fresh agents, from the family plan (`plans/magician.md`). It ran headless, two or three pours at a time at your request, so you weren't asked anything along the way. *No Accident* (magician-outlaw) was already approved and wasn't reopened. **All ten pours are drafts. None is flagged.** Every pour lints with 0 errors, all three voices signed every pour, and every name was settled by a vote with no three-way split. Nine of the ten are veto-free.

## Edges first

**Not veto-free (1 of 10)**
- ***Built to Hold* (magician-ruler): contains nuts, through the arrack.** Tomás added the `batavia_arrack` row in this room and classed it `nuts` on the safe side. *Imbibe!* (pdf 66) says Batavia arrack still has palm sap in it, while Oxford (pdf 150) says the sap "fell out of the formula", and no page names the palm. No other pour uses the row. The Jamaican-style rum version (50% or stronger) has the same balance and is veto-free, and the recipe points nut-allergic guests to it. If you clear the arrack under your distillation rule, the pour becomes veto-free.

**Allergen-table questions (nothing reclassified)**
- ***Even When You Know* (magician-jester):** `creme_de_cacao_white` is `[]`, but Tomás's `cacao_nib_tincture_gin` row (explorer-magician) is `nuts, dairy`, and they can't both be the safe side. Tomás recommends leaving the crème de cacao at `[]`. If you move it, this pour loses veto-free.

- ***Even When You Know* (magician-jester):** a three-layer Pousse Café, which has no *Codex* root. Tomás justified it layer by layer. The layer densities are unsourced estimates, so the method includes a test pour for new bottles.

## What the rooms found hard

- **The siblings had already taken the ground.** By the last rooms, the drafted pours from other families covered much of what Wren had planned for this one. In magician-sage she named four siblings that had taken the Clairvoyant's ground and narrowed the person to match. In magician-ruler she had to steer around *Before It Had a Name*, *By Design* and *Say So*.
- **Sparks that cost a veto.** In magician-ruler, the palm-sugar spark made the pour `nuts`. Its meaning ("put back the piece that left") ran against the reading's position, and the sources disagree about whether the palm sap ever left the arrack. So Tomás moved to demerara, but then the same evidence put the arrack itself in `nuts`.
- **Over-claiming epigraphs.** Three times Hester's audit caught an epigraph claiming more than the page: "Made the way…" (only the pour is the bar's), "This is how" (the recipe is lost) and "no grappa did" (the family's own 1967 bottling contradicts it). Each time, the fix was narrower wording.
- **Hester striking her own shorthand.** "The hands weren't his" (ruler), "the part the tree isn't grown for" (sage, which dropped "mainly"), and a freezer-temperature range looser than the page (lover). She caught all three in her own audits.
- **Lint after the last step.** Twice a motif overlap or a word count showed up only after the final wording landed (lover, ruler, sage). Each time Wren made a lint-only fix, and Hester confirmed it where it touched a fact.
- **Story swaps.** Two rooms replaced the plan's lead story (innocent, hero). The rest accepted it, usually with conditions.
