## Wren's three questions
- **y2, "back from the dead": FAIL as copy (R3).** The copied run isn't four words. v1's "bringing America's saloon culture back from the dead" is eight consecutive words of Oxford pdf 770 ("they can take credit for bringing America's saloon culture back from the dead"). "Back from the dead" alone is a common idiom, and fine on its own.
- **y3, "around 2000": FAIL, mildly (R4).** Oxford pdf 1332 says "by the early 2000s, they had spawned a movement". Use the page's span. The same sentence also has a bigger problem: "a new generation of bartenders" isn't the page's subject. Oxford's subject is "many people" who felt too much had been sacrificed and found each other online. They were drinkers as well as bartenders.
- **y4, "served drinks whose glasses were coated first with apricot brandy": FAIL on three counts (R5).** (a) "1890s Chicago": the dated record is Maloney's 1900 book and the *Police Gazette* of 1902-03 (C6). "Gay Nineties" is Regan's framing. (b) "apricot brandy": Maloney's was "Eagle Creme D'Apricot", red, a liqueur (B4, B5). Next to "clear, dry apricot brandy" two sentences earlier, the reading tells the guest ours is the same thing as his. (c) "in the decade the fern bars dressed up as" rests on (a). "Served" is acceptable on Regan's "you could order … from bartender James Maloney" (Joy pdf 41, secondary).
