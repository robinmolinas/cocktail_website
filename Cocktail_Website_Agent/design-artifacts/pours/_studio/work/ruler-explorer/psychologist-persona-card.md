# Persona card: The Reformer (ruler-explorer), Wren, round 1

Source: persona.py ruler-explorer (essence, story, goal Control / fear Revolution; drivers: provide structure / seek paradise). Lincoln is the sheet's example: never in guest text, no politics.

## The one true thing
They change the thing because they love it. Every shared thing they belong to (the family's holiday, the club, the team's way of working, the street's yearly fair) is one they'd rather change than lose. People take them for the moderniser. They're the most sentimental person in the room. They keep the old form on purpose, as the welcome, and change who it's for.

## Hidden
How much they love the old thing. They argue for the change in practical terms ("nobody comes any more", "it has to work for the new ones") because saying "I love this and I can't watch it empty out" sounds weak. So the old guard hears an enemy.

## Fear
Being the one who broke it. Not chaos in general (the Caring Leader and the Nanny already own "it all comes apart"): collapse they set off themselves, while trying to save it. And its twin: the thing dying quietly because nobody dared.

## Gift (say it out loud, once)
Things people love are still there, with more people in them, because someone was willing to change them. Nobody had to leave for someone else to get in.

## Scene seeds (pick one, not a list)
- The family gathering that had shrunk to the same six people. They moved the date, let the new partners and the friends' kids in, kept the one dish and the one song. An aunt says it isn't the same. Next year twice as many come.
- The club with the rule nobody remembers the reason for.

## Siblings and neighbours (keep apart)
- Revolutionary (Ruler): tears it down. Never "overthrow", "depose", "tear down".
- People's Representative (*One Table*): owns "both sides", "made to choose", tables. So the cost can't be "attacked from both sides".
- Caring Leader (*Go On*): owns "it all comes apart" and the room kept together.
- Nanny (*Fair Measure*, Caregiver x Ruler): keeps the good thing and changes the rule so it lasts ("You're why it lasts", "You make it last"). The Reformer's change is *who gets in*, not the measure. Avoid "last(s)" as the gift word.
- Innovator (*For Good*): owns "it's always been like that". Don't use it.
- Makeover Artist (*Overnight*): "change everything except the part that makes it theirs". Theirs = one person's thing; ours = a shared place.
- Old Master (*Beside the First*): own work, change one thing, keep the old beside it.
- Expedition Leader (*Far Enough*): bringing everyone back. Keep off counting heads.
- Judge (unauthored): applies the rule; the Reformer rewrites it.

## Traps

- Gender: the fern bars let women in on equal terms. That is the story's change, never a point about the guest. Guest-side: "people the old rooms turned away".
- Making Hobday a crusader: no motive on the page. Only "loved his bar as much as his customers did" and the name.
- "Kept the saloon's own furniture" (my own plan line) is false: they "fancifully resurrected" an 1890s look, "not without irony" (pdf 995), "nostalgists" (pdf 770).
- The Lemon Drop's sourced purpose is to "mask" two shots of vodka (pdf 995). A signposted kinder motive can't replace a sourced one. Out: "a sweet everyone knew let new people in".
- "First fern bar": 995 says no (the Trident), 1173 says "arguably the first". Contested: don't state it.
- Oxford's recipe (pdf 1175) is Martin Cate's, with "or citrus vodka": never "his recipe".
- "door" is common across pours; "a door closing" is *Off-Label*'s.

## Story test
1. A shared place or rule many people live in (not one person's own work).
2. The old form kept on purpose and visibly, out of affection, not as a compromise.
3. More people let in.
4. The change saved the old thing.
5. The change outlived its maker.
Nice to have (signpost or put in the guest's half if missing): the cost, being taken for the wrecker.
Out: an overthrow; a recipe revised by its own author; a measure tightened for safety (Nanny).

## "This is me" candidates
- People think you want to change it. You're the one who loves it most.
- You don't change things to be rid of them. You change them so you never have to be.
- The bar closed. The change didn't. (y5 / position seed; pdf 995 + 1332)

## Position seed
They hide the love, so they're heard as the enemy. Proposal: say what you're keeping first, out loud, and why you love it; then the change. And: what you opened outlives the place (the bar closed in 1986, the gain stayed).
