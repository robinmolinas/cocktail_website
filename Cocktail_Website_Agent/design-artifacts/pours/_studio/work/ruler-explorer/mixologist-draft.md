Story as the room agreed (all three kept the lead): Henry Africa's and the fern bars. Facts from Hester's card (`_studio/fact-cards/henry-africa-fern-bars-lemon-drop.md`, F1-F22, C1-C4). The drink is the Lemon Drop, Hobday's "most enduring original cocktail" (F18), served the "more elegant" way the original was: up, in a sugar-rimmed cocktail glass (F19). The one thing added is an old glass: the coupe is coated with apricot spirit, the way James Maloney coated his Bell-Ringers' glasses in his *20th Century Guide for Mixing Fancy Drinks* (Chicago, 1900; card B1-B7; *Police Gazette* 1902-1903). The lead came from *Joy* pdf 41. "Gay Nineties", "a long-gone category" and "category" are Regan's words and his judgment, not the record, and they stay out of guest text (Hester's fence, C5-C7). **Maloney's coating was a sweet red crème d'apricot** (B4, B5). The clear, dry eau-de-vie is our swap, a known one: Oxford says the eau-de-vie can stand in for the liqueur "for a less sweet and more delicate" drink (pdf 133, B8). So it's "the way Maloney coated his glasses", never "Maloney's apricot". Spec: `_studio/specs/ruler-explorer.json`.

**Glass:** cocktail coupe (about 200 ml), chilled, coated inside with apricot eau-de-vie, fine sugar on the outside of the rim
**Contains:** nuts

## Recipe
| amount | item | note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 60 ml (2 oz) | vodka | any plain, unflavoured vodka of 40% or stronger; not a citrus vodka |
| 22.5 ml (¾ oz) | fresh lemon juice | squeezed just before you mix |
| 22.5 ml (¾ oz) | simple syrup | one part sugar dissolved in one part warm water, cooled |
| 2.5 ml (½ teaspoon) | apricot eau-de-vie | a clear, dry apricot brandy (Hungarian barack pálinka or Austrian Marillenbrand style), not the sweet apricot-flavoured kind. It coats the glass, the way a Chicago bartender coated his glasses in 1900 |
| for the rim | fine (caster) sugar | on the outside of the rim only |

## Method
1. Put the coupe in the freezer for ten minutes.
2. Pour the half teaspoon of apricot eau-de-vie into a saucer, and spread a little fine sugar on a second saucer.
3. Turn the cold glass upside down and roll the outside edge of its rim through the apricot spirit, then through the sugar, until a thin band of sugar runs all the way round the outside.
4. Pour what's left of the apricot spirit into the glass and turn the glass slowly until the inside is coated. Leave it there. Nothing gets tipped out.
5. Put the vodka, lemon juice and simple syrup in a shaker, fill it with ice and shake hard for 10 to 15 seconds.
6. Strain it into the glass.
