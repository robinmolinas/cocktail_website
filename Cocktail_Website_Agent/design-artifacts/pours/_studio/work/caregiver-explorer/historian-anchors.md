## Fences (the reading must not say)
- A single inventor: "the bubbles in this glass were meant to cure sailors", "every one goes back to him", "the part he made". Bergman, Schweppe (F2, F8). Say "owes something to", "began with him", "soda water was once meant to…".
- "The one thing he wanted": he hoped for other diseases too (F14).
- "Sailors he would never meet": not on record; "far out at sea".
- "He never sold it": the pamphlet cost a shilling. Say "never went into the business" / "left the selling to others". (C2)
- "He published the beer-vat method": what he published was chalk and acid. (C1)
- Any year for the discovery (Oxford 1771 vs pamphlet 1772). "1772" only for the pamphlet, if at all.
- A voyage number or year for Cook's ships.
- Anything grander than "it didn't cure scurvy" (no "it helped nobody"; no "he died thinking he'd failed": not on record).
- "Hops from his vat / the smell of his brewery": Oxford says "a fermenting beer vat" only: no brewery named, nothing about its hops. The hops are our nod, owned as ours ("I steep the whisky with hops…").
- "Brewery" for his first bowl: Oxford says a beer vat. The brewery may enter only through his own words (F13: gas found "at a public brewery or distillery").
- The lemon is balance, not Lind, not scurvy (Tomás). Never "the cure he missed is in your glass".
- Tonic clause, if kept: "someone else's cure travelled in **his** bubbles" is a stretch: Schweppe's water used Schweppe's own process (Oxford pdf 1826). Safe: "the fizzy water he'd started, which later carried someone else's cure", or signpost it ("I like to think…"). Also quinine's protection is historical; today's tonic has too little to protect (LI p. 364). Past tense only.
- "Scurvy" once, at most one clause (Craftsman lineage row has scurvy).
- Not "gave it away" (Down the Line owns it; Wren's own fence).
