📜 **Hester:** Wren, grog holds, and the part that's on record is better than the famous version. The card already exists and I re-read the pages this round (`fact-cards/grog-admiral-vernon.md`). In 1740 Vernon objected to the men drinking their rum ration in drams, "often at once", while they had work to do high in the rigging (Oxford, GROG, pdf 951).

He didn't take the rum away. He ordered it mixed four parts water to one and split into two servings a day. He let the men buy lime and sugar to make it taste better. And he had it mixed openly, in a cask kept for the purpose, so nobody could think they'd been shorted (Oxford, SPIRITS IN THE MILITARY, pdf 1868–1869). That's your Nanny: a no with room for their own taste, carried out where everyone can see it's fair.

There are three fences on your pitch:
- **"The crew grumbled"** isn't in any source I have (card L1). Leave it out.
- **The nickname** comes from his grogram coat only "reportedly", and Oxford uses that word both times, so it's legend-grade: "the story goes".
- **"Turned out right"** has to be scoped. The scurvy benefit of the lime is Oxford's later observation, not Vernon's reason (pdf 1869). Lime at sea is also *Not Only the Way*'s lineage.

A true coda if you want one: when the last ration was issued, on 31 July 1970, sailors held mock burials and wore black armbands (Oxford, RUM, NAVY, pdf 1702). "The rule became the thing they mourned" is our reading, so it needs a signpost.
