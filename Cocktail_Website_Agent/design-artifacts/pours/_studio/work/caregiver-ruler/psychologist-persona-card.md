# Persona card: The Nanny (caregiver-ruler)

Wren, round 1. Sources: persona.py (essence "tough love, has your best interest at heart"; goal Service; fear Selfishness; driver Provide structure; Ruler fears chaos, vulnerability, failure), siblings, the Craftsman draft.

## Core truth
They say the no that nobody else will say, because they care more about how someone turns out than about being liked tonight. And the whole point of their rules is the day nobody needs them any more.

## Shows / hides
- **Shows:** firmness. The plan, the curfew-for-grown-ups ("text me when you're home"), the budget kept, the "one more and then we go", the honest answer nobody asked for. Calm in a mess. Seen as the strict one, sometimes the bossy one.
- **Hides:** that the no costs them. They replay it afterwards. They'd love to be the easy one, the fun one, just once, and they can't, because they can see where the easy yes leads. And the quiet softness after: the check-in the next morning, the favour done without comment.

## Hidden fear → hidden gift
- **Fear:** that stepping back is selfish (the Caregiver's fear), and that without someone holding the line it all falls apart (the Ruler's chaos). Under both: being resented by the people they're doing it for.
- **Gift:** structure that makes other people safe enough to relax. People can be careless *because* someone isn't. And the rules are scaffolding: built to be taken down.

## The "real me" line to aim for
- *"Everyone thinks the no comes easily to you. It's the one thing you do for them that costs you most."*
- Alternative, the turn toward the position: *"The rules were always for them. The worry was always yours."*

## Truth vs. legend
The true story, told straight. They respect a record kept honestly and they'd distrust a charming legend. But they'll smile at a story where the rule-maker was grumbled about and turned out right.

## Tone
Warm and direct. The bartender can be a little firm with this one: they respect a straight talker and would find gushing suspicious. A dry smile is welcome (the grumbling crew, the "naughty step" energy), never a joke at their expense. Never make them the villain, never make them a saint.

## Distinct from siblings
- **The Craftsman** (*Not Only the Way*): pride in *how they make things*, a method held for its own sake. The Nanny's order is for *other people's* lives, not their own work. Keep off method pride, "doing it properly", and the "it was nothing" ending.
- **The Rescuer**: arrives in the crisis. The Nanny builds the habits so there isn't one. Rescue is a moment; the Nanny is a routine.
- **The Reassurer / the Angel / the Understanding Spouse**: soothe, accept, say it'll be fine. The Nanny says what has to change. Comfort is not their first move.
- **The Doctor**: authority from knowledge, a diagnosis. The Nanny's authority is from presence and consistency: they're still there tomorrow.
- **The Auteur** (other family, *Making the Calls*): control over their own work, "rather be blamed for all of it". Avoid that "you'd rather X than Y" frame entirely.

## Traps
- **Bossy / control freak / "tough love" as a label.** Diagnosis and cliché. Show the scene, never the word.
- **Assuming children or parenthood.** The name and Supernanny pull toward it. Keep scenes adult and universal (friends, a trip, a team, a household). No gendered anything.
- **"You deserve to be looked after too"**: that's *Four Shares*' "You're allowed to be looked after too". Off limits.
- **Making the drink scold the guest** or the reading lecture them. The bartender doesn't impose rules on the Nanny.
- **Chaos in the glass.** The Ruler fears chaos: the drink should be orderly, exact, repeatable. Nothing "whatever you like", no free-for-all garnish.
- **The Craftsman's lime-cordial-for-sea-voyages lineage**: already used as an ingredient clause. If our story goes to sea, the angle has to be different (the order, not the ingredient).

## Story directions for Hester (the mirror to look for)
A rule imposed for someone's good, resented at the time, proved right, and ideally named after the one who imposed it.
1. **Admiral Vernon and grog (1740):** ordered the sailors' rum ration watered down for their own good; the crew grumbled, and the drink carries his nickname to this day. Need: the order, its stated reason, the nickname's origin, legends vs record.
2. **Runner-up: house rules in a bar** (e.g. Sasha Petraske's Milk & Honey rules, posted to keep the room safe and civil). Watch for gendered rules; only if sourced and kindly told.
