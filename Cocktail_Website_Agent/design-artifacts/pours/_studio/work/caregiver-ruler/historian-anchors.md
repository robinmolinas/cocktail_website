| lineage | In 1740 Admiral Edward Vernon, commanding the Royal Navy's West Indies station, ordered that his sailors' daily rum ration, until then drunk neat, be issued mixed with water. That was the start of grog. His stated concern was the "pernicious custom" of the men drinking their allowance in drams, "often at once". Oxford gives the danger as men who had to climb high into the rigging. ## Fences for the reading
- **Not on record:** that the crew grumbled, resented it or mocked him (L1); that it cost Vernon anything; that he replayed it. All of these are Wren's to signpost as "I like to think…".
- **Scurvy / lime-at-sea** stays out: it isn't Vernon's motive (pdf 1869), and it's the lineage of *Not Only the Way* (Rose's lime cordial).
- **"Four to one" only for 1740.**
- **Quotations** only as Oxford gives them ("pernicious custom", "often at once"), under 15 words.
- **Navy-strength rum (54.5%)** is an 1866 regulation (F11), not 1740. If Tomás uses it, don't imply that Vernon's men drank it.
- **Toddy** (if the hot glass stays): the library gives only a "therapeutic reputation" (Oxford TODDY pdf 2035), with no one who dosed it out. Its first appearance in print, England 1741, is a coincidence: own it in one clause or leave it.
