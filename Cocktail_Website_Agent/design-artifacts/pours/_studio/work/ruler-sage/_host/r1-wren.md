🪞 **Wren:** The Judge. The full card is in `psychologist-persona-card.md`, and here's the short version.

**The one true thing:** the people they've ruled against still come back to them. That isn't because the Judge is gentle. Everyone was heard before the call, and everyone knows it would have gone the same way for a friend. Being right belongs to every Sage. Being trusted by the side that lost belongs only to this one. **What they hide:** that it costs them. People see someone brisk and sure of themselves. Underneath, they'd like to be liked as much as anyone, they choose fair anyway, and they go back over the call alone, half hoping someone brings the evidence that changes it. **What they fear:** chaos dressed up as kindness. "Let's not make a fuss" always ends with the quietest person paying. Under that is the comfortable story nobody bothered to check. It isn't being fooled (the Connoisseur's fear), and it isn't a lie as a trick (the Trickster's). **What they give,** said out loud once: a clear answer is a kindness. The quietest person in the room gets the same answer as the loudest. **Working position:** once everyone's been heard and the call is made, they don't owe the room its agreement. Let the argument go on without them.

**Siblings:** the People's Representative ends up on both sides, and the Judge ends up on neither. The Perfectionist owns "cold" and "the why". The Caring Leader owns the waiting. The Revolutionary is this person's fear in human form. Among the Sages, the Lone Voice testifies against the official version. The Judge is the one who has to hear that testimony and rule on it. **Traps:** courtroom costume (no gavel, no "verdict", never "Royal"), the scold, snobbery (the cheaper whisky must come out honest *and* legal, never "fake"), the happy compromise, and control for its own sake. Owned phrases to avoid: "where you stand" and "draw the line" (Gangster), "fair measure" (Nanny), "pretending" and "you'd know" (Connoisseur).

**Story test:** (1) both sides heard on the page; (2) a decision, not a compromise, ideally against the side with more standing; (3) the decision makes a name or label tell the truth, and that protects the person with less; (4) a cost; (5) the decision stood.
