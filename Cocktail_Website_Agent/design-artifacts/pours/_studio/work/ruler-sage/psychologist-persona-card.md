# Persona card: The Judge (ruler-sage)

Wren, round 1, 2026-10-01. Sources: `persona.py ruler-sage` (essence, story, fears, drivers, imagery), the ten Ruler siblings and the eleven Sages in the registry and plans, Oxford WHISKY, GRAIN (pdf 2149) and ADULTERATION (pdf 68–70), read whole this session.

## The person in one line
They hear everyone out, then say the thing nobody in the room wanted said, and they'll say it against their own side if that's where it lands.

## The one true thing (no sibling shares it)
**The people they've ruled against still come back to them.** Not because the Judge is gentle, but because everyone was heard before the call was made, and everyone knows the call would have gone the same way for a friend. Trust earned by being unbuyable. Being right is the Sage's; being trusted by the losing side is only this one's.

## What they hide
That it costs them. People see brisk, certain, unbothered ("without apology", the persona's own words). Underneath, they'd like to be liked as much as anyone, and they choose fair anyway, every time, knowing what it'll cost in warmth. They also go back over the call afterwards, alone, looking for the thing they missed. They'd change their mind for one good piece of evidence, and part of them hopes someone brings it.

## What they fear
**Chaos dressed up as kindness.** The "let's not make a fuss", the "it's fine", the vague answer that keeps the evening pleasant. They know who pays for it: never the one saying it, always the quietest person there. And under that, the Sage fear in this one's shape: **the comfortable story nobody bothered to check**. Not being fooled themselves (the Connoisseur), not lies as craft (the Trickster): a falsehood left standing because checking would have been awkward.

## What they give (say it out loud once, Robin's rule)
**A clear answer is a kindness.** Because they'll say it, the quietest person in the room gets the same answer as the loudest. People know what's true and what's expected of them, and nobody has to guess. Their rules aren't for control's sake: a plain rule protects the ones who can't argue.

## The position (earned, never opened with)
Working direction: **once everyone has been heard and the call is made, they don't owe the room its agreement.** The argument can go on without them. Fairness was the hearing; it was never everyone being pleased. In their interest: rest, and the end of re-trying every case at night. (Kind: encouragement with a hard edge.) Alternatives held: "being fair doesn't mean you don't have a side"; "let someone check your call".

Sage cousins to keep apart: the Connoisseur (truth lovingly kept; "nothing pretending, you'd know"), the Doctor ("since when?", questioning), the Genius (waits years for the real answer), the Lone Voice (testifies alone against the official version: the Judge is the one who has to hear that testimony and rule), the Scientist (measures where others guess). Also the Adolescent (*smells a fake*) and the Gangster (*where you stand*, *draw the line*).

## Traps
- **Courtroom costume.** No gavels, juries, "verdict", "the court" or law as the guest's job. "Royal" never appears (archetype pun). The name "The Judge" is the only judge in the text.
- **The scold.** Judge Judy's briskness must not become harshness or a lecture. The Perfectionist owns "cold".
- **Snobbery.** A story about what's "real" whisky can easily read as "the cheap stuff is fake". It must read the other way: the ruling kept the cheaper whisky honest *and* legal.
- **The happy compromise.** Not "both sides got something" (the People's Representative). It was a decision, and one side lost the argument it cared about.
- **Control for its own sake.** Rules here protect people; never "you like being in charge".
- **Health framing.** Methanol and lead are one clause at most, never a cure-or-poison story.
- **Owned words to avoid:** "where you stand", "draw the line" (Gangster), "the why" (Perfectionist), "give your answer" (Caring Leader), "fair measure" (Nanny), "pretending", "you'd know" (Connoisseur), "smell a fake" (Adolescent), "both sides" in the title block (*Where You Stand*'s epigraph, *One Table*), "be liked" framed as "picked first by no one" (One Next Door), "ruled against" appears once in *Got You*: check the sentence before using it.

## My story test (what any lead must show)
1. **Both sides heard**, on the page (testimony, argument, evidence from each).
2. **A decision**, not a compromise, ideally against the side with more standing or the side the decider would be expected to favour.
3. **The decision makes something tell the truth** (a name, a label, a measure) and that truth **protects the person with less**.
4. **A cost:** someone stayed unhappy, the argument went on, the decider was resented.
5. **The decision stood.**
Out: a lone stand against officialdom (Lone Voice), questioning a plan (Doctor), a lovingly kept truth (Connoisseur), a clever deception (Trickster).

## Verdict on the plan's lead (1908 commission, grain whisky)
**Accept, with one claim to check before anything rests on it.** Re-read whole: pdf 2149 gives limbs 2–5 (pot-still makers said the new spirit wasn't whisky; "Legally, it was—as long as you called it 'grain whisky'"; "(legally, anyway)" is the cost; it stands as a category today). Limb 1 is on witness pages (Booth's pdf 317, the Demerara distiller pdf 1697, a genever maker in the *Minutes of Evidence*, pdf 875). Limb 3 is on pdf 70 (definitions and labelling, blending not outlawed, column-still spirit may be called whisky) and pdf 2149 (cheap, for urban industrial workers, in the 1830s).

**Flag for Hester:** pdf 2149's condition ("as long as you called it 'grain whisky,' and not just plain 'whisky'") sits awkwardly beside pdf 70 ("without… preventing column-still spirit from being labeled as whisky"), and as I remember it the commission's final report (1909, not the 1908 interim) defined whisky without regard to the still. I may be wrong; that's what her web visit is for. If the condition doesn't hold, the story still stands on pdf 70: they heard both sides and ruled that the new spirit was whisky, against the older makers, with labels bound to say what was in them. Either way, "1908" in guest text should match what the page dates (the commission and its interim report).

**Missing:** a human face. A commission is hard to see yourself in. Hester, can any page or one web visit give the chair, or a witness from each side, without inventing motive? If not, the reading leans on the two sides and the cheaper bottle, and that can work.
