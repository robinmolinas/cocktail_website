## Story: ruled. Gerald Murphy, and the card is the centre of it.
The mirror is sharper now. The world kept the charm: the man Fitzgerald partly drew Dick Diver from, and the phrase "just the juice of a few flowers", which sounds like a host's joke. The card shows the maker behind the charm: a real recipe in his own hand, measured to the half-ounce. For the Muse, the thing people quote is the idea of them, and the work behind it is theirs, exact, and hardly anyone asks to see it.

**Anchors: at most four, so history stays under half.**
1. The legend we grant first: partly the model for Dick Diver (F12, "partly").
2. The phrase "just the juice of a few flowers" (F1), which people take as charm.
3. The card, in his hand, to the half-ounce (F3).
4. The maker: fourteen paintings, seven survive; *Cocktail*, the lid alone four months (F7, F8).
Plus one clause, only if it helps the drink: "invented by me as were a great many other good things", said of **the Bailey**, never of the card (F2, C2).
To the dossier: Barry's "priest saying Mass" (F5) (one legend is enough, and it's a performance), the museum's reading of the lid (F9), the notebook (F11: a taken motif).

## Drink: the card, plus one addition (the Bailey's mint), because of what the mint says
- **Base: the card as written.** 1 oz orange, 1 oz grapefruit, ½ oz lime, ½ oz lemon, 1 oz gin, shaken; the rim wet with lemon and dipped in coarse sugar. It's his hand, his measures and his sweetener. The glass holds the maker, which the story test asks for.
- **One addition: fresh mint, shaken and strained out, from the Bailey.** This isn't decoration. It takes the one cocktail he claimed in writing ("invented by me") into the drink written under the phrase people quote as a joke. The claim goes into the glass, and that's the Muse's position in miniature: put your name on it. Robin's rule is to tie the twist to the story in words, so the reading says it in one clause ("the mint comes from the Bailey, the one he wrote that he'd invented").
- **Conditions:**
  - It's never "his recipe". It's "two of his recipes, and joining them is my idea".
  - Mint is a taste, not a julep: no crushed ice, no sprig bouquet on top (the Romantic's).
  - The sugar rim stays in the method only, never in the name or a motif (*Half a Rim*, *Beside the First*).
  - Don't name a gin (the card names none).
  - The grapefruit note on medicines goes in Checks.
- **Numbers are Tomás's.** If the card reads light or sharp, the Checks say so, and the rim is his answer to it. If a sweetener inside the drink is needed, it's one change, named as ours, and not honey (the Gold Rush's).
- If the mint fails on the numbers or the taste, pour the card alone and drop the Bailey clause from the reading. The card still carries the whole story.

## Early person-only drafts (not yet a reading; untested)
**whoYouAre v0**
Friends show you things first: the song before it has words, the first chapter, the dish they've never cooked for anyone else. Something in the way you pay attention makes them want to finish it, and they usually do. People are braver with their own work when you're around, and that's a rare thing to give. Sometimes you even turn up in it: a line in a song, a character who talks the way you talk. You make things too. You just don't often say so, because being someone's reason feels safer than being judged on your own. People have made beautiful things out of you. Very few have asked what you're making.

**Tagline candidates:** "Around you, people finish things." · "People make their best things around you." · "You're in half the things your friends have made."
**Epigraph candidate (cold, ≤16 words, no bare "he"):** "Its name sounds like a joke. The handwritten card gives it to the half-ounce."
