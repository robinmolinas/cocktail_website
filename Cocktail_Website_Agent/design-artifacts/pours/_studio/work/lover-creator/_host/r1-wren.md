🪞 **Wren:** Round 1. Here's who the Muse is. My card is `_studio/work/lover-creator/psychologist-persona-card.md`.

**The one true thing.** People have made beautiful things out of this person, and very few asked what this person was making. Friends pick the guitar up again around them and finish the thing they'd dropped. The Muse makes things too. But the version of them that gets around is the one inside other people's work: the friend in the song, the face in the story, "you were the reason". Being someone's reason is lovely. Being only that gets lonely.

**What they hide, fear and give.**
- **Hide:** they make things too, and they'd like to be asked about it. My own read, and it needs saying gently: being the spark is safer than being the maker, because nobody judges work you never show. So their name stays off the cover partly by their own choice.
- **Fear:** being loved as an idea (the portrait, the character) and never known as the person whose hands are in the work. They can be admired by everyone and still be alone.
- **Give:** attention that tells someone their idea is worth finishing. Robin's rule for the caregiver batch applies here: we say that out loud once.

**Siblings.** The Sex Symbol owns "wanted by everyone, known by very few", and the Romantic owns "surrounded and still unseen". The Muse is neither desired nor unseen. The Muse is *used as material*, seen clearly but through someone else's version. The Visionary owns "people want to help build it". The Muse's friends make *their own* things, so we never say "build on". The Geisha owns hosting and getting the room ready.

**Story test.** Every limb has to pass:
1. On the record, others made work from this person or because of them.
2. On the record, this person made work of their own.
3. The world remembers them through someone else's version. We grant that first, then correct it.
4. Their own work survives under their own name, ideally claimed in their own words.
5. The drink comes from their own hand.

Out: looks, contested attributions, "the woman behind the man", a tragedy as the reason they stopped, and the famous friend taking over the story.

**Verdict on Gerald Murphy: accept.** I read the *Gastronomica* page myself.
- He's partly the model for Dick Diver.
- He exhibited at the Salon des Indépendants from 1923, and seven of his fourteen paintings survive.
- The cigar-box lid in *Cocktail* alone took him four months to paint.
- His 1939 letter to Woollcott has the line I'll fight for. He called a cocktail "invented by me as were a great many other good things." A man the world knew as someone's character says, plainly: I made this.

**Hester, one correction.** Gin, grapefruit, fresh lime, fresh mint and "a great deal" of ice is the recipe he called **a Bailey**. On the page, "just the juice of a few flowers" is what he called his cocktails in general. The "Juice of a Few Flowers" recipe itself appears only in a photo caption, with no ingredients written out. The plan mixes the two up. The reading can't give the flowers drink those ingredients unless you find them on a page.
