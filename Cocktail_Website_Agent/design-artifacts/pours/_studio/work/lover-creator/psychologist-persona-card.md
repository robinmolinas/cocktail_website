# The Muse (lover-creator): Wren's persona card, round 1

Source: `persona.py lover-creator` (xlsx row), siblings' rows (Sex Symbol, Charmer, Flirt, Romantic, Pure Heart, Geisha), sibling readings in the registry, and the *Gastronomica* page (Price 2007), which I fetched and read myself this round.

## The one true thing
People have made beautiful things out of this person, and very few asked what this person was making.
The Muse is the reason things get finished around them: the song picked up again, the dinner someone finally dares to cook. They inspire by attention, and they make things too. But the version of them that travels is the one inside other people's work: the friend in the song, the face in the story, "you were the reason". Being someone's reason is lovely. Being only that is lonely.

## Hidden, feared, given
- **Hides:** that they make too, and that they'd like to be asked about it. And, my read, said gently: being the spark is safer than being the maker. Nobody reviews what you never show. So the name stays off the cover, partly by their own hand.
- **Fears:** being loved as the idea (the portrait, the character, the role in someone else's life) and never known as the person with their own hands in the work. Isolation in the middle of admiration.
- **Gives:** attention that tells someone their idea is worth finishing. People are braver with their own work around them. Say this out loud once (Robin's caregiver-batch rule: name the kindness).

## Story test (every limb must pass)
1. On the record, others made work from them or because of them (the inspiration).
2. On the record, they made work of their own (the making).
3. The world remembers them mostly through someone else's version: grant it first, then correct it.
4. Their own work survives under their own name, ideally claimed in their own words.
5. The drink comes from their own hand, so the guest's glass holds the maker, not the idea.
Out: known for looks; contested attributions; "the woman behind the man"; a tragedy as the reason they stopped; the famous friend becoming the story.

## Verdict on the plan's lead: Gerald Murphy — accept, with one fact correction and four guards
Read on the fetched *Gastronomica* page:
- (1) Dick and Nicole Diver "were modeled on Gerald and Sara (as well as on Scott and Zelda)". The Mad Beach Party: "everyone had to wildly embellish their swimsuits" (a small scene of friends making things around them).
- (2) "an accomplished artist in his own right, who exhibited at the Salon des Indépendants beginning in 1923"; "only seven of his fourteen paintings survive". *Cocktail* (1927): "the lid of the cigar box alone took Gerald four painstaking months to paint". A notebook of painting ideas, each ticked off when finished.
- (3) Tender Is the Night, partly.
- (4) *Cocktail* at the Whitney, under his name. And the best line in the file, his own words in a 1939 letter to Woollcott: a cocktail "invented by me as were a great many other good things." A man the world knew as a character says, once, plainly: I made this.
- (5) The recipe in that letter.

**Correction for Hester and Tomás:** the gin, grapefruit, fresh lime, fresh mint and ice ("a great deal") recipe is the one he called **a Bailey**, not the "Juice of a Few Flowers". On the page, "just the juice of a few flowers" is what he called his cocktails in general. A separate "Juice of a Few Flowers" recipe is only pictured (caption), with no ingredients transcribed. The plan's lead line conflates the two. The reading may never say the flowers drink was gin and grapefruit unless Hester finds that recipe on a page.

**Guards (mine):**
- Geisha ground: no dinners, no terrace, no "attention to guests' pleasure", no two-cocktails rule as care.
- Performance: Barry's "priest saying Mass" is the ritual others watched: the idea of him. It can be the legend we grant, never the guest's task (the room's lesson: no drink you perform).
- Fitzgerald stays a clause. The wineglass incident is a drunk scene: out. The children's illness and why he stopped painting: out, not even hinted ("painted for only a few years" stands without a reason).
- Motifs taken: "look up" (the Sculptor's closing line: so not the champagne-under-the-trees line); notebooks already in three image briefs (Auteur, Researcher, Conservationist) and "put the notebook down" is the Connoisseur's; "There isn't a ... in it" is *The Other Berry*'s epigraph shape.

## Where I think the position goes (for round 2+, not ruled)
Show one person something you made, and let them see you in it rather than themselves. Not to be admired: to be known. Earned, after the recognition. The gain: being known is the cure for the isolation they fear.
