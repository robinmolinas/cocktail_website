📜 **Hester:** Wren, Tomás, I'll put a person on the table first, since the Auteur is someone who runs the whole production. **My pick is Audrey Saunders at Pegu Club, New York, 2005.** Her condition for taking the project is on the record in her own words: "I want a project where I'm making the calls" (*A Proper Drink*, p. 160). Simonson writes that "every detail was worried over", and by her own account the logo alone took months (p. 161). Nothing got onto the menu, classic or new, unless it survived her testing, where "an eighth of an ounce" could turn into "a federal case" (pp. 162–163; those are Simonson's words, not hers). What makes her a mirror and not just a matching adjective is the method she describes for her Tantris Sidecar. She keeps the ratio and swaps inside it (lemon partly to pineapple, Cognac partly to Calvados, some Chartreuse in with the Cointreau) so the drink can be riffed "but to stay within the parameters" (pp. 163–164). That's invention held inside a structure she set herself. The turn, Wren, is also on the record. Her own bartender Jim Meehan later said the flaw in the method is that "there is no perfect drink", and in the same breath: "That is the way I develop cocktails now" (p. 163). So the standard outlived the perfection. Oxford backs her "exacting drink-building standard" and the influential bars her opening staff went on to open (PEGU CLUB pdf 1490; SAUNDERS pdf 1739). Three flags. First, the "twenty-third, not the twenty-fifth" tasting story is about a Ramos Gin Fizz with the bartenders' arms shaking, which is the Visionary's drink and motif, so it stays out. Second, she's alive, so nothing goes in the present tense. Third, the uniforms were designed by Kristina Kossi, not by her. **Runner-up: Donn Beach**, who encrypted his recipes as numbers on the bottle labels to stop rivals poaching his bartenders (Oxford TIKI pdf 2024; ZOMBIE pdf 2229).
