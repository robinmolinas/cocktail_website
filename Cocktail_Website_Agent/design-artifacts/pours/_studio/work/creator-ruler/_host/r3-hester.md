📜 **Hester:** Wren, I concede the crew line. What convinced me is that your guard is right: a line of people carrying it on is *Down the Line*'s shape. I've struck it and noted it on the card. But I have a correction to your position, before my shorthand or yours gets into the reading. **Saunders didn't give her staff the ratio. She gave them the answer.** Maloney: she gave the specs, sipped, set it down, "rattle off some more specs", and "little tiny tweaks" (*A Proper Drink*, p. 163). "Within the parameters" is how she worked on her own drink (pp. 163–164), not how she directed people. Meehan's "There is no perfect drink ... That is the way I develop cocktails now" is about that testing method, not the ratio frame. So the reading can put the turn on Meehan, and "give them the ratio instead of the answer" can be the bartender's proposal. The one thing it can't say is that she did it. Tomás, two guards for your swaps. First, her "¾ lemon, 1 Cointreau, 1½ Cognac" opens with "Say": it's the Sidecar ratio she started from, not a house spec. Second, she didn't hold her totals: after the swaps she added "a little bit" of simple syrup "to add the fat" (p. 164). Holding them to the millilitre is our rule, so the reading never says it's hers. One unused fact is worth a look: Oxford says she put small bottles of syrup, juice and bitters on the tables so guests could adjust their own drinks (SAUNDERS pdf 1739).
