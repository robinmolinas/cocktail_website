# Persona card: The Auteur (creator-ruler)

Wren, round 1. Source: persona.py creator-ruler (both archetypes' fears and tone), the nine authored Creator siblings, my own note in the Visionary room ("the name belongs to the Auteur"; "the Auteur would want it signed, not outliving them").

## Core truth
The Auteur doesn't want to make something good. They want to make something that is *theirs all the way through*, and they'll carry every piece of it, credit and blame, rather than let one part go ordinary in someone else's hands.

## Shows / hides
- **Shows:** certainty. The one who decides, says "that's the one", holds everyone to it, notices the wrong detail before anyone else does. People follow them and are a little afraid of disappointing them.
- **Hides:** that the certainty is often a choice, not a feeling. They've said "that's the one" when they weren't sure, because somebody had to. And they've quietly redone someone else's part late at night, then said nothing in the morning.

## Hidden fear → hidden gift
- **Fear (both archetypes together):** not failure as such. It's *ordinary with their name on it*. Creator fears mediocrity; Ruler fears chaos and vulnerability. The meeting point: if they let one thing slide, the whole becomes average, and everyone will see it was theirs. Showing doubt feels like letting the whole thing wobble.
- **Gift:** because they hold all of it, the people around them get to be part of something better than they'd make alone, and get to stop wondering. Their certainty is something they give other people, at their own cost.

## The "real me" line to aim for
- Lead: *"You'd rather be blamed for all of it than praised for part of it."* (ownership, the signature: only this personality.)
- Hidden side, for whoYouAre: *"You've said 'that's the one' when you weren't sure, because someone had to."*

## Truth vs. legend
The true story, precise to the detail, and the maker's own version of it. They'd respect someone who authored their own image, but they'd catch a sloppy fact instantly. Legends are fine only when named as legends. Nothing vague.

## Tone
Confident, decisive, short. The bartender sounds sure: fewer hedges than usual, no gushing. A dry smile is allowed about the control (the midnight redo), never a joke at their expense. Recognition, never praise: the xlsx is all compliments ("masterful", "commands respect"); none of it goes in.

## Distinct from siblings
- **The Visionary** (*Down the Line*): the dream is too big for one person, others finish it. The Auteur is the opposite: every frame is theirs, others execute it exactly. Visionary's gift = holding out for the full size; Auteur's = holding out for every piece. The Visionary's position was "write it down so someone can carry on, then give it to them". **Ours must not be a handing-on.**
- **The Sculptor** (*Quite Alive*): makes it for one person, to measure. The Auteur makes it for the work itself, and directs other people to make it.
- **The Obsessed** (*Rosetta*): in love with the thing. The Auteur is in charge of the thing. **The Minimalist**: takes away. The Auteur takes over.
- The distinctive element is the *other people*: the Auteur is the only Creator who directs a crew. Scenes should have people in them.

## Traps
- Flattery (the xlsx's whole vocabulary). Recognise what they do, not how great they are.
- "Control freak", or any diagnosis. Insult = misreading.
- Job labels and film puns: no "director", no "final cut", no "take two", no "auteur" in the text.
- A position that says "let go" or "hand it over" (Visionary's; and it asks them to become someone else).
- A chaotic or vague drink (Ruler fears chaos): every element should have a reason, measures exact, reproducible. And no drink to perform.
- A story where the maker loses everything and the lesson is "control fails": true or not, it tells the Auteur their fear comes true without giving them anything.

## Position (Ryu), first thought
Affirm the ownership, then one hard truth in their interest: the midnight redo. *Send it back instead of fixing it yourself: tell them what you see.* They keep every decision; they stop carrying it alone; the people around them get to see the whole picture too. Not a handing-on: a showing.

## Story lead for Hester (hers to verify or reject)
**Donn Beach** (Oxford Companion, pdf 241–242): born Ernest Raymond Beaumont-Gantt; built Don the Beachcomber's (1934) as a whole world, and took the bar's name as his own. "Donn Beach as he liked to be portrayed" (menu caption). Nationally imitated by 1937. To verify (Berry, secondary): the coded recipes and unlabelled bottles his own bartenders couldn't read, and the legal name change. The mirror: a man who signed his life with his own creation, and kept the whole picture where only he could see it. Caution: the ending (living in Trader Vic's shadow) is his fear come true; if we use it, the reading has to turn it.
