## 3. Swap test
- **Sculptor (Quite Alive):** made for one person, for what they can't say. Nothing here is for a person. The showing-and-watching-their-face beat is about being felt *with*, not about a gift for them. Theirs: "the quiet hope… that they'll understand what it was saying." **Close.** The difference: the Sculptor hopes the person understands *them*; the Obsessed hopes the person feels *the thing*. I've kept "felt it too" (the thing) and never "understand you". Flag for the room.
- **Experimentalist (Off-Label):** never the same twice, walks on. Ours goes back to one thing. Their contagion gift is a habit ("let me try something"); ours is a feeling (caring more). Distinct, but both are "people near you start to…". Flag, not fail.
- **Visionary:** sees the finished thing. Ours has no finish line in view. Clear.
- **Photographer:** "a second look at things they thought they knew." Ours is "notice something you missed", about their own thing, not the world's. Clear.
- **Old Master (unauthored):** no mastery, practice or reputation. Breaux is an outsider. Protected.
- **Muse (lover-creator, unauthored):** their love points at people. Ours points at the thing. Protected.
- **Trickster (No Accident):** "the truth is at the bottom of the glass, which is exactly where you would hide it." My first y3 ended "Careful on top, all that red underneath", the same picture. Caught and changed before the room saw it: "The feeling doesn't spoil the careful part. It's what gives it colour." The red is brought up, not hidden, and the line sets up y4. Clear.

## 4. Punch check
- **Tagline** *"Other people like things. You fall for them."*: stops a scroll, positive, about the person. Strong.
- **whoYouAre opening** *"There's one thing you keep going back to."*: the quietest line. It works because the list right after it lands. Weakest of the three.
- **Last line** *"…that's the part you've been waiting for them to feel."*: closes the loop with "nice", and the position is in their hands. Holds.
- **Epigraph cold:** *"The French word for the cloud in this glass also means disreputable."* Makes sense alone, teases (what cloud? why disreputable?), about the drink, shares no word with the tagline. Pass.

## 5. Rule check
- The proposal (y4) points to life: showing someone the thing they love. It doesn't assume the drink is in hand.
- Romance signposted: "He has always said he did it to set right an injustice. I believe him. I also don't think…" It's clearly mine.
- Not clever for its own sake: the louche line is the reading's one flourish, and it carries the person (blamed and beautiful under one name).
- History at about half. At the limit; if Hester's audit adds a clause, something in y2 comes out.
- "his own" isn't used for any bottle.

## Verdict
**Ready, pending Hester's fact audit and the room's view on one flag** (the Sculptor's "understand" beat). I'd put my name to the whoYouAre as it stands.
