## Guards (for Wren's reading and the audit)
- **Motive:** justice on record, not love. Any "fell for", "in love with" about Breaux is signposted ("I like to think", "the way I read it").
- **The two bottles are undated:** never "before the ban" / "pre-ban" (X2). Not his first taste (X1). "He says", not "he has always said" (X3).
- **No ban year** (1912 Oxford / 1915 Simonson). "Before the ban" only of absinthe in general, never of the two bottles (X2).
- **"He ended the ban"**: never. The 2007 US change was with Viridian's lawyers, and absinthe had technically been legal at low thujone for years. Dossier only.
- **Eiffel** single-source: out. **Hallucinations/madness**: only as the colleague's words, never as fact.
- **"Anise and fennel"** for absinthe as a style (Oxford canonical recipe). Not for a named bottle.
- **"Five to one"** is the tradition; ours is 4:1 plus syrup. Don't call our drip "the traditional ratio".
- **"More than ten years"** = 1993 → 2004 (eleven). Not "a decade" as if exact; not "twenty".
- **"Obsession"** is Simonson's word for Breaux. Never label the guest "obsessive" (Wren's trap).
- **Colleague's line** paraphrased, not quoted as his exact words in the pour.
- **The bottle (round 4):** Lucid, 62% ABV (card F15). Say "the absinthe made to Breaux's recipe" or "that Breaux developed", never "his own" or "his brand" (Viridian's, Hood River's since 2013). No "first legal absinthe" (brand framing; implies a ban year).
- **History ≤ half:** lineage + turn are the core; continuation can be one clause.
