# Mirror hunt: creator-lover, The Obsessed (Hester, round 1)

Persona: consumed by the art; the work is an extension of the soul; making and loving indistinguishable (Shakespeare in Love). Fear: the status quo, mediocrity; Lover's fear: isolation.

## A. Ted Breaux and absinthe (person + ingredient) — my pick
**Story.** In 1993 a petroleum research chemist heard one throwaway line about "the green liqueur" and couldn't let it go: he read one book three times, wrote to its author, tried to distil it himself. Then he got two sealed pre-ban bottles, tasted them, and said plainly his own was nowhere near. He chased that taste to a distillery in France; Simonson's word for it is "obsession".
**Why it mirrors.** Falling for something you've only read about, and building your life towards it. And the humility inside the obsession: he tasted the real thing and admitted his was worse, then kept going. The status quo he refused was the substitute everyone else had settled for. The ingredient carries the Lover: the louche, the word for its beauty and its bad name at once (Oxford), and a good louche is itself a test of craft.
**Sources.** *A Proper Drink* pp. 244–245 (his account, 2014 interview); Oxford ABSINTHE pdf 48–51, LOUCHE pdf 1195, ABSINTHE DRIP pdf 52. Card: `fact-cards/ted-breaux-absinthe.md` (F1–F12, C1–C6).
**Risks.** His motive on the record is an injustice to set right, not love: romance must be signposted. US ban year contested (1912 Oxford / 1915 Simonson): no year. "Eiffel's distillery" single-source. Never the hallucination myth. Living person.

## B. Stanislav Vadrna (person) — runner-up
**Story.** A Slovak bartender who took a five-hour train to Prague every weekend to barback, translated English cocktail articles into Slovak, and haunted internet cafés for news of London and New York. In 2005 he had a heart attack crossing Times Square after a night of drinking, told nobody, still went to Gary Regan's symposium, and put Regan down as his emergency contact. Ten days with Kazuo Uyeda in Tokyo were "everything".
**Why it mirrors.** Obsession and connection in one detail: the one name he wrote down was the man whose craft he loved.
**Sources.** *A Proper Drink* pp. 206–208. Not carded yet.
**Risks.** A living person's medical event: I'd keep it out of a guest's reading. Origin is the film *Cocktail* (comic). The drink isn't his (no Vadrna drink on record), so the story can't live in the glass.
