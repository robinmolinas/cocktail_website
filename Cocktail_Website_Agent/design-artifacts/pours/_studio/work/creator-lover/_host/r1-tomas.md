🍸 **Tomás:** Wren, the person is yours, but I'll put a spark on the bar before we all start guessing. There's a line in *Liquid Intelligence* (pdf 188) about caramelising sugar in a pan for a hot drink: "Let it get dark but not too dark." The *Flavor Matrix* on caramel (pdf 72) says why. The darker it goes, the more flavour it has, until the sugar burns and turns bitter and acrid. That's the Obsessed in a pan: you keep pushing because more devotion means more flavour, and one second past it the thing is ruined. The drink is a riff on Arnold's pan-style poker drinks (pdf 191). You take 12 g of sugar to dark brown, light 30 ml of cognac in the pan, then put the fire out with 60 ml of red wine and 90 ml of hot water. Add 2 dashes of orange bitters and serve it in a teacup, with orange peel squeezed over the top. The colour card is in the glass: deep red wine, burnt orange, the fire. It's also the first hot drink in the registry, and six of the nine pours so far are the daiquiri family. The numbers: 11.5% / 6.41 g / 0.17%, all in range, veto-free. My first tries had 45 ml of cognac and came out too strong at 15.3%. Arnold's own ale version reads OUT on our toddy ranges and has gluten in it. Two things balance.py can't model: the flame burns off some alcohol, and caramel tastes less sweet than plain sugar. Both make the real drink weaker and drier than the printout, so I'll test sugar from 10 to 15 g once we commit. The flame is a real edge for a home cook, and the method will have to spell out how to do it safely. Hester, a lead from memory that I haven't checked: "burnt sack", which was hot sugared sherry in Shakespeare's time. Figures and specs are in `work/creator-lover/mixologist-notes.md`.
