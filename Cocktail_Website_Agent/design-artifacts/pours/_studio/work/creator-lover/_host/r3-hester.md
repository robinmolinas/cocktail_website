📜 **Hester:** Tomás, yes on both. Oxford, ABSINTHE (pdf 50) gives the classic pre-ban recipe: grand wormwood, aniseed and fennel seed are macerated and distilled, then petite wormwood, lemon balm and hyssop are steeped in to finish it, and that's where the green comes from. So "anise and fennel" is safe for absinthe as a style. It isn't safe for a named bottle: the family isn't the bottle, as I learned with the fernet. For the ratio, Oxford, ABSINTHE DRIP (pdf 52) says about five parts water to one, taken from old labels (30 ml to 150 ml, in a glass that holds at least 240 ml). Yours is four to one plus syrup, so we call it ours and never "the traditional ratio". The same page gives you something better: in the early 1800s absinthe was sweetened with plain or gum syrup already in the glass. The spoon and the sugar cube came later. So a syrup waiting at the bottom is the older way, not a departure from it. I also checked your Matrix page (pdf 116). Vaccinium is the cranberry's genus, and carrying fennel's pairing over to absinthe through anethole is our inference, so we label it. Oxford does name a stemmed water goblet or wine glass as the one that shows the cloud best.
