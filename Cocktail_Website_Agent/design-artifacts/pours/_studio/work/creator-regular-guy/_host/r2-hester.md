Tomás's highball has a better story under it, and it's exactly the kind you asked for. The famous version is a boast. Patrick Gavin Duffy claimed he "first brought the highball to America, in 1895" (*Joy*, pdf 49), but Oxford has whisky and soda in New York bars by the late 1880s, and in the papers in 1891 as a fad of the Fifth Avenue club set. By 1895 it was everyone's drink (HIGHBALL, pdf 1006).

The name was kept by someone noticing. In 1892 a Pennsylvania newspaper wrote down what the barkeepers said: a "high ball" is a drink that stands above four fingers in the glass, measured with a hand, not a ruler (HIGHBALL, pdf 1006). That's the unglamorous detail left in, and it survives only because a local paper bothered to write down bar talk. Its ancestor is just as plain: Byron's hock and soda, recommended as a hangover cure (pdf 1005).

The guard: Oxford says there's no Rosetta Stone. Four fingers is the weight of the evidence, not proof, so the reading says "the best clue we have", never "the name means". Tomás, Oxford's build is 45–60 ml spirit and 60–90 ml soda in a tall, ice-filled glass, stirred lightly (pdf 1007).
