# Mirror hunt: The Photographer (creator-regular-guy)

Hester, round 1. Persona (persona.py): documents the ordinary, affectionately; life as it is. Fears mediocrity / the status quo (Creator) and being left out (Regular Guy). The need I hunted for: **to be the one who noticed**. The ordinary moment is precious because someone was paying attention.

## A (my pick): Hugo Ensslin, the bartender who recorded what New York actually drank
**Kind:** a person (the one who wrote it down), plus an ingredient (crème de violette).

1. A young German who had trained as a painter and a photographer became a bartender at a Times Square hotel, and in 1916 he wrote down what his New York was drinking, just before Prohibition closed it.
2. The book flopped and he was never famous, but Oxford rates it the most exact record of what was actually drunk on the city's cocktail route; the Savoy Cocktail Book took from it wholesale, and his Aviation survived there without its crème de violette, colourless.
3. When his book resurfaced in 2003 and the violette returned in 2007, the drink got its sky-blue colour back from the original record, not from the famous copy.

**Why it mirrors:** the fear of mediocrity is answered by the ordinary made lasting: he didn't invent (nobody can say he did), he *noticed*, and the noticing is what survived. Belonging: it's a record of everyone's drinks, not his own showpiece.
**Sources:** fact card `_studio/fact-cards/hugo-ensslin-aviation.md` (F1–F13, C1–C5). Oxford ENSSLIN (pdf 728), AVIATION (pdf 175), CRÈME DE VIOLETTE (pdf 601), HAUS ALPENZ (pdf 975); *Imbibe!* pdf 241–243; *Joy* pdf 237. All primary except Imbibe/Joy (secondary).
**Risks:** his suicide (C2) and dates (C1) are contested and stay out. Never "he invented" (F5). "Black-and-white copy / colour restored" is **our metaphor**, signposted. Gin again (third London dry gin) and a sour: menu note only, Tomás's call; the Blue Moon or September Morn are other Ensslin-first drinks.

## B (runner-up): the Boilermaker ("Boilermaker and His Helper")
Oxford, BOILERMAKER (pdf 306–307): a drink that seems too plain to have a history, yet has one: beer with whisky was once an odd order in America; the name that stuck (1930s) was "Boilermaker and His Helper", an industrial metalworker and the extra muscle beside him, shortened by the 1940s.
**Why:** the ordinary worker named in the glass, and a pair, not a hero (Regular Guy). Weaker on the Creator side (no noticing, no making). Not a card yet.

## Foil (dossier only)
Stanley Clisby Arthur, also a photographer by training, founded cocktail history and retouched its evidence (Oxford, ARTHUR, pdf 158). Useful to Wren as the contrast, not for the reading.
