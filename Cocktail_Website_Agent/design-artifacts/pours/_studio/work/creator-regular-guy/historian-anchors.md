| lineage (person) | Hugo Ensslin, a German-born bartender who had trained as a painter and photographer before he came to New York at sixteen, worked the bar of a hotel in Times Square. In 1916 he published *Recipes for Mixed Drinks*. It never caught on, and he was never one of the city's celebrated bartenders. One cocktail historian judges that no other book records as exactly what New York's bars were actually serving just before Prohibition. | He kept an ordinary season, the drinks people actually ordered, in plain detail. | ingredient (the lost colour) | The Aviation as his book gives it has gin, lemon, maraschino and two dashes of crème de violette, which tints it a pale sky blue. A far more famous London cocktail book of 1930 printed it without the violette, and that colourless version is the one that survived and was revived. The liqueur itself fell out of cocktail use by the 1930s and was gone from the US for decades. Why the 1930 book left it out is not on record. | The famous version lost the colour that was really there. Only the plain record had kept it.
