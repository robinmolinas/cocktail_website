**Ceilings (every row, and the reading):**
- Money: "handed over" and "all the cash he made" (never "every bit of money", my own round-2 slip), never "gave" or "gift" (Proper calls it "lending … seed money", p. 96, and him an "investor", p. 113). Never "wages" or "his pay": "all the cash he made at the restaurant" (p. 88). "For months", uncounted.
- He wasn't alone and didn't save the bar: other school friends took on credit-card debt (p. 88); the bar "would not have happened without" the pals, plural (p. 87); a neighbour and DeGroff got it through the first year (p. 89).
- The thirty copies: what he did with them, and why, is not on record. Pride in his friend is our reading, signposted every time. The page itself has Siegal disagreeing with Petraske, and Simonson siding partly with Petraske (p. 93).
- Nothing about what Siegal did or didn't say to Petraske in private, and no "the closest he came" except inside "I like to think".
- Siegal's gloss is "it wasn't a request to invest" (that one sentence), never "never". Petraske's reason for not asking isn't on record: "someone who hadn't asked", never "couldn't bring themselves".
- No year for the Gold Rush (2000 in *PUNCH*, 2001 in Proper: C1). The only dates in the reading, if any: New Year's Eve 1999 (opening), May 2000 (the *Times*); the renovation and the money running out are "the late 1990s", never "1999".
- The name has no hidden meaning, by Siegal's own account (*PUNCH*): never read one into "Gold Rush".
- Never "his recipe" or "his measures": our honey is ½ oz, the printed spec ¾ (p. 96). "His order" is the sour on the rocks, no egg, no garnish, with the honey swap (*PUNCH*); the hard shake and the one large cube are the printed recipe, so "starts from his order, as the recipe prints it". Never "the bourbon they used" (F21).
- The chalkboard: the Gold Rush came last, after the Old-Fashioned, Manhattan, Sazerac and Martini. Never "after the Old-Fashioned and the Manhattan" alone.
- Shaking: "the cold's done by twelve" (closing line, kitchen sense of Arnold's "you will be OK", p. 98) / "barely changes" (LI pp. 97–98: "almost no further chilling"; the reading uses "After about twelve seconds, the drink barely changes"), never "needs twelve", "changes nothing" or "as cold as it will get".
- Name and brief: Siegal's rule is no "hidden meaning or connection to the origin of the drink" (*PUNCH*), not "the name means nothing". "Any day" is the bartender's line, never Siegal's. No page gives the drink's colour: any hue in the brief is Tomás's read, never guest text.
- The honey syrup: "the house honey syrup"; "his friend's" only as Siegal's account (Petraske, p. 96; Maloney, *PUNCH*: C5).
- The late-1990s restaurant cash (p. 88, undated) and the 2000 Midtown shift (*PUNCH*) are never merged into one job.
- Out: Petraske's death, his army years, the rules, the speakeasy, hand-cut ice, p. 113's business talk (Wren), the Penicillin beyond one mention (Innocent's), any role label for Siegal (C4).
