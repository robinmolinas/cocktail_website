# Persona card: The Rough Diamond (lover-regular-guy), v1

Wren, round 1, 2026-09-30. Source: `persona.py lover-regular-guy` (sheet essence, story, goal Intimacy/Connection, fear Isolation; secondary Belonging/Respect/Fairness, fears Exclusion/Discourtesy/Individuality); siblings and neighbours read in the registry and their pour files; *A Proper Drink* pp. 86–96 and 113 read on the page.

## Core truth
This person loves in a language with no feeling-words in it, and understands it when it's spoken back: they hear "help" inside a sentence about the car or the plumbing, because that's exactly how they'd have said it, and they answer with the thing done, for as long as it takes. What they've never managed is the other sentence, the one that says why.

## Shows / hides
- **Shows:** blunt, practical, there. Turns up. Fixes it. Doesn't make a fuss, doesn't want one made.
- **Hides:** that the feeling is full-size. Saying it out loud feels like wearing someone else's good clothes, false on them, so it stays in.

## Hidden fear → hidden gift
- **Fear:** that one day the person will be gone and it was never said (the sheet: "losing someone because they could never quite say it"). Under it, Isolation: being loved back only by people who speak the same silent language, and missing everyone who doesn't.
- **Gift:** nobody they love ever has to ask properly, and nobody has to sit through a speech or a thank-you. Nobody has to find the words on either side. The people on the other end always know where they can lean. The reading says this out loud once (Robin's "say the kindness" rule).

## The "real me" line to aim for
- *Nobody you love ever has to ask you. They only have to mention it.* (tagline candidate: positive, intriguing)
- Alt: *You heard "help" before they could say it.*
- The scene for whoYouAre: someone you love mentions the car's making that noise again. They don't ask; they'd never ask. On Saturday you're under it. (Never money: the Saviour owns taking the bill.)

## Truth vs. legend
The true story, plain, no varnish. This person distrusts polish. Legends granted only briefly; no romance about Siegal's motive without "I like to think".

## Tone
Conversational, genuine, warm (Regular Guy) under passionate (Lover), said through a blunt voice. Short sentences, dry humour ("I bought thirty copies" should be allowed to be funny). The reading must not make a speech to someone who hates speeches.

## Distinct from siblings and neighbours
- **The Sculptor (*Quite Alive*)** owns "You never say it. You make it." and "you rarely know what to say. You know what to make": a crafted object carrying a message, hoping it's read. Ours isn't crafted, isn't a message, isn't waiting to be decoded. No made-by-hand, no "let it say it for you", no "look up".
- **The Rescuer (*Off Duty*)**: speed in a crisis, for anyone. Ours is slow and dull (months, not one night), and only for their own.
- **The Saviour (*Next One's Mine*)**: takes the bill, says "Put it on me" out loud. So the guest's scene is never money.
- **The Knight (unauthored)**: the gallant rescue, seen. Ours is unglamorous and unseen. McClane stays out (rescue, violence, a wife to save).
- **The Romantic / Charmer / Flirt**: all the words. This one will never write the letter.
- **The Geisha (*Hoping You'd Come*)** owns "the plain sentence" and "What you'd never tell them is…"; **Standing By** owns "say it back, gently"; **the Muse** y4 owns "the way one sentence got him"; **Before the Room** closes on "say one thing you'd usually leave outside". "Just say it" is taken four times.
- **The True Friend (*Four Shares*)**: fear of being left out, allowed to be looked after. Not ours.

## Position (direction)
Not *whether* to say it but *when*: one rough line while the hands are busy (under the car, driving, halfway through the job, not looking at anyone). That's where people like this talk. Ends on the gain: they finally hear it, and no speech was needed. Grep against every y5 and closing line before drafting.

## Traps
- Money as love: never "gift" (p. 96 "lending… seed money"; p. 113 "investor").
- Siegal as saviour of the bar: p. 87 "childhood pals", plural (others took on credit-card debt, p. 88); p. 89 credits a neighbour and DeGroff with the bar surviving.
- Absence claims ("never said", "never asked for it back"): not on the page.
- Petraske's genius story, rules, speakeasy, hand-carved ice (p. 90; *Half a Rim*), army years, his death (2015). p. 113 ("I like making money") stays out: it turns love into business.
- Anything that makes the guest sound cheap, clumsy or inarticulate as a flaw. "Rough" is the name's word, not the reading's.
- The reading never requires a partner (friendship love here).
- "Make it with yours" (a signposted reading of the Gold Rush ask) would trip the bare-"yours" lint if used in the reading: rephrase ("with his friend's honey").

## Story test
A real person whose love for one other person was paid in unglamorous, costly deeds over time; who answered an ask that was never quite made; whose only words on record are practical ones that turn out to carry the feeling; the love known on the page. Out: rescue, strangers, violence, health, public gestures.

## Verdict on the plan's lead (T. J. Siegal): accept
- Unmade ask, heard: "talking about it a few months" before the plumber line; "It wasn't 'T. J., I want you to invest.'" (p. 88)
- The deed: "For months" all the cash he made at the restaurant (p. 88); ran the bar with Joseph Schwartz for Petraske's six months in London (p. 95).
- Words sideways: "I bought thirty copies." (p. 93) as his answer to his friend's fury at the press; the Gold Rush ask, "as simple as that", with the friend's honey syrup (p. 96).
- Known: "one of the few friends he made at Stuyvesant" (p. 86); "largely unheralded" (p. 96).
- Drink as proof: "exceedingly simple" → "a rich, nearly decadent mouthfeel" (p. 96): a blunt order that turned out rich.
- Coincidence to own or avoid: the 30-second shake and the thirty copies.
