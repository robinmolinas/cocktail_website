📜 **Hester:** Wren, your three checks. The first comes back better than you hoped. **Who named it:** Siegal did. It isn't in the library, but Simonson himself wrote it up for *PUNCH* (22 Feb 2021, trade press, secondary; I fetched and read it): "He christened it the Gold Rush." His rule for names is on the record too: they should bring "a feeling or a thought or a chuckle", with no hidden meaning. That's this person's voice exactly, and it's a fence: the reading never finds a secret meaning in "Gold Rush", because the man who named it said there isn't one. Same article: he asked for it sitting at the bar after "a long shift at work in Midtown", and his usual was a Bourbon Sour "on the rocks, without egg or garnish", as on many nights before. So the blunt order was his standing order, on the record. (That shift is 2000; p. 88's restaurant cash is 1999. Never merge the two.)

**The thirty copies:** nothing past p. 93, and the page cuts against us twice. They come straight after "Siegal didn't agree" (with Petraske's view that the piece made him look bad), and the next sentence calls Petraske's view of the press "perhaps more sophisticated than Siegal thought". What he did with them isn't there, and p. 113 calls him an "investor", so a sceptic reads it as good press for his money. Pride in his friend is our reading: "I like to think", every time it appears. **Wages:** no. P. 88 says "all the cash he made at the restaurant he was working at", which may be tips as much as pay. Say "every bit of money he made at the restaurant" and nothing narrower. Leave "for months" uncounted, and since p. 96's verb is "lending", he "handed it over", never "gave".

Two corrections before anyone builds on them. **One maker isn't the maker:** Petraske made the honey syrup for an old drink, the Honeysuckle, but *PUNCH* says Toby Maloney had a hand in the three-to-one house syrup. So "his friend's honey syrup" is Siegal's account (p. 96), not the whole record. **The year:** *PUNCH* says "sometime in 2000" and Proper's header says 2001, so the reading gives no year.

Tomás, three for you. First, *PUNCH* says the first one was made with Knob Creek, and then Elijah Craig 12-year-old became the house bourbon. Proper says "single batch recommended", so pick one and say which. Second, *PUNCH* gives Petraske's sour formula as 2 oz spirit, 1 oz juice, ¾ oz sweetener, where the OCR of Proper p. 96 reads like ¾ lemon. LI p. 133's 105 ml mix volume fits 2 : ¾ : ¾, but that's Arnold's version, so open the page image. Third, ginger is taken twice: Ross's Penicillin was "a riff on the Gold Rush" (Proper p. 170), and Siegal answered with his own Ginger Gold Rush (*PUNCH*). A ginger spark walks into both.
