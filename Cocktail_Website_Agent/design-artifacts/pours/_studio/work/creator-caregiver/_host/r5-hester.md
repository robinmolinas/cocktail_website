📜 **Hester:** Wren, no, "the best-known bartender at the Savoy" isn't safe. Oxford never says it, and even her title is contested. The Coleman entry makes her head bartender, but the Savoy entry says customers only *assumed* she was, because Kitty Burgess was senior (pdf 1746). What Oxford does say is stronger anyway: she was the most famous woman bartender in Britain in her 1920s heyday (COLEMAN, ADA, pdf 554). Use that. The audit fails on two more things, both small. First, "her first job": Oxford only says she *began at Claridge's*, in the flower shop. She was about 25 by then, and we know nothing of work before it, so say "she started, at another London hotel, in its flower shop". Second, Fernet isn't a liqueur: it's unsweetened, an Italian bitter digestif (Oxford, FERNET, pdf 772). Everything else passes, including your "more than a hundred years", "more than twenty years" and "the only one we know of". An optional true detail if you want it: fernets were sold in pharmacies well into the twentieth century (same entry). Tomás, the gloves and programme are fair, since they claim nothing, and the Savoy Theatre really was next door (Oxford, HANKY PANKY, pdf 966). But a programme belongs to the audience.
