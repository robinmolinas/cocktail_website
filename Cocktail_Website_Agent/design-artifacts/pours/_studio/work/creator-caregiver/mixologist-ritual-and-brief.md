# Tomás: Ritual + image brief draft, The Sculptor (creator-caregiver), round 4

## Recipe

| amount | item | note (shown) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 60 ml | London dry gin | |
| 30 ml | sweet vermouth | |
| 5–10 ml | Fernet-Branca (recommended; or any dark, minty Italian fernet) | the measure is the person |
| 1 strip | orange peel | |

Glass: a coupe (a stemmed cocktail glass), from the freezer. Spec written at 7.5 ml, the midpoint; balanced at 5, 7.5 and 10 (see `mixologist-checks.md`).

## Method (draft)
1. Put a coupe in the freezer.
2. Pour the gin and the vermouth into a jar or mixing glass, then add one barspoon of Fernet-Branca (about 5 ml).
3. Fill it with ice and stir for about thirty seconds, until the outside of the glass is very cold.
4. Taste a drop off the spoon. Then look up at the person it's for. Are they worn out tonight, and do they need the bitter? If they do, add another half spoon and stir a few more turns. Never more than two spoons in all.
5. Strain it into the cold glass. Squeeze the orange peel over the top so its oils fall on the drink, and drop it in.
6. Hand it to them yourself.

## Round 6 notes
- Fernet wording: my copy never calls it a liqueur. It is an Italian bitter digestif (Oxford, FERNET, pdf 772). Oxford says fernets are *traditionally* unsweetened, but Fernet-Branca measures 8 g sugar/100 ml in Arnold's table (LI pdf 140–141), which is the value balance.py uses. So the guest copy should say "dark, bitter Italian digestif", not "unsweetened".
- Fact card F12 says "traditionally unsweetened". That's true of the style, but Branca's own bottle measures 8 g/100 ml (LI). Flag for the dossier: don't let "unsweetened" reach guest copy.
