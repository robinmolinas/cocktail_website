# Persona card: The Everyman (regular-guy-sage)

Wren, round 1, 2026-10-01. Built from persona.py, my family sketch (`plans/work/regular-guy/psychologist-sketches.md`), all ten sibling readings, the Sage plan's Dude row and the registry.

- **Core truth:** most of what they know, somebody told them, and they still remember who. They carry other people's know-how with the names attached, and they've quietly tried most of it for themselves: what they hand on is what survived.
- **Shows:** the plain answer. Someone brings them a mess (a first week in a new job, a row with a brother, a cake that sank) and leaves with one sentence they can use tomorrow, usually starting "my aunt used to say…".
- **Hides:** the sifting. Not everything they were told was right, and they've dropped the bits that weren't, without telling anyone; it feels a little disloyal. And they can't always say *why* something works, only who taught it to them. That gap is where their confidence goes in front of people with letters after their names.
- **Fears:** that the chain stops with them. The sayings, the method, the way of doing it: if they don't hand it on, it goes, and so does a bit of the person who gave it (exclusion across time, not across a room). Second: being heard as "just an old saying", nodded at and set aside.
- **Gives (to be said out loud once, Robin's rule):** something plain that works, and the people who taught them, kept in the room. When they say "my grandad always said", he's there for a second.
- **"Real me" lines to aim for:**
  - "Most of what you know, someone told you. You've never once forgotten who."
  - "You didn't keep all of it. You kept what worked, and you still give them the credit."
  - Position direction: someone already starts a sentence with your name; hand it on now, not someday (and say which bits you dropped).
- **Truth vs. legend:** the true story, plainly. This guest sifts sayings for a living, so the reading itself can't pass on lore as fact: no first-frost or pricking lore, no "digestive".
- **Tone:** conversational, genuine, supportive (primary); informed and thoughtful (secondary). Warm and a little wry: the pub competitions over whose family recipe is best are funny in a kind way. Never quaint, never sad-nostalgic.
- **Distinct from siblings (all ten closed):**
  - *Got You* (Prankster) owns the chain shape for a joke: "Someone got you first… every joke since has been you passing it on". Never "passing it on" here; ours is know-how and voices, not getting someone in.
  - *Good for Years* (Mechanic) owns objects kept going, "a grandparent's lamp", "just get a new one", and the jar. Ours is words and methods; never the throwaway world, never a jar.
  - *Good as It Is* (Bumpkin) owns turning yourself down a notch in clever rooms and the half-smile. So ours is **not** "goes quiet among experts" (that was my sketch; withdrawn): it's "can't always say why it works".
  - *Word Gets Round* (People's Champion) owns knowing how systems work, for people, and refusing the chair. Ours comes from people, not forms; no power, no seat.
  - *Right Here* (Hidden Talent) owns a gift that doesn't match the picture, "one of theirs". Ours is knowledge received, not a talent.
  - *Whoever Comes In* (Samaritan) owns "made days ahead… waits until someone's there". Keep the sloe gin's long wait off that shape.
- **Neighbours outside the family:** the Dude (Sage plan: Fortaleza, family land "for generations", *Los Abuelos*; owns "learned by living", the plain thing kept against fuss). The Old Master (*Beside the First*) revises *their own* work; ours sifts *inherited* work. The Doctor (*Instead*) owns "because you know to ask from experience". The Connoisseur owns truth versus legend. The Mentor (Sage plan) owns the named teacher and student who surpasses. *In Your Own Hand* owns "put your name on it"; *Is It Just Me* owns "start with I". *Whoopee* (Thrill Seeker) has already described sloe gin.
- **Traps:**
  - "The Everyman" is the branding's word for the whole archetype: the tag only, never a description, never "ordinary" or "simple" about the guest. No Gump, no IQ.
  - "Old wives' tales" pull straight to remedies: health is out, including the sloe gin "digestive".
  - Don't assume the guest's elders are dead, or that the giver is a grandmother: vary who gave it (a neighbour, an uncle, a first boss).
  - No gloating that the experts were wrong, or that the trade was foolish to forget it.
  - No "common sense" (Barnum).
  - Family formula: seven siblings open whoYouAre with a list of three concrete examples and name the gift with "it deserves saying / Here's what you give / So I'll name it / It should be said". Vary both.
  - Phrases owned in this family: "passing it on", "one of them / one of theirs", "turn yourself down", "a grandparent's", "jar", "just get a new one".
- **Story test (refined):** (1) know-how handed **person to person**, outside formal teaching; (2) kept by **ordinary people**; (3) through a time when the trade or fashion **dropped it**; (4) still **used or tested among peers** today; (5) the source shows the handing down: named giver and receiver best, "generations of families" acceptable. **Out:** folk medicine of any kind, superstition or lore as fact, gloating, Jerry Thomas's revisions, the Dude's "keep it real", invented givers.
