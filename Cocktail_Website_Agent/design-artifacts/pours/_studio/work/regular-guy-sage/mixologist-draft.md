Spec: `_studio/specs/regular-guy-sage.json` (v1, unchanged). A stirred sloe-gin Blackthorn: sloe gin and London dry gin, dry vermouth, orange bitters, a lemon peel. The recipe gives the guest a rule of thumb: the two gins always make 60 ml between them, and the sweeter the sloe gin, the bigger the share of plain gin. Every stop of the rule is proved below.

**Glassware:** small stemmed glass (about 150 ml), chilled, no ice
**Contains:** nuts (the sloe gin, on the safe side; see Allergens)

## Recipe

| amount | item | note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 30 ml (1 oz) | sloe gin | a traditional sloe gin: bought, made by someone you know, or your own. Sloe gins can contain almond, and some makers crush the stones: if you avoid nuts, don't make this one |
| 30 ml (1 oz) | London dry gin | any London dry gin, about 43% or stronger |
| 22.5 ml (¾ oz) | dry vermouth | French-style dry white vermouth; keep it in the fridge once it's open |
| 2 dashes | orange bitters | |
| 1 strip | lemon peel | about the length of a thumb, with as little of the white pith as you can |
| | *the rule:* the sloe gin and the gin always add up to 60 ml (2 oz) | most sloe gins: 30 ml of each. Sweeter than most: 22.5 ml sloe gin, 37.5 ml gin. Sweet as a syrup: 15 ml sloe gin, 45 ml gin |

## Method

1. Put the glass in the freezer for 10 minutes, or fill it with ice and water while you make the drink.
2. Pour a little of the sloe gin and taste it on its own. Every bottle is sweetened by its maker, and a home-made one is sweetened to its maker's taste, so this sets your measures: the sweeter it is, the less sloe gin and the more plain gin, choosing the line from the rule. The two always add up to 60 ml.
3. Pour both gins, the dry vermouth and the bitters into a mixing glass (or any tall, sturdy glass) and fill it with ice.
4. Stir for about 30 seconds, until the outside of the mixing glass feels very cold.
5. Empty the serving glass if it's holding ice, then strain the drink into it. No ice in the glass.
6. Hold the lemon peel skin side down over the drink and pinch it between finger and thumb, so its oils fall onto the top. Then drop it in.

**closingLine:** *Sixty between the two gins, whatever the bottle. When you teach it, say how you split the sixty.*

Alternatives considered: *Sixty between the two gins, whatever the bottle. Teach someone that, and tell them who taught you.* (r3; it repeated the reading's "teach… someone" and carried the giver, not the position's change) · *Taste the sloe gin first. It'll tell you how much of the plain gin it wants.* (no position).
