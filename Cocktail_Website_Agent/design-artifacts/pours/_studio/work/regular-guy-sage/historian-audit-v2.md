## X1, the crossing: facts for Wren's ruling

- **Tomás's proof** (his numbers, Checks "The rule, swept"): every stop is in range or at an edge from a 20% sloe gin up, with gin at 43%+. Only below 20% does the 30/30 go OUT on strength.
- **Legal minimum (Tomás's ask): not on any page I can cite for sloe gin by name.** The only floor in the library is the general liqueur one: "like any liqueur", more than 15% alcohol and at least 100 g/L sugar (Oxford PARFAIT AMOUR pdf 1475). That doesn't close the 15–20% gap, and it doesn't name sloe gin. I believe the EU spirit-drinks regulation (2019/787, which Oxford cites at pdf 875) gives sloe gin its own category with a 25% minimum. That's memory, not a page, and the web budget is spent, so it is **not claimable** (card S26, lead for Robin). A home batch has no legal floor at all.
- **So both wordings are true.** "A traditional sloe gin" (Wren) makes no strength claim. "Whichever sloe gin you have" (Tomás) is backed by his proof from 20%, and he says plainly in Checks that below 20% is unproved. Either way the facts hold. The choice is about the person and the drink: Wren's words in the reading, Tomás's in the recipe. They should agree, and Wren rules in round 5. If the recipe keeps "whichever", Checks should keep its 20% bound as written.
