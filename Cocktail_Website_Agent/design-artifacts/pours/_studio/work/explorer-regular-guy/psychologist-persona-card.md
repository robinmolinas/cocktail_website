# Persona card: The Tourist (explorer-regular-guy)

Wren, round 1, 2026-09-30. Read from Robin's sheet (persona.py), the family plan row, my family sketch (`plans/work/explorer/psychologist-sketches.md`), and the sibling pours *Left Standing*, *For Good*, *Four Shares*, *Everybody's*, *As It Was*. I read Oxford COOPER, RON (pdf 573–574) and DEL MAGUEY MEZCAL (pdf 638, 640–641) myself before ruling.

## Core truth
For this person, going away and coming home are one act. A find isn't finished until it's been handed to someone back home: the photos, the tips, the story over dinner. The way back isn't their safety net. It's where the trip gets told.

## The scene to build on
Halfway through the best meal of the trip, they're already working out who they'll send there. They come home with the name of the street, what to order and who to ask for, and friends use the list for years.

## Shows / hides
- **Shows:** the glow, the photos, the tips, a planned trip with one unplanned street in it.
- **Hides (1):** a small embarrassment. They like the guidebook and the return ticket, and they've felt looked down on by people who make a show of never booking anything.
- **Hides (2), the tender one:** the best night of a trip is sometimes the one after it, at their own table, telling it. They'd never say so, because it sounds as if they didn't enjoy being there.

## Hidden fear → hidden gift
- **Fear, in its own word: stuck.** Never "trapped" (the Expedition Leader's word). A place with no way back is a place they can't share. So here the family's fear of the cage and the secondary's fear of being left out are one fear: being stuck somewhere, even somewhere beautiful, with nobody to tell.
- **Gift:** they bring the world back to people who didn't go, and they give them the way there. That is permission: "if they did it, I could". Robin's caregiver rule applies: say this kindness out loud, once.

## The "real me" line to aim for
*"The best night of a trip is sometimes the one after it."*
Runner-up: *"You never just say it was good. You say where."*

## Truth vs. legend
The true story, told warmly. They'll happily pass on a legend if it's labelled as a legend. They're relaxed people, and a lecture would lose them.

## Tone
Conversational, genuine, supportive (from the secondary) with a spirited, direct warmth (from the primary). This is a sunny reading and it can be funny. Its gravity comes from the fear of being stuck, and it shouldn't stay there long.

## Distinct from siblings
- **Conservationist** (*Left Standing*): leaves no trace and doesn't pick the flower. The Tourist brings the place home and hands it round.
- **Sense Seeker:** goes in close, sometimes alone, for the sensation itself. The Tourist goes for the story it will make, told to others.
- **Trailblazer:** refuses the approved path. The Tourist loves the approved path plus one street.
- **Pioneer:** goes first, under watching eyes. **Expedition Leader:** brings a group back from being trapped. The Tourist brings a *place* back.
- **Innovator** (*For Good*): fixes the thing everyone puts up with. The Tourist fixes nothing. They bring something back.
- **Secondary siblings:** *Four Shares* (inclusion at home, the bowl coming round), *Everybody's* (the same you for everyone), *As It Was* (keeps the Tuesdays). The Tourist keeps the holiday and gives it away. So the sharing is of the find and its story, **never of the drink**: no bowl, no pitcher, no round for the table.

## Traps
1. **Condescension, the big one.** The name can read as a put-down. The return ticket gets its dignity, and nothing sneers: not at the guest, not at the worm bottle, not at the people who bought it.
2. **Café.** *Left Standing*'s whoYouAre opens with "The café that might have closed." The sheet has a café, but this reading finds another place (a side street, a stall, a kitchen, a bar with no menu in your language).
3. **"Take someone" / "don't go alone"** is *Left Standing*'s y5 position. It's out.
4. **Scale:** "godfather", "game changer", "the mezcal boom". That's the Visionary's size and time, so one clause at most.
5. **Money:** no sales figures, prices, "brand", or the Pernod Ricard sale.
6. **Labels:** artist, adventurer, traveller, globetrotter, foodie, travel blogger. "Adventure" is Oxford's word (pdf 573, 638), not ours. One travel word (journey, map, horizon…) per reading at most. Never "curious" or "free" (family-wide horoscope words).
7. **"Discovered", and saviour stories.** The villages always had it. Never use pdf 640's off-record "It took an American…", and keep the emigration lines (640) in the dossier.
8. **The plant and the bats** are *Left Standing*'s. This reading never talks about the agave.
9. **A visitor carrying American drinks home in a travel book** is *Hoping You'd Come*'s (the Willard captain). That counts against the Dickens backup.
10. **Holiday-joke drinks:** no umbrella, no worm, no salt-and-shot ritual, no souvenir kitsch. And none of the sheet's brands or TimeOut.
11. **Echoes:** *Everybody's* "every traveller getting off a plane"; *Four Shares* "pass the bowl"; *Off-Label* "go back to it" (so "come home", not "go back").

## Story test
Someone **went somewhere, found one thing a step past what everyone else settled for, brought it home and told people where it came from**, and it came to be loved there. On record: the going (ideally with company), the find, the coming back and telling. Small risk, a way back, nobody vanished, nothing taken. Out: exile, emigration, getting stranded (that's the fear itself), plunder told as fun, a holiday-joke drink, anyone ending up alone.

## Verdict on the plan's lead: Ron Cooper passes. Keep him.
- **Going, with company:** "in Oaxaca for the holidays with friends", he found the pure village mezcal, not the industrial kind (COOPER pdf 573).
- **The find as it is:** he brought it to market "as is, rather than blending it to suit a brand". Each came from a different Zapotec village (DEL MAGUEY pdf 638) and was sold under that village's name (638, 640).
- **Loved at home:** a cult following for years, then bartenders "clamored" for Vida (573). Mezcal bars opened in New York, London and elsewhere (641).
- **Unpompous:** ask him about business figures and he sends you elsewhere. His attention is on the spirit and the people who make it (574).
- **The recognition line:** *he came home and put the village's name on the bottle.* That's the Tourist who comes home with the address. It also turns the plunder trap around, because the credit goes to the place.

**Weaknesses, turned into conditions:**
- (a) He wasn't a one-time holidaymaker. He first tasted agave spirit in Baja in 1962 (638), and Oxford calls it a "dogged quest" (573). The reading tells one trip and the coming home, never a lifelong quest.
- (b) The two entries tell the find twice: a holiday with friends in 1986 (573), and a café in Tlacolula in 1990 (638). Hester picks one scene and keeps it with its own page, with no year, and never glues the two together. I want the friends: it gives us company, and it keeps us off *Left Standing*'s café. His memoir (*Finding Mezcal*, 2018, cited on 574) may settle it on a secondary page.
- (c) See traps 7 and 8.
- (d) This is the family's third agave pour in a row (*Left Standing*, *For Good*). Robin's rule allows it, which makes a drink shaped by the person matter more.

**Backup (Dickens and the Sherry Cobbler):** second at best. Telling it was his profession, not a guest's gesture. He crossed by sea, which is out. *American Notes* carries condescension. And *Hoping You'd Come* already has the British visitor printing American drinks for home.

## For Tomás (asks from the person; the drink is his)
- The village's name should be in the recipe: a named single-village bottling, because the name *is* the story. Vida is the "bartenders clamored" beat if a mixer is needed, but it's a weaker mirror.
- One glass, not a round (see the secondary siblings).
- No holiday-joke tells (trap 10).
- If a spark fits, the one I'd hope for sets the far find beside something from home.

## Position direction
Stop apologising for the return ticket. It's the reason you can go one more street: you're coming home to tell it. End on the gain, a better story, never on restraint. This must not echo "one village further" word for word.
