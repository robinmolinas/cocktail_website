# Wren, rework round 1 (2026-10-06): the read, the rulings, the fences

Not a reading draft. The reading waits for the drink.

## The person, as she stands in the pour
- **One true thing:** they know how the thing really works, and they use it for the people who don't. ("You put what you know on the side of the people nobody told.")
- **Hides:** that they like knowing. It's power, and they won't use the word.
- **Fears:** the seat. Knowing this much already puts them near the side of the counter that says no; one step further and their own people might see them as one of *them*. Exclusion, in the shape of becoming management.
- **Gives:** people who'd have paid full price, signed the wrong thing or taken no for an answer, don't.
- **Position (unchanged):** take the seat. Being where things get decided doesn't take you away from your people.
- **Traps (unchanged from r1, 2026-10-01):** charm and being let off (Scoundrel); crisis calm, "they all look at you" (Legend); hours of practice, credit given away (Firefighter); fixing (Mechanic); the hidden skill (Hidden Talent); fallback, stand-in, second choice (One Next Door, Genie); "boss" (Entrepreneur); "small print" (*The Long View*); "told everyone" (*Fine by Me*); speeches and politics.

## (a) The story stays
The mirror was never in the Rickey. It's in the fruit and in the giving: an owner, already in the seat, who handed the trade's saving to ordinary drinkers (F5, F6), asked for the honest recommendation "and tell them why" (F7), and remembered having nobody to talk to (F10). The Rickey was the shape Tomás chose so the fruit had nothing to hide behind. That shape goes; the story doesn't.

**What the new drink must carry for the story to stay in the glass:**
1. **Fresh lime, measured by volume, never counted by fruit.** That's Heugel's trick, physically in the glass: any size of lime gives the jigger the same 15 (or 20, or 22.5) ml. The epigraph ("Measures don't mind") and the closing line's first sentence ("Juice the odd-sized limes") both depend on it.
2. **The fruit has to show.** Heugel's other point was quality (F7). Sugar may come in now (it's part of what makes this not a Rickey), but nothing that buries the lime: no ready-made cordial (that's the Craftsman's Gimlet), no liqueur loud enough to cover it.
3. **The measure has to matter.** In a sweetened drink, the lime-to-sugar balance is exactly where guessing fails. A measured jigger is what lets the odd fist-sized lime make the same drink every time. True every time it's made (Tomás to confirm on paper), so it passes the flourish test.
4. **The lemon, if it's kept, as the honest alternative,** tasted against the lime ("and tell them why"; F7; lemon was the older cocktail citrus, F15). Never as the stand-in.
5. **No Key lime as the underdog mirror.** If Tomás wants Key lime for taste, it comes in as know-how (Oxford: sweeter and more acidic, so less of both, F14), never as "the tiny fruit nobody wanted" (*First Choice*'s wound). The packer's case stays the story.

If the new drink can't hold measured fresh lime, the story has to go too. I'd want to know that in round 2, not round 3.

Facts that leave with the Rickey: Regan's "the secret is in the amount of lime" (*Joy* pdf 376), Wondrich's "half a lime or a whole one if the limes were small" (*Imbibe!* pdf 118), the 1890s spread, the DC line (Q007), and "no sugar is the classic's own rule". y4 needs a new page for whatever the new drink claims.

## (b) The shape of drink the person needs (not the drink)
- An everyday drink people already order at a counter, the kind you could tell a friend how to make right. Bottles anyone can buy. Nothing showy, nothing that charms (that keeps it off the Scoundrel).
- Lime-led, sweetened or lengthened by something that isn't plain soda, so it's clearly not a Rickey.
- One glass for one guest. The person isn't the host: bowls and pitchers belong to *Four Shares* and *Leave It With Me*.
- A step in the method that's know-how the guest can pass on (measure, taste the two juices). No flourish to perform.
- Let it sound worth having (Robin's rule): a drink with a name people recognise, made properly.
- My lean, Tomás's call: long. The Regular Guy row already has four Daiquiris and no other Highball. If it goes short, it's the row's fifth Daiquiri and needs a reason in Checks. (The Ruler column already has three Highballs: *Fair Measure*, *Somewhere to Land*, *Note to Self*.)

## (c) Tagline, whoYouAre, name: all stay true
- **Tagline** ("Nobody near you pays extra for not knowing."): about the person; no drink in it. True.
- **whoYouAre:** no drink in it. True. Two variety notes **for Robin, not rewritten** (his rule): "So I'll name it." announces the kindness (V2: say it "without announcing it"), and "That counts for a great deal more than you let it" grades it.
- **Name** (*Word Gets Round*): the person's gift, and how Heugel's trick travelled. True as long as the story stays, and true of the person even without it.
