Spec: `_studio/specs/regular-guy-ruler.json`. Archived in this folder: the first pour's bourbon Rickey (`mixologist-spec-rickey-2026-10-01.json`, `mixologist-draft-rickey-2026-10-01.md`) and rework v1, the vodka Collins (`mixologist-spec-collins-v1-2026-10-06.json`, `mixologist-draft-collins-v1-2026-10-06.md`). **Why v1 went:** asked by the host whether the vodka Collins is the same cocktail as *Not Too Polite*'s Tom Collins under check 3, I ruled **yes** (reason under Owned near-misses). So the drink changed within this round. Story unchanged (Bobby Heugel and the packer's cases, *PUNCH*, 2 April 2014), and so are Wren's five conditions: fresh lime measured, never counted; fruit you can taste; a reason the measure matters; the lemon as the honest alternative; no Key lime.

**Glass:** coupe (about 180 ml), chilled
**Contains:** veto-free

## Recipe

| amount | item | note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 60 ml | apple brandy | a Calvados or an American straight apple brandy, 40 to 50%; not applejack, which is blended with neutral spirit |
| 20 ml | fresh lime juice | measured, not counted: any size of lime will do, so buy the odd-sized ones if your shop sells them cheaper. Or use lemon, if the lemons taste better that day |
| 17.5 ml | grenadine | made from pomegranate: equal weights of 100% pomegranate juice and sugar, shaken in a closed jar until the sugar has gone. If you buy it, check the label says pomegranate. Measure it as carefully as the juice |

## Method

1. Put a coupe in the freezer.
2. Squeeze a lime and a lemon. Taste a drop of each juice. Use whichever tastes better today, and if anyone asks, tell them why.
3. Measure 20 ml of that juice into a shaker with 17.5 ml (3½ teaspoons) of grenadine and 60 ml of apple brandy. Measured out, it makes no difference what size the lime was.
4. Shake hard with ice for about 12 seconds.
5. Strain into the cold coupe. No garnish.

**Owned near-misses, and the ruling that changed the drink:**
- ***Not Too Polite* (the Campaigner): is my v1 vodka Collins the same cocktail as its Tom Collins? Yes.** The two had the same template: a spirit, citrus, sugar syrup and soda, the soda poured in first, short-shaken onto it, ice last. That's the *Codex*'s Collins build (p. 138), and *Not Too Polite* uses it step for step. What differed was the white spirit (vodka for gin), the citrus by default (lime for lemon), and the syrup (plain for jaggery). With lemon chosen at the taste step, even the citrus was the same. Robin ruled that two whiskey Rickeys are the same cocktail even with a different whiskey and water. Swapping one clear spirit for another in an unchanged Collins is that kind of change. Oxford itself files the Vodka Collins as a later version of the Tom Collins, in the TOM COLLINS entry (pdf 2042, via Hester). So v1 is archived and the drink is now a Jack Rose, which no pour holds. **Hester agrees** (audit v4): Oxford gives the Vodka Collins no entry of its own; it sits inside TOM COLLINS, after "the drink's formula lends itself nicely to other spirits" (pdf 2042), the way the Rickey takes "any liquor" (pdf 1661). **Wren's view was no** (the spirit changes kind, and Oxford names the Vodka Collins as a drink). She left the ruling to me as the drink's owner. **For Robin, a note, not a flag:** Wren asks how far D12 reaches. Does it merge only same-kind spirit swaps (two whiskey Rickeys), or any spirit swap in an unchanged template (gin to vodka in a Collins)? This pour has moved off the Collins either way.
- ***By Design* (the Sex Symbol, Ruler column)** also makes grenadine from pomegranate juice and sugar shaken in a jar, with thyme, and closes on "Make the grenadine yourself, the day before." Mine is a different cocktail (apple brandy and lime against white rum, pineapple and lime), with no thyme and no day-before step. Bought pomegranate grenadine is allowed, and the grenadine never reaches my closing line.
- ***A Place Kept*** (a red-wine float on a bourbon sour) and ***The Long Answer*** (the Ward Eight: bourbon, lemon, orange, pomegranate grenadine and seltzer) are the registry's other sours with red in them. *The Long Answer*'s y2 also tells its guest about imitation grenadine and the pomegranate kind, which is why my grenadine stays out of the interest. Neither is a Jack Rose, and neither uses apple brandy. My method keeps clear of *Their Night*'s closing line ("Shake it until the tin is ice-cold"): mine is a plain 12-second shake.
- ***The Kind You'd Want*** (the Elf/Imp) is the registry's other apple-brandy base: a bottled highball from the fridge, a different family and glass.
