## §M. The measure: a page for "measured, not counted" without the Rickey
Each of these is on its own family's page, so use only the one that matches the drink chosen:
- **Mojito:** the first printed recipe (1929) counts by the fruit: "the juice of a lime" (Oxford pdf 1343). Oxford's own modern recipe measures 15 ml.
- **Mule:** the original was vodka and ginger beer "topped with a squeeze of lime" (*Codex* p. 139).
- **Buck:** the traditional method squeezes a citrus *wedge* into the empty glass (Regan, *Joy* pdf 328, in his Mamie Taylor). And the Mayflower Club's extra dime bought "a proper splash" of fresh lime, not drops of lemon (Oxford pdf 369).
- **Collins:** no page counts fruit. The *Codex* gives the reason a measure matters in a sweetened long drink: soda's carbonic acid makes a drink taste sharper, so they often set citrus and sugar 1:1, unless they want it extra-tart (p. 139). That's a bar book's rule of thumb: "one bar book says", never a law. **Struck (rw3, Tomás's catch):** the *Codex*'s reason (carbonic acid tastes sharper) conflicts with *Liquid Intelligence* pdf 292 ("carbonation doesn't taste acidic"), so the reason is never told. I carried it in my rw2 turn without the conflict.
- **Any family:** the *Codex*'s "taste the juice" (p. 115) still carries the lime-or-lemon step. Oxford pdf 466 adds that some spirits suit lemon better than lime, "especially whisky". If the base is whiskey, that's a page for keeping the lemon as the honest alternative, never the stand-in.

**Guard for Tomás's physics:** "with sugar in, guessing fails" is Wren's ask. It becomes a fact only from your balance numbers (how far the acid moves per 5 ml of juice at this sugar), never from any page above.
