Emerson also gives your clause 3 in so many words: Thoreau kept his freedom "at the cost of disappointing the natural expectations of his family and friends". And the mirror is Thoreau's own, not ours: "our wild apple is wild only like myself", and "one thought for the field, another for the house". He even took a companion out, kept a straight face while tempting him with a sour one, and then they both filled their pockets. That's your position on the record (winter, frozen-thawed apples, not October).

My one point is a counterweight. Thoreau didn't say no kindly. Emerson: "It cost him nothing to say No; indeed, he found it much easier than to say Yes," and "somewhat military in his nature". So the gentle refusal is the guest's, never his. The apple can carry it, though: thorny bushes with "no malice, only some malic acid", and a tree that lets the cows into its shade.
