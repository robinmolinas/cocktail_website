📜 **Hester:** Step 2, the full table is in `historian-audit-v2.md`. My strongest objection is y5, and the shorthand was mine first: nothing on the page says Thoreau brought his companion out. It says he "cheated my companion, and kept a smooth face" with the crabbed apples, and "Now we both greedily fill our pockets" once they'd thawed. The page never says who brought whom, and the tease came before the frost. Wren, old → new:

- y5: "One winter, Thoreau brought a companion out to the frozen apples, kept a smooth face while tempting him with a sour one, and then the two of them filled their pockets." → "Thoreau once kept a smooth face while tempting a companion with one of those sour apples. By winter, when frost and thaw had turned them sweet, the two of them were filling their pockets together."
- Epigraph: "A famous bar book made this drink sweet in 1862. Some kept it sharp anyway." → "In 1862 a famous bar book printed this drink with sweet cider. Others went on pouring it tart." (A book records a drink, it doesn't make it anything, and nothing says the 1869 drinkers knew about the book.)
- y1: "the small, sour apples farmers left on the branch as unsalable" → "the small, sharp apples farmers left out as unsalable". Outdoors he calls them sharp, not sour, and much of the fruit lay on the ground.
- y1: "the choicest fruit there was" → "the choicest fruit"
- y1: "Indoors, it was another fruit: one he kept on his desk was sour enough to set a squirrel's teeth on edge." → "Indoors, he wrote, it tasted like another fruit: one he took out of his desk was "sour enough to set a squirrel's teeth on edge"."
- y3: "Nothing in the apple changes when it's carried in." → "The way he tells it, nothing in the apple changes when it's carried in."
- y4: "I made it the old, sharp way: aged rum and a dry, still farmhouse cider" → "I went back to its older pairing of rum and hard cider, and kept it sharp: aged rum and a dry, still farmhouse cider". As written, the colon makes our 3:8 recipe the old one, and only the pairing is old.
- y4: "for the apples he kept back from the mill" → "for the apples he'd rather have eaten in the field"
