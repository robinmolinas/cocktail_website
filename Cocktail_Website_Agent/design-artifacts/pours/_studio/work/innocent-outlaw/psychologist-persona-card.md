# Persona card: The Outsider (innocent-outlaw), Wren r1, 2026-10-04

## The one true thing
They chose a small life on purpose, and it's the most deliberate thing about them. Everyone else reads it as something missing (drive, nerve, ambition). From inside, it's exactly the right size. They didn't lose the race; they never wanted to be in it, and they left without a fight.

Draft "this is me" line (not for the reading yet): *People keep offering you a bigger life. You keep turning it down kindly, and they never quite believe you mean it.*

## Hidden / fear / gift
- **Hides:** that the hard part was never living with less. It's being loved by people who think you're wasting yourself. The "you could do so much more" stings because it comes from people they love. They smile, and don't argue.
- **Fears:** being brought back in. Not by force: by kindness. A job they'd be good at, a favour, "just for a while", a plan someone made for them. And that, once indoors, the thing people liked about them goes sour. (Primary fear "punishment", in this person's shape: being counted at fault for opting out. Secondary: dependence, restriction.)
- **Gives (say it out loud once):** around them, other people remember they could want less. They make "enough" look like a choice instead of a defeat, and people leave an afternoon with them lighter. They show you can say no and still be kind.

## Siblings and neighbours (what's theirs, not ours)
- Innocent siblings: True Friend (sharing, kinder world), Free Spirit (questions), Dreamer (hope, "probably not"), Naive Artist (never saw what was wrong), Underdog (didn't hear how far they'd come), Giggler (laughs first), Heart-throb (*The Big Words*: never loves anything a little; the Outsider's opposite, small on purpose), Big Sibling (watch), Little Prince ("kept it alive"), Wise Kid.
- **Trap 1, the Adolescent (*Kept*):** fears becoming fake through their own good reasons, one by one; spots pretence at the table. Ours isn't a detector and isn't guarding against self-corruption: ours fears the *pull from others*, well-meant, and the judgement that comes with refusing it.
- **Trap 2, the Fugitive (*Rent-Free*):** Stevenson's Silverado, a quiet house in the hills, Cicero on living as you please in the country. A writer's chosen retreat into nature is their ground. So: no cabin, no squatting, no candle, no "live as you please", and Thoreau's Walden stays out (already guarded).
- **Trap 3, the Minimalist (*Half a Rim*):** takes away until it's true. "Making his wants few" must not become a craft of cutting. Ours isn't subtracting; it's staying out.
- **Trap 4, the Entrepreneur (*Loose on Top*):** "live your way, pay your own way", left to be free and built something. Ours built nothing to show and wants no boss *and* no empire.
- **Also near:** the Conservationist (*Left Standing*: touch little, leave it for whoever comes next); the Bumpkin (*Good as It Is*: easy delight); the Dude (lets things go). And *Into the Wild* (the persona's own example): never.

## Traps in the wording
- Never call the guest "unsalable", "unambitious" or "lazy". The market and Emerson say those about Thoreau and his apples; the reading turns them, it never applies them.
- No lone-hero romance, no anger, no "society" as villain (Innocent tone: honest, optimistic).
- No "rather" tagline (*Left Standing* owns "You'd rather know it's there"); "outside" is everywhere in the registry, so a recognition line can't rest on the bare word.
- Proposal must not drift into the Geisha's ("call the one you'd most like to see") or the Entertainer's ("let one person into the practice").

## Story test
1. Something or someone that stayed out of the market or the race **by choice**, not by defeat.
2. Without a fight: no protest, no villain.
3. The cost said aloud by someone who loved them (the gentle "punishment").
4. It's still best where it was left: true today, not only then.

## Verdict on the plan's lead (Thoreau's wild apple): ACCEPT, with conditions
Passes all four clauses. The apple that tastes right only out in the October air, and "unexpectedly crude" when carried indoors, is this guest's fear in one sentence. Emerson counting it "a fault in him that he had no ambition" is what they hide, said by a friend who loved him. "He chose to be rich by making his wants few, and supplying them himself" is the gift.
