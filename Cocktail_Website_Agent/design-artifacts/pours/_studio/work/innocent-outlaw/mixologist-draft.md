# Tomás: drink draft, innocent-outlaw (The Outsider), v1.3, round 4 step 3 (Hester audit-v2 B17, B18 landed; name vote; drink unchanged)

Spec: `_studio/specs/innocent-outlaw.json` (v1). A Stone Fence on its older pairing of rum and hard cider, kept sharp: aged rum and dry, still farmhouse cider in a heavy tumbler over two or three small lumps of ice, no sugar, and a thin slice of raw sharp apple cut last. Story: Thoreau's wild apple (Wren r1, plan lead). Facts in the notes follow Hester's anchors (`historian-anchors.md`, card `fact-cards/thoreau-wild-apples-stone-fence.md`).

**Glassware:** heavy tumbler (about 300 ml), two or three small lumps of ice
**Contains:** veto-free

## Recipe

| amount | item | note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 45 ml | aged rum (not spiced, about 40%) | rum was the Stone Fence's original spirit, before whisky |
| 120 ml | dry, still farmhouse cider, the sharpest you can find (about 5-6%) | not sweet, not sparkling. Small, sharp wild apples were thought to make the best cider, Thoreau wrote, though he still begrudged them to the cider-mill |
| 2 or 3 | small lumps of ice | as Jerry Thomas asked for in 1862 |
| 1 thin slice | raw sharp apple, skin on (a crab apple, or the sourest apple to hand) | the apple he'd rather have eaten in the field: raw, cut last |

## Method

1. Keep the cider cold in the fridge until you need it.
2. Drop two or three small lumps of ice into a heavy tumbler and pour in the rum.
3. Pour the cider in slowly over the ice, and give it one turn with a spoon.
4. Cut one thin slice from the sharpest apple you have, skin on, and slip it into the glass last.

**closingLine:** *Carry it out of doors before you taste it. Whoever follows you out will understand.*
