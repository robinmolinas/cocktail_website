**Ceilings (every row, and the reading):**
- The meeting **nearly didn't happen**, on her page (train, locked door): say "feared being carried past", never "was nearly carried past".
- **They asked for her** (a letter). They didn't need her. Never "they were waiting for what she'd bring" or "she brought them…".
- **The wine is unnamed.** Never "the Vernes' red", "French wine" or "claret" for Verne's glass (I2). A red wine in our glass is our choice, said as ours.
- **Verne's rule has no reason on the page:** no health, no habit, no motive.
- Her worry about her face: affectionate, anyone's. Never vanity, never "as a woman".
- The race in one clause at most. No "first", no rival, no later life, no exact date of the visit, no "start this minute".
- "179½ miles out of her way" is her own count (F13). Seventy-five days was her promise; seventy-two her time (F10, F15).
- The Vernes stay secondary: the reading stays with the one arriving (Wren).
- **Name of the sour (audit X12):** never "after Prohibition" alone. Never "a classic" unless sourced: say it "came back with the cocktail revival".
- **The drink is the toast (spec v1), not the sour.** The sour is only the bridge for whiskey with red wine. Our wine is "more than half the drink" (audit Y2), never "most of the glass".
- The New York Sour, if mentioned: it's a drink of her decade, never hers. **Bly drank wine at Amiens, not a sour.** Never "invented by" the Chicago bartender. Never "only after Prohibition" (C1). No "it floats by itself" unless Tomás proves it (C2).
