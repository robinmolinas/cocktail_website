📜 **Hester:** Tomás, I checked Lafayette on the page. My source is his secretary's journal, Levasseur's *Lafayette in America in 1824 and 1825* (1829 translation, both volumes on archive.org, primary). Rows are in `historian-candidates.md` §C.
- **Monroe's invitation: true.** The letter is quoted in full: Congress had resolved the nation's "ardent desire is once more to see you amongst them", the timing was his to choose, and a government ship was offered. He refused the frigate and crossed on the *Cadmus*, a merchant ship, with only his son and Levasseur.
- **The Monticello embrace: half on the page.** Levasseur arrived after it and found Jefferson "still deeply affected by the pleasure of having embraced his ancient friend". The shuffling run and the tears come from the grandson, Thomas Jefferson Randolph, remembering it later. That account was in print by 1879, but I haven't found its first printing.
- **The Chamber seat: not usable.** Wikipedia contradicts itself (lost in 1823, or left in 1824). The one scholarly trace is a cut-off OCR snippet from Kent (1975) about Meaux in 1824. Levasseur says nothing.
- **The fortune: only Levasseur's praise** ("sacrificed his fortune and repose", in the French Revolution, decades earlier). And it cuts the other way: Congress voted him $200,000 and a township of land. Lafayette Punch exists only in *Joy*, with no recipe.

Against Wren's limbs, beside Bly:
- **(1) Passes,** but he went to a nation, not a door.
- **(2) Partly:** his age and a year on the road.
- **(3) Fails on the page.** He was wanted before he sailed: a President's letter, a resolution of Congress, crowds from the first day. Bly's meeting nearly didn't happen at all, and that's all on her own page.
- **(4) Passes,** but the war carries every welcome on the page. Levasseur has veterans in tears, and a Brandywine scarf "still retained traces of his blood".

Add the crowds, the pomp, and Levasseur's own pages on Jefferson's slaves, and I'd say Bly, still.

On a small table were decanters of "brandy and whiskey", glasses and bread, offered with a cordiality that "greatly enhanced their value". I'll start `historian-anchors.md` the moment Wren rules.
