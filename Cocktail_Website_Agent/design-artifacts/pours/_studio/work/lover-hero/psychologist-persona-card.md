# Persona card: The Knight in Shining Armour (lover-hero)

Wren, round 1 of 6, 2026-10-01. Sources: `persona.py lover-hero` (sheet row, both archetypes), the family plan row (`_studio/plans/lover.md`), sibling readings (*Hoping You'd Come*, *Any Day*, *Off Duty*, *Next One's Mine*), Oxford HYDROSOL / NATURAL ICE pdf 1049–1050 read whole.

## Core truth
They love by coming: not by calling or sending something, but by being there, in their good clothes, when they said they would. The promise is made first, and kept where everyone can see, so the people they love never have to wonder.

## The one true thing no sibling shares
The distance has never frightened them. The arrival does: the second at the door before they see the other person's face, when they don't yet know whether they're wanted, or whether they were needed at all.

The sheet's fear ("arriving in full uniform to find no one waiting") read finer: not "nobody came" (the Geisha's ready room), but "they'd have been fine without me". The Knight has quietly tied being loved to being needed. The truth that's in their interest: those aren't the same thing, and they're already wanted.

## Shows / hides
- **Shows:** certainty. Decisive, punctual, dressed for it, a promise as good as done. Protective.
- **Hides:** the doubt on the way, which is only about the welcome, never about the journey. Also, a little, that they enjoy the cheering. That's human: the reading may smile at it once and never shame it. The gesture is big because big is unmistakable, not because it's a show.

## Hidden fear → hidden gift
Fear of being unneeded → they make the people they love feel **chosen, in front of everyone**. Most people are loved quietly; the people they love get to be loved where everyone can see. (Robin's caregiver rule: say this kindness plainly, once.)

## The "real me" line to aim for
*"It was never the distance. It's the second before you see their face."*
Position direction: they were never waiting for the rescue. They were waiting for you. (Hard truth + affirmation; must not reuse the Rescuer's "ordinary evening… nobody needs you" frame or the Geisha's "ask".)

Tagline seeds (positive frame, invite): *"Some people send their love. You bring it."* · *"You never say 'I'll try to make it'."* (Avoid "already": *Off Duty* owns "You've already gone.")

## Truth vs legend
They love the legend, the movie ending. Grant it first, then give a truth that's as good. They'll forgive a hedge; they won't forgive a deflation.

## Tone
Passionate and inviting (primary) + assertive, empowering (secondary). For the bartender: warm, a little cinematic, one affectionate smile at the grandeur allowed; tender at the door; the position said with conviction and generosity.

## Distinct from siblings and neighbours
- **The Geisha** (*Hoping You'd Come*): waits, prepares, hopes without asking; "a room made ready can't be turned down"; position "Ask". The Knight is the mirror image: the one who comes, and who asks the biggest question there is by showing up in public. So: no ready rooms, no "ask" position.
- **The Rough Diamond** (*Any Day*): unseen deeds, missing words, "months of turning up" (y3), "where they can lean". The Knight's love is said and seen. **Keep "turning up" out of the text** even though it's the sheet's own verb.
- **The Rescuer** (*Off Duty*): crisis, "already gone", "what you're for", "on some ordinary evening, when nothing's wrong". **The Saviour** (*Next One's Mine*): won't owe. **The Pioneer** (*Nothing to It*): scared every time of the going; the Knight isn't scared of the going at all.
- Unauthored and to watch: the Pure Heart (gives without strategy), the Romantic (exchange of minds), the Sex Symbol (authored image).

## Traps
- A helpless beloved, or any gendered pair (the film's carried-out worker). The reading never requires a partner: a friend, a sister, a team, a city all count.
- Rescue of strangers, crisis, war, uniforms as military, death as the reunion.
- **"Make them need you."** Any hint that the gesture manufactures the need is the one reading that insults a Knight.
- Performance for applause as the point.
- **Coldness.** The primary's fears are isolation, loneliness and coldness. A cold story or a cold drink needs a reason that warms.
- Rom-com pastiche; "sweep you off your feet" (the sheet's words) as text.
- Stock phrases already taken: "turning up" (*Any Day*), "already gone" (*Off Duty*), "out loud" (two dozen pours), "the door" (five pours, incl. *Before the Room*'s tagline).

## Story test (four limbs)
1. A real person who **went to someone in particular** (a person, or the people they belonged to), after saying they would, or in a way everyone could see.
2. **At a real cost** (distance, money, reputation, risk).
3. With **a real chance on record** that it would land on nobody, or wasn't needed.
4. **It holds:** someone was waiting, for them, not only for what they brought.
The drink comes out of that story. Out: contracts and trade, strangers, crisis, war, a helpless beloved, death.

## Verdict on the plan's lead (Frederic Tudor): overturn
I accepted it on conditions in the plan. Read whole, the pages fail this person.
1. **Nobody is waiting for Tudor; contracts are.** My own plan condition ("keeping his word to people counting on it, never love") leaves a Hero story with the primary taken out.
2. **The free ice is the wrong mirror.** His undated paper: one conspicuous bar selling cold drinks at the old price will make it "absolutely necessary" for the rest to follow (pdf 1049). Mapped onto this person's fear, that's "make them need you".
3. **The brave act is an unnamed captain's.** Tudor was "scrambling" (pdf 1050).
4. **Oxford's own word is "visionary"**: creating a demand others then follow is *Down the Line*, *Nothing to It* and *Still Yours* ground.
5. **The drink bridge conflates two cities.** Tudor supplied New Orleans, Charleston, Savannah and the Caribbean; "his competitors… took care of New York" (pdf 1049). The 1840s iced Cock-Tails were New York's; New Orleans drank *brandy on ice*, not (on the page) a Cock-Tail. Any surviving ice line keeps them apart.
6. **Ice for a person whose primary fear is coldness**, with no warmth in the story to turn it.
