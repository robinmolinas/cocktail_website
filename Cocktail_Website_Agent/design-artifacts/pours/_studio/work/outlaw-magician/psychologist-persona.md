# Persona card: The Disrupter (outlaw-magician), Wren, round 1

Source row: essence "uproots order, embraces chaos because they know it needs to happen for things to change"; story: breaks things so they can be rebuilt, shakes up the team, the habit, the relationship gone stale, "not out of anger but because change is overdue"; fear: "the slow decay of things nobody dares to touch"; goal liberation; driver: leave a mark. Shiva is the row's example: religion, kept out of every guest line.

## The one true thing (no sibling has it)
**They only break what they love.** The thing they pull down is never an enemy's. It's the tradition, the team, the friendship, the way of doing things they've loved longest, which has gone stale while everyone stays too fond or too comfortable to touch it. They break it *for its own sake*, so it can be good again. The break is a gift to the thing broken.

Sibling swap (why no sibling owns this):
- Maverick (*Can't Watch*): breaks a rule for other people's fairness; owns "can't watch", "helpless", "where people can see". Ours is for the thing, not a group.
- Subverter (*With the Bite In*): laughs at power; owns "everyone's nodding along" and the meeting / family-dinner opener.
- Rule Breaker (*Asked In*): makes something new without permission; owns "Tell you no", "the yes you don't trust". Ours clears the old, makes nothing new.
- Adolescent (*Kept*): refuses the false; owns "nodding along to something that isn't true", the slow gentle drift of the self, and "show what you love". Ours acts on a thing outside them, and the decay is the thing's, not their own.
- Thrill Seeker (*Whoopee*): owns "padded and polite", "the life that gets planned for you", "tear it up".
- Gangster (*Where You Stand*): owns the "It's never X. It's Y." fear frame.
- Fugitive (*Rent-Free*): owns Napa, the cellar, the cold, "stale" rules picked up by the new life.
- Gentle Giant (*In One Piece*): strength held back. Devil's Advocate (unauthored): argues; ours acts. Leading Edge (unauthored): spots the new; ours clears the old.
- Mirror pairing, the Trickster (*No Accident*): owns "pulling the rug out from under a room", "shake a room", "see the strings", the trick with a gift inside. Ours is no trick: a fair test, in the open.

## Hidden
They love the old thing, often more than the people defending it, and they miss it after. And the night-after doubt: did I go too far, was it mine to break? They'd never say either aloud: it would sound like regret, or like an apology to the people who are angry.

## Fear
The slow, polite death of something good: the yearly trip nobody enjoys, the team that hasn't changed in a decade, the friendship kept going on habit. Powerlessness here is being the one who sees it dying and does nothing, and so helping it die. (Not "helpless" and not "watching": the Maverick's words.)

## Gift
They give the old thing its second chance. The people who were angriest are often the ones who gain most, later. Say it out loud once (Robin's batch-caregiver rule): what their way gives others is worth something.

## The price
Blamed by the very people who loved the old thing, at the time; thanked late, if ever. The decay was nobody's fault; the break has a name on it, theirs.

## Traps

- The makeover shape (*Overnight*): they don't remake the thing; they break the assumption around it.
- Victory over France. The losers are never fools or villains: they loved French wine, as he did. Never mock the judges' notes (TIME's "Gallic gems" is TIME's sneer, not ours). Odette Kahn's ballot is told with sympathy or not at all.
- "He set out to beat France" (Bella Spurrier: his aim was to introduce wines he thought wonderful).
- Spurrier alone: Patricia Gallagher co-organised (Wikipedia; she was also a judge). One clause.

- Rent-Free's ground: no Napa cellar, no Stevenson, no cold, no Mount St. Helena; say "California" over "Napa" where possible.
- Words owned in the registry: "nodding along", "helpless", "can't watch", "tear it up", "rubble" (regular-guy-lover), "nobody dares" (lover-explorer), "the rot" (two pours), "stale" (nine pours; usable only with a fresh frame), "the truth at the bottom of the glass".
- Scenes owned: the meeting and the family dinner.

## Story test (five points)
1. Overturns an established thing everyone tolerated and that was quietly going stale.
2. Does it deliberately and with care, by a fair means, not in anger.
3. Is blamed or resented for it at the time, by people who loved the old thing.
4. The hidden half: they loved the old thing too.
5. Past the page: what grew back, and it was better, ideally the broken thing itself. (Outlaw lesson: the ending must hold after the source stops.)

## Position direction (for v1, not final)
The French judges did the breaking themselves: the test was fair, blind, and theirs. So: the next time something you love is going stale, don't break it alone. Set it up so the people who love it most can see it for themselves, then stay and go on loving it out loud. What they gain: less blame carried alone, and the thing gets its second life sooner.
