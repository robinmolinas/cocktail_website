The main source is Kerin O'Keefe, "Barbaresco Breaks Rank", *The Wine News*, 2006, with Gaja's own words quoted to her:
1. **What was going stale:** his own single-vineyard Barbarescos made the family's "historic wine" look lesser. It got called "normale" and "basic", "which I never intended".
2. **On purpose, and he said why:** in late 1999, starting with the 1996 vintage, he took his famous crus out of Barbaresco's top classification, "in support of Barbaresco", so the house would have "one Barbaresco only".
3. **The blame:** it "shocked the wine world". Critics lamented Barbaresco's lost reputation, rumours said he wanted foreign grapes, and he knew what journalists "have said and continue to say".
4. **He loved it:** O'Keefe calls him "the very winemaker who had so faithfully championed it". "Beloved" is her word, not his.
5. **What grew back:** the other producers held to the wine's character, and O'Keefe says Barbaresco came out with a stronger identity. Then, from the 2013 vintage, his children brought the three crus back into Barbaresco, now all Nebbiolo, "thanks also to the support of our father". Gaia's words: "every generation has its own path to follow" (Decanter 2019, two pieces). As of my last source, 2019, the crus were back in Barbaresco; I haven't checked their labels in 2026.
