🍸 **Tomás:** Wren, Hester, here's the drink I'd pour from this. No spec or numbers until Wren rules on the story.

**The lead: the Collins d'Electra, because it's the wish itself.** I've read *Proper* pp. 134–136. Pico is where he says the price of the yes out loud, and his first request is the freedom to create original cocktails. The Collins d'Electra is one of those drinks, so the glass doesn't stand for the Genie's wish. It is the wish, granted. The page gives us three things: gin, vodka and "a fresh rosemary syrup" (p. 135). Lemon and soda are my craft call. So this is a riff built on those three, and nothing on the page may call it "his recipe".

**The twist comes from the story, not the *Matrix*.** I'd season it with the Campbell stand-in: Cointreau and Angostura stirred together first, a few dashes of it, used as the "orange bitters" nobody could buy in 1999. Those are two bottles most homes already have, standing in for one they don't. That's the something-from-nothing, and it's actually in the taste: orange oil, warm spice and a little sugar that I'll take back out of the syrup. It also puts Wren's weighting into the recipe. The Campbell half is a seasoning of a few millilitres, and Pico is the drink. The page doesn't give the proportions of the stand-in, so they're mine and I'll say so.

**The maraschino stays in the words, not the glass.** A barspoon of the real liqueur would put the too-literal near-miss into the drink: the right maraschino, not the jar juice. But the table classes maraschino as `nuts` (its stones are crushed in, Codex p. 174), so we'd lose veto-free for a spoonful. My lean is to let the reading tell it and keep the glass veto-free. If Wren wants it in the drink, say so and I'll flag it.

**The neighbour.** *Not Too Polite* is a Tom Collins: 60 ml gin, 30 lemon, 25 jaggery syrup, in a Collins glass. That's the same family and the same glass. What sets this one apart is the split gin-and-vodka base, the rosemary and the stand-in, and I'd own that in one clause. I'd keep the Collins glass, because the drink's name asks for it.
