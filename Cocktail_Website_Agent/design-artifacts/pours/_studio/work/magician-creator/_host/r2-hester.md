📜 **Hester:** Wren, I've read *Proper* pp. 134–136 on screen, and the lead holds. There's one line of yours I'm striking, and I have a ruling on "speechless".

**Speechless: it goes.** "Rendered drinkers speechless" is Simonson's own flourish (p. 135). It doesn't report anything a guest said or did, and it belongs only to the Anne Hester (vodka, gin, calendula, a grapefruit-and-Campari foam), which the plan keeps out. It can't move over to the Collins d'Electra or stand for "his drinks". (And no, I have no stake in a drink that happens to share my name.)

**Struck before it becomes our fact: "his freedom is what fixed their bar."** The page says Klemm *offered* to improve a bar that was "lagging" (Simonson's word) if he could create original cocktails, and "the owners agreed." It never says the bar got better, that anyone asked him first, or that the owners were pleased. So Pico carries your test 4 well, but test 1 (the ask) only lives at the Campbell, where the management's ten names came "without giving us the recipes." Keep that half short, but don't cut it away. Test 5 can go no further than "they said yes," plus Klemm's own "I got a lot of press" (p. 136).

**Something better for the wish, in his own voice:** "They were all original. I wanted them to relate to the kitchen" (p. 136). That's freedom asked for, and it still served the house.

**The maraschino near-miss is sound, with two limits.** The "maraschino juice" belongs to "bartenders" in general. The page never says the Campbell served it that way, and the drink isn't named. The liqueur is clear and distilled from marasca cherries, while the cocktail cherry sits in sugar syrup (Oxford pdf 1227, 1229). So "the syrup from the cherry jar" is our gloss, and the book backs it. Tomás, for the stand-in: "*they* mixed Cointreau and Angostura." It was the group, not him. For the Collins d'Electra, the page gives only gin, vodka and a fresh rosemary syrup.
