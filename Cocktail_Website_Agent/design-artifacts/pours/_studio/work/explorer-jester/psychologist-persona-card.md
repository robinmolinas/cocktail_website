# Persona card: The Mad Inventor (explorer-jester)

Wren, round 1. Source: `persona.py explorer-jester` (essence: "The fun of exploration and discovery. You might not expect it to work, but it does."; story: Wallace in the cellar, a machine to make breakfast; "rules are closed doors"; half collapse in springs and they laugh loudest; the other half work, "to everyone's surprise, including their own"; "Nothing deflates them faster than being told to be sensible." Goal freedom, fear entrapment, drivers seek paradise / BUILD CONNECTION). Read against the Explorer ten (five drafted) and the Jester pours (*The Other Berry*, *For Kicks*, *Anyway*, *Worth the Trip*).

## The one true thing
**The long way round is the fun.** They would rather build the thing that does the job than do the job. The point isn't the toast, it's the machine that makes the toast, and they want someone in the room when it goes off. When it works, nobody is more surprised than they are, and that surprise is the best part. They don't need a problem to start. "Why not?" is the whole reason.

## Shows / hides
- **Shows:** the delight. The contraption on the kitchen table, the explanation nobody asked for, the grin. They're easy to laugh with because they laugh first.
- **Hides:** how much one sentence lands: **"You could just buy one."** (or "there's an easier way, you know"). It's said kindly, as common sense, and it deflates them faster than any collapse, because it turns the joy into a waste of time. They laugh it off and change the subject. Also hidden: that they often don't know if it'll work either. And the quiet pride when it does.

## Fear → gift
- **Fear:** the box. A life where everything comes ready-made and every job is done the quick way, so there's nothing left to build. Being told no. (The sheet's entrapment, in their size: not a cell, a shortcut.)
- **Gift:** when it goes off, the room perks up. People who'd never try the long way get to watch it, laugh, and want a go. They turn an ordinary job into an event, and they make "let's see if it works" sound like an invitation, not a risk. The driver is BUILD CONNECTION: the build is something to share.

## Versus the siblings (and the family's pours so far)
- **Conservationist (*Left Standing*):** holds back and leaves it as it was. The Mad Inventor takes it apart to see if it could be a toaster.
- **Innovator (*For Good*):** builds to get rid of an annoyance, and the fix should disappear. The Mad Inventor needs no annoyance, and the contraption is meant to be seen. *For Good* owns irritation, "the weekend to save ten minutes", "nobody remembers the problem".
- **Tourist (*Brought Home*):** one street further, and the telling at home. Not ours.
- **Pioneer (*Nothing to It*):** scared, and makes it look easy. **The Mad Inventor makes it look hard on purpose.** That's the joke. Never "make it look easy/possible".
- **Discoverer (*In a Minute*):** stops to see what a thing does. The Mad Inventor builds to see what it does. *In a Minute* owns "You wanted to see what it would do": keep that phrase out.
- **Experimentalist (*Off-Label*) and Researcher (*Serviceable*): DANGER.** "Most of what you try doesn't work" and "the tries that went nowhere" are theirs. The collapse can be laughed at once, in passing. No list, no keeping, no persistence arc.
- **Genius (unauthored):** knowledge and the question held for years. The Mad Inventor's engine is joy, not understanding. Any story person who's also a science figure must be read for the fun, never the expertise.
- **Jester pours:** *The Other Berry* (Surrealist) owns "the sensible version" in its whoYouAre and notices oddness that's already there; *For Kicks* (Wacky One) owns "expected you to grow out of it" and "normal could happen quietly"; *Anyway* (Morale Booster) owns the list of the ones that didn't take; *Worth the Trip* (Reassurer) owns the laugh that eases someone else.

## Traps
- "Mad", "crazy", "mad scientist" said to the guest (mental-health words). The tag stays the tag.
- "Inventor" or "tinkerer" as a label in the reading. Show the scene: the kitchen table, the shed, the thing built for a job a kettle could do.
- Wallace & Gromit pastiche (cheese, the dog, the cellar) and Rube Goldberg as the touchstone unless Hester ties it to a page.
- **"Sensible":** registry collision with *The Other Berry*. My word for the cage is **"you could just…"** (grep: unused in every pour). "Sensible" stays out of the reading.
- "Grow up / grow out of it" (*For Kicks*), "make it look easy" (*Nothing to It*), "see what it would do" (*In a Minute*), "the long way" appears in *Serviceable*'s reading: check before using.
- Injury or danger played for laughs.
- A show. The room's lesson is **no drink you perform** (Blazer, colour change, the Knickebein withdrawn from *The Other Berry*). A Mad Inventor drink must be **over-built where the building does real work in the taste**, safe at home, and true every time the drink is made. Not a stunt.
- Gimmick for its own sake (Robin: clever for its own sake).

## Story test
Someone on the record who **(1) chose an over-built, silly-looking way to an ordinary end, (2) when an easier way plainly existed or they'd been told no, (3) for the joy of it, with the enthusiasm on the page in their own words or a witness's, and (4) it worked better than anyone expected**, ideally with people around them perking up. Must hold: joy, not persistence; no failure lists; no injury as the fun; no showmanship-for-approval (that's the Jester family's own ground). The build either does real work in the glass or stays in the story.

## Verdict on the plan's lead: William Schmidt. Overturned (my own acceptance reversed)
I read Imbibe pdf 56–57 whole. The plan quoted half a sentence. The reporter's full line is that there's no reason for a goblet each "except it is more bother to do it that way, **and William likes to impress you with the idea that he is taking a great deal of trouble on your account**." Schmidt's own words on the iced glasses: "more to please the eye than anything else." So the motive on record is impressing the guest. That's showmanship, which my condition (1) excluded. And the rest of the page is discipline: "theatrical but precise", stand erect, move only the arms, "the fastidious, craft-obsessed, and slow bartender". That's ceremony and craft, not tinkering. No build, no surprise, no laughing at a collapse: he's a master in control. The drink then has to be performed (pour from above your head), against the room's lesson. Plus the Spanish Cocktail sits on *A Brother's Care* (whisky + sherry, stirred) and carries *Rosetta*'s absinthe. Schmidt is a Jester-primary story (the jester plan doesn't claim him): offer him there.
**Backup (Knickebein): out.** A drink the drinker performs, withdrawn from *The Other Berry* for exactly that, and raw yolk.

## Proposed lead for Hester to verify: Dave Arnold (A Proper Drink pp. 223–226; Liquid Intelligence p. 177)
Read on the page this round (Proper is reliable secondary, its quotes on the record; LI is Arnold's own voice):
- "a kid-like 'why not?' enthusiasm in everything he does" (Proper p. 224). The persona's own engine, on the record.
- He'll put a penny on every railroad track just to see what happens; he once built a periscope for deep-fry camera work, never used it, "a fact that disappoints him" (p. 224). Built for joy, no problem needed, and funny.
- The French Culinary Institute refused to buy a rotovap; he bought a used one on eBay and "got it to work somehow" (p. 224). Told no, built it anyway, it worked to everyone's surprise. His quote about being denied is too strong for the guest page: paraphrase or leave out.
- The red-hot poker, custom-made, modelled on the hot irons early American innkeepers stuck into customers' cups (p. 224). In his own book: colonial flips were heated with a poker; later boiling water took over "and something crucial was lost"; the poker is "a high-temperature flavor maker" (toasty, burned, caramel); compare it with the pan version "and you will never want the pan version again"; "When somebody is making a poker drink, everybody perks up a bit" (LI p. 177). **That's the whole persona in one page:** the ridiculous-looking way, the easy way right there (a pan), it tastes better than anyone expects, and the room perks up.
- His intent: every bizarre technique should actually improve the drink; "I want you to feel happy to be here" (Proper p. 226). The gift.
- **Guards:** the St. George fire stunt, the burns and hospital, his wife's ultimatum and "hasn't learned his lesson" stay out (injury as comedy); so do the gingerbread Nagasaki, the illegal loft and its hallway, and "charlatan". Proper's "Mad Hobbyists" phrase is never quoted (the "mad" trap). He's the author of the Mixologist's own book: own that in one clause. *For Good* uses an LI method but doesn't name him: no collision in the reading. Genius boundary: tell it for the "why not?", never for the science.
- **Drink, for Tomás (round 2, no spec yet):** the poker is the story. Whether a poker goes in the glass is his call against "no drink you perform" and home safety (LI's soldering-copper method is cheap, but it's hot metal in alcohol). My test for the glass: the over-built step has to change the taste, every time, and feel like tinkering in a kitchen, not a show for the guests. If the poker can't be made safe at home, put the toasty-caramel flavour it makes into the glass another way and keep the poker in the story.

## Position direction
Not "stop apologising" (that's *For Good* and *The Other Berry*). **When someone says "you could just…", don't defend it. Hand them the other end** (BUILD CONNECTION): the build is better with two, and they'll want a go once it works. Grep "other end", "hand them": unused.
