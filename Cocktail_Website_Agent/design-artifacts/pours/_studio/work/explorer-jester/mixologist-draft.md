## Recipe

- **serves:** 1
- **glassware:** a wide china teacup (about 200 ml), warmed with hot water first
- **contains:** `["gluten"]`

| amount | item | note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 11 g | white sugar | about 2½ level teaspoons; use 9 g if your ale tastes sweet |
| 30 ml | water | 2 tablespoons, to loosen the sugar once it's dark |
| 90 ml | malty Belgian-style abbey ale (dubbel style, 6–8%) | low in hops, as Arnold chooses for hot drinks |
| 30 ml | aged rum (40–43%) | colonial taverns heated rum with beer and sugar |
| 5 ml | fresh lemon juice | 1 teaspoon; Arnold uses acid sparingly in hot drinks |
| 3 dashes | orange bitters | |
| 1 | small pinch of fine salt | |
| 1 | strip of orange peel | squeezed over the top, then dropped in |

## Method
1. Fill the teacup with hot water to warm it. Measure everything else and set it beside the stove, because once the sugar is ready the rest goes in quickly.
2. Put the sugar in a small heavy pan over a medium-high heat. Don't stir: swirl the pan now and then as it melts, and let it go dark brown and smell toasty.
3. Take the pan off the heat and pour in the water. It will spit and steam, and the sugar will seize into a lump, so keep your face and hands back. Put the pan on a low heat and stir until the lump has melted again.
4. Pour in the ale while you stir (this goes best with two people, one pouring and one stirring). Warm it until it steams, but don't let it boil.
5. Take the pan off the heat and away from the flame. Stir in the rum, lemon, bitters and salt.
6. Empty the teacup and pour the drink in. Squeeze the orange peel over the top so its oils fall on the drink, then drop it in.

**closingLine:** *Get someone to pour the ale in while you stir. Then you both built it.*

- v1.2 (round 6): "made" became "built" (Wren). *For Good*, in this family, ends "You made it.", and *For Kicks*' tagline opens "You made it up." "Built" is the persona's own word, and "build" is ordinary bar language for making a drink. *Off-Label*'s epigraph has "built on them", but that's an epigraph, not a closing line, and the phrase isn't shared.

- It carries Wren's position (don't defend it, hand them the other end, bring them in) as something to do. The other end is the job at the pan, which step 4 really does need: one pours while the other stirs. It isn't about when to take the sugar off (*Nothing to It*'s closing line), and it has no "amber", "close" or edge-of-burning beat. Wren's own seed, *Get someone to hold the pan.*, was rejected on safety: that pan holds sugar at caramel heat, and step 3 spits.
- Avoided: "theirs" (*Fair Measure*'s "are theirs"; *Their Night*), "next" (*Next One's Mine*, the Saviour's "Don't get the next"), "hand one over" (*In Your Own Hand*), "watch" (*The Entertainer*). Wren to judge "you both built it" against y5's "Building is better with two" (it's the same idea, but it doesn't share the words).

## Names (≥3; bartender-sayable)
- **Why Not?** (Wren's, and my pick). It names the person's whole reason, and the drink is the joke in it: why not just pour the syrup? Simonson's "why not?" is in the reading, attributed, so the name recognises it without telling the turn.
- **Something Crucial** (second). It's Arnold's phrase for what the pot lost (*LI* p. 177 (pdf 181)). It names the drink: the taste that came back. And it names the person: the long way is the crucial part. Wren to judge whether quoting the reading's line in the title spends it twice.
- **Pan Style**: Arnold's own label for the kitchen version (p. 187 (pdf 191)), said with a grin. Only works for a reader who's had the story.
- **The Long Way Round**: plain, but it leans on the tagline's "easier way", so it's held.
- Checked against the registry: no name clash, and no opening shared with a sibling (*Left Standing*, *For Good*, *Brought Home*, *Nothing to It*, *In a Minute*).
