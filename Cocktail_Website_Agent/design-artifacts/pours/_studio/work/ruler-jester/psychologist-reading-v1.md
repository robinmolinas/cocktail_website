## whoYouAre

The last train's cancelled, and there are twelve of you on a cold platform. Someone sighs. You make a joke, it lands, and somewhere in the middle of it there's a plan, and everyone hears it: the café that's still open, the walk across town, a bet on who gets home first. Years later, it's the night everyone brings up.

Nobody calls it being in charge, because to them it feels like being asked along. You like it like that. Being obeyed bores you, and it would turn you into the boss. You seldom admit how much you enjoy having your hand on the wheel, and that when you make that first joke, you don't yet know it'll work. What you dread is the night it gets away from you, or the night the fun runs out and you're just someone telling people where to go. And look what you give them: around you, a plan falling apart is where the good part begins. People go further than they ever would on their own, and they come back glad they went.

## yours

1. Six hundred pounds bought you three weeks at the London Academy of Bartenders. It opened in 1996, and it never had enough money. One of its founders, Alex Turner, said they were always one student short of being able to run the next course, so the founders worked behind other people's bars at night and taught in the mornings. Eventually the school went bust.

2. The name didn't. Douglas Ankrah and Richard Hargroves had a bar called LAB as well, meant, by one account, as a living advertisement for the school. The school didn't survive. The bar did, and it was never a quiet advert. Its owners wanted a party bar, loud and busy every night. Ankrah said other bars were "very, very passively boring". Jamie Terrell, a bartender he brought in, remembered what they were after in all that noise: "the best drinks in the world". He and Ankrah wrote five cocktail menus together, with two hundred drinks on each.

3. It could have gone quiet. Early in 1999 it was slow, and hardly anyone noticed the place until good press turned it. After that, it seemed everybody in the trade ended up there, to hire, to get hired, to meet people, and it helped make London's bartenders into names people knew. Someone who saw it remembered the bartenders sipping a drink through a straw, then adding a bit more mint, a little more sugar, a dash of bitters. My favourite detail: in a room that loud, someone was still tasting. I like to think you'd have got on with them. When a plan falls through, you don't mourn it. You throw the party, and you mean the party to get somewhere.

4. The cocktail I've made for you comes the way LAB served its drinks: a big glass, piled high with crushed ice. Under the ice, fresh raspberries are crushed with vodka and lime, for a bold red, and the raspberry syrup has a pinch of toasted cumin in it, a turn you won't see coming that works out. Then it's over to you. When you make it, taste it through the straw and make one call: a few more mint leaves, a little more syrup, or a dash of bitters. That step is there as a nod to those bartenders tasting in all that noise, and the drink is built so that whichever call you make, it lands.

5. Before you go, a thought, and I mean it kindly. Somewhere near you is someone who never makes the first joke, because you always do. When the evening next falls apart, wait a moment. Leave it to them, and follow wherever it goes. You won't lose the wheel. What you'll get is something you almost never have: the fun of not knowing where you're going while someone else steers. I hope it's somewhere wonderful.

## Resonance (v1)

**The "this is me" line:** *"Nobody calls it being in charge, because to them it feels like being asked along."* It's the line I'd stop at as this guest: it says the thing they do on purpose, and it says it kindly. Runner-up: *"when you make that first joke, you don't yet know it'll work."*

**As the guest.** The platform is mine. I've done exactly that, and the plan really was in the joke. "Being obeyed bores you" is true, and I've never heard it said. The two fears are my two nightmares, and the gift sentence gives me the thing I'd never claim. LAB isn't a saint's story: it went bust, it was slow, it was loud. And the two quotes on one page are me, the party and the best drinks in the world at once. y5 asks me to give up the one thing I'm best at, for one night, and then tells me what I gain. I'd try it.

**As the sceptic.** Horoscope: the platform is a scene, "being obeyed bores you" cuts out most of the family, and the hidden like of the wheel is the Ruler's alone.
