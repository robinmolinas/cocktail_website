## whoYouAre

You're somewhere you're meant to sit still: a hotel breakfast with its little rules, a waiting room, the long middle of someone's speech. Ten minutes, and something small has caught you. The sachet that won't tear. The toaster that sends the bread round twice. You give it all your attention, as if it were the only problem in the world, because right then it is. It all goes sideways, and nobody there will forget how. You look up, and that's when you find the room.

You never set out to be funny. When the story gets told later, people assume you've improved it. You haven't. The part you laugh off is the hush: the "not now", the look from whoever you came with, the hand on your arm. Being told to sit still and behave has always sounded like being told to be less, and what you dread is managing it, and nothing happening at all. Hushing you would cost everyone else their best stories. Ask anyone which dull mornings they still tell, fondly, years later: you were at every one. And after you, nobody there has to pretend the rules made sense.

## yours

1. In 1924, at Wembley, the West Indies pavilion of the British Empire Exhibition poured the Green Swizzle. P. G. Wodehouse put Bertie Wooster there that year, in a story, trailing round after a friend whose mind was being broadened. Bertie's one joke at lunch had earned a raised eyebrow. Now it's stuffed fish and jars of seeds, and exhibitions, he admits, have never been his thing.

2. Then, rushed past a bar, he spots a man stirring long, icy glasses with a stick, and the urge to see more of him won't go away. He and his friend slip away to it, and a few glasses later Bertie declares that any son of his will be named Green Swizzle Wooster.

3. The Green Swizzle was long taken for fiction, like Bertie. It wasn't: old newspapers show it was real, and for a while quite popular back home.

4. *(waiting for Tomás's drink v1)*

5. I like to think you'd have seen the man with the stick before anyone else in the hall did, and stopped. Next time you're being steered past the one thing you'd actually like to see, look round for the other person who's had about enough. There usually is one. Go together. Give it all your attention, and if something wonderful comes of it, make a promise far too big for it. Somebody will be telling that afternoon for years, and it'll all be true.
