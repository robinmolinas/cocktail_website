## Open for y4 and the drink (round 4)

- **"The Trinidad way."** Oxford pdf 756 calls the Wembley drink Trinidadian; pdf 945 and *Imbibe!* pdf 124 disagree on origin (C3). The reading may say the drink is rebuilt from the parts of the bottled Trinidad base (Carypton: rum, lime, sugar, West Indian herbs; Oxford pdf 112). It must **never** say this is the glass Bertie drank, or which island Wembley's came from. And never "Carypton's recipe": its herbs aren't known.
- **The tangerine.** It's a tip Wondrich credits to Eleanor Early's 1937 travel book (*Imbibe!* pdf 125). It's not from 1924 or Wembley. Say "a later tip" if it's dated at all.
- **Wormwood for home use (Tomás's question).** The library can't confirm that it's sold. My web budget is spent, so I haven't checked it. The one sourced hint is Wondrich telling home readers to use "5 or 6 sprigs" or "1 ounce dried" (*Imbibe!* pdf 125, secondary). Treat availability as unsourced. **Guest note, my recommendation:** steeped wormwood is "extremely bitter" (Oxford pdf 2212), so the dose stays at about a barspoon. Thujone, found in wormwood, is "a lethal neurotoxin in high concentrations", and absinthe avoids that because thujone "resists distillation" (Oxford pdf 50). A home steep isn't distilled. No library page gives a safe level for a home steep. So: (a) the method gives the amount exactly and never says "to taste, more if you like"; (b) the substitute Wondrich gives, a wormwood-forward absinthe (pdf 125), is offered plainly beside it; (c) no health words in guest text either way, because medicine is the Caregiver family's ground. I'm flagging the steep's safety for Robin as a gap. Tomás, if you'd rather the absinthe led and the steep were optional, I'd sign either way.
- If the honest count is seven, Bertie's "at least seven ingredients" (l. 952) is usable as his joke.
