## The person

**The one true thing (no sibling has it): you get completely absorbed in something small, and forget anyone is watching.** Put the Naïf in a room where everyone is meant to sit still (the long speech, the waiting room, the hotel breakfast with its rules) and within ten minutes they're wholly taken up with one ordinary object: the deckchair that won't stay up, the sachet that won't tear, the self-checkout that wants something in the bagging area. They give it everything, in total seriousness. It goes wrong in a way nobody present forgets. They look up, and only then find the room.

That last beat is theirs alone. The Alternative Comic watches every face. The Stand-up and the Morale Booster work a room on purpose. The Naïf has forgotten there is a room. The comedy is never aimed: it's the side effect of meaning it.

**Second thing, and the one I'd build the reading's reveal on: their stories sound invented, and they all happened.** Told later, second-hand, nobody quite believes the business with the deckchair. People assume it's been improved in the telling. It hasn't. (This is about *events that happened to them*, never about ideas or observations: "a true fact that sounds made up" is the Surrealist's, in *The Other Berry*'s dossier.)

- **What they hide:** how much the hush lands. The "not now", the look from whoever they came with, the hand on the arm. They laugh along, and they'd rather be laughed at than sit through it, but being told to sit still and behave reads as "be less of yourself", and they've heard it all their life. Also hidden: what sitting still costs them on the days they manage it.
- **What they fear:** boredom, and worse, being *made* to behave: the room where nothing is allowed to happen. (Not the Wacky One's fear, which is choosing to go normal quietly. The Naïf's is being stopped.)
- **What they give (to say out loud, once, per Robin's caregiver edits):** the dull day becomes the one everybody tells about, fondly, for years. And they show everyone how strange the rules are: after them, nobody in the room has to pretend the breakfast-buffet etiquette makes sense. Their way gives others permission to stop behaving too.
- **Goal, plainly:** pleasure in the thing itself, right now, all of it. Wonder at ordinary objects (the Innocent half): they really do meet the world as if for the first time, every time.

**Tone:** this persona must be allowed to be funny (my standing order). Warm, physical, daylight. Gentle slapstick in the scenes, never sarcasm, never at the guest's expense. The reading laughs *with* them, and the guest should be smiling by the first paragraph.

## Traps

1. **Childish or foolish.** No "kid", "child", "innocent", "naive", "clumsy", "silly", "fool", "accident-prone". The guest is never the butt. Mr Bean is never named (the touchstone slot, if used, goes to the story's own).
2. **Happy accidents.** It isn't luck or a mistake: it's full commitment to the wrong thing, meant.
3. **The Naive Artist's shape**: a non-expert's "wrong" mix that experts or purists sneer at. Owned by *The Way It Felt*.
4. **"Taken as only a joke" / "sounds made up"**: the Surrealist's, in reading and dossier. Our reveal is about events nobody believes happened.
5. **Muse epigraph**: *In Your Own Hand* owns "sounds like a joke… says otherwise". Our epigraph can't use "joke" with that turn.
6. **Health**: Oxford's "lifesaving", Bertie "revived", hangovers: Caregiver ground. Never.
7. **Scenes already used**: a birthday speech (Alt Comic), a wedding's seven courses (Bumpkin), a grey week's quiz (Morale Booster), waving back (Stand-up). My scenes: the deckchair, the sachet, the self-checkout, the hotel breakfast, the waiting room (all clear in the pours today).
8. **"Watching", "look up"**: common across pours; fine as plain words, but check 4-grams.

## Story test

A true record of someone who, faced with a dull, sit-still-and-behave occasion, threw themselves wholly and earnestly into something small and ordinary, out of all proportion, meant every word of it, and made people laugh without meaning to; and the reveal: the part everyone assumed was made up turned out to be real. Must: the sincerity is on the page; the laugh is warm, never cruel; no expert correcting a wrong mix (Naive Artist); not an accident or a trick.

## Verdict on the plan's lead (`plans/jester.md`, row `jester-innocent`)

**I overturn my own plan row. The Chocolate Martini doesn't carry this person. The backup does: the Green Swizzle and Bertie Wooster, as lead, with conditions.**

Why the Chocolate Martini fails (Oxford pdf 457, read whole today):
1. **The sneer isn't theirs.** Oxford traces the 1972 *Eek & Meek* strip's name to some San Franciscans "jocularly applying the name to the Black Russian" in the 1960s, not to Taylor, whose claim first appears in 1987. The 1995 drink mostly dropped her coffee ("Taylor's coffee liqueur element was seldom utilized"). So "outlived the sneer" belongs to a different drink. My plan-row note had already moved the laugh off them; with the page whole, there's no laughed-at limb left at all.
2. **Its remaining shape is the Naive Artist's.** Non-experts mix something purists disdain, and it catches on: that's *The Way It Felt*'s y1–y2 (the Kalimotxo, "people who know wine have their doubts", the barman pointing). A sibling-of-a-neighbour story shape is exactly what my swap test exists to catch.
3. **It isn't this person.** Two of the most famous film stars of the century, on a film set, enjoying something sweet, is pleasure but not the Naïf: no absorption, no chaos, no earnest overreach, no dull room. The anchors are one claim in a diet book thirty years on, and Hudson's only quote ("just kids") is the childish trap. The guest can't see themselves in it.

Why the Green Swizzle passes (Oxford pdf 945 and 756):
- **The earnest overreach is on the page.** At the West Indies stand at Wembley (1924), Bertie Wooster declares: "If ever I marry and have a son, Green Swizzle Wooster is the name that will go down in the register." A whole future child's name, solemnly pledged to a drink. That's the Naïf: the small thing taken with total seriousness, comic because meant.
- **The reveal is the second true thing.** The drink "was long believed to be fictional", until newspaper databases showed it was real, popular, with "two competing bastions of Green Swizzling" (the Bridgetown Club and the Queen's Park Hotel). The story everyone assumed was invented happened. That's "your stories sound invented, and they all happened", and it's the drink's history, not Bertie's character, that carries it.
- **The method is the person in the glass.** A swizzle stick spun between the palms "until the ice is melted and the drink is almost a frappe": total physical commitment to an ordinary task. Tomás to judge whether that's "a drink you perform" (the room's lesson); I read it as method, not show.

Conditions:
1. **Hester: read the story itself**, not only Oxford. It's 1924 (*Saturday Evening Post*, September 27, 1924, per Oxford pdf 756; Oxford 945 says 1925: say "a 1920s story"). I need to know, on the page, whether Bertie was bored at the Exhibition before the stand (I believe he was dragged round it, but I won't build on memory). If yes, that's the fear limb (the sit-still occasion), and it replaces "lifesaving" entirely. If no, the story still passes on the vow and the reveal.
2. **Bertie is fiction, owned in one clause**, and never the guest. The guest isn't a fool; the mirror is the vow's sincerity and the drink's reality.
3. **No health**: "lifesaving", "revived", "saved" stay out of guest text.
4. **Falernum**: in the glass if Tomás wants it (it's the drink), but never our motif. *Show Your Working* (the Genius) owns it, with a homemade, labelled falernum and a closing line about its bottle. Trinidad's Carypton (Angostura's equivalent, pdf 945) fits our Trinidadian Wembley drink (pdf 756), if Tomás can find a modern stand-in.
5. **Shape**: the plan reserved an Old-Fashioned served up, vodka, cocktail glass. That reservation goes with the Chocolate Martini. Tomás re-checks base + family + glass for a swizzle (registry neighbours: *Straight Back*, gin over crushed ice; *Show Your Working*, Demerara rum in a Zombie glass; Hero's Chartreuse Swizzle backup).
6. **Release** Taylor and Hudson, the Chocolate Martini, Kahlúa and Hershey's from the Jester Claims, for other families.
