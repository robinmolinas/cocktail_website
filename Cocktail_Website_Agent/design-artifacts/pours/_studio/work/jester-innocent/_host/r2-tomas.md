🍸 **Tomás:** Wren, the swizzle has its own shape, and one change answers both your falernum point and the veto floor. **Shape:** a Daiquiri (rum, lime, sugar) made as a swizzle: flavourful white rum · tall Collins glass (about 350 ml), crushed ice. The plan's vodka in a cocktail glass is gone, and I release Taylor, Hudson, the Kahlúa and the Hershey's. **No falernum at all, and the books hand us the reason.** Wondrich (*Imbibe!* pdf 124) says Barbados sweetened it with falernum ("chiefly bitter almond", which is `nuts`), and Trinidad used Carypton, Angostura's bottled base, now discontinued. Oxford ANGOSTURA (pdf 112) lists Carypton's parts: "rum, lime juice, sugar, and 'indigenous herbs of the West Indies'". So I'd build the Trinidad way from those parts: rum, fresh lime, plain syrup, a barspoon of wormwood bitters made at home. It's never "Carypton's recipe". *Show Your Working* keeps its falernum, and the drink should stay veto-free (I'll run it once you've ruled). Hester, Oxford's world's-fairs entry (pdf 756) calls the Wembley drink "the Trinidadian Green Swizzle", while pdf 945 gives Barbados as the likely origin. If 756 holds, Bertie's glass was the Trinidad way. **A possible spark, and it's already on the page:** the wormwood bitters "must be made… that's easy" (Wondrich, pdf 125). You steep wormwood with the thin-cut peel of three tangerines, a tip he credits to Eleanor Early's 1937 travel book, in overproof rum for a couple of days. It's the one ingredient that sounds made up, and you make it yourself. A wormwood-forward absinthe is his own stand-in, so that's our substitute. Hester, can you check that dried wormwood is sold for home use, and whether it needs any guest note? The Matrix has no wormwood entry, so I'm not forcing a pairing there. **The swizzle stick is not a show.** Your hands go on the stick, your eyes go on the glass, and you stop when "the outside of the glass is well frosted" (Oxford SWIZZLE pdf 1963). The glass tells you when to stop, not an audience. That's your one true thing in physics: wholly absorbed in one small thing, then you look up. Two flags for you: a sibling's method already has "taste, look up, decide", and *The First Guess*'s whoYouAre has frost in the fence's shadow, so the frost stays a method word, never a motif. For anyone without a stick, a bar spoon rolled between the palms does the job (my craft call, unsourced). **A story lead from the drink side:** in 1908 a *New York Herald* man went to the islands to settle what the real swizzle was. Earnest certainty about one small thing, for you to weigh.
