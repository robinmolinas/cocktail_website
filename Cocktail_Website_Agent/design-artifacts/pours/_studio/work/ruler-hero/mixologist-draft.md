The drink is a New York Sour: bourbon, lemon and sugar, shaken and served up, with a thin float of dry red wine resting on top. The bourbon is Jim Beam's white label, the whisky that went out under his own name once the old one was gone. Everything underneath is the steady sour. One thing is allowed to show: the red line on top. A Chicago bartender told a reporter in 1883 that the red wine "makes the drink look well and it gives it a better taste" (*Imbibe!* pdf 96).

**Glassware:** small stemmed wine glass (about 250 ml), chilled. No ice in the glass.
**Contains:** veto-free.

## Recipe

| amount | item | note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 60 ml | Jim Beam bourbon (white label, 40% as of about 2021) | recommended: the whisky that took his own name. Or any Kentucky straight bourbon of 40% or stronger |
| 22.5 ml | fresh lemon juice | |
| 20 ml | simple syrup | equal parts sugar and water, stirred until clear |
| 15 ml (1 tbsp) | dry red wine | a young, dry, juicy red such as a Cabernet Sauvignon. Floated on top, never shaken in |

## Method

1. Chill the glass in the freezer, or fill it with ice water while you mix.
2. Put the bourbon, lemon juice and simple syrup in a shaker. Fill it with ice.
3. Shake hard for about 12 seconds, until the shaker is frosty on the outside.
4. Empty the glass. Strain the drink into it, through a small sieve as well, so no chips of ice get in. Let it settle for a few seconds.
5. Hold a spoon upside down just above the drink, and pour the red wine slowly onto the back of it, so the wine spreads out and rests on top as a thin red layer. Don't stir. No garnish.
