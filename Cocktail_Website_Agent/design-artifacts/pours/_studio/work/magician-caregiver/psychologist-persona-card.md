# Persona card: The Fairy Godparent (magician-caregiver), Wren, round 1

Sources: persona.py (xlsx row), my family sketch (`plans/work/magician/psychologist-sketches.md`), plan row (`plans/magician.md`), Oxford PINK LADY COCKTAIL pdf 1521 (read this round).

## The one true thing
They change someone's big moment without being asked, and they make sure the moment stays the other person's. The help is unasked by definition, so it carries a private question nobody else hears: *was it mine to do?* They usually leave before the answer arrives.

## Hide / fear / give
- **Hide:** that they acted without permission, and that they worry about it afterwards. Not a wish for thanks. A wish to know it was right.
- **Fear:** meddling. A kindness that turns out unwanted, or turns into something they never meant once it's out of their hands (the sheet's "unintended results").
- **Give:** the other person gets a night that is wholly theirs and owes nobody for it. Help nobody has to thank you for is help nobody has to repay. (Robin's rule: say this out loud once.)

## Versus siblings (same primary)
- **Genie:** asked, and arrives loud. The Godparent is never asked and stays unseen.
- **Omnipotent One:** arranges the whole picture for everyone. The Godparent changes one person's evening.
- **Trickster (No Accident):** the trick has a gift inside and fears being ignored. The Godparent fears the opposite of being ignored: having overstepped. No strings, no hoax, no "three moves ahead".
- **Clairvoyant:** sees it coming. The Godparent acts on what someone needs before it's said, which is noticing, not foresight.
- Entertainer / Illusionist: performance. The Godparent's work has no audience.

## Cross-family traps (the real danger)
- **The Geisha (Hoping You'd Come):** love in the getting-ready, fear of the ready room with nobody in it, position "ask". A surprise party sits right on this. Keep apart: the Geisha hopes to be joined; the Godparent wants the night to be someone else's and fears having intruded. Never "expected", "getting ready", "before anyone arrives", an empty room.
- **The Healer (Till Spring):** disguises help so it's easy to take, waits for it to work. The Godparent never offers a yes at all. The question is permission, not patience. Keep "easy to take" and "before they do" out.
- **The Rescuer (Off Duty):** "You've already gone." Don't use "gone" or "already" for slipping away.
- **The Sculptor (Quite Alive):** a thing made to measure for one. Here it's a moment arranged, and the drink isn't the gift.
- **The Craftsman (Not Only the Way):** "stop saying it was nothing". The position must not be "take the credit".
- **Four Shares:** "You're allowed to…" is owned.

## Persona traps
"Godmother" or any gendered echo; Cinderella as the mirror; matchmaking or romance; pumpkin, wand, "bibbidi"; midnight once at most; martyrdom ("never thanked, poor you"); job labels (organiser, fixer, party planner).

## Story test (unchanged from the plan)
(1) someone quietly changed another person's big moment, unasked and uncredited; (2) the moment is real and theirs, the helper absent from it (absent from the record counts); (3) a decision made without permission, which could have been overstepping; (4) the help did no harm; (5) the cost: they never saw it land, or it became something they never meant.

## Verdict on the lead (Pink Lady, Hazel Dawn's surprise party): accept, with a sharper reading
Re-read against the page (Oxford pdf 1521). Passes 1-5. What I see now that the plan row didn't say:
- **Test 5 is the sheet's own fear, in the record.** The drink made for her night is "probably not the one that ultimately entered the books". The name went on and became something else: gin and applejack (Straub 1913), then by Prohibition's end "crude but effective", and a reputation Oxford calls a label. That is exactly "a kindness that turns into something you never meant once it's out of your hands".
- **And what held was the night.** Decades later, Hazel Dawn still told it as a surprise party in her honour. The recipe drifted; the party is what she kept. That's the reassurance the Godparent never gets, and the reading can give it.
- **Question for Hester:** when did she tell Grimes's correspondent? She died in 1988, so it must be late in her life, but "decades later" needs a source or a signpost. The plan cites "Grimes's 2009 correspondence": check what that is.
- **Ruling kept:** the reading's person is whoever arranged the party and made the drink, never Hazel Dawn. Every line about them is signposted. We don't say they left the party (unknown): their absence is from the record.

## Position (provisional; the room shapes it)
A surprise is the one kind of help where you get to watch it land: the moment the door opens. So: next time, stay for that moment. See their face. That answers the worry better than doing less. Not "take the credit" (Craftsman), not "ask" (Geisha), not "you're allowed to" (Four Shares). Backup position, if the room prefers the fear angle: what becomes of it after isn't yours to answer for; the night was.
