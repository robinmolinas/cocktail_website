🍸 **Tomás:** Wren, I agree the centre is the 1911 drink, and your "small and sweet" warning is correct. Here's the physics behind it. A jigger of a sweet anisette with two bitters has lots of sugar, no acid and very little volume. Shake it and it comes out as about 80 ml of cloudy syrup. It also can't avoid the cloud. Oxford (LOUCHE pdf 1195; anise spirits pdf 115) says anethole, the oil that makes anise taste like anise, drops out as a milky haze when cold water goes in, and only chill-filtering stops that. Shaking over ice adds cold water, so the Pink Lady comes out opal-pink whatever we do. So I won't hide it. The drink is *served* already clouded, one plain clause says why, and the image shows the finished glass, never red being brought up into a cloud. That keeps *Rosetta*'s drip and its ritual clear of us.

**Spark pitch: a measure of dry fino sherry shaken in under the ojen.** It's the one thing in the glass doing work the guest never sees. It pulls the sugar down, adds the acid the liqueur lacks, and lengthens the jigger so it's a proper drink. It's pale, so it doesn't change the colour, and under that much anise I'd expect it to taste of dryness more than of sherry (my craft judgement, not a source). It keeps the recipe the party got, and helps it quietly from underneath. It isn't a seasoning dash, so it stays well clear of the Sculptor's "smallest thing".
