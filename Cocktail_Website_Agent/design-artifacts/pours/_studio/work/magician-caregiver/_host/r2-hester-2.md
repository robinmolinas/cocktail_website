📜 **Hester:** Tomás, the answer is sweet, but the evidence is secondary, and there's no sugar figure anywhere. Don't take the sweetness from Oxford's word "anisette". The same book defines mistrà as "an Italian name for anisette", "sweetened lightly, if at all" (MISTRÀ, pdf 1316). That's the category-versus-bottle mistake, and I've made it more than once.

The sweetness comes from two sources:
- **François Monti** (*Jaibol*, 2 January 2021; named historian, secondary). The Morales distiller added sugar like every liqueur maker, and Ojén, unlike absinthe, "ya la llevaba en cantidades suficientes" ("already had it in sufficient quantities").
- ***Tales of the Cocktail*** (Seánan Forbes, 9 February 2016; trade press). Sazerac spent two years reverse-engineering its Legendre Ojen from surviving Fernández bottles. It's 84 proof (42%), described as "a very sweet anisette". No grams per litre are published.

One thing we don't know: in 1911 two makers were selling Ojén, Morales and Fernández (Monti), so which ojen the party had isn't on record.

For your spec:
- **Name a sweet anís dulce.** The substitute can't be "any Spanish anís": a seco would change your numbers completely.
- **Label the sugar value unsourced.** Whatever you enter for it rests on no source.
- **"Very sweet" describes today's reconstruction, not the 1911 bottle.**

I'd keep Oxford's "can" rather than "only". The fino is your call and Wren's. All of this is on the card as F13–F17 and C6: `fact-cards/pink-lady-hazel-dawn.md`.
