## whoYouAre
You rarely set out to start anything. You just like what you like a little before everyone else does: a song, a way of dressing, a word, a place nobody goes yet. You wear it or play it or order it because it's yours. Then, a while later, you notice other people doing it too, and it honestly surprises you every time.

What nobody sees is what happens next. Once it's everywhere, it's usually done a bit worse, and the people whose taste you respect start rolling their eyes at it. Quietly, you go off it. You've let go of things you loved just because too many people started loving them. What you can't stand is being told what to like, and that includes being told to stop. What you give people is the nerve to love something before anyone has said it's allowed.

## yours
1. Most likely in 1988, at the Odeon, a restaurant in lower Manhattan, a bartender named Toby Cecchini heard about a drink from San Francisco called the Cosmopolitan. The way he tells it, it was cheap vodka with bottled lime and grenadine, and he didn't think much of it. So he remade it the way the bar made its Margaritas: a lemon vodka that was new that year, fresh lime, Cointreau, and a splash of the sweetened cranberry juice the bar kept for another drink. It was for the staff.

2. Then customers started asking for it, which puzzled him. Other bars began making it, "wrong and badly", he says. By 1990 it was all over New York, and it became the one drink of its time that nearly everyone knew by name. The bartenders who came after turned against it, flavoured vodka, carton juice and all. Young ones would shout at him for it, and he'd apologise. For years he played it down. In 2014 he said that five years earlier he'd have asked to be "unshackled" from it.

3. Then that changed. He still makes it, at his own bar in Brooklyn, and he likes it tart. I like to think he found out what you keep finding out: going off something because of who else has it is just another way of letting them choose for you.

4. So yours keeps his proportions: two parts lemon vodka to one each of orange liqueur, fresh lime and cranberry, shaken hard and served in a wide, shallow glass, on the sharp side. [SPARK: Tomás's one touch, told as the mark that makes it yours, never as a correction of his.]

5. So here's my position. The next time something of yours turns up everywhere, and the eye-rolling starts, don't quietly let it go. Wear it, play it, order it anyway, out loud. You never gave something up because everyone else did. Don't give it up because everyone else found it.
