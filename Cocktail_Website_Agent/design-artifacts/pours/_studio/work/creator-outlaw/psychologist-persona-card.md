# Persona card: The Trend Setter (creator-outlaw)

Wren, round 1. Source: `persona.py creator-outlaw` (essence, story, goal Innovation, fear Status Quo/Mediocrity; secondary fears Powerlessness, Dependence, Restrictions; drivers "provide structure / LEAVE A MARK"), plus the authored Creator siblings and the Trickster (the other Outlaw pour).

## Core truth
They get there early, when there's nobody else there yet, and the hardest part isn't that lonely stretch. It's the day everyone arrives. Then they're tempted to leave something they still love just because it isn't only theirs any more.

## Shows / hides
- **Shows:** ease. The jacket, the song, the way of doing it, worn as if it cost nothing. The "effortless cool" in the source.
- **Hides:** that it isn't effortless. There's a stretch where the new thing just looks odd on them and nobody follows yet, and they carry on anyway. And the other half nobody sees: the small drop in the stomach when the thing they found turns up everywhere, done a little worse.

## Hidden fear → hidden gift
- **Fear:** being absorbed. Turning into one more copy of the crowd they started (Status Quo / Mediocrity), and underneath that, having their choices decided by other people (Outlaw: dependence, restrictions).
- **The trap in the fear:** running from every crowd is still letting the crowd decide. Following it and fleeing it are the same leash.
- **Gift:** they go first, in public, before it's safe. Other people borrow that nerve. Most people who "follow" them aren't copying the thing. They're copying the permission.

## The "real me" line to aim for
> *You've let go of things you loved because too many people started loving them.*

Runner-up (the gift side): *People don't copy what you do. They borrow your nerve.*

## Position (where the reading should end, Ryu's order)
A hard truth told kindly: **leaving because everyone arrived is still letting everyone decide.** The proposal: next time the thing you found turns up everywhere, stay with it. Let them catch up, and keep it anyway. Freedom for this person is keeping what they love when it's no longer rare.

## Truth vs. legend
The true story, including the uncomfortable part (who got there first, who got the credit, how it went mainstream). They'd enjoy a legend only if it's owned as one. What they'd hate is the tidied-up brand history.

## Tone
Source: inspirational (Creator) + provocative, bold, direct (Outlaw). For the bartender: direct and a little dry, the way you talk to someone who doesn't need flattering. Short lines. Some wit, never gushing. No "cool" (the word itself is the cliché they'd roll their eyes at).

## Distinct from siblings
- **The Visionary** (*Down the Line*): a long dream others build on over years; the fear is making it smaller. Here it's small, everyday things, done early and in public, and the fear is the thing getting *bigger* than them and no longer theirs. Nobody builds on the Trend Setter's plan; they just start doing it too.
- **The Experimentalist** (*Off-Label*): already owns "people start saying what you say" and "being asked to repeat yourself feels like a door closing". So: no influence-on-friends line, no fear of repeating. The Trend Setter is happy to repeat a thing for years, as long as it's still theirs.
- **The Obsessed** (*Rosetta*): already owns "people start to care about things a little more than they planned to". The Obsessed never lets go of one thing; the Trend Setter's pull is to let go too soon.
- **The Trickster** (*No Accident*, the other Outlaw): owns rule-breaking with a plan, "rather misunderstood than boring", "being ignored". The Trend Setter breaks no rule on purpose and isn't afraid of being ignored; being ignored is the early stretch, and they're fine there.
- **The Makeover Artist** (*Overnight*) owns "looked like a mistake / the ugly middle". **The Surrealist** (*The Other Berry*) owns "sounds weird, then a minute later it doesn't". So the early stretch must not be told as "it looked wrong, then it was right". Tell it as *being alone with it*, not as being vindicated.

## Traps
- "Cool", "iconic", "ahead of the curve", "influencer": labels and compliments. Recognise what they *do* early, don't praise their taste.
- Fashion-only framing. The source's example is a fashion figure; the reading must work for someone whose early thing is a band, a word, a recipe, a route to work.
- A reading about being followed or admired (that's flattery and the Experimentalist's ground).
- Vindication ("they all came round in the end") as the payoff: it's the Surrealist's and the Makeover Artist's.
- Anything that makes the drink a performance (room lesson: no drink you perform).

## Round 2 revision (after Hester's Petraske and Tomás's Ward)
- **Story pick: Petraske's 50-50 Martini** over Ward's Oaxaca Old-Fashioned. Ward's fit is the *drink* being copied; Petraske's is a *person* choosing: he dressed as he dressed, poured it wet when the whole trade went dry, and it spread under someone else's name. That's the guest.
- **Core truth, sharpened.** The Trend Setter isn't chasing new ("innovation is not what this is about", Petraske, *A Proper Drink* p. 86). They keep what's theirs while the crowd moves. Sometimes what they "start" is something everyone else dropped. That's a clean split from the Experimentalist (never the same twice).
- **The symmetry that carries the position:** they never dropped something because everyone else did, so they shouldn't drop it because everyone else picked it up.
  - Draft position line: *You never gave something up because everyone else did. Don't give it up because everyone else found it.*
- **"Looked insane" is a fact, not the payoff.** The reading tells the stretch as being alone with it, never "and then they all came round" (Makeover / Surrealist ground).
- **The name going to someone else:** a true, quiet hurt, but never a grievance. It's not about credit.
- **Open for Hester:** once the 50-50 was everywhere as the Fitty-Fitty, and speakeasies were copied everywhere, what did Petraske do? Did he keep pouring it at Milk & Honey? Sourced, it's the reading's turn; unsourced, it stays out.
- **Open for Tomás:** the Sculptor's *Quite Alive* is also a gin-and-vermouth Martini in this family (60 gin / 30 sweet vermouth / Fernet, coupe). The spark has to put daylight between them. The fridge-kept small bottle of vermouth is the right kind of detail: keeping something fresh so it stays good.

## Round 3 revision: Cecchini's Cosmopolitan
- **I drop Petraske.** Tomás's numbers (22.8% / 1.05 g / 0.21%, OUT) and the second gin-and-vermouth coupe beside *Quite Alive*, plus Hester striking "looked insane" herself, and no recorded moment where he chose to keep it or leave. Cecchini has that moment.
- **Why Cecchini is the guest:** made for the staff, not to start anything ("puzzled" when customers ordered it); watched it made "wrong and badly" everywhere; the people whose opinion mattered turned on it; wanted to be "unshackled"; then "That changed", and he still makes it his way.
- **The wound, corrected by Hester's limit:** it isn't only the crowd arriving. When something you started is everywhere, the people you respect start rolling their eyes at it, and you're tempted to disown it. Wanting out is still letting them decide how you feel about your own thing.
  - "Real me" line, v2: *The moment something you started is everywhere, part of you wants to disown it.*
  - Position (unchanged spine): take it back. Make it your way, even now it's everyone's.
- **Guards:** credit is never the point ("shirked the credit" supports that). No TV show and no decade-of-fame shorthand. "Wrong and badly" and "people still get it wrong" are told with a smile, never as grievance. Anything in the present tense ("today he makes it") carries its source's date.
