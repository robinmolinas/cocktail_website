| lineage (the before) | A drink called the Cosmopolitan was already around San Francisco: a newspaper writer met it in 1987 as a Kamikaze with cranberry, and Cecchini remembers it as cheap vodka, bottled lime and grenadine (accounts differ). Most likely in 1988, at the Odeon in New York, he remade it: a lemon vodka new that year, the fresh lime and Cointreau the bar used in its Margaritas, and a little of the sweetened cranberry it kept for another drink. | He didn't invent something from nothing to be first. He fixed a thing he didn't think much of, for himself, with what was to hand. | lens: The real me · soughtFor: Taste |
| gesture (the staff drink) | He made it as a staff drink, and was puzzled when customers started ordering it. | Going first is private before it's public. The Trend Setter isn't launching anything; they're just liking it. | gravity: Instinctive · drawnToward: Freedom |
| the spread | Other downtown bars began making it, "wrong and badly" in his words. By 1990 it was all over New York, and it became the one cocktail of its era that was a household name. | Once it's everyone's, it's usually done a bit worse. Said plainly, no bitterness. | drawnToward: Change · texture: Bright |
| the turn (the eye-rolling) | The young craft bartenders who came after rejected it (the flavoured vodka and the sweetened cranberry), and some bars wouldn't stock cranberry so they'd never have to make it. For years bartenders gave him grief for it and he'd say sorry. He shirked the credit, and in 2014 he said that five years earlier he'd have asked to be "unshackled" from it. | The real wound: not the crowd, but the people you respect rolling their eyes, and the pull to disown your own thing. Wanting out still lets them decide. | lens: The real me · soughtFor: Courage |
| reveal ("That changed") | "That changed" (Simonson). As of 2026 (Lascelles, secondary) he still makes it at his own Long Island Bar in Brooklyn: 2:1:1:1, in a saucer coupe, with a lemon twist, and he likes it "shrill". | Take it back and make it your way, even now it's everyone's. Nothing on record says he ever stopped making it, and that's the point: he stopped letting others decide how he felt about it. | lens: My future self · texture: In control |
| spark (ours, not his) | Three basil leaves, shaken with the drink and strained out. The *Flavor Matrix* gives basil as a surprise pairing for the berry family, cranberry included (pdf 52). The proportions stay his (2:1:1:1). | His spine, your mark. Keeping something and still making it yours: a seasoning, not a correction. | texture: Sharp · flavours: Fresh |

## Guards (for the reading and Checks)
- **Never "invented"** in our voice (F7 is his claim; C2: DeGroff and Cook). Never "the only one". "Most likely 1988", never flat.
- **C1:** the grenadine and the "swap" for cranberry are his account only (the 1987 paper already had cranberry). Signpost them wherever they're restated.
- **C4:** no TV show. It was already everywhere in New York by 1990.
- **Not on record:** "before everyone sweetened it"; "carton"; "shout"; that he ever stopped *making* it (he shirked the *credit*); that the bar already had the Citron; that the eye-rollers were people "he respected" (our reading of "young bartenders").
- **Present tense** (Long Island Bar, "shrill", the saucer coupe) is one secondary source, 2026: dated in the text, listed as secondary in the sources.
- **The drink:** "his proportions", never "his recipe" or "the original" (basil, and a generic lemon vodka and orange liqueur, are ours). Absolut Citron and Cointreau only as *recommended* named bottles (F5: the Citron's release dates the drink), with the substitutes given as styles.
- **Living person:** quotes only as printed, each under 15 words.
- **Siblings:** no "people start saying what you say" (Off-Label), no "go back to it" (Off-Label's y5), no vindication (Overnight, The Other Berry). Cranberry is *Rosetta*'s ingredient, not its motif: keep the cranberry here as juice from the shelf, never layered or "brought up" (Rosetta's closing line).
