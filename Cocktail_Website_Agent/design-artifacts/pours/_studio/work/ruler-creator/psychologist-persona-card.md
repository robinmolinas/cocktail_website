# Persona card: The Perfectionist (ruler-creator)

Wren, round 1, 2026-10-01. Sources: persona.py row (story, goal Control, fear Revolution, Anna Wintour, drivers "provide structure", imagery: marked-up proofs, clean grids, editing); plan row `plans/ruler.md`; *A Proper Drink* pp. 101–103 and Oxford COLLINS, WAYNE (pdf 557), both re-read whole this round.

## Who this is
Someone who can't let the careless version go out. Their standard sets the rule of the room, and it reads as cold: the no, the sent-back draft, the "not like that". From the inside it's the opposite. A sloppy thing hurts them a little, like a crack in something (the sheet: "a small disorder, a crack in the world"), and what they're guarding is the person at the other end, who'll never know what was refused for them.

## The one true thing (no sibling shares it)
**Their strictness is for someone else, and they'd rather be thought cold than explain it.** The standard isn't their taste being indulged. It's a promise to whoever receives the thing. They keep the reason to themselves because saying it out loud sounds soft or self-praising, so the no arrives without its why.

## What they hide
- That it's care. They let people think it's ego, or taste, because "I did it for you" sounds like asking for thanks.
- That it costs them: the flaw nobody else saw keeps them up. They notice every crack and mention only some.
- That they're hardest on their own work, before anyone else's.

## What they fear
Something careless going out under a name that's meant to mean something (theirs, their team's, the word on the menu). Losing hold of the result once it leaves them. (Register trap: don't write "your name on it"; *In Your Own Hand* owns it.)

## What they give
A room where good is normal. People's work gets better near them, often without knowing why. The guest at the end gets the real one. (Robin's batch-caregiver rule: say this out loud once, plainly, and say it's worth something.)

## The siblings (primary: the other ten of this family) and how this one differs
- **Diva** (closest): insists on how the whole picture should feel; thought difficult, proved right. Perfectionist: the unseen detail, rarely proved right in public, never thanked. Diva is seen insisting; Perfectionist mostly isn't seen at all.
- **Judge:** checks what's real, protects people from fakes. Perfectionist isn't exposing a lie; it's keeping a name worth something. Keep the fruit "Martini" fight off fraud and truth.
- **Reformer** (changes the institution), **Revolutionary** (overthrows, becomes the rule; scorn for peers: Downey, left free), **Noble Ruler** (keeps a promise of equal places), **Caring Leader** (calm in the heat), **Charismatic** (setback into adventure; LAB is theirs), **Magnetic** (warmth, one at a time), **Legend** (dismissed, then the crisis), **People's Representative** (crosses camps).

## Cousins with Creator in them (where the real collisions are)
- **The Auteur** (*Making the Calls*): theirs all the way through, credit and blame, redoes others' parts at night; ending lets someone else make the second. Perfectionist sets the bar *others* meet; never "I'll do it myself", never "let someone else make it".
- **The Craftsman** (*Not Only the Way*): hides **pride** ("Pride feels like making it about you, so you call it 'just doing it properly'"). Perfectionist hides **care**. Opposite direction, same neighbourhood: no "properly", no "about you" in guest lines.
- **Visionary** (*Down the Line*) owns "see the finished thing first", which is the sheet's own first sentence. Don't use it.
- **Old Master** (questions own classic), **Minimalist** (takes away; "stop cutting"; owns clear-ice trimming), **Obsessed** (in love with the making), **Sculptor** (one person, made to measure).

## Traps
1. Snobbery. No contempt for the fruit drink or anyone who loved it. "Serious drinkers", "classicist", "jungle room", LAB scorn stay out.
2. Diagnosis: no "perfectionism", "control", "high standards" as a label. The name already says it; the reading mustn't repeat it.
3. Flattery: "impeccable taste", "eye for detail". Say what they do, and for whom.
4. The cheap position: "let it go", "lower the bar", "good enough is fine". It betrays the person. The position keeps the standard and gives the reason.
5. Overstating the page: Collins's article gives a naming reason ("I didn't like that it was called a Martini"), not a guest-care reason. The "it's about the guests" line is **Chappell's**, said to the young Collins about the Martini. Never say Collins wrote the article for the guests.
6. High Holborn's closing is the restaurant's failure (pp. 102–103), never his cost.
7. Registry phrases taken: "your name (on it)", "properly", "about you", "the reason", "the finished", "stopped" (*Go On*), "tell them/say" as closing verbs.

## Story test
Must, on the page: (a) a standard that others came to meet; (b) a refusal of something popular to protect it; (c) a reason that's care for the receiver, not vanity, on record; (d) a careless thing carrying a name that's meant to mean something (the fear). Signpost-only: any cost in warmth. Out: redoing others' work, revising one's own classic, taking away, a vision handed on, being proved right, unmasking fakes.

## Verdict on the plan's lead: Wayne Collins, accept, with the reveal moved
Re-read pp. 101–103 and pdf 557 whole. All four musts are on the page:
- (a) Oxford: Mixxit "driving up service standards" in the UK trade; Conigliaro: High Holborn "influenced a lot of people", "its heart was so in the right place".
- (b) He "hated the fruit Martini" and wrote an article in *Class* bashing it.
- (c) and the reveal Hester's row underuses: p. 102, as Collins tells it, the young bartender asked "Why would anyone drink gin and vermouth?", and Chappell answered "You better start liking it. It's not about you, is it? It's about the guests." The standard he later defended wasn't his own taste. It was handed to him, against his taste, for the guest. That is this guest's hidden truth in one scene, and no sibling has it.

## Direction for the position (mine, to test in round 2)
Keep the standard and **give the no its reason.** Chappell's line made a bartender because he said why. The Perfectionist's no looks cold only because the why stays inside. Gain: they're understood, and others start holding the bar for the same reason. Second half, the yes: don't only refuse the careless one; put the real one in front of people (the drinks not on lists around London). Closing verb must dodge "tell/say".
