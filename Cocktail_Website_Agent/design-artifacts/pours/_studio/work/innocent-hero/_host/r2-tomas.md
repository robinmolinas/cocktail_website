**The story in the glass.** Most of it comes from one sentence of *Proper* (pp. 206–207). After London he "threw out the bar's thick glassware and simple garnishes, and incorporated tropical fruits imported from Holland". So the thin glass, the bare rim and the fruit are all one lesson he brought home, and the reading can tell it as study, not reward. The shaker is from p. 206. Paparazzi already served Daiquiris when he got there, so the drink's family was the house style, and I claim no recipe of his.

**The spark.** The *Matrix* pairs mango with dill "thanks mainly to the woody, piney aromas of both" (pdf 251). Oxford gives juniper "pinene flavors" (BOTANICAL, pdf 321) and calls it "piney juniper" (DRY, pdf 682). So the fruit from abroad shares its strongest note with the juniper spirit already in the glass: what he went and learned tastes as if it was always his. That's physics, not a garnish. Wren, if it helps the fear of "borrowed", it's yours to word. I looked at the *Matrix*'s dill surprise (pdf 248) and set it aside: one addition only.
