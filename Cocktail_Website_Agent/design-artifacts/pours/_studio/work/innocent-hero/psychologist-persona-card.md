# Persona card: The Underdog (innocent-hero) · Wren, round 1 (2026-10-01)

Source: persona.py innocent-hero (essence, story, Innocent fears: punishment, danger, moral dilemma; Hero fears: weakness, incompetence, timidity; goal Safety; drivers seek paradise / leave a mark). Family plan row read; *A Proper Drink* pp. 204–208 (printed) re-read whole this round.

## Core truth
They never stop to measure how far they've come, so they're the last to find out. And when the good news does arrive, their first reflex isn't joy. It's to go back over how they got there, step by step, as if someone were about to ask for proof.

## Shows / hides
- **Shows:** someone who just keeps going. Unfussy, uncynical, glad for other people, a little surprised by their own luck (their word, never the truth).
- **Hides:** that they don't fully believe it either. Somewhere they learned that good things get taken back, so a win feels like a question they might be asked to answer. They can account for every step of the road, because each one cost them, and they half expect to have to.

## Hidden fear → hidden gift
- **Fear:** being told it wasn't really theirs: that it was luck, a shortcut, borrowed, or a cheat, when they only ever played it straight. (Innocent's punishment; Hero's fear of being found weak or not good enough, turned outward as "they'll think I didn't earn it".)
- **Gift:** they never suspect anyone else. The trust they're afraid won't be extended to them, they give to everyone else first: they assume the other person got there honestly too. And because they got there the long way, with nothing but the work, they're proof to the people around them that the straight road actually goes somewhere.

## The "real me" line to aim for (drafts, not final)
- "Everyone else could see how far you'd come. You were the last to hear."
- "When something good happens to you, you don't celebrate first. You check how it happened, in case someone asks."
- Gift line (Robin's "say the kindness out loud"): "You never once wonder whether someone else cheated. That's rarer than you think, and it's why people trust the road when they see you on it."

## Truth vs. legend
The true story, plainly, with nothing inflated. This guest fears being caught in something that isn't quite true, so the reading itself must never round up: no invented triumph, no "overnight", nothing sourced loosely. The kindness is in the accuracy.

## Tone
Innocent: honest, optimistic, straightforward. Hero: assertive, encouraging. Translated: warm, plain, a little proud on their behalf, the way a bartender who's watched someone for years finally says it. Lightly funny is allowed (a career started by a famous film; a prize learned of by post). Never solemn about hardship; never cheering.

## Distinct from siblings
- **The True Friend (Four Shares):** makes sure everyone belongs. The Underdog is about whether *they* will be believed.
- **The Naive Artist (The Way It Felt):** made it "wrong" and was corrected. The Underdog did it right, the hard way, and fears being accused, not corrected. The Naive Artist owns "someone explains, kindly, how it's usually done".
- **The Dreamer:** hope before proof. The Underdog didn't hope for the prize at all; they hoped to get a bit better at the work.
- **The Outsider:** stays out of the race on purpose. The Underdog is in the race without knowing it's one.
- **The Angel (caregiver-innocent, a cousin):** being good keeps their place. The Underdog fears losing what they already won.
- **Hero-family cousins to keep clear of:** the Firefighter (*Anyone Would Have*: hides the practice hours; "you were never the only one who was ready"), the Pioneer (*Nothing to It*: goes first, scared, shrugs after; "looks effortless / it isn't"), the Idealist (*The Long Answer*: a letter to a paper, "see who writes back"), the Visionary (*Down the Line*: "earned" is in its epigraph).

## Traps
- **Luck as the reason.** The whim is the posting, never the win. No casino, no dice, no fate imagery (the 2008 ceremony was at a casino: never named).
- **Imposter syndrome as a horoscope.** Everyone feels like a fraud sometimes. What's specific here: the last to know, and checking a win for the catch.
- **Poverty or provincial colour.** "Village", "Eastern Europe", "obscure" can read as a sneer. Vadrna was born in Trnava, a city of 400,000 (p. 206); Zemné is where the pub was, never where he's "from".
- **Copying.** A menu built from trips abroad and internet cafés is study. Told as borrowing, it is this guest's wound exactly.
- **Humiliating the doubters,** and rags-to-riches cheer.
- **"Explain" as a motif:** the Obsessed, the Understanding Spouse and the Naive Artist all use it. Find another verb.
- **"Earned"** in the title block (Down the Line's epigraph). **Sending something in and waiting for a reply** as the proposal (The Long Answer).
- No archetype puns (hero, innocent); the personality name itself spends the line if it recurs.

## Story test (for any candidate)
1. Arrived somewhere improbable by the long, honest road, without cunning, connections or a plan to win.
2. Doubted on the page: told they didn't belong, didn't look the part, or couldn't have done it (accusation of fakery is the ideal; doubt of belonging is enough if the fear is carried as the guest's).
3. Really played it straight, and the source shows the work.
4. Ideally the last to realise; the recognition came from outside.
Out: luck as the reason, hustles, a triumph that humiliates the doubters, poverty as colour.
