| story | The day after seeing the Tom Cruise film *Cocktail*, in 1994, he asked the boss of the village pub he worked in to buy him a cocktail shaker (*Proper* p. 206, F1–F2). | The road starts with one plain request, not a plan to win. | They never set out to win anything; they set out to get a bit better at the work. |
| road | In the late 1990s he barbacked at a formal Prague bar every weekend, five hours each way by train, and ran a restaurant in Slovakia during the week (*Proper* p. 206, F5). | The long honest road, shown in hours, with no shortcut. | Got there with nothing but the work. |
| doubt | The Prague bar's owner asked "What about the hair?", and he trimmed his dreadlocks back a bit (*Proper* p. 206, F4). | Someone wondered if he belonged; he answered by adjusting, not arguing. | Being doubted, and going on anyway. (The fear of being called a cheat stays the guest's: C3.) |
| study | At Paparazzi in Bratislava the menu changed with every trip he took; when he wasn't travelling he studied in internet cafés and translated articles into Slovak (*Proper* pp. 206–207, F6–F8). | Learning in the open, from whatever he could reach. | Study, never borrowing: the work is visibly theirs. |
