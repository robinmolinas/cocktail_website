# Persona card: The Have A Go Hero (regular-guy-hero), Wren, round 1

Sheet: "Gets involved, gets stuck in; fights the good fight." Doesn't hesitate; acts out of conviction that change is possible; an everyday champion. Goal belonging, fear exclusion. Drivers: build connection / leave a mark. The family fears standing out and discourtesy; the secondary fears weakness, incompetence and timidity.

## The one true thing
They say yes before they know how. Something everyone around them grumbles about and waits for "someone qualified" to fix: they say "how hard can it be?", start, and learn the job in front of everyone. Their not-knowing is in plain sight, and they go anyway.

## Hides
- That they're out of their depth most of the way through, and know it. They read up at night so nobody sees how much they're learning.
- The first failure stings far more than they let on. They laugh it off in public and replay it later.
- They move fast partly because thinking too long would talk them out of it.

## Fears
- "Who asked you?" / "Leave it to the professionals." Both fears meet here: looking incompetent (the secondary) and putting yourself above the group (the family).
- Deeper: that the doubters were right, and it shows.

## Gives
- Things get done that everyone else was waiting on someone official for.
- An amateur's go has room in it for other people. An expert's project needs no helpers; theirs does, so people get pulled in ("I need your help"). That's where the belonging comes from: they don't wait to be included, they start something people can join.
- RETIRED from my sketch: "gives permission, once one person has a go everyone is allowed to". On re-reading, that is the Pioneer's whoYouAre almost word for word (*Nothing to It*: "somebody behind you decides they could do it too"; tagline "You went first, so they knew they could").

## Traps
- "Hero" as praise; anything that flatters ("fearless", "brave").
- The UK press sense of "have-a-go hero" (a bystander tackling a robber): no crime, no violence, no physical bravery.
- Jamie Oliver (the sheet's example) never.
- "First" (Pioneer), loophole / found the law (Trailblazer), "make it look easy" or "look possible" (Pioneer), "anyone can" (Firefighter), the shrug (Firefighter, Photographer), "it's always been like that" (Innovator), "a way in" (Trailblazer).
- Money, the sale, or a buyout as the ending. Lobbying (argument is the Campaigner's).
- A guest who never founded anything must see themselves: the street's broken bench, the school play nobody would direct, the club that needed a treasurer, the wall they decided to rebuild from a video.
- Tone: this person can be funny ("how hard can it be?"). Let it.

## Story test (revised)
1. The person had no title, training or authority for it, **on a page** (not our shorthand).
2. A problem or project bigger than them, taken on practically, not by argument.
3. A public setback, doubt or laughter on the page (a plan that failed in front of people counts).
4. They kept going and learned on the job.
5. The result is on a page and reached others.
6. Bonus: others joined because the amateur asked.
The mirror is the amateur having a go. Never "being first", never "finding the rule".

## Verdict on the plan's lead (Erenzo / Tuthilltown)
Accept, re-centred. Oxford HUDSON (pdf 1037) checked whole; CRAFT DISTILLING (pdf 592-593) names Tuthilltown only for the 2010 sale (out).
- Condition 1 (training) now met by *Hudson Valley One* (2021): "complete amateurs". Note: "learned the art of distilling from the ground up" is said of Gable, not Ralph; the reading attributes it accordingly.
- The beats Oxford leans on are other pours' ground: "first distillery in New York to obtain the new class of license" (Pioneer) and "research led Ralph to recognize the opportunities afforded by new legislation" (Trailblazer). Both stay as facts in one clause at most; neither is the mirror.
- The mirror: a climbers' campground blocked by the neighbours, so a group of complete amateurs decided to make whisky in a two-hundred-year-old mill instead, and Ralph rang his son: "I'm building a distillery, I need your help!" (Hester to re-confirm the quote on the fetched page).
- Still missing: (a) doubt or laughter about the distillery itself (the neighbours objected to the campground); half-fail accepted if nothing turns up; (b) the result for many beyond the first licence. Hester: a page showing New York's small distilleries after Tuthilltown, or the rye's reception. Also Oxford's index lists "Erenzo, Ralph, 366": a page the search doesn't find; worth a look.
- First product was a vodka from New York apples, before the rye: an amateur's first go before the thing they're known for. A possible spark for Tomás (apple beside the rye), his call.
