**The one true thing.** They say yes before they know how. There's a problem everyone around them grumbles about while they wait for "someone qualified". This person says "how hard can it be?", starts, and learns the job in front of everyone. Most people keep their not-knowing out of sight. Theirs is in plain view, and they go anyway. That's what no sibling has.

**Hides:** they're out of their depth most of the way through, and they know it. They read up at night so nobody sees how much they're learning. The first failure stings far more than they let on. They laugh it off in public and replay it later. And they move fast partly because thinking too long would talk them out of it.

**Fears:** "Who asked you?" and "leave it to the professionals". Two fears meet there: looking incompetent, and seeming to put themselves above everyone else. Underneath sits a deeper one: that the doubters were right, and it shows.

**Gives:** things get done that everyone else was waiting on someone official to do. And an amateur's attempt has room in it for other people. An expert needs no helpers, but they do, so people get pulled in. That's how this person belongs: they don't wait to be included, they start something people can join.
