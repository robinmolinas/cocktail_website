# The Dreamer (innocent-magician) · Wren's persona card, round 1

Source: persona.py innocent-magician (xlsx row: "wishing, done properly, changes things"; notices signs, trusts hunches, carries hope for people who have run out of it; "there's no place like home, and home is worth dreaming about too"; "being scolded for hoping is what hurts them most"; goal Safety, fear Punishment).

## The one true thing (no sibling has it)
Their hopes are never vague. They're built out of things they've actually noticed, close by: this person, this street, this year. So to them a hope is just what the evidence will add up to once it's had time; to everyone else it looks like wishing. They hope *ahead of the proof, about what's already near*.

## What they hide
"Don't get your hopes up." They've heard it all their life, nearly always kindly, from people who love them and want to spare them. So they've learned to hope quietly, and to put a little disclaimer in front of every hope ("it probably won't happen, but…"). The disclaimer is armour, and it's said first so nobody else has to say it.

## What they fear
Being blamed for the hope. Not the disappointment itself (they can carry that) but the told-you-so after it, and worse, "you got my hopes up" from someone whose hope they kept going. Punishment, for this guest, is being made to feel foolish for having believed.

## What they give
They hold hope for people who've put theirs down, and keep it safe until the person wants it back: the friend after the third rejection, the family that's stopped mentioning the house. They don't argue anyone into it. They just keep saying "it could still happen" in a voice that makes it sound reasonable. And they see what's good in where we already are.

## Siblings (the line between)
- Free Spirit (*The First Guess*): curiosity, questions "told off". Mine is not a question but a hope, and the telling-off is the *kind* kind ("don't get your hopes up"). No "told off".
- Heart-throb (*The Big Words*): says it out loud, same size. Mine hopes quietly; the position must not be "say it out loud / don't shrink it".
- Giggler (*There It Goes*): the held-in laugh, "being told off so often that one day you stop". The "one day you stop" fear is hers: avoid it.
- Underdog (*Straight Up*): doesn't check the good thing for a catch. Mine is before the good thing, not after.
- Naive Artist, Big Sibling, True Friend: making, watching, nobody left out. Clear.
- Outsider (unauthored, Thoreau's wild apples): the overlooked thing near at hand. Keep the Dreamer on *hope*, never on the value of the overlooked.

## Traps (owned elsewhere)
- "dream" (*Down the Line*: "The dream is yours"); "hoping" in a name (*Hoping You'd Come*); "stopped hoping" (*hero-explorer*'s closing line); "hunch" (*Note to Self*'s closing line); "home", "near home", "where you live", a local ingredient bought nearby (*Brought Home*); "Scotland… at home" (*lover-innocent*, Taketsuru).
- Dorothy, ruby slippers, "no place like home", rainbows: never.
- Barnum: "you always see the bright side", "you're an optimist". Recognition, not a compliment.
- The story's Out list: healing, empires and markets, pure luck, the occult, hope cruelly disappointed.

## My story test
1. A hope held before any proof, about something near or already there.
2. Held against a "no" on the page: a rule, a dismissal, or a settled belief that it can't be done here.
3. The hope, once said, opened a door for others, not only the hoper.
Out: as above. Lobbying or argument told as a fight.

## Verdict on the plan's lead: Bill Lark, accept with conditions
Read on the page: Oxford AUSTRALIA AND NEW ZEALAND pdf 171–172; WHISKY, SINGLE-MALT, GLOBAL pdf 2173.
- (1) Hope before proof: the question in the highlands (secondary, his words). The "foolish" limb is an *event* and isn't on any page read, so I don't invent it. But the "no" is on Oxford's: the 1901 Act's minimum still size (150 imperial gallons of wash an hour) kept distilling for big industrial firms only; after the 1970s "Australian consumers disdaining local brands"; whisky production "had effectively ceased by 1977". A country that had stopped believing its own whisky could be good, and a man who looked at what was around him and thought otherwise. That's the "don't get your hopes up" at the scale of a country, and it's on the page. The personal scolding goes in the guest's half.
- (2) Already there: the water, barley and weather, and whisky distilleries on the island by the late 1820s/early 1830s (pdf 171). The thing he wanted had been there before.
- (3) Changed things for others: "several craft distillers soon followed in Lark's footsteps" (pdf 172); the new Australian single-malt industry dates to 1992 (pdf 2173). His hope opened the door for people who hadn't dared ask.

Conditions:

## Position (direction, not a draft)
Not "say it out loud" (Heart-throb), "offer your guess" (Free Spirit), "don't hold it in" (Giggler). The Dreamer's: drop the disclaimer. Or: find out what's actually standing between the hope and the thing; it's often an old rule nobody has checked in years. To be chosen in round 3.
