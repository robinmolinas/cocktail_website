Card `fact-cards/bill-lark-tasmania.md` (F1–F18, C1–C6), anchors in `work/innocent-magician/historian-anchors.md`, and the source text saved beside them (Kiely, *The Spirits Business*, 21 Nov 2022, Lark quoted; one of my two web visits, plus the search).

**The setting (F5).** A day out trout fishing with his father-in-law. The two of them were sitting up in the Tasmanian highlands, talking over what the island already had: the water, the barley, a climate that seemed right. And nobody was making whisky. Then, in his words, Lyn "turned up" and said let's try it (F6). So the first reply on record is a yes, from her, and neither of them had ever distilled: he was a land surveyor, she a survey draftsman (F7). One correction to your r1, and it's mine to make before anyone builds on it: it wasn't "one man looked around and thought otherwise". On the page it was a conversation between two men, and Lyn made the decision. Lyn gets more than a clause. The licence was "Lyn and I" too (F10).

**Helping the ones who followed (F11–F13): yes, his motive on the record.** Within two weeks of the licence, John Grant of Glenfarclas rang from Scotland at 10 at night, offering to help them make good whisky. His reason, as Lark remembers it: someone's first whisky should be a good one, so they'll go on to explore the rest. Lark: "As other producers came along, I thought I should help them as well." So the help was passed down: someone kept the hope going for them, and they kept it going for the next ones. That's your gift limb, sourced. Oxford's "several craft distillers soon followed" (pdf 172) is the outcome.

**Three things to keep out (conflicts).** (C1) "Effectively ceased by 1977" is pdf 172. Pdf 2173 in the same book says the last two big distilleries, in Victoria, closed in the 1980s. So no date: say "the big makers had stopped". (C3, C4) No still size, and no "first in 150 years". The article's "first Australian distillery … in 154 years" is false as worded, since Victoria was still distilling. (C5) No year for the fishing trip. Also: "a country had stopped hoping" is our reading, not Oxford's. Oxford says consumers looked down on local brands, so put it after "I like to think". "He never left the island" isn't on any page either, so drop it. Oxford's verbs for the law are "lobbied" and "persuaded", and "instantly" is Lark's own word ("as he tells it"). Never the bees line, never "home", "kitchen table" or the cul-de-sac (*Brought Home*'s trap), and never Lyn's exact "give it a go": it echoes the Have A Go Hero.

**Tomás, for v1.** From the island, and not honey: Tasmanian distillers tend to use local brewing malt, the local peat gives different aromatics, and they use Australian wine and fortified-wine casks, often small ones (pdf 2173). Lark himself played with small fortified-wine casks (SB). Your Makeable flag is now sourced: Australian craft spirits are "often significantly more expensive" (pdf 172). He's alive, so date the present tense (2022).
