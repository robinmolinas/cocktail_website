# Persona card: The Storyteller (hero-jester) — Wren, round 1

Source: persona.py hero-jester (sheet row, both sheets' archetype lines). Re-read on the page this round: Oxford BERGERON (pdf 258), Oxford MAI TAI (pdf 1209–1212), *Joy* pdf 327.

## Who this is

The sheet: turns a flat tyre into an epic; people lean in; taken for a show-off, but "the truth is kinder": they tell their struggles as adventures **so the struggle doesn't win**, and so others believe they can come through theirs. Fear: "a plain life, told plainly, that nobody remembers". Goal mastery, fear weakness (Hero), with the Jester's horror of the boring version. Drivers: leave a mark, build connection.

**The one true thing (no sibling has it):** the size of the story is a gauge nobody else can read. *The bigger the story, the worse it really was.* The breakdown on the hard shoulder in the rain is, by Friday, a saga with a tow-truck driver everyone wants to meet. What's cut is the twenty minutes when they felt small, frightened and alone. They don't lie: they choose which version gets to be true. Telling it big is how they win it after the fact.

- **What they hide:** the plain version, and how it felt. Not secrecy so much as a rule: the frightened part never gets the microphone, because told plainly it would be weakness, and it might also be forgettable.
- **What they fear:** two things that are one thing. Being seen as weak (a hard thing told as it was), and being ordinary (a life told as it was). The plain telling risks both at once.
- **What they give:** people walk away from their stories with their own bad week feeling survivable, even funny. They hand others a way to tell their trouble so it doesn't win. People ask for the same story again. (This is the "say the kindness out loud" clause.)
- **The real me line, candidate:** "The bigger the story, the worse it really was." (Specific, private, and only this persona carries it.)

## Traps

1. **The show-off.** The sheet warns about it. The reading must never read as "you exaggerate". Grant the stories as a gift first.
2. **The liar.** Never call the stories lies, the guest's or Vic's. They're true in what matters.
3. **Disability as metaphor.** The mirror is **the telling**, never the loss. No guest's "weakness" is ever compared to a missing leg; no "despite", no inspiration framing. A guest who has lost a limb must be able to read this safely. "He lost a leg as a boy", plainly, once.
4. **Medicine.** No cause named (Oxford: tuberculosis; Difford's: cable car: contested). Illness stays in the dossier.
5. **Rationed words:** "hero", "brave", "fight", plus "epic" and "legend": at most one of each family in the whole guest text.
6. **"Story" fatigue.** Stand-up, Morale Booster, Trickster and Idealist all lean on "story/tell". Grep at drafting; the reading can show the telling without saying "story" every line.
7. **The second tall tale.** Vic's Mai Tai origin is contested (Phoebe Beach). Tell it as "the drink he said he created in 1944" and let the leg be the only legend the reading works with. "Let's get the record straight" (his 1970 release) stays **out of guest text**: next to the shark it reads as a dig at him.
8. **Gender.** Guest neutral. Vic is "he", fine.

## Story test

A drinks figure (1) had a real hardship of their own; (2) told it bigger or braver than the record, in their own telling; (3) the plain record is sourced, **and is itself worth telling**; (4) built something real, so the legend isn't the whole person; (5) the room grants the legend first and never makes the teller a fraud.

## Verdict on the plan's lead: Trader Vic and his leg — **accept, with conditions**

Re-read whole (Oxford pdf 258): it passes all five. The record under the shark is a plain life told plainly: a sickly boy who lost a leg (cause contested, so unnamed), left school, a string of short-lived jobs "selling everything from paint to tires", and then, at about thirty-two, a barbecue shack in Oakland that became twenty-odd restaurants on three continents and the Mai Tai. That plain record is the position's proof: the life without the shark was already worth telling. (The tyres are on the page; if the guest text uses the job list, the coincidence with "a flat tyre" stays unforced and unowned unless whoYouAre also uses a tyre. If it does, own it in one clause.)

Conditions: the shark rests on Difford's only (secondary, fetched in plan); Hester confirms it on the page, ideally with a second source (Berry on *Gastropod*, Beyl in *Marina Times*) before it's an anchor. No age ("at six" is Difford's only) unless labelled. The ice pick in the leg stays out until a page holds it.

## New on the page this round (for Hester and Tomás)

- **Oxford MAI TAI (pdf 1210–1212) corrects *Joy*:** Carrie Guild said "maita'i", Tahitian for "good", and **confirmed the story in a signed affidavit**; Donn Beach's Mai Tai "tastes almost nothing like Vic's". So "the drink he said he created" can be firmer: his formula, with a witness. Vic's 1970 telling ("Out of This World—The Best") is bigger than Oxford's "good". Tempting as a tall tale in miniature, but *maita'i roa a'e* may really mean "the very best": don't build on it until Hester checks.
- **The name outran the drink:** by 1955 the name had spread across the islands but the recipe was a trade secret, so rival hotels mixed rum with juice and called it a Mai Tai; "the only place making it properly was Trader Vic's". This is the persona's shape again (the telling travels, the real thing is better), so by my own rule it's **one clause in y4 at most**, as the drink's provenance, never a second reveal.
- Oxford's recipe (pdf 1212): 60 ml oldest Jamaican rum, a lime's juice, 15 ml curaçao, 15 ml orgeat, 7.5 ml sugar syrup; shaved ice; double Old-Fashioned; mint and a spent lime shell. It calls the formula "elegant, layered, timeless".

## Where the reading is heading (not a draft)

- **whoYouAre:** a scene (the breakdown that becomes a saga); the gauge ("the bigger the story, the worse it really was"); the hidden plain version; the gift said out loud.
- **Position (Ryu, earned):** not "admit you were scared" (Pioneer's), not "let one person in" (generic). **Tell one thing exactly as it happened, nothing added, and see that they lean in anyway.** The fear is that the plain version is forgettable; the bartender bets it isn't, with Vic's record as the proof. It ends on what they gain: they get remembered as themselves. The stories stay: never ask them to stop.
- **For Tomás (the person, not the spec):** don't make the drink "the plain version". The stories are a gift; the glass can be generous and showy (the lime shell and mint are Vic's own), with the real thing doing the work underneath. Orgeat → nuts: declaring it is honest.
