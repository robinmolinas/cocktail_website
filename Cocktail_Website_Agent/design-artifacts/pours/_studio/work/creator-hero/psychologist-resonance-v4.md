## 3. Swap test
- The siblings in the same family (Sculptor, Photographer, Experimentalist, Minimalist, Surrealist, Obsessed, Makeover Artist, Trend Setter, Auteur, Old Master) aren't authored yet, so I read against their essences. Nothing here fits the **Obsessed** (who loves the making, not the finished thing) or the **Old Master** (depth, not size). The closest risk is the **Auteur**, in sentence 1. The fear line and the handover (p3, p5) keep it the Visionary's: the Auteur would never hand the shaker over.
- Across families: p5's "ask for their hands" is close to Four Shares' "let someone pass the bowl to you". The difference is intended (recruiting help for a big thing, not receiving care), and I'm flagging it for Robin anyway.
- Phrases: no "That's you, isn't it?", no "Here's what I'd ask of you". No "hand to hand" (belongs to Four Shares), no "can't rush it", no solera. Epigraph frame no longer copies No Accident's.

## 4. Punch check
- **Tagline:** *Slow you can live with. Small you can't.* Stops a scroll. v3's tagline repeated the whoYouAre punch line almost word for word and spent the reveal before the guest reached it, so I changed it.
- **whoYouAre, first line:** *You see the finished thing first.* **The weakest of the three.** It's a true and quiet line, and it's the Auteur-adjacent one. I'm keeping it because the whoYouAre builds from it, but it's the one Robin is most likely to sharpen.
- **yours, last line:** *Things this size are never finished by one person. But someone has to hold out for the whole of it.* Holds. In v3 this line said "smaller" for the fourth time, which turned it into a slogan, so I rewrote it.

## 5. Rule check
- Drink in hand? No. p4 is how to make it, some other day. The proposal is about life.
- The romantic reading is signposted: p3 opens "I don't know why, but I like to think". Nothing about his motive is stated as fact.
- No gendered words about the guest. "His arms" and "one man's" are about the historical shakermen and Ramos. No archetype names.
- The shaker boys are told plainly, with no feelings or beliefs given to them.
- Clever for its own sake? "It can't be made halfway. It can be shared." is the nearest. It earns its place by pointing to the proposal.
- The epigraph is about the drink. It doesn't echo the tagline, the name or the closing line.

## Verdict
**Ready.** The line is the fear line. The weak spot is the opening sentence of whoYouAre. For Robin: the flagged orange flower water (Tomás/Hester), the p5 ending's closeness to Four Shares, and the shaker boys edge (Hester).
