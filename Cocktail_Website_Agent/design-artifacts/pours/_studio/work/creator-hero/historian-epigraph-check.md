# Hester: epigraph fact floor, creator-hero rework (2026-09-25)

Robin's brief: the epigraph is about the cocktail, read cold before the reading, a tease; near "the vision is owned, the road is shared", or built on *earned*. The words are Wren's. This file only says which words the record will carry.

## Why the current one fails cold
*"He gave the recipe away. The cloud has to be earned."* Facts pass (F10, F18), but "He" has no one to point to yet and "cloud" only means foam once p1 has explained it. Both are a cold-read problem, not a fact problem.

## Robin's two words, against the record
- **"shared"** fits the **recipe**, not the **shaking**. The shaking was hired work: young Black men hired only to shake (Oxford, RAMOS, HENRY CHARLES, pdf 1606; F6), then thirty-five shakermen on at Mardi Gras 1915 (*Imbibe!*, pdf 111; F7). Calling their work "shared" dresses up paid labour as a common dream, which is the line Wren and I held in rounds 2–4. What was freely shared is the recipe: he had no problem giving it out (Oxford, RAMOS GIN FIZZ, pdf 1609; F10), and strangers have made it for more than a century since (same page; F15).
- **"owned"** fits the **name**, not the **recipe**. He gave the recipe out all his life. The only "owning" on the record comes after his death, when the Roosevelt Hotel bought the exclusive rights to market the drink from his heirs (Oxford, RAMOS GIN FIZZ, pdf 1609; F14). The name is still his: the only great classic named for its bartender (F16), which Wren keeps to one plain clause at most (it's the Auteur's mirror).
- **"earned"** is safe if it's the foam or the finish: his 1925 standard is shake until smooth and snowy white, no time given (*Imbibe!*, pdf 112; F18). Never attach "earned" to a duration.
