## Tagline
All four options are about the person and make no historical claim, so there's nothing for me to strike. *Your plans were never meant to fit in one lifetime* doesn't lean on Ramos's life, and it shouldn't be glossed as "like Ramos, who didn't see…". He did see his drink finished and famous (F5). What outlived him was the carrying (F13–F15).

## p5 (the handover ending)
**Pass.** "Write it down that way… clear enough that someone else could pick it up and carry on. Then give it to them." is addressed to the guest, and it's not a claim about Ramos. The echo is sound, with one guard: Ramos *dictated* his recipe (F12), he didn't write it down (I struck "He wrote down" in round 6). p2 already says "dictated", so the parallel works as an echo. It must never be turned into "as Ramos wrote it down". "Give it to them" rests on F10 (he gave it out), and "carry on" on F15, which is already signposted in p3's "I like to think".

## Result

## Addendum, round 11: p4 "It can be handed on." (Tomás)
**Pass.** It's a method line about the guest's own shake, where the shaker changes hands halfway (*Codex* working method), so it isn't a historical claim. It's also true on both historical counts, if a reader takes it that way. The shaker was passed from man to man in the 1915 chain (*Imbibe!*, pdf 111; F7), and the recipe was handed on to anyone who asked (Oxford, RAMOS GIN FIZZ, pdf 1609; F10). One guard: mid-shake passing is on the record only for 1915. In 1899–1909 each bartender had his own shaker boys (F6). So never date the chain earlier than 1915. p2 already doesn't.
