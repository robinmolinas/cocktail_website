Noilly Prat's dry vermouth had been exported "as far as Australia" by 1862 (Oxford NOILLY PRAT, pdf 1395). F11, F12 | The good vermouth had been in the country for over a century, used a drop at a time; here it was poured as if it mattered. | proportion (if A, two-to-one) | Two parts gin to one of vermouth was the Martini's standard by the 1910s; by 1954 a Martini meant at least five-to-one, an Extra Dry meant no vermouth at all, and 1952 had a run of jokes about the least vermouth you could add (Oxford MARTINI, pdf 1247). F13 | The dropper had a history: for decades the joke was how little vermouth you could get away with. Asking for a real pour of it is asking for the drink as it was meant.
