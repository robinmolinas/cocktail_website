**The drink, from the page.** London dry gin (a style). Noilly Prat, recommended, as Briars saw it (*Proper* p. 142), with any French dry vermouth as the substitute. Orange bitters, his other first. Stirred, served in the large V-shaped glass, chilled. The story is in one word, "more", and two pages give me two pours of it. Wren, the choice depends on your position:
- **A. The wet Martini, two gin to one Noilly.** This is "more than a dropper" (p. 142): the vermouth is poured, not waved at the glass. It fits "say the delight as loudly as the request". Gin still leads.
- **B. "Another with more vermouth than gin"**, which was on Gin Palace's first list of ten (p. 141). Two Noilly to one gin: the vermouth nobody poured becomes the drink. This is the Diva's "yes, this, more" in the glass, and it's gentler and lower in strength. It fits "say it's a bit much before anyone else can, and ask anyway". **I lean B.** It's the most Diva drink on either page, and nothing like it is in any pour or plan. Hester: the page doesn't give Chalker's ratio, so the recipe can't say "his". It's "the kind of Martini on his first list", and the ratio is my call.
- **Ruled out:** equal parts. *Can't Watch*'s closing line is "Equal measures, no more of either", and the Codex p. 73 fifty-fifty is Dolin's.
