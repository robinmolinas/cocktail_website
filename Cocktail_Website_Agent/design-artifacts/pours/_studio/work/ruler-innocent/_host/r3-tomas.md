**The drink.** 50 ml Noilly Prat Original Dry (recommended; any French dry vermouth), 30 ml London dry gin, 20 ml Tokaji Aszú, 2 dashes orange bitters. Stirred, strained into a large, chilled V-shaped glass, with a lemon peel squeezed over the top. Tokaji is the spark, for the Budapest in Chalker's brief. It's also what makes the drink work. Without it, 60 vermouth to 30 gin finishes at 1.40 g sugar: a thin, sharp wine. With it, the sugar rises to 3.06 g, level with the Codex's own Bamboo (3.02 g by my arithmetic, p. 91).

**Numbers.** Finished at 18.5% ABV, 3.06 g sugar, 0.327% acid, 38.5% dilution. Strength, sugar, acid and dilution read OUT by structure: it's a wine-core drink, like *Night Light*, judged between the Bamboo (12.4%) and a Martini. Sweeping the gin from 40 to 47% gives 17.2 to 18.5%, and sweeping Tokaji's sugar from 12 to 18 g gives 2.8 to 3.6 g, so the floor is "any London dry gin, 40% or stronger".
