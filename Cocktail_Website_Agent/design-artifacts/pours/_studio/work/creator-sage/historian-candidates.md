# Hester: mirror candidates for The Old Master (creator-sage), round 1

Person: revered for knowledge and craft, years of expertise; goal innovation; fears mediocrity/status quo (Creator) and deception/ambiguity (Sage). Da Vinci: the notebooks behind the work.

## 1. Chosen proposal: Murray Stenson and the Last Word (a person)
Card: `_studio/fact-cards/murray-stenson-last-word.md` (F1–F13, L1–L2, C1–C5).
Shape: thirty years of quiet study (old books from the 1980s, F3) became the most "new" drink of the revival, and it was a 1916 recipe (F5–F8). The newest thing on the menu came from the oldest shelf. The reverence came after, and he didn't want it (declined the 2010 award, F9). Fear of mediocrity: he didn't invent ingredients (F4); his originality was knowing what everyone had forgotten.
Coincidence to own (Wren's call): Oxford's "four-part symmetry" (equal parts) against the Vitruvian Man's proportions. Owned in one clause, never claimed as meaning.
Risks: living person (date present tense); many small conflicts (C1–C5), all avoidable with plain wording; Fogarty is a legend (L2).

## 2. Runner-up: green Chartreuse (an ingredient)
F12–F13. A formula kept by a handful of people for centuries; the monks still made a new thing from it (the 1840 liqueur from the medicinal elixir). Knowledge that lasts because it keeps being remade. The 1605 manuscript is brand lore (L1): legend only. Can live inside candidate 1 as the drink's heart.

## Round 2: Wren's brief (a master changed their own famous recipe; got better)
Stenson doesn't meet it: he revived someone else's recipe; nothing on record of him revising his own. Demoted to runner-up.
**New lead: Jerry Thomas's 1876 Improved Cocktail.** Card: `_studio/fact-cards/jerry-thomas-improved-cocktail.md` (F1–F10, C1).
Shape: the man who wrote the first book with a cocktail section went back to his own standard fourteen years later and printed "Improved" beside it (F2, F5). The change that mattered was the newest, most fashionable ingredient of the day, the absinthe dash (F3). The improvement outlived him: Wondrich reads the Sazerac as that very drink (F7).
Limits: "better" is Wondrich's word ("nothing wrong with it at all"; improvement "subtle"). Nobody junior corrected him; the "Bogart's" fix was a printer's slip (F4). Not the 1887 edition (F10).
For Tomás: F9 (Wondrich puts curaçao back in place of maraschino) keeps it veto-free; the absinthe dash is the slot where a master's correction goes (Chartreuse?).
