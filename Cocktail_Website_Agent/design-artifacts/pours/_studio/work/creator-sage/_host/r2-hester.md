📜 **Hester:** Wren, you're right that Stenson doesn't fit your brief. He revived somebody else's drink, and nothing on record shows him revising one of his own, so he drops to runner-up. Here's a story that does fit, from the top of the canon: **Jerry Thomas's "Improved" Cocktail.** His 1862 *How to Mix Drinks* was the first book with a section of recipes devoted to the Cocktail, thirteen of them (*Imbibe!*, pdf 173). In the 1876 edition he went back to his own standard and added three "Improved" Cocktails (pdf 17, 173, 179). The curaçao came out and maraschino went in. More importantly, he added a dash of absinthe, the fashionable new bottle of the decade (pdf 179–180). He printed them beside the old recipes, not in place of them. That's my reading of "adds", so I'd put it as "printed beside" and nothing stronger. The improvement lasted longer than he did: Wondrich reads the Sazerac as "nothing more than" Thomas's Improved Whiskey Cocktail with the bitters shuffled (pdf 183–184). Three fences before anyone builds on it. First, "better" is Wondrich's judgement ("nothing wrong with it at all"; the improvement is "a subtle one"), not a fact. Second, nobody junior corrected him. The "Bogart's" that became "Boker's" was a printer's slip (Oxford BITTERS, pdf 274), so never "he fixed his own mistake". Third, the 1887 edition isn't his. He died in 1885, and an unnamed bartender revised it (pdf 17). *Joy* calls it his second book, and it's wrong there. Tomás, two things for the drink. Wondrich himself puts old orange curaçao back in place of the maraschino (pdf 180), which keeps us veto-free. And the absinthe dash is the slot where the master's correction went, which could make it the place for your Chartreuse.
