The drink he's remembered for came out of that reading. He found the Last Word in Saucier's 1951 *Bottoms Up*: a 1916 Detroit Athletic Club drink of equal parts gin, green Chartreuse, maraschino and lime. It had "zero renown", and most bars didn't even stock two of its bottles (Proper p. 70; Oxford LAST WORD, pdf 1166). He brought it back in the early 2000s, and it became the drink the revival built on (Final Ward, Naked and Famous). So the newest thing on the menu came off the oldest shelf. Nothing in it was his own invention. Clarke calls his style "basically classic", with "no infusions" (p. 70). His originality was knowing what everyone else had forgotten. The reverence came afterwards and he didn't want it. The young bartenders "hoisted" him onto a throne, and when Tales named him Best Bartender in America in 2010 he didn't travel to collect it (p. 69). Two labels before anyone builds on this.
