# Persona card: The Old Master (creator-sage)

Wren, round 1. Source: persona.py creator-sage (essence, story, goal Innovation, fear Status Quo/Mediocrity; secondary fears Deception, Conflict, Ambiguity; drivers "Provide structure / SEEK PARADISE"; imagery: sketches, blueprints, hands still refining).

## Core truth
They've been good at their thing for a long time, and the danger they feel isn't failure: it's that respect has made people stop telling them the truth, so they could go stale without anyone saying a word. They still want to be surprised by their own craft.

## Shows / hides
- **Shows:** calm authority. Knows why every rule is there. People come to them to check, to learn, to be told it's right.
- **Hides:** the quiet fear of repeating themselves. The private notebook where they're still a beginner at something. How much they miss being corrected.

## Hidden fear → hidden gift
- **Fear:** becoming the thing they used to push against: the safe version, the way-it's-always-been-done, and being too respected for anyone to notice. (Creator's status quo + Sage's deception, turned on themselves: the flattering silence.)
- **Gift:** because they refuse to settle, they give the people around them permission to question anything, including them. Their mastery is the reason the room is allowed to try things.

## The "real me" line to aim for (draft)
- *"You know the rules better than anyone. That's exactly why you're allowed to break them."* (safe, maybe too general; keep as fallback)
- **Aim:** *"The worst thing anyone could do for you is agree."* / *"What you dread isn't being wrong. It's that nobody would dare tell you."*

## Truth vs. legend
The true story, plainly. A Sage half: ambiguity and flattery both betray them. If a detail is contested, say it's contested; they'll respect that more than a pretty legend.

## Tone
Respectful without deference. Speak to them as a peer, never a fan (a fan's tone *is* their fear). Warm, a little dry. One light touch of humour is allowed; no awe.

## Distinct from siblings
- **The Visionary (Down the Line):** a dream others build on and carry forward, handed on. The Old Master isn't about succession or legacy; it's about staying alive in the craft now. Avoid "handing on", "unfinished", lineage-as-chain.
- **The Auteur (Making the Calls):** wants all the credit and blame, redoes others' parts. The Old Master *wants* others to question them. Avoid "praise", "blame", "let someone else make it".
- **The Connoisseur (A Brother's Care, other family, Sage primary):** can't forgive being fooled by a label. The Old Master's deception is gentler and closer: the kindness of people who won't disagree. Avoid "fooled", "pretending".
- **The Experimentalist (Off-Label):** tries everything, never the same twice. The Old Master has a mastered classic and chooses to change *it*, from deep knowledge, not play.

## Traps
- Awe and reverence in the bartender's voice (flattery; the exact thing they fear).
- "Old" as age. The name is about mastery, not years; a 30-year-old can be this. No age, no retirement, no "late in life" about the guest.
- A lecture. History at most half; don't out-expert the expert.
- The Da Vinci cliché (Vitruvian Man in the reading). Imagery only, if at all.
- A drink that's a museum piece. The classic-untouched would be the status quo on a plate.

## Story shape for Hester (not a fact, a brief)
A master who changed their own famous recipe, or took a correction from someone junior, and the drink got better. Or an ingredient/house whose recipe was mastered and still revised. Not the Hanky Panky (the Sculptor's). Not Ramos/handing on (the Visionary's).

## Drink shape for Tomás
A classic an expert would recognise instantly, with one change a master would make and a novice wouldn't dare. The spark should feel like a *correction*, not a costume.
