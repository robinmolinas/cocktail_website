# Persona card: The Firefighter (hero-regular-guy), Wren, round 1

Sources read for this card: persona.py (sheet row + siblings), plans/hero.md (row + family guards), plans/work/hero/psychologist-sketches.md (my sketch), the two sibling pours just closed (hero-caregiver *Next One's Mine*, hero-creator *The Long Answer*), *Off Duty* and *Not Only the Way* whoYouAre, and the plan's lead on the page: *A Proper Drink* pp. 151-153, plus pp. 73-75 (the replacement lead below).

## The person

**One true thing (no sibling has it):** "Anyone would have done the same" isn't modesty. They say it because they spent years making sure they *would*. They don't trust themselves to rise to the day, so they don't leave it to the day: they practise until the hard moment feels ordinary. What looks like calm is rehearsal.

**What they hide:** the hours nobody saw. The route driven twice before the interview. The speech said to the mirror. Knowing where the stopcock is before the pipe bursts. And under the hours, the reason for them.

**What they fear:** being the one who isn't enough on the day someone needs them. Not failure in general: being the weak link in a crew that was counting on them.

**Why the shrug (the second half, the everyday side):** being singled out feels like being left out. Praise puts them in front of the crew, and they want to be *in* it. So the credit goes to everyone, and the fear stays with them alone. They share every bit of the credit and none of the fear.

**What they give (say it out loud, Robin's caregiver rule):** the people around them are all right on the bad day because of a hundred dull ones they never mention. Their calm is borrowed by everyone near them. That's how they look after people.

**Tone:** plain, steady, warm, a little dry. Not funny for its own sake, not grave. They'd distrust anything that sounds like a medal.

**Position direction (for later, not ruled):** the fear is the only thing they don't share. "You've practised for everyone else's bad day. The people beside you have practised for yours." Being enough is the crew's job, not theirs alone. It gives something back (they're held too). It is not "take the credit" (the Craftsman's), not "let them see a crack" (the Warrior's), not "let someone help" (the Saviour's), not "sit down / rest" (*Off Duty*'s).

## Traps

- **Praise.** Hero readings slide into "you step up". Recognition names the fear.
- **Rationed words:** brave (also *Off Duty*'s), courage, hero, save, strong, fight. At most one, and I'd use none.
- **Fire words in guest text:** fire, flame, smoke, burning, heat of the moment, under fire. The name already says it. No real fires, no 9/11, no death toll, ever.
- **Uniform and job labels:** "first responder", "on call", "on shift". The guest may be an accountant: the day it counts is ordinary (the burst pipe, the handover, the speech, the family crisis), not an emergency service.
- **Gendered crew words:** "brothers", "the guys", "band of brothers". War words: "trenches", "foxhole".
- **Medicine:** no first-aid, no CPR scenes (family guard).
- **Swap test:** a line that fits the Rescuer or the Craftsman isn't finished.

## Verdict on the plan's lead: Phil Ward (*Proper* p. 152): replace

The plan has me accepting it. I've now read p. 152 whole, with pp. 151 and 153, and on the page it fails the two clauses that make this person:

- **Clause 3 fails.** The page frames the spec-feeding as ambition: "Ward got himself noticed", and after the promotion "he craved the main stage". The credit wasn't given away. It was withheld by the owner and then won. The Firefighter shrugs the stage off; Phil wanted it. A reading built on him ends on "they'll notice you eventually", which is the Muse's or the Craftsman's position, not this one.
- **The day and the fear are missing.** There's drill, but no moment that counts and no fear. Every night is a rush, and none is *the* day.
- **The arc turns on looks.** He was kept back because he "didn't have the look", and he got the job after "Take a shower, cut your hair, cut the mullet off". That can't be told kindly, and without it the story has no ending.
- Also on the page: the "shoot myself" line and "Dirty Phil".

**Replacement lead (for Hester to audit): Marco Dionysos, *A Proper Drink* pp. 74-75.** It passes all five clauses on the page:
1. Practice: on a Friday night, "a lot of it is still muscle memory at this point" (p. 75, his words).
2. The moment, and it held: "There's a moment on a Friday night when I am so buried and I have sixteen drinks going… That moment where: I got this." (p. 75). "Buried" is the fear, and "I got this" is the practice holding, in his own words.
3. No glory: he "didn't seize the mantle or seem to want it"; "He's not really looking for glory, though he deserves it a lot" (Camper English); "the rare startender who seemed most contented simply being a bartender" (p. 75).
4. Crew and place: at the Starlight Room the bartenders competed on busy nights over whose coat was cleanest at the end of the night, and he won every time, never needing it dry-cleaned (Thomas Waugh, pp. 74-75). At Smuggler's Cove he stayed for years, "long after the rest of the original bar staff had moved on" (p. 75).
5. No danger, no uniform, no fire.

The clean coat is the image: the busiest night of the week, and nothing on him at the end of it. That's the practice made visible without anyone having to say so.

## Round 3: story ruling (Wren)

**Ruled: Dionysos, as Hester scoped him, with the Sling as the hinge.** Re-read *Proper* pp. 72-73 myself.
- The story's shape: busboy to bartender at a Portland restaurant, making mostly frozen drinks "and liked it" (p. 72) → a guest orders a Singapore Sling, he asks two senior bartenders, nobody knows → "That didn't sit right" (Simonson's narration) → first cocktail book, "He kept going back" → years later, a Friday night, sixteen drinks, "muscle memory… I got this" (p. 75) → wants to be known for "consistently excellent cocktails"; mentors "Probably dozens… I take something from each person" (2010, Hester's card).
- The Sling is the fear shown (someone asked, and the crew couldn't answer). p. 75 is the practice holding. "Dozens" is the credit given away. All four of my clauses are met, in order.
- **The mirror is the night of the Sling, not the man.** The cocky years ("spinning cocktail trays", p. 73) get one clause at most, and are never read onto the guest. No line says he never wanted attention.
- **Never blame the two senior bartenders.** Nobody knew; that's the point, not their failing.
- **Name:** he chose it, so it isn't a coincidence and never reads as luck. One plain clause, neutral, spelled Dionysos: he chose it himself, from the god of wine, the same god this place takes its name from. No joke and no reading of character into it. Still flagged for Robin (the second name note in the family, after Gallant).
- "Frozen Margaritas" is *For Good*'s ground: "frozen drinks" at most.
- Waugh's contests (coat, out-ring) stay out, as Hester advises (they put him in front, and the coat brushes the Superhero's jacket). I withdraw the clean-coat image.
