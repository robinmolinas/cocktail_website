## Fences (live)
- Never "only one change": v4 also moves the syrup volume, the soda and the garnish (*Codex* p. 138).
- "Everything… except the sugar" (the epigraph) holds only while the gin, lemon, soda and orange stay ordinary. Re-audit it after any spec change.
- "Almost everyone agreed on gradual" is **her** description (F11): attribute it (audit X1).
- Her feelings aren't on record: "I like to think" only.
- Never "slave-free", "clean", "right" or "healthier" sugar. The inscriptions are the campaign's claims (F17–F18).
- Rum is not in her pamphlet (F23). Demerara is not in this glass (the Collins uses white sugar).
- Jaggery's taste ("rounder, a little toasted") is Tomás's tasting note. Matrix pdf 240 is about cane syrup, not jaggery.
- Allergens: groundnut clarifier sourced (F20); dairy kept on the safe side, unsourced.

## Dossier only (true, sourced, not in the reading)
- Her name isn't on the 1824 title page (F2); Fox's isn't either (F8). Left out by Wren: too close to *Serviceable*.
- A later 1824 printing reports Leicester, Nottingham and Brighton abstaining (F3); its Advertisement names a deputy from Demerara (F13); demerara is named after the Demerara River (F14).
- Fox 1791: "West India Sugar and Rum", with an arithmetic of lives (F8–F9); the bowl that counts "one Slave less" (F18).
- Runner-up: the Ward Eight (Oxford pdf 2120; *Imbibe!* pdf 243): its origin is legend, and Lomasney was a "wily" boss who abstained from alcohol.
