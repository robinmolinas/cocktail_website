# Hester: candidates for the Campaigner (round 1, 2026-09-29)

Wren's shape: frightened but speaks anyway; loud for strangers, quiet on their own turn; never counts the wins (powerlessness).

## A. Elizabeth Heyrick and the sugar bowl (lead) — card `fact-cards/elizabeth-heyrick-sugar-abstention.md`
- Loud for strangers: a pamphlet against the leaders of her own movement (*Immediate, not Gradual*), and a campaign that turned on what ordinary households put in their tea (F2–F4).
- Quiet on her own turn: her name isn't on the title page (F2). Owned: Fox's isn't either (F8), so it's ordinary for the time; the motive is ours, signposted.
- Never counts the wins: she died in 1831 (F1); the Act that abolished colonial slavery is of 1833 (V1 to confirm day-level dates).
- Powerlessness answered in print: "what can a few families do?" / "It can do wonders" (F7). Her argument, not her feeling.
- In the glass: the sweetener. Her ask was not "no sugar" but "change where it comes from" (F5). Fox's boycott covered rum too (F8): a rum base must own the irony.
- Fence: *Serviceable*'s tagline "You care about people you'll never meet". Heyrick's cause is the same shape (distant strangers). Wren should keep the reading on the fuss and the silence, not the distance.

## B. Ward Eight (Tomás's lead) — runner-up, legend
- Oxford WARD EIGHT pdf 2120: the most-cited origin (Locke-Ober, election eve 1898, Tom Hussion, Lomasney's Hendricks Club) is disputed: Hussion started there only in 1900; grenadine rare in the US before the 1910s; rival claims Billy Kane, Charlie Carter (Puritan Club, 1903). First recipes: *Boston Herald* 1907, *The Cocktail Book* 1913.
- Imbibe! pdf 243: Carter's 1934 letter to the *New York Sun*; the election he names was actually 1905. "None of them better supported."
- Safe as fact: the drink is tied, in the accounts, to celebrating Lomasney's election wins; Lomasney abstained from alcohol and probably wasn't at the party (Oxford: "probably").
- Why not the lead: a ward boss, "powerful and wily", is the opposite of Wren's frightened speaker, and the name's origin is legend. A long sour with soda, so the structure is still Tomás's to use.
