## Fences
- Rum: the 1824 pamphlet never names it (F23); only Fox's 1791 title does (F8).
- "Almost everyone agreed on gradual" is **her** description (F11): attribute it.
- **v2 (round 4) scope:** the *Codex* Tom Collins (p. 138) is 60 seltzer, 60 gin, 30 lemon, 22.5 simple syrup, garnished with an orange half wheel and a brandied cherry. v2 changes the sugar, its amount, the soda and the garnish. So never say "only one thing changed" or "the same drink with one change" unless the garnish goes back; "the one change that matters is the sugar" is fine.
- The Tom Collins uses plain (white) simple syrup, not demerara (*Codex* p. 138). No line may imply demerara is this drink's usual sugar.
- Jaggery's sugar: 65–85% sucrose plus 9–15% reducing sugars (F19). Never "healthier", "cleaner" or "right" sugar (Wren's guard).
- Allergens: the sourced clarificant risk is **groundnut** (F20), not milk. Tomás's call on the class.
- Demerara: **the name only**, never "demerara sugar comes from a slave colony" today (F14 note). The Advertisement has no named author: never "she wrote about Demerara".
- The bowl's words are the campaign's claim (F17). Never as a fact about Indian labour.
- Never "she was frightened / nervous / shy": her feelings are not on record. Wren's frightened heart belongs to the guest; for Heyrick, "I like to think…".
- Never "slave-free sugar", "fair-trade", or any claim that East India sugar was free of forced labour (F5 note).
- Never "she started the boycott": Fox's 1791 abstention came first (F8), and she says the earlier "species of abstinence" was discontinued (pamphlet p. 4, both copies).
- The Leicester/Nottingham/Brighton lines are the campaign reporting on itself (F3); never "a quarter of Leicester" (web, unsourced).
- "Changed their minds" / the 1830 turn of the Anti-Slavery Society, the bull-baiting, Susanna Watts's door-to-door: **not verified** (card V2–V4). Out of the reading until seen.
- *Serviceable* owns "people you'll never meet": keep the distance out of the tagline and "yours".
