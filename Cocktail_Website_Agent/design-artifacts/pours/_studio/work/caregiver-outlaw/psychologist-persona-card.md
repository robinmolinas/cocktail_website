# Persona card: The Campaigner (caregiver-outlaw), Wren, round 1

**Source row:** "Causes a fuss to help care for people in the world." Goal service; fears selfishness (primary) and powerlessness (secondary); drivers "provide structure / LEAVE A MARK". Example The Body Shop: care for everyone, no one forgotten.

## Who this is
The one who says it out loud. At the meeting, the school gate, the family table, when something is unfair to someone who isn't in the room, they're the one whose voice goes first. Their care is loud. It's the only caregiver whose love looks like a fuss.

## What they hide
**They aren't fearless.** People assume they like the fight. They don't. Their heart goes before they speak; they rehearse it in the car; they lie awake after, wondering if they were too much. They speak up anyway, because staying quiet feels worse: silence, to them, is a kind of selfishness (the primary fear, literally). The quiet option is comfortable, and comfort while someone is forgotten is the thing they can't forgive in themselves.

## Hidden fear (secondary)
That the fuss changed nothing. Powerlessness: all that noise, and the thing stays the same. So they rarely count the wins; they only see the next one.

## The gift
Because they speak up, other people don't have to be brave alone. Someone quieter in that room was relieved. "Someone had to say it" and it's usually them.

## The one true thing (distinctiveness)
**They make a fuss for everyone except themselves.** They'll stand up for a stranger and swallow it when it's their own turn. (Fuss for others, silence for self.)
- Caution: "look after yourself too" is Four Shares' ("keep one cup back for yourself") and belongs to the whole family. Don't end there. The distinct angle is *courage spent on others, never on their own behalf*, and *the fear that it didn't matter*.
- Caution: the Craftsman owns "misheard" ("heard as 'my way is better'"). Don't frame the Campaigner as misunderstood; frame them as frightened and doing it anyway.

## Siblings to keep apart
- Rescuer: one dramatic save of one person. Campaigner: a cause, many people, often people they'll never meet.
- Nanny: structure imposed on people for their good. Campaigner: structure demanded from those in charge, on others' behalf.
- Craftsman: quiet care in a method. Campaigner: loud care in public.
- Trickster (No Accident, Outlaw secondary elsewhere): breaks rules with a purpose and fears being ignored. Keep apart: the Campaigner doesn't want the attention; the attention is a cost.

## Positions I'd consider (Ryu's order, earned)
1. **Count the ones you won.** Encouragement against the powerlessness fear: the thing they never do.
2. **Make one fuss for yourself.** Hard truth, the distinct inversion, but must not echo "keep one cup back".
3. Plain affirmation: "Being called difficult is what it cost. It was worth it."
Leaning 1 or 2; the story should decide.

## Story directions for Hester (leads, not facts)
- A person who campaigned *for others* around drink: e.g. Pauline Sabin and the women's campaign for Repeal (WONPR), who argued Prohibition was harming the young. Care made loud. Needs sourcing.
- The owned irony: temperance campaigners (Carrie Nation) — true caregiver-outlaws who campaigned against the very glass. Riskier, possibly funny.
- An ingredient with a campaign in its history (fair trade, a boycott: e.g. sugar boycotts of the abolition movement, "anti-saccharites"). Strong if sourced: the guest's cause in the sweetener.
- A drink named for a fight on others' behalf.
Whatever mirrors: someone who was frightened, spoke anyway, and never got to see the whole win.

## Tone
Serious with warmth; a little dry humour allowed ("you've rehearsed it in the car"). Never preachy: the reading must not campaign at the guest.
