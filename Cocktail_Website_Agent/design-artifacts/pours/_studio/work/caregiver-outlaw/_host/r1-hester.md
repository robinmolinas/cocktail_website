It was a twopenny pamphlet, and it went after the leaders of her own movement for being too polite. I read the 1824 London scans on archive.org (the Harvard and Southampton copies). These points are on the page, primary: (1) **her name isn't on the title page**; (2) what she asked of every household was the sugar bowl: "sugar, especially". And she didn't ask anyone to give it up, only to change where it came from: "we only need substitute East India for West India sugar"; (3) she answers the powerlessness fear in print herself.
