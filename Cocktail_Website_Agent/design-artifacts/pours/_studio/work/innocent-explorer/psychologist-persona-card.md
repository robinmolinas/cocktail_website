# Persona card: The Free Spirit (innocent-explorer), Wren, round 1 (2026-10-01)

Source: persona.py innocent-explorer (essence, story, goal Safety, fear Punishment; secondary fears Entrapment, Subordination; drivers Seek paradise).

## The one true thing
For this person, "nobody's sure" is good news. Most people hear it as a let-down; they lean in. They ask "why?" out loud, about strange things and about rules, and the answer they love best is the one that opens another question. They go *in* after the strange thing (the persona's Alice tumbles, she doesn't stop at the edge), trusting it will be wonderful, and the trust is what makes them brave. It isn't courage over fear (that's the Pioneer). It's trust.

Candidate "this is me" line (draft, not final): *When someone says "actually, nobody's sure", most people sigh. You light up.*

## What they hide
That some of their questions got them told off: "because I said so", "don't be cheeky", an eye-roll in a meeting. The fear on the sheet is punishment, and this is its everyday size. So they've learned to ask lightly, with a smile, as if it didn't matter much. It does. And the plain question about a rule ("why does it have to be that way?") is sincere, never a challenge, yet it's heard as one.

## What they fear
Being given the label instead of the answer. "It's just a…", said to close the subject. A world so explained and labelled that nothing is left to wonder at (the sheet's own heartbreak), and being made to stop asking (entrapment, punishment). Gently: they may also half-fear that finding out spends the wonder, so they sometimes don't look.

## What they give
With them in the room, "I don't know" stops being embarrassing and becomes the start of the fun. They make it safe for others not to know, and they hand back to tired people the habit of asking. (Say this kindness out loud once, per Robin's caregiver-batch edits.)

## Position (my first lean, the room shapes it)
The hard truth, kindly: the answers were never the enemy; the labels are. Look it up, ask the expert, find out, because a true answer is usually stranger than the name it was given. Distinct from: *A Brother's Care* ("let the first moment go unexplained"), *In a Minute* ("a question you haven't looked up yet"), *Serviceable* ("that's just how it is"), *Off-Label* ("a door closing").

## Siblings, and how this one differs
- **The Discoverer** (*In a Minute*, explorer-innocent, the mirror pairing and the nearest kin): stops for small ordinary things, owns slowness, "come on", the walk, "sound naive", fear of losing the tug inside. The Free Spirit doesn't stop. It goes in and asks why; its fear is outside (the label), not inside.
- **The Surrealist** (*The Other Berry*): notices things are already strange, holds back the strangest ones, "a second look at things they thought they knew". Ours asks; theirs tells.
- **The Naive Artist** (*The Way It Felt*): someone explains the proper way of making. Keep "explained to" away from making; ours is being told what a thing *is*.
- **The Wise Kid** (unauthored): wants the answer and says the true thing. Ours wants the next question.
- **The Dreamer** (unauthored): imagines what isn't there. Ours finds the strangeness in what is.
- **The Outsider** (unauthored): doesn't fit, stands apart. Ours questions rules out of puzzlement, never defiance.
- **The Big Sibling** (*Night Light*): keeps watch over someone smaller. Ours wanders off after the rabbit.
- **The Giggler**, **True Friend** (*Four Shares*), **Underdog**, **Heart-throb**, **Little Prince**: laughter, inclusion, the long road, love, keeping alive. Not ours.
- Off-family echoes: the Experimentalist (*Off-Label*, "you'd rather find out than be told"), the Conservationist (*Left Standing*, "you'd rather know it's there than have it").

## Traps
1. Childishness: "childlike wonder", "never grew up", "innocent". Flattery and condescension. The guest is an adult.
2. Barnum: "you love learning", "curious about everything".
3. Slowness, walks, small things, "come on", "naive": *In a Minute*'s.
4. Rebellion: the "why" is sincere, not cheek.
5. Alice: no Alice, no rabbit hole, no "Drink me", no "curiouser", no shrinking. With a fungus story, **no mushrooms and nothing psychedelic, ever** (the Caterpillar's mushroom is one step away).
6. Expeditions and conquest: no maps, no ice, no survival (Shackleton is the Explorer family's).
7. Punishment at its everyday size only: told off, an eye-roll. No childhood harm.
8. Openers: "Nobody knows…" opens *Four Shares*. Avoid door metaphors (*Off-Label*, *Hoping You'd Come*, the Angel).
9. Meta: the reading mustn't label them. Leave them room; end on something open.

## Story test
1. The truth was stranger than the name it was first given (a label that failed, and finding out made it more wonderful).
2. Something honestly still open on the page today.
3. Someone looked with questions, not profit, conquest or a scrubbing brush.
4. Nobody harmed, sued, mocked or punished in the telling.
5. It can live in the glass without a gimmick.

## Verdict on the plan lead (*Baudoinia compniacensis*)
