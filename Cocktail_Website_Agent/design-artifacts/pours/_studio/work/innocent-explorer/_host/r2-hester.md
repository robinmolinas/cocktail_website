📜 **Hester:** Wren, your five questions, answered from the page. The full card is `_studio/fact-cards/baudoinia-compniacensis.md`. One answer makes the story better.

1. **"Soot, then alive."** Strike it. No source says anyone took it for soot. Scott's paper (*Mycologia* 99(4), 2007, the paper that named it) only says it looked "black, sooty". The true sequence of names is stranger. **1872:** a Cognac pharmacist, Antonin Baudoin, shows the growth on walls and roof tiles to two botanists. They suggest a *Xenodochus* (Scott). **1878:** Baudoin prints his own short study and calls it a nostoc, an algae-like organism. That's wrong (BnF record: *Étude sur le nostoc des murs de Cognac*, 15 pages). **1881:** Richon and Petit call it *Torula compniacensis*. Scott explains *Torula* was a catch-all that over 300 names were put into, nearly all moved out since. Then, after the 1950s, the science papers say nothing at all, and industry studies on three continents keep naming the same ordinary everyday fungi. That's "it's just a…" on the record, the exact fear in your read.
2. **Prediction.** You're right. Oxford pdf 238 says there's no simple formula to predict its *growth*. "Where" goes.
3. **The decade.** The paper is Scott, Untereiner, Ewaze, Wong and Doyle, *Mycologia*, July 2007, not the 1990s. Oxford is wrong, but under our rule we give no decade. "More than a century later" is true either way.
4. **Who Baudoin was.** A pharmacist (Scott). Rogers in *Wired* (2011), secondary, adds that he directed Cognac's agricultural and industrial chemistry laboratory. The same "A. Baudoin" wrote a book on making cognac in 1893 (BnF, and Oxford's bibliographies at pdf 444 and 619), which gives Tomás's cognac a person. Clause 3 now holds. Here's the line I'd fight for: he got it wrong, and the 2007 genus is "named in honor of Antonin Baudoin" anyway. Rogers reports that the team picked him as the man who first brought it to mycologists. For a guest whose questions got them told off, that's the mirror: the one who asked, and got the answer wrong, is the one whose name it carries. Fact, not motive.
5. ***compniacensis* means "of Cognac".** Rogers translates it as "the Torula from Cognac", and Richon and Petit's title names "the walls of Cognac". That's secondary plus a primary title, not a library page. Good enough, labelled.

**One correction to your conditions.** Drop "related only to". Oxford overstates here. Scott says its *closest* relatives are *Friedmanniomyces*, a group of rock-dwelling fungi "known only from the Antarctic", so: "its nearest known relatives live on rock in Antarctica". It's still believed to live on something besides alcohol (Oxford "it is believed"; Rogers leaves it as a question), so the open ending stands. Also never write "Baudoin identified it" or "he called it *Torula*". He raised it. Others named it.
