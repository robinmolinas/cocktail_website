**The person:** the Omnipotent One holds the whole thing at once (the trip, the move, the evening), every piece and how each leans on the others; everyone else walks in and it simply works. What they hide: the map never switches off. They've lived the evening once already in their head, every way it could go wrong, so the others get the magic and they get the relief. The fear: a piece they didn't place, something with a mind of its own. The position: surprise isn't the crack in what you built; don't put the moved piece back on the spot, it may be what people came for.

**Story:** the plan's lead, accepted by Wren in r1: James Ashley's London Punch House (Oxford ASHLEY, JAMES pdf 160–163, Wondrich). In 1731 he took a big tavern on Ludgate Hill and named it for four trades; behind the counter, cellars, spirits aged in-house and (it seems) a Seville-orange shrub bottled ahead, so any punch was "as soon made as a Gill of Wine can be drawn", one part spirit to two. The hands at the counter were mostly someone else's (Mrs. Gaywood and the young women). Coffee, beer and ale stopped being the heart of the business, and nobody knows who moved it. Wren found the page the plan hadn't used: in the 1750s Ashley handed the bar to his son, and when he died in 1776 he was back running it with him (signposted, never "couldn't let go").
