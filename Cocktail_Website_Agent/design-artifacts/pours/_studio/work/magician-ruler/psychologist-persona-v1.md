# The Omnipotent One (magician-ruler): Wren's read, round 1

Source: `persona.py magician-ruler` (xlsx row). Essence: "All powerful, all knowing." Story: likes to hold the whole picture; the Sistine ceiling, reaching out to give life; wants to make the world and set it in order; plans the trip, runs the project, knows how every piece fits; "when it works, it feels like magic". Fear: surprise, "the one piece that moves on its own and undoes everything they set up". Drivers: leave a mark / provide structure.

## The one true thing (no sibling has it)

**They hold the whole thing at once.** Not a part, not a person, not a moment: the trip, the project, the evening, every piece of it and how each piece fits the others. Other people walk into something that simply works. The Omnipotent One is the only one there who can see why it works, all of it, all the time.

Its other face is the fear the row names exactly: **the piece they didn't place.** Not failure in general (that's the whole Ruler side), but surprise: one thing moving by itself inside a world where everything else was put there on purpose.

## What they hide

- The map never switches off. At their own party, on their own holiday, part of them is running the whole thing in the background, checking the one piece that might move. They've already lived the evening once in their head, every way it could go wrong.
- So they rarely get to simply *be in* what they built. Everyone else gets the magic. They get the relief.
- They'd never say it, but stepping back is harder than building. Hand it over and they drift back to it.

## What they fear

Surprise. The guest who wasn't on the list, the supplier who changes the order, the friend who "improves" the plan. Not because it's a disaster, but because it's proof that something in their world had a will of its own.

## What they give (say it out loud, once: Robin's caregiver rule)

Everyone around them gets to stop thinking for a while. A whole room gets to be carefree because one person held all of it. That's a real gift, and it's the one they hear thanked least, because when it works, nobody notices there was anything to hold.

## Position (direction for y5, not wording)

Surprise isn't the crack in what you built; it's what a well-built thing can carry. Next time a piece moves that you didn't place, don't reach for it on the spot. Leave it one evening and see what it does. What you built is stronger than you fear, and the piece that moved may turn out to be the thing people came for. The guest gains: for once, getting to be surprised inside their own world, like everyone else.

Fences for the position: never "let go" or "let the rest run" (*Before It Had a Name* y5 owns "Let the rest run in other people's hands… how you'll know it was real"); never "let someone else make the second" (*Making the Calls*); never "be there for that second" (*Their Night*); no "wait" as the closing verb (*Go On*: "Let it wait on the ice").

## Traps

1. Diagnosis or insult: never "control", "control freak", "can't delegate". Say what they do.
2. God talk: no "Creator", "God", "almighty", "playing God", no ceiling in guest text. The Sistine imagery is for the image brief only (Tomás's).
3. Owned phrasings above: "looks effortless / it isn't", "nobody sees the plan", "finished thing first", "three moves ahead", "let the rest run", a name wandering off.
4. Grandeur: the guest reads the name "The Omnipotent One" first; the reading must make it human, even a little funny, never grand.
5. Flattery of competence ("you're brilliant at running things"). Recognise the cost instead.

## Story test (for the room)

1. Someone designed a whole system (a bar, a menu, an event, a way of serving) with every piece planned.
2. The scale of the unseen planning is on the record.
3. Others experienced it as easy.
4. One piece moved on its own, and it held or went well.
5. The planner couldn't simply stand back and enjoy it (motive may be signposted).

Out: sole creative credit (the Auteur's), rules for safety or fairness (the Nanny's, the Good Wizard's), a happy accident, a name taken by the public.

## Verdict on the plan's lead: ACCEPT James Ashley, with rulings

Read whole: Oxford ASHLEY, JAMES, pdf 160, 162 and **163** (the plan stopped before 163, and 163 changes the row).

- (1, 2) On the page: cellars, spirits bought in large lots and aged himself, a shrub premixed, bottled and cellared (circumstantial evidence: "it seems"), prices "in proportion" down to an eighth of a pint, so any quantity was covered.
- (3) Any punch "as soon made as a Gill of Wine can be drawn"; the house "perpetually thronged".
- (4) He opened it for four trades (coffee, punch, beer, ale). Before long the other three ceased to be central and it was the punch people came for. That's the piece that moved, and it held: the most famous house of its kind, for forty-five years.
- (5) **Now partly on the page (pdf 163):** in the 1750s he passed the bar's management to his son and turned to dealing spirits from his cellars, but when he died in 1776 he was back running the bar, with his son. No motive given.

### Rulings

1. **Test 4 is told as what people came for, never as the name.** *More Than One Head* owns "a name out in the world, nobody asked the family" and *Their Night* owns "the name wandered off". The short name appears only as Oxford's evidence ("known simply as…"), never as the public renaming it.
2. **The return is the reading's hidden truth, signposted only.** Never "he couldn't let go" as fact. "I like to think…" one sentence. It's gently funny; let it be.
3. **The system is the story:** the cellar under the crowded room, the shrub made before anyone ordered, any amount as fast as a glass of wine. Mixing in front of the customer appears only as part of the system's speed, never as honesty or a fair measure.
4. **Stature in one clause, keeping "perhaps"** ("perhaps more than any other person" behind the modern bar). Never "handed on" or a chain (*Down the Line*).
5. **His mark, once, neutrally:** the inscription painted across the front, "Pro Bono Publico", recounting what he'd done, is the "leave a mark" driver on the page. Never "obsessed with his reputation", never the litigation.
6. **Mrs. Gaywood**, his chief bartender, is welcome in one clause (the system ran in other hands); Hester's call.
7. **Out:** Franklin (*Overnight*), Boswell's "dissolute youths", bowl or portion language (*Four Shares*), nutmeg (`nuts`), *Say So*'s whole-bar look. Arrack's colonial origin: one plain clause at most, no romance.
8. **History share:** the page is rich; at most half of yours.
