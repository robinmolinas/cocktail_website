## whoYouAre

You started something of your own. Maybe a Sunday football team, a stall at the weekend market, a supper that turned into a monthly thing, or a whole business. When people ask why, they expect a big reason. The real one is smaller, and you rarely say it: you wanted to live your way, and pay your own way doing it.

There's something else you keep quiet. Somewhere along the line, other people started treating it as theirs. They turn up early, bring friends and save each other seats. And part of you worries about ending up in the corner, keeping the whole thing running for everyone else to belong to. It should be said, because you won't say it: the freedom you went out for always turns out to have room for other people in it.

## yours

1. Tomas Estes taught English in Los Angeles and wanted to live in Amsterdam, a place where, he said, he could learn a lot about himself. A teacher's job couldn't get him there. A Mexican restaurant could. "My main goal was to support myself," he said, though one writer later called it his way to freedom.

2. He opened Café Pacifico in Amsterdam in 1976, with little money, on a street people called too dangerous. Within six weeks, his partner Phil Bayly remembered, there were queues. A London Pacifico with a big bar followed. Young bartenders with few places to go found it, and its barman spent ten years making Margaritas for the ones just off their shifts. At another of his London places, one bartender said he felt at home.

3. Bayly remembered his rule for the staff: "Tight on bottom, loose on top." Get the work right, then do it your way. I like to think that's the whole of him: he left to be free, then handed the same freedom on. In 2017 he said he was passing most of his share in the London restaurant to the people who ran it, keeping a little because he wanted "to be part of the community". He added that he should have got out of the way five years sooner.

4. The cocktail I've made for you is a Margarita, a renowned classic. I made it with the two bottles his London barman said Estes chose: Sauza blanco tequila and Giffard triple sec, shaken hard with fresh lime and served ice-cold, with no salt. The bottom is measured to the drop, as a nod to the tight half of his rule. [Loose top, Tomás's pick, one clause: what it is, and that it's left to whoever makes it, for the loose half.] It's built so that whatever they choose, the drink underneath still holds.

5. Don't leave it as late as he did. Whatever you've started, the team, the stall, the thing with your name on the door, hand someone the piece you've kept hold of and let them do it their way. It won't be how you'd do it. It'll be theirs, and that means it's working. Then turn up the way everyone else does: early, with a friend, for no reason except that you like it there. You went out on your own to be free. You don't have to stay on your own to keep it.

## Closing line (Tomás's; for round 4)
Tomás's draft: *Make the bottom exactly. Then let whoever's behind the bar finish it, and sit down.* **Flag:** "let someone make you this, and sit down while they do" is *Off Duty*'s y5 (caregiver-hero), and "sit down"/"sitting down" also appears in *Off Duty*'s closing line and in *Cold Water First*. Being looked after belongs to the Rescuer and the True Friend. Ours is handing over control and belonging, not rest. An offer: *Measure the bottom exactly. Then hand the top to someone else, and stay for the first one.*

## Resonance

- **As the guest:** the "how did they know" line is the tagline, and in the reading it's "the real one is smaller, and you rarely say it". The private one is the corner: keeping the thing running while everyone else belongs. The gift is said plainly ("room for other people in it"). The position gives something back: a seat in what they made. Last line: "You don't have to stay on your own to keep it."
- **As the sceptic:** "you started something" isn't true of everyone, and that's on purpose. The examples (five-a-side, stall, supper, business) keep it from being a founder label. No "vision", no "dream" (that's *Down the Line*'s), no "first" (*Nothing to It*'s). No praise of ambition.
- **Siblings:** not *Right Here* (no "one of them", no "above yourself", no hidden talent); not *Whoever Comes In* (nobody is rescued); not *Brought Home* (Mexico is never the gift, and no "ticket"); not *Still Yours* (bartenders came on their own, nothing was "made for the staff"); not *Four Shares* or *Off Duty* (the position is to hand over control, not to be looked after).
- **Facts held to the card:** "freedom" is signposted as the writer's (F3/L1). "Restaurant with a big bar" (C1). No year for Bayly, no count of places, no "first", no celebrities. "Said he was passing" (F18: in the process in 2017, outcome unread). The regret is quoted from the same answer, and "ashamed" stays in the dossier. The La Perla line is "another of his London places", with the place unnamed.
- **History share:** y1–y3 are 216 of 405 words (53%). Without y3's signposted "I like to think" sentence it's 48%. y4's loose-top clause will add words outside the history and bring the share down. If it doesn't land at or under half, I cut y2's La Perla sentence first.
- **4-grams** (lint's own `ngrams()`, against every pour): clean after these fixes: "what you don't say" (*Is It Just Me*, *The Other Berry*), "plainly what you give" (*Whoever Comes In*, magician-caregiver), "a market stall, a" (*Brought Home*), "after a trip to" (innocent-hero), "do the job well" (*Why Not?*), "most famous drinks there is" (innocent-hero), "a renowned classic, made" (three pours), "the part you've been" (*Rosetta*), "wait as long as" (*Fair Measure*), "deserves saying out loud" (innocent-caregiver), "that's how you know" (*For Good*).
