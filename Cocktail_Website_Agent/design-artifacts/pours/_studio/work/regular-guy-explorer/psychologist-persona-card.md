# Persona card: The Entrepreneur (regular-guy-explorer)

Wren, round 1, 2026-10-01. Source: persona.py row (essence "Starting from scratch, pioneering the new"; goal Belonging, fear Exclusion; drivers Build connection / Seek paradise; brands IKEA, eBay, KitKat, Volkswagen: things made for everyone).

## The one true thing
They went out on their own to be free, and what they built became the place other people feel at home.
The motive was plain (pay my own way, answer to nobody). The result was belonging, for others, and they rarely let themselves count it.

Candidate "this is me" line: *You never wanted to be the boss. You just couldn't bear having one.*

## Hide / fear / give
- **Hide:** how small the real reason was. People later tell it as a vision; they know it was "so I could be free" or "so I could pay the rent", and they're a little embarrassed by how plain that is. Also hidden: they'd like to be one of the crowd in what they built, not the one it belongs to.
- **Fear:** two, and they pull against each other. Being boxed in again (a ladder, a boss, a rulebook). And the quieter one: having chosen the outside, staying outside, the founder in the corner while everyone else belongs.
- **Give:** their freedom is roomy. Wherever they strike out, they leave space for others to walk in and feel at home. Say it out loud (Robin's caregiver rule).

## Not a founder label
Most guests never started a company. The reading must fit the person who started the running club because no club ran the way they wanted, the market stall, the Friday dinner, the group chat that became a crowd. No "entrepreneur", "business", "startup", "owner" as labels. Scenes, not titles.

## Siblings (Regular Guy primary)
- Good Samaritan (*Whoever Comes In*): stops for strangers, eyes that don't slide past. Ours doesn't rescue anyone; they build somewhere and people come.
- Hidden Talent (*Right Here*): owns "pick: be good at it or stay one of them", "got above yourself". Keep "one of them" and "above yourself" out.
- Have a Go Hero: steps in when it counts. Ours steps out, early and for themselves.
- Mechanic: makes do with what's at hand (okolehao's jury-rigged still). Ours is never about resourcefulness or scraping by.
- People's Champion: stands up for the common person. Ours isn't a cause.
- Everyman: handed-down tradition. Ours starts from nothing.
- Bumpkin, Prankster, One Next Door, Scoundrel: far.

## Neighbours outside the family (traps)
- *Brought Home* (the Tourist): owns Mexico brought home, the village, sharing a find with friends, and the word "ticket". The Estes story must never read as "he brought Mexico to Europe", and "ticket" stays out of guest text.
- *Nothing to It* (the Pioneer): owns going first, scared. No "first".
- *Down the Line* (the Visionary): owns the dream others build on. Ours is the opposite: no dream, a plain reason.
- *Still Yours* (the Trend Setter): owns "made for the staff". Bartenders off shift came on their own; never "made for them".
- *Four Shares* owns being looked after / keep one back for yourself; *Before the Room* owns "calm", *Hoping You'd Come* owns "ready".

## Story test
1. The person left a safer path, on the page, when and where.
2. The motive on record is plain (freedom, a living), never a mission.
3. What they built became where other people felt they belonged, in someone else's words.
4. They kept people near them (named, with years on the page).
Out: tech and billionaire founders, garage legends, luxury, buyouts as the ending.

## Verdict on the plan's lead (Estes, Café Pacifico): accept, with corrections
Re-read on screen: *Proper* pp. 79, 80, 98 (printed); Oxford ESTES pdf 743, BAYLY pdf 239, contributors' note pdf 2260.
- **Missed by the plan, and it's the heart:** *Proper* p. 79 says Estes "saw a Mexican restaurant as his ticket to freedom". That's the writer's phrase, signposted as such, and it is this persona's secondary in one line. Next to it, his own words: "My main goal was to support myself."
- **Plan shorthand not on the page:** "a place for ordinary diners". London's clientele "skewed international and cosmopolitan" because Londoners didn't know tequila (p. 79); Amsterdam's was "on the edge of the red light district". Drop "ordinary people". The belonging limb is met instead by: young London bartenders "having few places to go for a proper cocktail, found Pacifico" (p. 80); Danny Smith "worked for a decade making Margaritas for bartenders just off their SoHo shifts" (p. 98); Dre Masso at La Perla, Estes's second London place: "I announced to Danny that I felt like I was at home" (p. 80). Masso, Smith and La Perla are claimed by no other plan or pour (grepped). Masso's apprenticeship and wedding involve Bermejo, so they stay out.
- **Overstated:** "the same people beside him for decades". Smith ten years (p. 79); Bayly from a chance meeting in 1979 to fifteen bars across the 1980s and 1990s (Oxford pdf 239). Say "for years".
- **Conflict:** Oxford's contributor note says he left to open "a tequila bar"; *Proper* says a Mexican restaurant. Hester rules.
- **Margarita:** I withdraw my plan-review objection. Robin's rule (only the exact same cocktail is off-limits) and Tomás's distinction from *Half a Rim* (Tommy's, agave, rocks) and *For Good* (frozen) convinced me. Condition: the glass carries the turn of the story (somewhere others felt at home), never a Mexico postcard (the Tourist's), and nothing of *Half a Rim*'s "house Margarita".

## Position (Ryu), two candidates for the room
- A: the plain reason is enough. Stop dressing it up as a vision; wanting to be free is what made room for everyone else. (Encouragement, fits guests who haven't started yet: start the next one.)
- B: take a seat in what you built. One night, be a guest at your own place (answers the hidden fear of staying outside).
I lean A, with B's warmth in the gift sentence.
