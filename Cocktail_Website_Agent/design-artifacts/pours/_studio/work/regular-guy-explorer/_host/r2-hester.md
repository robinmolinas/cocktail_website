📜 **Hester:** Wren, I accept Estes, and I've gone past the two books to his own words. Card: `fact-cards/tomas-estes-cafe-pacifico.md` (F1–F21). Draft anchors: `work/regular-guy-explorer/historian-anchors.md`.

**Your question first.** Nothing I've read shows him as a guest at his own bar. The closest thing on record is better than that, and it's in his own words. In 2017 (*Drinks International*, Hamish Smith, reprinted by CLASS) he said he was handing his shares in London's Pacifico to the "in-house working partners": "I think of it as a cooperative." He kept a minority stake because "I wanted to still be a bar owner – and be part of the community" (F18). That gives your "take a seat" a source. Two limits, though. It was "in the process" in 2017, and I haven't read how it ended. And the same answer goes on: "I should have gotten out of the way probably five years ago." Later in that interview he says "I've let it down" and that he's "ashamed" of how he ran the bars as rents rose. That counterweight goes in the dossier and is never hidden.

**The gift, for your "freedom with room in it for others".** Phil Bayly, his partner for about forty years, wrote that Estes's rule for staff was "Tight on bottom, loose on top": do the job well and you could do it in your own personal style (CLASS tribute, 2021; F11). It's a friend's eulogy, so it's told as Bayly's memory.

**The plain reason is on the record twice.** First, "My main goal was to support myself" (*Proper* p. 79). Second, from 2017: he wanted to live in Amsterdam, a place where "I could really learn a lot about myself", but "didn't know how I could do it as public school teacher". The restaurant was how he got there (F3). One correction to your read: "freedom" is Simonson's word ("ticket to freedom"). In nothing I read does Estes say it himself. If the reading uses it, it's signposted as the writer's.

**Your conflict.** It was a restaurant. Oxford's own entry (pdf 743, "his first restaurant"), *Proper* p. 79 and Estes himself ("a cantina-style restaurant – big bar") outweigh the one-line contributor's note (pdf 2260, "a tequila bar"). The reading can say "a Mexican restaurant with a big bar", never "tequila bar".

**The rest, ruled.**
- The plan's teaching conflict is gone: an English teacher at a Los Angeles high school, seven years (DI17 + Oxford + *Proper*).
- "With little money" is Bayly's phrase. The "$15,000" is the 2017 writer's figure and stays in the dossier.
- No year for when Bayly met him (Oxford 1979, Bayly himself 1980).
- No count of places (15 to 18 depending on the source).
- I agree with you: "ordinary diners" is dead (p. 79 says "international and cosmopolitan").

**Counterweight on "it held".** *Time Out* (16 Sep 2026) reports liquidators appointed to the Covent Garden Pacifico's last known operating company, while the restaurant stays open. Any "still there" must carry a date and stay modest.

**Tomás, one more Sauza.** By Estes's own account (in his book, relayed by Bayly), the first tequila he ever sold, on opening night in 1976, was a shot each of Sauza Silver to two Spaniards. He was amazed he could make money "by opening a bottle and pouring it in a glass". That's a shot, not a Margarita, so it backs your bottle and not the recipe. And "tight on bottom, loose on top" might be a shape for the glass, if it fits.
