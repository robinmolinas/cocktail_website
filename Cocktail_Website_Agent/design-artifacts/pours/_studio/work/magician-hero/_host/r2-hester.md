Over ten years to find a rootstock from Texas for the deep chalk, and the floral Folle Blanche gave way to Ugni Blanc. Test 4 passes in the source's words. Four counterweights, though, before anyone builds on my plan's shorthand:
1. **It isn't lost.** ARMAGNAC pdf 139: Folle Blanche is still about 5% of Armagnac, its spirit floral and fruity, best young; it's a parent of baco blanc; and cognac may still be made from it (*Joy* pdf 419). So "the lost Folle Blanche" goes. It gave way *in Cognac*. That helps your hopeful proposal: what the rescue left behind is still there to make room for.
2. **The cause is rot, not refusal.** pdf 545: it didn't suit grafting because its bunches grew so tight they rotted (pdf 139: "fragility and difficulty in grafting"). Never "couldn't take the graft".
3. **Munson can't be pinned to Cognac's chalk.** Oxford names nobody. The *Handbook of Texas* (secondary, read in full) has the 50,000 miles, the French asking for his rootstock, Jaeger credited beside him, and the 1888 delegation to Denison. It never says Cognac, Charentes or chalk. My only bridge is archive.org OCR fragments that cut off mid-sentence (Viala's 1893 *Revue* advertising berlandieri seed Munson collected). So tell it as Oxford does, "a rootstock found in Texas", with Munson as one of the men who sent vines, Jaeger named. Viala, 1887 and berlandieri stay out. Never "Munson saved Cognac".
4. **The save isn't a restoration.** 280,000 ha in the 1870s, 40,000 by 1893, about 76,000 today on the best chalk (pdf 546). "Still France's biggest single-product vineyard" is true. "Brought it all back" isn't.
