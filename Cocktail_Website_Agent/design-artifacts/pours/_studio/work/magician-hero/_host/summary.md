**The person:** the Good Wizard is the one who says what has to change to save a thing, and does it step by step. What they keep to themselves is the cost: the part they had to let go, which they still think about after it worked. Their fear is the side effects, not failure. The gift is said out loud: things that would have been lost are still here because of them.

**Story:** Wren replaced the plan's lead in r1. Taylor and Bottled-in-Bond went out because a lobbied law reads as the Ruler's. In came Cognac after phylloxera: more than ten years to find a rootstock, from Texas, that would grow in the deep chalk. The floral Folle Blanche, whose bunches rotted when grafted, made way for Ugni Blanc. Hester's counterweights shaped it. It isn't lost: about 5% of Armagnac, still allowed in cognac. Nobody is named as the finder, so Munson (with Jaeger) stays in the dossier. No year is given, and the vineyard was never restored to its 1870s size.

**What changed along the way:** Tomás's verjus spark fell in r3 because *Are You Quite Sure?* (sage-innocent) already has it in a grape-brandy drink, and gooseberry came in. The shaken sour lost to a stirred Old-Fashioned, because a sour's acid would drown a quarter-measure of floral spirit. "Roots from Texas" became "a rootstock from Texas" (Oxford says it was found there, not that the roots in the ground were Texan). Ten audit fixes landed in round 4, step 1.

**Drink:** a stirred Old-Fashioned on one large cube: 45 ml VS cognac, 15 ml young Armagnac labelled Folle Blanche, 10 ml homemade green-gooseberry syrup, no bitters. 24.4% · 4.39 g sugar/100 ml · 0.140% acid (top edge); veto-free.
