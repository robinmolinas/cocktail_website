# Persona card: The Good Wizard (magician-hero) · Wren, round 1

## Core truth
When something is going under, they're the one able to change it enough to save it, and they will. But they never pretend a rescue comes free. They save it by changing it, and they remember what the change took.

## Shows / hides
- **Shows:** the plan. When everyone else is frozen for fear of making it worse, they say "this is what we change", and they carry it through. People feel safe because the most capable person in the room is also the most careful with it.
- **Hides:** that the side effects keep them up, not the failures. It worked; and still they replay the part the fix took with it: the thing that had to go so the rest could stay. They don't mention it, because saying it out loud would spoil the relief for everyone else.

## Hidden fear -> hidden gift
- **Fear:** unintended results. Not "will it work?" but "what will it do that I didn't mean?" Their power is real, so its side effects are real too.
- **Gift:** they act anyway, with a plan. Most people, afraid of the side effect, do nothing and lose everything. They choose the version that saves the most, and they own the choice so nobody else has to carry it. Said out loud (Robin's rule): because of them, things that would have been lost are still here.

## The "real me" line to aim for (drafts, mine to keep working)
- "Everyone remembers that you saved it. You remember what you had to change."
- "You've never once fixed something without counting what the fix took."

## Truth vs legend
The true story, cost included. A tidy rescue would read as a lie to this person: they know rescues have a price. Tell the save and the loss in plain words, and let the save be bigger.

## Tone
Assertive, warm, a little grave. Cinematic in shape (storm, then calm), plain in words. No magic vocabulary, no "saved the day", no Harry Potter.

## Distinct from siblings (Magician primary, plus the Hero neighbours)
- **The Sorcerer's Apprentice** (unauthored) fears the spell running away from them: a beginner's fear of losing control. The Good Wizard is in control; their fear is the side effect of a spell that *worked*.
- **The Makeover Artist** (*Overnight*: "You change everything except the part that makes it theirs") keeps what makes it theirs. The Good Wizard knows that sometimes you can't, and saves it anyway. The honest inverse: own it in the reading, never borrow the frame.
- **The Healer** (*Till Spring*: "the few who didn't get better stay with you") carries the ones who didn't mend. The Good Wizard carries something different: what their own decision took. Not a failure; a trade they chose.
- **The Legend** (*The Long View*: "The worse it gets, the clearer you are") owns clarity in a crisis and the words. **The Rescuer** (*Off Duty*) owns speed and going first. **The Firefighter** (*Anyone Would Have*) owns preparing so the day is ordinary. The Good Wizard is none of these: the point is the change and its price, not calm, speed or practice.
- **The Noble Ruler** (*A Place Kept*: "You never said what it cost you") owns the cost to oneself. The Good Wizard's cost falls on the thing saved.
- **The Pioneer** (*Nothing to It*) is afraid for themselves and goes; it owns "how close it came". The Good Wizard is afraid for others.

## Traps
- "What it cost (you)" (*A Place Kept* owns it). "Go back to it" / "go back over" (*Off-Label*, *Till Spring*). "Calm/clear in a crisis" (*The Long View*, *Off Duty*). "People you'll never meet" (*Serviceable*'s tagline). Invisible safeguards nobody notices (*What It Rests On*, *For Good*, *Go On*).
- Medicine words: never "cure", "disease", "heal", "remedy" (the Healer's and the Doctor's ground). A pest is a pest.
- Magic words in the reading: no spells, wands, wizards, "magic".
- Military imagery (both stories carry it: "Colonel", "the war on the louse").
- Never "saved the day", never bravery for show.
- Grief told heavily: the loss is one clause, the save is the headline (Robin: intrigued, never frightened).

## My story test for this pour
1. A real crisis with hope running out, on the record.
2. A plan built from long, patient knowledge, not a lucky save.
3. Rescue *by transformation*: something had to be changed so it could be saved.
4. A cost on the record that the plan itself brought (an unintended result), not a loss from somewhere else.
5. What was saved still stands, and is bigger than what was lost.

## Verdict on the plan's lead (Taylor and the Bottled-in-Bond Act): replace
- It misses the persona's heart. Taylor's losses (OFC, the fight over his name, Prohibition) are business setbacks and a later, bigger law. None is a price his own plan brought (test 4), and nothing was transformed to be saved (test 3).
- Its ground is crowded: "drinkers he'd never meet" is *Serviceable*'s tagline; honest labels and fakes are *A Brother's Care*, *Plain to See* and *Kept*; "rival distillers said it wasn't whisky" is *Either Way*'s epigraph; and the rectifiers he fought used column spirit, which our own sibling *One More Adjustment* celebrates. Two Magician pours would sit on opposite sides of "what counts as whiskey".
- A law won by lobbying reads as the Ruler's or the Campaigner's (*Not Too Polite*), and the motive is tangled with his own trade.

## Replacement lead: the Texas rootstock that brought Cognac back, and the grape it couldn't save
Promoted from the plan's backup, with a cost I found on the plan's own page.
- **Oxford Companion, COGNAC (pdf 545–546), primary:** by the 1870s the Cognac vineyard was 280,000 hectares, the biggest in the world. Phylloxera "proved a disaster", and it took over a decade to find a rootstock, in Texas, that would suit the region's deep chalk. The old grape, the floral **Folle Blanche**, proved unsuitable for grafting (its bunches were so tight they rotted), so it was replaced by the much less aromatic Ugni Blanc. By 1893 the vineyard was down to 40,000 hectares; recovery was slow.
- **Munson** (Handbook of Texas, secondary, per the plan): years and 50,000 miles of collecting and studying wild grapes; the French, "knowing of Munson's expertise", asked him for rootstocks; a French delegation honoured him at Denison in 1888.
- **Passes:** (1) a decade of a disaster; (2) the plan came out of one man's long passion for wild grapes (with others: name them); (3) the vines were saved by being grafted onto other roots; (4) the rescue itself couldn't carry the floral grape: Oxford's own words; (5) Cognac still stands.
- **What it gives the reading:** "the friend they could not save" is in the source, as a grape. The proposal can be hopeful: once it's safe, make room again for what the rescue had to leave behind (Folle Blanche is still grown; Hester to source where and how).
