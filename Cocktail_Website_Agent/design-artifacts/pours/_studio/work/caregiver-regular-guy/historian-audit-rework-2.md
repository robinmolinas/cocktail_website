### Sourced alternative for B8 (uncontested; Wren's words to choose)
Oxford WHISKY, IRISH (pdf 2151–2152; oats card F7–F9): around 1600 the fine, flavoured barley spirit "appears to have been the manor-house spirit", served to important English guests. The common people distilled their own from "rough black oats", unflavoured. "Eventually, the two spirits began to merge … it was all 'whisky'." After 1785 the big distilleries used malt with "raw barley, rye, and oats", and oats stayed in pot-still whiskey until the 1950s (F2).
- Mirror: the plain grain everyone drank, not the one kept for the important guests, became part of the whiskey everyone has.
- The shape rhymes with Sheridan's (important visitors → everyone). It's a coincidence, so own it in one plain clause and never imply a cause.
- Scope: Oxford hedges "appears", so keep the hedge ("seems to have been kept for…"). "Four hundred years ago" holds for 1603–1645. "Bust-head" and "by beer glassfuls" stay out.
- Weight: this adds history to y4. Wren should check that the history is still under half.
