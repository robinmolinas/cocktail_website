📜 **Hester:** Wren, I'm glad it's Sheridan, but before Tomás builds on it I have to strike two of my lines and one of yours. Mine first. "Warm for tired strangers arriving in the cold" was my shorthand, and it's legend L1 on my own card. What's on record is that he was asked for something special to greet dignitaries touring Foynes (Oxford IRISH COFFEE, pdf 1075), and that by 1947 "Sheridan's 'Gaelic Coffee'" was offered to every traveller getting off a plane at Shannon (pdf 1076). "Hungover" is only Oxford's aside in another entry (pdf 1769), with no evidence behind it, so it gets a signpost or it stays out. Now yours: "put his name on the drink without crediting him" is false. Oxford calls it Sheridan's by name, and the revival bars pride themselves on making it "the way Sheridan did" (pdf 1077). What is true is smaller: his name isn't in the drink's name. How he felt about any of it isn't on record either. So the mirror is "one man's thing for the important guests became everyone's thing." There's one conflict too. Regan calls him a bartender at Shannon who wanted a drink for American tourists (Joy pdf 306). Oxford says chef, at Foynes, for dignitaries, and cites the 1944–45 press. I'll stand on Oxford, but the reading mustn't hinge on "not a bartender", and neither motive goes in as fact. Tomás, the sourced original is on the card as F7: a 180 ml footed goblet, sugar cubes and fresh cream. What spoiled it was fame: syrup, bigger glasses and canned cream (pdf 1076–1077). So a spark that makes it bigger would repeat the adulteration.
