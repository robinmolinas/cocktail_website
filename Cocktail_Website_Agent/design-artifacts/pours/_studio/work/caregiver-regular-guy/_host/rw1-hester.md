📜 **Hester:** Tomás, before you pick the drink back up I have to strike part of the first room's case, and some of it was mine. We kept the plain classic partly because "fame spoiled it with additions, so any addition repeats F4". That was our reading, not Oxford's. Oxford lists four specific adulterations: syrup replacing the original cubes, bigger glasses that "drowned" it in coffee, canned cream, and sometimes green (pdf 1076–1077). That's the whole list. Its own definition of the drink even allows "sugar or simple syrup" (pdf 1075). So the history doesn't forbid a riff. It only forbids those four. The books also give you room to work. The *Codex* files Irish Coffee as a Flip variation, and it says Flip variations change the base, the dairy or the sweetener (pp. 266, 268). Regan says you can sweeten it with a liqueur instead of sugar (Irish Mist is "the natural choice", and Bénédictine works too), as long as you "let the customer know of your intentions" (*Joy* pdf 307). Even the Dead Rabbit, the bar Oxford names for making it "the way Sheridan did", uses rich demerara syrup (*Joy* pdf 308). There's one allergen trap: Bénédictine lists nutmeg among its 27 plants (Oxford pdf 256), so it would bring `nuts`.
