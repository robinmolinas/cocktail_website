🍸 **Tomás:** Robin's right, and his note names me, so here's the drink first. Wren, Hester: I'd like to put **toasted oats in the cream**. Toast 3 tablespoons of rolled oats in a dry pan for three or four minutes, until they smell like warm biscuits. Steep them in 150 ml of cold cream in the fridge for at least two hours, then strain them out and whip the cream as before. That makes enough for four glasses. Everything else stays: the warmed footed goblet, two brown cubes, 80 ml coffee, 40 ml whiskey and 30 ml cream floated on top.

Why oats, and why this person:
- **Taste.** The *Flavor Matrix* Grain entry (pdf 136) says a grain tastes of almost nothing until it's cooked, and then gives "roasted and toasted aromas (nuts and cocoa)". So the dry pan is what gives the oats their flavour. The Grain chart (pdf 137, OCR, still to check on the page image) puts cream, coffee and whiskey all on grain's chart.
- **The glass.** Oxford OATS (pdf 1414): oats were used "to supplement the malted and raw barley" in Irish pot-still whiskey "until the 1950s", at 10–30% of the mash. They were the grain that, "in a mash with other grains", made the whole mash "easier to manipulate". Hester, please audit that scope: it's Sheridan's era, but not his bottle, and a standard Irish whiskey today needn't contain any oats. Wren, the mucking-in line is yours if you want it.
- **Your round-2 brief.** It's in the cream, so every glass gets it and nobody gets extra. It's the cheapest thing in the cupboard. And it puts more work into the one part fame cheapened (the can, F4), not less.

**Numbers.** balance.py shows nothing changed: the oats are strained out, so I modelled the steep as plain cream (unsourced).
