# Persona Read — The Nurse (caregiver-regular-guy)
_Wren, Round 1_

## Who This Person Is

The Nurse shows up. Not because anyone asked, not because it will be noticed, but because there's something that needs doing and they are there. The Regular Guy secondary matters here: this is not the heroic rescuer or the expert healer. They wouldn't want to be called either. They'd say anyone would do the same.

That's the thing they hide. Not that they care — they know they care. What they won't say is why. The fear is selfishness, and that fear runs deep. They give care constantly, reliably, without complaint, partly because it is genuinely who they are, and partly because giving care means they never have to sit still and wonder whether they deserve to receive it. When you are always the one handing things out, you never have to ask for anything back.

The Regular Guy secondary deepens this: they don't want to be special. Special makes you separate. Their care is their way of belonging — you can't be left out if you're always the one making sure no one else is left out.

## The Hidden Need

They need to feel that their ordinary, daily care — not the dramatic gestures, the Tuesdays — is enough. That being present matters. That you don't have to be the expert or the hero; just being the one who shows up is a real thing.

## What They Give

Presence. Warmth without theatre. They remember the name of your sister, the thing you said two weeks ago, the coffee you take. They make the room feel managed without managing it. People feel safer without knowing why.

## What They Fear (Precise)

Not that they'll be selfish. That they already are — that somewhere underneath the endless giving is a person who needs things, and if they ever stopped moving long enough to find out what, it would make everything they've done feel false.

## The Siblings Check

| Sibling | What makes them different |

| Nanny (caregiver-ruler) | Imposes structure; care as management |
| Doctor (caregiver-sage) | Care through expertise; the answer |
| Angel (caregiver-innocent) | Purity of care; no complexity |
| True Friend (innocent-regular-guy) | Regular Guy primary; care as inclusion, party |

The Nurse's distinction: they don't need to be the expert, the hero, or the manager. They muck in. Their care is everyday, non-specialist, and that's precisely the point. The "real me" line is about the ordinary, daily presence — not the dramatic moments.

## Candidate "This Is Me" Line

"You don't wait to be asked. You just see what needs doing and do it. And afterwards you'd say anyone would."

## Tone Note

Warm but not sentimental. This person isn't soppy about their care — they're practical. A reading that gets too dewy would feel false. The right tone: the warmth of someone who trusts you with the plain truth.

## Story/Drink Directions for the Room

The drink needs to mirror: warmth, comfort, ordinary-but-right, something people have been making for a long time because it works. Not flashy. Maybe something with a history of actual use in care — hot toddies, possets, remedies that became drinks. But the Craftsman (Not Only the Way, Uyeda) already took the "warm remedy" territory from the memory notes. Need to steer away.

Directions worth exploring:
1. Something with a history of nurses or hospital workers — the flask kept in the ward, the drink that marked the end of a shift
2. Something that feels like it was made by someone who knew what you needed before you did
3. An ingredient with a care/comfort history that doesn't overlap with the Craftsman's caraway/Uyeda anchor
4. The punch bowl belongs to the True Friend (Four Shares); avoid sharing vessel
