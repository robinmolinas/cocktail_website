# Hester, rework round 1: what the facts allow the drink to be (2026-09-30)

Robin's note asks for an interesting, personalised drink. The first room kept the plain classic partly on a historical argument: "fame spoiled it with additions, so any addition repeats F4". That argument was ours, not Oxford's. Correcting it here.

## What Oxford actually calls adulteration (pdf 1076–1077; card F4, F8)
1. Syrup replacing the original sugar cubes.
2. Bigger glasses than the 180 ml footed goblet, so the drink was "drowned in coffee".
3. Canned whipped cream instead of fresh.
4. Sometimes it was green.

That's the whole list. Oxford's own definition (pdf 1075) allows "sugar or simple syrup". It never says a liqueur, a spice or a different sweetener spoils it.

## What the books license (card F9–F11)
- Regan, *Joy* pdf 307: sweeten with a liqueur (Irish Mist "the natural choice", Bénédictine works), omit the sugar if so, and "let the customer know of your intentions". Or change the spirit and rename it.
- *Codex* pp. 266, 268: Irish Coffee is a Flip variation; Flip variations swap the base, drop the egg or dairy, or change the sweetener.
- The revival bar Oxford names (Dead Rabbit) uses rich demerara syrup (*Joy* pdf 308). "The way Sheridan did" is the bar's pride, not a matching recipe.

## What a riff must still avoid, factually
- A bigger glass or more coffee (F4 item 2), canned cream (item 3).
- If the reading keeps "it began with cubes, and the syrup only came with fame", the glass keeps cubes; if Tomás moves to a liqueur or syrup, that y4 line goes.
- A touch only the important guest gets contradicts the gesture anchor (our reading, not a fact, but it's the reading's spine).
- Allergens: Bénédictine lists nutmeg among its 27 plants (Oxford pdf 256) → `nuts`. Irish Mist's formula is proprietary (herbs, honey; *Joy* pdf 421): safe side.

## Lines that will need re-audit if the drink changes
- y4 "Yours is close to his", "a little less whiskey than today's usual recipe", "Two cubes each".
- Glass anchor ("Original serve … Our spec: 2 cubes, 40 ml …").
- Closing line "Two cubes for everyone" (only if the cubes go).
- y3: Oxford never says who decided to offer it to everyone; keep "I like to think" on any restatement.
- Never "Sheridan's recipe" or "the way Sheridan did" about our glass once it's a riff.
