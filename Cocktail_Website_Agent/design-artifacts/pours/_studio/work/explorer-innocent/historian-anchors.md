| story | From 2004 Conigliaro put Manhattans and El Presidentes in glass vessels for years, to see how they evolved (*Proper* pp. 228, 236; purpose is Simonson's narration) | An everyday drink, made and drunk in minutes, given years | the one true thing: more time than anyone else would give an ordinary thing |
| story | His own reason: oxidation might help a premixed cocktail if controlled the way it is when wine ages (Oxford, BARREL-AGED COCKTAILS pdf 216, his words) | He asked what time does to an ordinary thing, borrowing the question from wine; open, not blank | patience as trust: expecting the world to be interesting |
| story | At Roka, for years, "nobody paid a bit of attention" to the idea; "No one was interested" (*Proper* p. 228, his words) | The cost is time and other people's indifference, not a fight | the "come on" they've heard all their life |
| story | In October 2009 Jeffrey Morgenthaler tried the five-year-old Manhattan at 69 Colebrooke Row, off the menu, after a tip to ask for it (Oxford pdf 216-217; *PUNCH*, 19 June 2019) | The thing given time reached someone because he asked | the gift reaching others |
| story | Back in Portland, Morgenthaler and his bartenders aged a Manhattan in a small barrel that had held Madeira, and settled on about six weeks (Oxford pdf 217; *Proper*'s Negroni recipe says seven, so "weeks") | The hurried one's version: weeks in wood, not years in glass | time vs hurry, told warmly |
| lineage | Before long bars across the US, then the world, were barrel-aging cocktails; by 2010 on menus everywhere (Oxford pdf 217; *Proper* p. 236). Wood-aged bottled cocktails existed around 1900, unconnected (Oxford pdf 216) | It turned out to matter; never "first"; barrels spread, not his bottles | now and then, the thing they gave time to matters to everyone |
| ingredient | El Presidente: rum, vermouth, dashes of curaçao and grenadine; first printed in Havana in 1915 and widely popular at home and abroad (Oxford, EL PRESIDENTE pdf 715). Oxford's recipe is white Cuban rum and sweet white vermouth 45 ml each, 5 ml each curaçao and grenadine, stirred (pdf 716). Ours (spec v1) is 60 white rum, 15 blanc vermouth, 10 Madeira, 7.5 curaçao, 2.5 grenadine: the proportions are ours. One of the two drinks Conigliaro aged in glass (*Proper* p. 228) | The ordinary thing he gave years to, made the day it's poured; a Cuban classic, never "renowned" (its popularity didn't outlast the 1950s, pdf 716) | the one true thing, in the glass; let the drink sound worth having |
| ingredient | Madeira is a wine "marked by oxidation" (Oxford, ALDEHYDES pdf 88); by law every DO Madeira waits about two years or more before sale, in wood or heated (Portaria 38/2015 and 39/2015) | Time and air used on purpose, which is Conigliaro's wine question (the Madeira in Morgenthaler's first barrel, Oxford pdf 217, stays in the dossier: Wren r5) | patience you can taste the day it's made; "years", never oak (M1); the barrel is not in the reading |
