📜 **Hester:** Tomás, your two asks, and one strike on your own page. **"Three years in oak" fails as a category claim.** The Madeira law (Portaria 38/2015, *Jornal Oficial da Madeira*, 13 February 2015, read in the Portuguese original) defines "Rainwater" only by colour (pale to gold) and sweetness (1.0–2.5° Baumé), with an age of *at most* ten years. There's no minimum age and no oak. The Codex's "minimum of three years in oak" (p. 251) is a house belief, not the rule. What the law does give every Madeira: canteiro wine spends at least two years in wood and isn't bottled before three, and estufagem wine is heated (no more than 50 °C) for at least three months and not sold before the end of October in the second year after the harvest (Portaria 39/2015, arts. 9 and 13). So "a wine that waits years before anyone may sell it" holds for every bottle. "Years in oak" only holds for a named bottle, for instance Barbeito's Rainwater 3 Anos: estufagem, then three years in oak casks, 18.27%, 58 g/L sugar (producer's sheet, secondary; numbers for your balance). If the reading wants oak, name a bottle. If not, the spec says "a rainwater Madeira" and the copy says "years". **The oxidation has its second page:** Oxford lists Madeira among the wines "marked by oxidation" (ALDEHYDES pdf 88). One fence: Oxford's OXIDATION entry (pdf 1458) says that in most wines, oxidation kills the freshness. Madeira is the deliberate exception, so the copy never says oxidation is good for wine. **"The Madeira stands for his barrel" is fair as our symbolism,** because his first barrel-aged Manhattan really did go into a barrel that had held Madeira (Oxford pdf 217). Word it "a nod to the barrel Morgenthaler used, which had held Madeira". Never "the Madeira his Manhattan aged in" as a taste, and never that he chose it: by his own account it was seasoning the barrel for something else. **Two flags on the recipe.** "Bright, citrusy acidity" on p. 251 sits under the Broadbent bottle, not rainwater in general, so don't let the acid case rest on it unless Broadbent is the bottle. And Oxford's recipe says "white Cuban rum" (pdf 716): "lightly aged" is yours, so source it or say white. Card updated (M1–M6), and the ingredient rows are in the draft anchors.
