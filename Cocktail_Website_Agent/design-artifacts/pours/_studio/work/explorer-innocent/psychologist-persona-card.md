# Persona card: The Discoverer (explorer-innocent)

Wren, round 1 (2026-10-01). Read from `persona.py explorer-innocent`, my Explorer sketches (`_studio/plans/work/explorer/psychologist-sketches.md`), the family plan row, and the four family readings so far (*Left Standing*, *For Good*, *Nothing to It*, *Brought Home*), plus *The Other Berry* and *Overnight*.

## Core truth
Their patience is a kind of trust. They give an ordinary thing more time than anyone else would, before there's any sign it'll be worth it, because they expect the world to be interesting, and it usually is. **Most people need a reason to stop. They need a reason to hurry.**

## The scene
Any walk: a few steps behind the others, crouched at something (frost that stays only in a fence's shadow; a bee going into a flower the wrong way round; the same café sign, read properly for the first time). Somebody up ahead says "come on". They've heard "come on" all their life.

## Shows / hides
- **Shows:** unhurried, pleasant, a little distracted; the question asked a beat after everyone else has moved on.
- **Hides:** (1) how much a small thing can make their day, played down because it can look naive; (2) a faint guilt at holding people up. The old telling-off for dawdling never quite left. (The secondary's fear of punishment, softened into being hurried and made to feel slow.) They'd never call it hurt.

## Hidden fear -> hidden gift
- **Fear:** getting busy and sure. The day they walk straight past something and don't feel the tug, and don't even miss it. Becoming someone who already knows.
- **Gift (say it out loud once, Robin's rule):** the people who walk with them see more than they would alone. The ordinary comes back to them new. And now and then the thing they gave time to turns out to matter to everyone.

## "Real me" lines to aim for
- Tagline candidate: *Most people need a reason to stop. You need one to hurry.* (4-gram check later against *Four Shares*' "don't wait for a reason".)
- whoYouAre candidate: *You've been hearing "come on" for as long as you can remember. You were never dawdling. You wanted to see what it would do.*

## Truth vs legend
The true story, told gently. Their own emblem (Newton's apple) is half-legend, so the story must be on the record. They'd enjoy a legend granted and set aside, but no villain, no grievance: the kind version of the truth.

## Tone
Warm, light, unhurried sentences; honest and plain (the secondary's "honest, optimistic, straightforward") with the primary's directness in the ending. A smile, not a joke. Never twee, never wide-eyed.

## Distinct from siblings
- **Surrealist (*The Other Berry*):** sees the oddness and makes an odd thing, says "this is going to sound weird". The Discoverer waits on the ordinary and is surprised by it. *The Other Berry* also owns the hide "you mostly don't say it", so it isn't ours. Keywords "strange", "weird", "notice" are out.
- **Tourist (*Brought Home*):** one street further, brought home. Its y5 owns "a find twenty minutes from your door". The Discoverer needs no new street: the same walk, slower.
- **Conservationist (*Left Standing*):** counts what's going; y5 owns "take someone and show them what you see". Our proposal can't be showing someone.
- **Innovator (*For Good*):** annoyed, fixes. The Discoverer is delighted and gives it time.
- **Pioneer (*Nothing to It*):** goes first, scared. Nothing here is a first.
- **Genius (unauthored; Berry lead):** owns "ignored for years, then proven right". The Discoverer isn't chasing anything and has no one to prove wrong.
- **Spiritualist (unauthored; Regan lead):** owns slowness as a practice (breathing, ten minutes). The Discoverer's slowness isn't calm or a practice; it's an appetite for being surprised. My sketch's ending ("leave one gap in the week") is retired: it reads as wellness.
- **Makeover Artist (*Overnight*):** owns time changing a drink ("leave it", "a night", "don't fix what you find"). **Photographer (*As It Was*):** owns the Tuesdays, keeping, the book that never caught on.
- **Idealist (*The Long Answer*):** patient with other people's silly questions. The Discoverer's patience is with things, not with questions.

## Traps
Newton, the apple, "Eureka"; "curious" as the point; childlike, wide-eyed, dreamer, daydream, innocent; slow read as lazy; slow living or mindfulness; the Barnum "you love the little things"; "first"; the Genius's vindication arc; nationality jokes (Simonson's "impatient American"); in closing lines "wait" (*Rosetta*), "keep one" (*Four Shares*, *Half a Rim*), "go back to it" (*Off-Label*), "tell / say" (taken verbs).

## Story test
1. an ordinary thing many people handled every day and moved on from;
2. someone who gave it time without knowing what they'd find (open, not engineering to a target);
3. it turned out to matter;
4. cost: time, and other people not being interested;
5. all of it on the record. (Surprise, if not on the page, is waived, not faked.)

## Verdict on the lead (Conigliaro, bottle-aged cocktails): accept, with conditions
Read on the page: Proper p. 228 ("a patient man"; Manhattans and El Presidentes in glass "for years to see how they evolved"; Roka: "nobody paid a bit of attention"; 69 Colebrooke Row opened 2009), Proper p. 236 (started 2004; Morgenthaler tasted an aged Manhattan at 69, "being an impatient American" used small barrels; by 2010 on menus worldwide), Oxford BARREL-AGED COCKTAILS pdf 216–217 (his own words: oxidation "in a positive way… the same way that it is with wine when it ages"; Morgenthaler tried his "five-year-old Manhattan", then a one-gallon barrel that had held Madeira; six weeks). Dates agree across both books: 2004 + five years = the 2009 visit.
1. Recognition rests on "to see how they evolved", never on "nobody paid attention, then the world came round" (the Genius's). Roka = the cost, one clause.
2. Morgenthaler: someone in a hurry, told warmly and a little funny (tasted five years, couldn't wait five years, found six weeks). No "impatient American", no "took it to the bank", no credit fight. The slow one's find carried round the world by a fast one is the gift reaching people.
3. Never "first". Gas chromatography stays in the dossier (a lab; it drifts towards the Experimentalist).
4. Any trust or hope in Conigliaro's head is signposted.
5. For Tomás: the story has to be in the glass. If nothing in it has been given time, it isn't. Not "leave it": time you can taste the day it's made. Offered, his call: a spoon of a wine made by time on purpose (his own reason is wine; Morgenthaler's barrel had held Madeira). If Madeira, its history stays out (the sea).
6. Ending direction: busy comes for everyone, and the stopping is the first thing it takes. Have one thing going that can't be hurried, and look in on it. Wording checked against "keep one", "wait", "go back to it".
