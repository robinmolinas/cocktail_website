## Recipe

- **serves:** 1
- **glassware:** a small stemmed wine glass (about 200 ml), chilled, no ice
- **contains:** `[]`

| amount | item | note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 60 ml | white rum, Cuban style (a full-flavoured white rum) | |
| 15 ml | Dolin Blanc vermouth (recommended), or any sweet white (blanc) vermouth | the sweet white style the original was made with |
| 10 ml | rainwater Madeira | a wine that has already had its years |
| 1½ teaspoons (7.5 ml) | orange curaçao | |
| ½ teaspoon (2.5 ml) | grenadine, made from pomegranate | for colour as much as sweetness |
| 1 strip | orange peel | |

## Method
1. Put the glass in the freezer for ten minutes, or fill it with ice and water while you mix.
2. Before anything else, pour a little of the Madeira into a spoon and taste it on its own.
3. Pour the rum, the vermouth, the Madeira, the curaçao and the grenadine into a mixing glass or a jar.
4. Fill it with ice and stir with a long spoon for about 20 seconds, until the outside feels very cold. The melting ice is part of the drink: it adds about two-fifths to what you poured.
5. Empty the serving glass if you filled it, and strain the drink in. No ice.
6. Hold the strip of orange peel over the glass, skin side down, and squeeze it so its oils fall on the drink. Then drop it in.

**closingLine:** *Try a spoon of the Madeira on its own before it goes in. It took its time getting here.*

- Carries the position: the one thing in this glass that someone else gave years to, and the guest giving it a minute of their own before it disappears into the mix. It's attention given before there's any sign it'll pay, which is the one true thing (Wren r1), made into a step at the counter. It doesn't repeat y5's proposal ("take the minute"): the reading asks for the walk, the Ritual asks for the spoon. The second sentence ends on what the spoon holds, as Wren asked, and it turns the tagline's word: the guest is usually the one not hurrying, and here someone else didn't hurry either.
- **Avoided:** "wait" (*Rosetta*); "keep one" (*Four Shares*, *Half a Rim*); "go back to it" (*Off-Label*); "leave it" and "find" (*Overnight*'s closing: "don't fix what you find"); "make it a day ahead" (*For Good*); "thirty seconds" (*Any Day*); "pick out" (*Making the Calls*' epigraph); "years" (the epigraph's); "the part nobody" (*Not Only the Way*); "look up / hold it up first" (*Quite Alive*, *As It Was*); "next to the one you know" (*Beside the First*); any stir/no-stir gesture (*No Accident*); "notice", "strange", "curious" (Wren's list). No registry closing line uses "taste for".
- **Fact basis:** "getting here" means reaching the guest. By law no Madeira may be sold until about two years or more after the harvest, whether it's aged in wood or heated (Portaria 39/2015, Hester). The line claims only the wait, never how the wine was made.
- **Rejected:** *Nobody rushed that one* (Hester r6b: estufagem heats the wine to speed it); *Nobody made that one in a minute* (Hester's, sound on fact, kept for Robin; it repeats the name, *In a Minute*, in the Ritual's last word); *Then taste for it in the drink* (*Beside the First*'s shape, Wren r5); *Those are the years* (Wren r5; it echoes the epigraph's "years in a bottle" and could blur the Madeira's years with Conigliaro's bottle); *That's the part nobody hurried* ("the part nobody" is *Not Only the Way*'s image line, "the part nobody sees").

## Names (≥3; bartender-sayable)
- **In a Minute** (Wren's; my pick too). It's the guest's own answer to "come on", said kindly, and it's easy to order ("an In a Minute, please" gets a smile). It resonates with the person, not the history. It doesn't caption the drink, and it holds whichever closing line we keep. Risk: the opening word "In" is shared with *In Your Own Hand* (the Muse, a different family). That's minor, but Robin sees it.
- **A Few Steps Behind.** Straight out of whoYouAre's scene. Warm, but longer, and "behind" can read as lagging for half a second.
- **Something in It.** From y3 ("he trusted there was something in it"). The trust is the one true thing. As an order it sounds odd ("a Something in It"), which is also why people would ask about it.
- **Take the Minute.** y5's last line as a name: it spends the proposal twice, so I wouldn't.
- *Rejected:* "Five Years" (a figure, and the Manhattan's, not this drink's); "Aged" / "Bottle-Aged" (the method, and *Overnight*'s ground); "Worth Stopping For" (opening clash with *Worth the Trip*); "Still Looking" (opening clash with *Still Yours*); "Come On" (it's the "come on" they're tired of hearing).
