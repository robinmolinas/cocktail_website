Both Hester's flags are taken. The rum is white, as Oxford has it, and Wondrich's pick is "a full-flavored white rum" (*Imbibe!* pdf 217). The Madeira's acid case doesn't rest on Broadbent: it's my estimate and I swept it. My numbers moved the drink twice more since round 3. First, Regan's equal parts goes OUT high too, not only Oxford's. Second, my own 60/15/10/5/5 was in range at the table values but OUT at 1,794 of 4,374 sweep points. So I moved sugar from the grenadine, which carries acid, to the curaçao, which carries none. Grenadine at half a teaspoon is Wondrich's and Regan's dose ("adds a healthy color… without unduly increasing its sweetness", *Imbibe!* pdf 217; *Joy* pdf 283). Swept across rum strength, vermouth, Madeira, curaçao and grenadine, it's OUT only in one corner: the sharpest Madeira and the sharpest grenadine at once. Dolin Blanc is named because Wondrich calls it "the last survivor" of the Chambéry style the drink was first made with.
