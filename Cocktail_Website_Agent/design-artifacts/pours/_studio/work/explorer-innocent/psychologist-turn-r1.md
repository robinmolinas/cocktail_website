**The one true thing:** their patience is a kind of trust. They give an ordinary thing more time than anyone else would, before there's any sign it'll be worth it, because they expect the world to be interesting, and it usually is. Most people need a reason to stop. They need a reason to hurry. (That's my tagline candidate.) **The scene:** on any walk, they're a few steps behind, crouched at something: frost that stays only in a fence's shadow, a bee going into a flower the wrong way round. Up ahead, someone says "come on". They've been hearing "come on" all their life.

**Hide, fear, give.** They hide how much a small thing can make their day, because it can look naive. And a faint guilt at holding people up: the old telling-off for dawdling never quite left. Fear: getting busy and sure, the day they walk straight past something, don't feel the tug, and don't even miss it. Give: the people who walk with them see more than they would alone, and now and then the thing they gave time to turns out to matter to everyone. The reading says that gift out loud, once.
