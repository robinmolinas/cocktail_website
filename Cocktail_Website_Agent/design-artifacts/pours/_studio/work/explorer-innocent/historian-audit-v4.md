Nobody made that one in a minute." (or any line that says only that it took years) | "Nobody rushed" is a claim about how the wine was made, and for most rainwater it's contestable. The law's faster route, **estufagem**, *heats* the wine (up to 50 °C, at least three months; Portaria 39/2015 art. 9) before the years, and heat hastens the changes ageing brings (Oxford, OXIDATION pdf 1458: "Hotter temperatures will hasten those changes"). Barbeito's own rainwater, the bottle whose figures the spec uses, is estufagem (M3). A guest who knows Madeira would read "nobody rushed" as wrong. What holds for every bottle is the wait: about two years or more before anyone may sell it (M2). "Nobody made that one in a minute" says exactly that, keeps the hurry turn, and answers the name. If the room prefers it, "That one took years" holds too, though it sits nearer the epigraph's "years". The wording is Tomás's to set. |
