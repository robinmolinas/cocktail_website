🍸 **Tomás:** Wren, Hester, the drink is on paper, provisional on Wren's ruling: `mixologist-draft.md`, spec `_studio/specs/regular-guy-jester.json`, sweeps `mixologist-sweep.py`.

**The spec.** 60 ml Jeppson's Malört (recommended; the substitute style is a Chicago-style bäsk of 35% or stronger), 60 ml white grapefruit juice frozen hours ahead as two cubes, 1½ tsp maple and a generous pinch of salt. It's shaken until the cubes are slush, with no other ice, and strained into a chilled coupe. No garnish.

**The numbers.** balance.py, `freeform` (no water goes in: the grapefruit is the ice): **16.4% / 10.02 g / 1.127%**. Strength is in Arnold's shaken range. Sugar and acid sit above it, just as his own Shaken Drake does (15.6 / 10.2 / 1.03, *LI* p. 149): a grapefruit sour needs the sugar to match its acid, and with Malört's bitterness I sit at the sweet end. That's an edge, flagged with his precedent. The sweep:
- Malört's sugar is unsourced. At 3 or 6 g/100 ml the maple drops to 1 tsp.
- 20 ml of stray melt pulls it to 14.2%, hence "serve straight away".
- A 30% bäsk reads 14.1%, hence "35% or stronger".
- The same drink on ordinary ice reads 11.4%. That's why the juice has to be the ice.

**Allergens.** allergens.py says veto-free, and the AD-4 floor holds. The new row, `jeppsons_malort`, is at 35% and touches no veto class.

**The story in the glass is physics.** The bottle is in plain sight at full measure, and the guest is willing (Oxford's word). The company makes it good. Grapefruit meets the bitterness with bitterness, and salt and maple round it off (*LI* p. 149; *Codex* pp. 120, 133). Because the juice is the ice, the company never waters the Malört down. And the setup comes first: hours ahead, like any good joke.
