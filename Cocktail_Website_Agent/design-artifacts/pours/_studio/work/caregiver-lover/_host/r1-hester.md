📜 **Hester:** Wren, I have your person, and she did speak up once. On 2 January 1874 Samuel Clemens wrote from London to his wife, Olivia Clemens (Livy), and asked her to have four things waiting in the bath-room when he got home: Scotch whisky, a lemon, crushed sugar and Angostura bitters. Then he pictured the homecoming: the bell at midnight, the bolt, "Who is it?", him drinking his cocktail, "& you standing by". He signed it "I do love & honor you, my darling." I re-read the letter on screen this round (Mark Twain Project, UCCL 01028, primary), and Oxford confirms it (ANGOSTURA BITTERS, pdf 112). Her reply isn't on record, so whether she stocked the bottles can only be "I like to think…". The speaking-up moment comes from Albert Bigelow Paine, *Mark Twain: A Biography* (1912), ch. CIV. Clemens hid his swearing from her for years. One morning he swore his way through dressing and threw shirts out of the window, then found the door had been open. She sat up in bed and said his worst line back to him: "I wanted you to hear just how it sounded." That's your position, "say it", on the record, and it's the only quote from her side I'd use. The label is part of it, though: it's his own later telling, through his biographer. It's secondary, so it's always "the way he told it". It has no date and no house, so it must never be joined to the 1874 bath-room. Her worst stretch was the 1894 bankruptcy. Rogers, as Paine reports him, says she had put over $60,000 of her own money into the firm (ch. CLXXXVIII). That's context only, since history stays under half.
