## For Tomás (method, not in the reading yet)
- **1874 method is contested; claim neither for Twain.** Jerry Thomas (1862) shook his whiskey and gin cocktails and strained them off the ice, and stirred his brandy one. The 1869 *Steward & Barkeeper's Manual*: "A cocktail should never be shaken"; Bevill (1871): shaken well. Wondrich: judging by pictures, 1860s–70s consensus favoured shaking, "or perhaps it was just the more picturesque one" (*Imbibe!* pdf 178). The *New York Sun*, Aug 1873: the New York Club wanted its cocktail "shaken in ice, not stirred" (pdf 193). The letter doesn't say how his was made.
- **Wine-glass: period-true.** In 1862 there was no dedicated cocktail glass, "so a small wineglass had to do"; the coupe came in by 1876 (pdf 178).
- **Lemon peel: period-true as the Thomas style**, "a piece of lemon peel thrown on top" for the Fancy cocktail; "the twist … comes in at the end" (pdf 178). Still not claimable as *his* use of the lemon.
- **Don't import:** Wondrich says Twain liked his *toddy* without sugar (pdf 137). That's the toddy, not the 1874 cocktail. It must not become "he didn't take sugar anyway".
