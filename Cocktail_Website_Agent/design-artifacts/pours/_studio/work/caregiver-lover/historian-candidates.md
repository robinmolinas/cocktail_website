# Hester's mirror hunt: caregiver-lover (The Understanding Spouse), round 1

Persona: always there; the calm; knows when to advise and when just to be there, presence speaking. Fear: selfishness (and, from the Lover side, isolation).

## A (my pick): a person the drink was asked for, Livy Clemens, 1874
- **Story (3 sentences).** In January 1874, Mark Twain, lecturing in London, wrote home to his wife asking her to have four things waiting in the bath-room: Scotch whisky, a lemon, crushed sugar and Angostura bitters. Then he pictured coming home: the bell at midnight, "Who is it?", kisses, him drinking his cocktail "& you standing by". He signed it "I do love & honor you."
- **Why it mirrors.** The one who is there. What he longs for isn't the drink, it's the doorway and the person standing by; the persona's "presence speaks volumes" in three exact words. Lover side: it's an intimate letter, the opposite of isolation.
- **Sources.** Mark Twain Project letter UCCL 01028 (primary, read in full); Oxford ANGOSTURA pdf 112 (primary). Card: `fact-cards/twain-livy-cocktail.md`.
- **Risks.** Her reply is not on record: whether she stocked it is "I like to think" only. Her being teetotal is secondary-only: out unless verified. The digestion claim is his, never ours. Keep the bath-room and undressing tasteful. The mirror is the *spouse*, not Twain: the guest is the one standing by.
- **For Tomás.** A whisky cocktail of 1874 (scotch, sugar, bitters, lemon) sits in the Old-Fashioned family; the letter doesn't say how the lemon was used.

## B (runner-up): an ingredient/drink, the posset
- **Story.** A warm drink of curdled cream or milk with sack, wine or ale, sweetened, often thickened with egg; it set into layers, a foamy "grace" on top, the drink below, sipped through a spout from underneath (Oxford POSSET pdf 1553). In Shakespeare it's mostly a bedtime nightcap: Mistress Quickly promises one "soon at night" by the fire (pdf 1553). Obsolete by 1800 and never successfully revived (pdf 1554).
- **Why it mirrors.** Made for someone at the end of their day; the care is in the waiting.
- **Risks.** No spouse in it on record (the 1554 betrothal set went to Mary I and Philip, a sad marriage; leave out). Wedding-night posset custom: not in the library, web only, unchecked.
