# Mixologist draft: caregiver-lover (The Understanding Spouse), v1.3, round 5

Glassware: small wine glass, chilled, no ice
Contains: veto-free

## Recipe
| amount | item | note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 60 ml | blended scotch whisky | any blended scotch; the letter asks for "Scotch whisky", no brand |
| 7.5 ml | passion fruit syrup | equal weights passion fruit juice (fresh or bottled, unsweetened) and sugar, stirred until clear; takes the place of the crushed sugar |
| 2 dashes | Angostura bitters | |
| 1 | strip of lemon peel | pinched over the glass so its oils land on the drink, then dropped in |

## Method
1. Put a small wine glass in the freezer.
2. Pour the scotch, syrup and bitters into a mixing glass or jar full of ice.
3. Stir for about 30 seconds, until the outside of the glass is very cold.
4. Strain into the cold wine glass. No ice.
5. Hold the lemon peel coloured side down over the glass, pinch it so its oils land on the drink, and drop it in.

## y4 (proposal for Wren)
Three of his four things are in this glass: the Scotch, the Angostura, and the lemon, just the oils pinched from its peel. The fourth, the crushed sugar, I've swapped for a small measure of passion fruit syrup. It gives the drink all the sweetness it needs, and something he never asked for: a bright, sharp note you can't mistake for agreement. Use less and the drink is thin; use more and it's all you taste. At this measure it's what holds the drink together. It's served in a wine glass, the way he took his.

## y4 audit (Tomás, round 4, on reading v2)
- "the only sweet thing in the glass": Angostura carries a trace of sugar (4.2 g/100 ml, LI table: 0.08 g in 2 ml vs ~4.6 g from the syrup). Write "the only sweetener".
- "The scotch, the bitters and a twist of lemon peel are just as he asked": he asked for *a lemon*, not a peel (F1; Hester: peel is period-true, not his). Write "The scotch and the bitters are just as he asked, and the lemon is only its peel."
- "stirred cold and served in a small wine glass, the way he took his": the method is contested (Hester, pdf 178, 193), so "the way he took his" must attach only to the glass. Write "It's stirred cold, and it comes in a small wine glass, because that's how he took his."
- Method stays **stirred** (clear, and all seven stirred lines in range); shaken also lands (22.0% / 4.06 g / 0.12%). No claim about Twain's method anywhere.
- Hester's trap noted: Twain's sugarless *toddy* (Imbibe pdf 137) never becomes "he didn't take sugar anyway".
