## Checked and passing (Wren's four, and the rest)

- **"The acids that make lemons and apples tart": pass.** *LI* p. 59 (citric is "the primary acid in lemon juice", malic "the primary acid of apples"); Oxford pdf 65 agrees.
- **The star: pass, as the book's fact.** The channel is the *Florida Times-Union*'s report of the publisher's launch (10 Dec 1933): he "has starred the drinks that he considers in the best tradition". These are not his words. Nobody in the room has seen the book (library gap). Fine as y2 tells it.
- **"Ran the bar … in the 1890s":** see B2. **"Someone who knew him remembered":** see B3.
- y2: "In December 1933 he told a reporter how his book came about"; three years; "not much to do but look after his two houses"; "gathering the recipes"; "because people asked for them" (his "big call"); "wouldn't want it said he'd recommended them"; "on sale by the time the ban ended": all pass on the *Eagle*, 1 Dec 1933, and A11.
- y3: signposted ("Maybe … I like to think"), and it grants the plain reading first. Pass. F16 stays in the dossier (Wren's ruling, flagged for Robin).
- y4: Herald Punch, the *Herald*, 1901, "the one drink of his that caught on", the 1901 recipe in words, "a corker", "too gentle to hold a sour up" (*LI* p. 60): pass. The taste words are Tomás's, labelled unsourced craft. "It just holds" and "a nod to the work he did before anyone needed it" are our intent. Pass.
- Epigraph's "came out as the ban ended": pass (A11). Tagline, whoYouAre, y5 and the closing line make no fact claims. The closing line's "before anyone's thirsty" matches step 1 (juice made ahead).
- Draft: the intro, recipe notes, spark (Arnold's 32 g / 20 g, the glut of oranges), "about half the way to lime" (3.0 of 6.0%), rum note and "an amber Trinidadian one", Oxford 467 and 1732–1733, *LI* p. 150 ("brightness without extra acidity"), the hot-weather fruit and the peel as Tomás's own: all pass.
