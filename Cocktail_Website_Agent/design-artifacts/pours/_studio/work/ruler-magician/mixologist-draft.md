The drink starts from Patsy Duffy's Herald Punch, his one drink that caught on (Wondrich, *Imbibe!* pdf 92), which he dedicated to the *New York Herald* in 1901. It's rye, the juice of an orange, a teaspoon of sugar and "enough St. Croix to flavor", shaken and strained into a sour glass. Despite the name it's a single glass, a sour made on orange. My one change is to sharpen the orange. On its own, orange juice is "not acidic enough to use in a proper sour" (*Liquid Intelligence* p. 60), so I stir in a little citric and malic acid first, about half the way to lime's sharpness. The drink stays all orange, with no lemon or lime, and it holds up like a real sour.

**Glassware:** stemmed sour glass (about 240 ml), chilled. No ice in the glass.
**Contains:** veto-free.

## Recipe

| amount | item | note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 60 ml | straight rye whiskey | 43% or stronger; 50% is ideal |
| 15 ml (½ oz) | Virgin Islands (St. Croix) rum, lightly aged | a style; Duffy asked only for enough to flavour it, and half an ounce is Wondrich's measure. Any lightly aged rum of about 40%, not spiced |
| 60 ml | sharpened orange juice | fresh orange juice with a little citric and malic acid stirred in (see step 1); in winter, try blood oranges |
| 1½ tsp (6 g) | caster sugar | stirred into the juice first |
| 1 strip | orange peel | squeezed over the top |

## Method

1. Sharpen the orange. Squeeze about 4 oranges for 250 ml of juice, on the day you'll serve it, and strain it: many navel oranges turn bitter if the juice sits. Weigh in 3.4 g of citric acid and 2.1 g of malic acid, and stir until both have dissolved. If you only have citric acid, use 5.5 g. Keep it in the fridge until the first drink.
2. Chill the glass in the freezer, or fill it with ice water while you mix.
3. Put 60 ml of the sharpened juice and the sugar in a shaker, and stir until the sugar has dissolved.
4. Add the rye and the rum, fill the shaker with ice, and shake hard for about 12 seconds, until the outside is frosty.
5. Empty the glass. Strain the drink into it, through a small sieve as well so no chips of ice get in.
6. Hold a strip of orange peel over the drink, skin side down, and squeeze it so its oils fall on the top. Then lay it on the rim.
