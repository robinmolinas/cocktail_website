# The Legend (ruler-magician): persona card

Wren, round 1, 2026-10-01. Built from `persona.py ruler-magician`, my family sketch (`plans/work/ruler/psychologist-sketches.md`), the plan row (`plans/ruler.md`), the authored siblings, and Duffy's pages read whole this round (Oxford pdf 687–688, 1323; *Imbibe!* pdf 91–92, 241, 304; *Joy* pdf 49, 63–64).

## Core truth
**The worst day is the one they were made for.** On ordinary days they can seem like too much: too serious, too long a view, warning about things nobody else is worried about yet. When everything shakes and everyone else is lost for words, they go clear, and they find the sentence people hold on to.

## Shows / hides
- **Shows:** certainty, gravity, a sense of occasion, the long view, a liking for things done properly and said well.
- **Hides:**
  1. The quiet years hurt. Being thought too much, out of step, not needed, stays with them.
  2. Part of them comes alive when things go wrong, and they're a little ashamed of that.
  3. The words that seem to arrive on the day were turned over for years.

## Hidden fear → hidden gift
- **Fear:** chaos winning on their watch, and being remembered as the one who let it. Under that, the Magician's fear of insignificance: that the moment never comes, or comes and finds them wanting.
- **Gift:** when people are frightened, they give them words to stand on: what's happening, what we do next, why it'll hold. They lend their certainty to people who have none left. Said out loud (Robin's rule): people get through the worst days because of how you talk about them.

## "Real me" line to aim for (drafts)
- *On an ordinary day you can seem like too much. On the worst one, you're the person who knows what to say.*
- *You've never been much good at ordinary days. Nobody's better on the other kind.*
- (position-flavoured) *The years nobody asked weren't wasted. That's where the words came from.*

## Truth vs. legend
The true story, with its weight. They care how things are remembered, so a tall tale passed off as fact would insult them. They enjoy a legend granted and then corrected. Grandeur is fine when the page earns it.

## Tone
Ruler tone (confident, decisive, commanding) with the Magician's sense of the moment. The bartender is steady and direct, speaks to them as an equal, takes a firm position, and allows a dry wit. Never reverent, never hero-worship, never a speech.

## Traps

- "You're a legend", or the word "legend" anywhere in the guest text. It's the name tag, and in the studio it also means an unsourced story.
- Assuming the guest is famous, powerful or in charge of anything. Keep it in a life: the family, the team, the friend's worst week.
- Saying they *need* disaster. The shameful half is named gently, once.
- Diagnosis (the "black dog"). Grandiosity as flattery. A plain comeback or revival with no crisis in it.

## Story test
A person (or ingredient) **whose offer wasn't wanted for years** (dismissed, out of favour, even outlawed), as an *event* on the page. A **crisis or a broken moment**, on the page. **Their words**, spoken or written, at that moment, and the page says they helped turn it. A **long view**: the effect lasted. The cost of the wasted years can be signposted. Out: war as the story, drinking as strength, the Churchill legends, fame for its own sake, a plain revival.

## Verdict on the plan's lead: Patrick Gavin Duffy and the *Official Mixer's Manual*
**Accept, reframed, on conditions.**
- **Holds on the page:** he left the trade in 1907, and from 1921 he ran rooming houses in Brooklyn and worked on his book (Oxford pdf 687), right through the dry years. *Imbibe!* calls his world "the world that Prohibition destroyed" (pdf 91). The manual was "one of the two or three books that did the most to bring back the bartender's craft after Repeal" (pdf 92), and it stayed in print for forty years (Oxford). So the years out, the broken moment, the words at the right time and the long view all hold.
- **The reframe:** *Imbibe!* pdf 91 says Duffy "kept his mouth shut and his ears open", and that his memoir is mostly about other people. That cuts the plan's "a bit much / tall tales / dismissed" reading. The out-of-favour limb becomes **what he knew was out of favour: a craft made illegal.** The guest's half of the reading carries "too much". "Dismissed" is an event, so it can't be signposted (Sage lesson): drop it from the story.
- **Conditions:**
  1. **Never "his recipes", "his own words", "from memory" or "what he knew".** *Imbibe!* pdf 241: the manual "plundered" Ensslin's 1916 book "wholesale". *Joy* pdf 63–64: it leaned on Craddock's Savoy book. Say "his book". What's his is the **order**: he sorted the recipes by base spirit (*Joy* pdf 64), which is structure, the person's own driver. Ensslin stays out of the guest text (an injustice is never a mirror) and is owned in the dossier.
  2. **The year is contested:** 1933 (Oxford DUFFY pdf 687 and its bibliography, *Imbibe!* pdf 92) against 1934 (Oxford MIXOGRAPHY pdf 1323, *Joy* pdf 63 and 348). Hester settles it, or the guest text says "as Prohibition ended", with no year.
  3. **One of many:** Oxford pdf 1323 has it among "dozens of celebratory recipe anthologies", "a particularly ambitious example". Never "the book" or "the one that". "One of the books that did the most" is the page.
  4. **Motive is not on the page:** why he spent the years on it is "I like to think…". "Just maybe a speakeasy or two" is Wondrich's joke: leave it out, and never claim he was far from any bar in the dry years. The pen name and the son's name on the Herald letter (pdf 304, "for reasons of his own"): no guessing, leave them out.
  5. **Keep apart from *A Place Kept*:** no age, no "back to work", never open on Prohibition. The turn is the book, not the man's return.
- **Leads for Hester's web visits, not musts:** Oxford's bibliography lists two public words of his own: a letter to the editor, *New York Times*, 25 October 1927 (inside the dry years), and an interview, *Brooklyn Daily Eagle*, 1 December 1933 (days before Repeal: Hester checks the date). If either has him speaking in the wilderness or on the eve of the moment, the words limb is in his own voice instead of resting on a borrowed manual.
- **Backup:** still none. I checked two shapes on the page and neither passes: the Texas rootstock in the vine-louse crisis (Oxford COFFEY STILL pdf 545: no words, and it's the Alchemist's entry) and beet spirit "held in low repute" until the 1870s (BEETS pdf 252: no person, no words).

## Position direction (my lean, to rule in round 2–3)
**The quiet years count.** The Legend's secret belief is that they only matter on the bad day. The position: you don't have to wait for the worst day to be worth hearing, or *the quiet years were never the wait*. Duffy mirrors the second: twelve years on a book nobody could use yet. Guard: keep it off the Firefighter's hidden hours. The Legend's quiet years are about being unwanted, not about practising.
