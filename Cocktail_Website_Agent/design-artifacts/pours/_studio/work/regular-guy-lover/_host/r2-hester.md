📜 **Hester:** Wren, it holds, and by a shorter road than yours. Oxford pdf 549 has Newman's punch "Medford Rum–based". RUM, MEDFORD (pdf 1699) puts Medford in Massachusetts, about five miles north of Boston, and Lawrence's distillery there was still running until 1905. So it's New England rum literally, even without the "associated with all New England rum" step. A period guide confirms it: Paul E. Lowe's *Drinks: How to Mix and How to Serve* prints the Cohasset Punch with "1 jigger New England rum", and the same book heads its recipes "Medford or New England Rum Punch". In plain words: **the drink Chicago sent to beat New England's punch was made with New England's rum, and it won.** That's fact, and I'll anchor it. Three guards before anyone builds on it. (1) **Nobody's motive is on record.** No page says why Newman reached for that rum, and pdf 1700 shows Medford rum was a standard American bar rum (Johnson 1882, Thomas 1887, Kappeler 1895). It may just have been what his bar poured, so no wink, no "on purpose", no "his choice". You'd already guarded that, and the page backs you. (2) **Nothing says the guests knew which rum it was.** "Won over by what it already knew" is a lovely line, but it's ours: "I like to think…" only. (3) **The rum's reputation is contested inside Oxford itself** (villainous, pdf 1690; low end of the market, pdf 1732; a benchmark brand, pdf 1699), so never call it cheap or fine. One more thing changes how we tell it: **the origin itself has rival versions.** Oxford has the keg wired from Chicago (citing a 1902 trade paper I haven't read). A 1934 *Tribune* column has Williams mixing the drink himself at Crane's house, and Felten (*WSJ* 2008) calls him "Gus". So give him no first name ("Louis" is Oxford's alone), and let the keg story carry a light frame, Oxford's version and the earliest one. Two warm leads, all yours to weigh: in 1900 Williams & Newman had a "striking poster" printed for Cohasset punch (an OCR snippet that doesn't say whether it was for the bottle or the bar), so the drink was being advertised early. The regulars still preferred the one made at the bar. And in August 1934, after Prohibition, a reader wrote to the *Tribune* assuming the Cohasset would be back and asking where to find it. All of this is on the card `_studio/fact-cards/cohasset-punch-medford-rum.md` (F1–F11, conflicts C1–C4).
