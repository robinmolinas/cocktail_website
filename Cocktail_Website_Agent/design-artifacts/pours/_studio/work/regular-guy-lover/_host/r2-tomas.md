🍸 **Tomás:** Wren, I accept the Cohasset Punch, with your mirror. It's in the glass whatever Hester finds: a stirred drink the regulars picked over its own bottle. Three things from the drink side, so the story doesn't promise what the glass can't do.

**1. My numbers caught me: it isn't a Martini.** In the plan I filed it under Martini. I ran Oxford's printed spec (pdf 550: 60 ml amber rum, 60 sweet vermouth, 5 rich syrup, a dash of orange bitters, half a lemon muddled; probes in `work/regular-guy-lover/mixologist-probe-*.json`) and modelled the lemon as 10, 15 and 20 ml of juice. As `stirred` it's OUT on all seven: at 15 ml it finishes at 17.7% ABV, 7.37 g sugar per 100 ml and 0.65% acid, against the stirred range's 21-29%, 3.7-5.6 g and 0.10-0.14%. That's sour-level acid in a stirred drink. As `shaken` the strength and sugar land, but the acid is too low (0.58% against 0.76-0.94). So it sits between the two families. The plain call is a vermouth-sweetened sour that is stirred on purpose. Arnold does the same thing himself: his Schokozitrone (LI p. 209) is stirred with 15 ml of lemon and finishes at 19.2% / 7.4 g / 0.70%, almost the same numbers. So the bar's "stirred, and at length" is a real choice with a book behind it, not a mistake I need to fix. Stirring keeps it rounder and less frothy than the shaken version. Two cautions for the spec: Arnold stirs his briefly, so "at length" means more water, and I'll sweep that. And 60/60 makes about 190 ml after dilution, which is why Oxford pours it into a *large* coupe. I'll prove a 45/45 version for ordinary glasses.

**2. The rum.** I read RUM, MEDFORD (pdf 1699) for the style. An 1881 source calls it aged until "high flavored, ripe and mellow", a molasses rum. So "amber rum" stays a style with no named bottle, and the substitute words will say aged molasses rum. Whether Newman's keg proves your mirror is Hester's call. I'm only taking the rum's flavour from the page.

**3. The spark, for you to rule on before I write a spec.** The Cohasset is already the *Flavor Matrix*'s peach in a glass: stone fruit's Best Pairings are "citrus… fruit brandy… wine" (pdf 236), which here are the lemon, the brandied peach and the vermouth. So the touch has to come from the Surprise Pairings: "beer, sage, soy sauce". Sage is *A Brother's Care*'s leaf and beer is the Prankster's, so my lead is **a few drops of soy sauce: the brown bottle everyone has in the kitchen**. A drop of salt in a stirred lemon drink is Arnold's own move (two drops of saline in the Schokozitrone). Soy sauce does the same job and adds a savoury depth (that part is my own craft call, unsourced). It's the most ordinary bottle in the house, the one nobody thinks of for a drink, and it's what makes this one this person's. Two guards I'm stating now. First, it is **never** the regular's brown bottle: that dash stays one man's memory, signposted, and ours is ours. Second, soy sauce is usually brewed with wheat, so I'll declare `gluten` on the safe side and the pour loses veto-free (tamari, "check the label", only as a note). If you think soy sauce makes them "secretly sophisticated", or the gluten flag is too high a price, my fallback is veto-free: lemongrass (also a Best Pairing on pdf 236) steeped into the rich syrup. Either way the peach stays fruit in the glass, a slice soaked in brandy, so it never reads as *Left Standing*'s spoon of crème de pêche in a tequila Martini.
