# Persona card: The One Next Door (regular-guy-lover)
Wren, round 1, 2026-10-01

## Source (persona.py)
Essence: simple, down to earth, polite; "the kind of person your family would love you to bring home for tea". Story: simplicity, approachability, warmth; kindness and normalcy; familiarity and safety. Goal Belonging, fear Exclusion. Primary fears: Exclusion, Discourtesy, **Individuality**. Secondary (never shown): Intimacy, Connection, Passion; fears Isolation, Loneliness, Coldness. Example on the sheet: a sitcom's steady, likeable one who waits years to be picked (romance on the sheet; never needed in ours).

## The one true thing
People relax with them at once, and so they get taken as easy. Under the ease is a want as strong as anyone's: to be somebody's first choice, not their comfortable one. They keep it quiet because wanting something out loud is standing out, and standing out is the thing they fear most. So they stay gracious about coming second.

## Hides / fears / gives
- **Hides:** the wanting. Not hidden depths, not secret sophistication (that would betray "simple"): a plain, warm want, kept low.
- **Fears:** being liked by everyone and picked by no one. Being the default, the fallback, the "you around?" text when other plans fell through (and nearly always being around).
- **Gives:** home. People stop performing with them. They're who you bring someone new to meet, because everyone's at ease there. Robin's rule: say it plainly, once, and say it's worth something.

## Siblings and neighbours (keep apart)
- **Bumpkin (*Good as It Is*):** "let in as the sweet one, never as one of them". Theirs is condescension about taste; ours is affection taken for granted. No "sweet one", no being underestimated.
- **Hidden Talent (*Right Here*):** fears getting above themselves over a *skill*; owns "You thought you had to choose". Ours is about the heart, not a gift; tagline can't hinge on "choose" in that shape.
- **Entrepreneur (*Loose on Top*):** ends in the corner keeping the place running for others to belong to. Ours never hosts or runs anything.
- **Prankster (*Got You*):** "if nobody gets you back, are you really in?" Ours isn't about being in: they're in everywhere. It's about being *picked*.
- **True Friend (*Four Shares*):** fears being left out, so makes sure nobody is. Ours is never left out; that's the point.
- **Rough Diamond (*Any Day*, the reverse twin):** can't say the feeling. **Geisha (*Hoping You'd Come*):** owns "ask" and the waiting room. **Nurse:** the same you for everyone, the closest get no more (keep off "only the close get the real you"). **Entertainer:** owns "that's what you came for" (so my sketch line "the one they came for" is retired).
- **Campaigner (*Not Too Polite*):** owns "polite", "the argument", "say it". Ours must never use them, even though "polite" is on the sheet and "counterargument" is on the Oxford page.
- **Tourist (*Brought Home*):** owns "Home" in a name.

## Traps
- Never requires a partner or romance (first choice of a friend, a team, a family, a boss all count). No seduction, no sensuality.
- The sheet's "Boy/Girl Next Door" is gendered: never echoed.
- Not a pity reading: "nobody's first choice" said flat is an insult. Gentle, and the guest leaves valued.
- No "nice" as a compliment; if "nice" appears, it's the word that stings.
- Not secretly complex. Simple is true of them and stays a good thing.

## Story test (refined from the plan)
The story's person, drink or ingredient is the **familiar, everyday, home one** (a fixture, a local, the regular), and a source shows it **picked on purpose** by the people who knew it best, over something it could have lost to. Warmth, not drama. "Taken for granted" is welcome only if a page says so; it isn't required.

## Verdict on the plan's lead (Cohasset Punch, Oxford pdf 549-550, read whole)
**Accept, with the mirror moved.** Every limb is on the page: a Chicago fixture from the 1890s to 1986; the sign "Home of Cohasset Punch"; regulars who agreed neither the printed recipe nor the bottle "did the drink true justice", and kept coming for the one made at the bar; and the birth: a punch sent to be picked, which "rapidly won over the crowd". The plan's half-fail (no showier rival) dissolves once the test is "picked", not "loved for being familiar".
- **The position is in the story:** Williams "was not satisfied with the verdict" (his host's party had given punch to New England). He didn't accept second place graciously; he sent something good. Position direction: don't be gracious about being someone's safe choice; let them see you want to be picked. Words to avoid: polite, argument, say it, ask.
- **A delight for Hester to verify, not for me to claim:** Newman's punch was "Medford Rum-based" (pdf 549), and Medford Rum was the name "associated with all New England rum" (Oxford, RUM, MEDFORD, pdf 1699). The case for Chicago was made with New England's own rum. If it holds, it's the reading's best mirror: they were won over by what they already knew. Not "his choice" unless a page says so.
- **Guards:** the demolition never; "after Repeal" a date only; Crane one clause or none; the brown-bottle dash is one regular's memory, signposted; "taken for granted" not claimed of the drink; no "Home" in the name (*Brought Home*); the image brief may use the lighthouse sign (soft yellow).

## Lines that might land (drafts)

- "You're who people text when their plans fall through. You nearly always say yes."
