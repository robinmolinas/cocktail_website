| lineage | The Cohasset Punch (rum, sweet vermouth, lemon, a piece of preserved peach) was a Chicago fixture from the 1890s until 1986 (Oxford Companion, COHASSET PUNCH, pdf 549, David Wondrich). | The neighbourhood's own drink: there for ninety years, never needing to explain itself. | Belonging |
| origin | As Oxford tells it, Williams, who kept a Chicago saloon, was a guest at a summer house in Cohasset, Massachusetts, when the party decided New England made the best punches. He "was not satisfied with the verdict" and wired his partner, Tom Newman, to make a new punch and ship a keg of it, express. It "rapidly won over the crowd" (Oxford pdf 549, citing a 1902 trade paper; later tellings differ on who mixed it, so the story is framed as Oxford's). | Not content to be the comfortable second thought: they sent something good, and it was picked. | fear of Exclusion; Connection |
| ingredient | Newman's punch was "Medford Rum–based" (Oxford pdf 549). Medford is a Massachusetts town about five miles north of Boston, and by then its name stood for New England rum in general (Oxford, RUM, MEDFORD, pdf 1699). An early bar guide prints the Cohasset Punch with "New England rum" (Paul E. Lowe, *Drinks: How to Mix and How to Serve*). | The case against New England's punch was made with New England's own rum, and it won. Why he used it, and whether anyone noticed, is not on record: only "I like to think…". | Belonging |
| gesture | The punch was bottled from 1899 and printed in 1901, but the saloon's regulars agreed neither version "did the drink true justice". At the bar it came with a brandy-soaked piece of peach and was stirred "at length" (Oxford pdf 549). | The people who knew it best chose it made by hand, by someone who knew them, over its packaged self. | Intimacy; Connection |
| riff | Our version follows Oxford's printed spec (pdf 550: muddled lemon, amber rum, sweet vermouth, rich syrup, orange bitters, stirred, coupe, brandied or canned peach) at three-quarters size. The bar stirred it "at length" and served a peach soaked in brandy "and some liqueur" until soft (pdf 549). The full minute's stir and the spoonful of syrup in the peach jar are ours. | The familiar thing, given care ahead of time, made the way the people who knew it best liked it. | Intimacy; Belonging |
| place | In 1916 the drink passed to the Ladner brothers. After Repeal their bar put up a neon lighthouse sign: "Home of Cohasset Punch". The sign and the punch stayed a Chicago fixture until 1986 (Oxford pdf 549–550). | A drink a place was proud to be the home of. | Belonging |

## Legends (never anchors)
- **The brown-bottle dash:** one regular's memory, "a dash of something from a brown bottle" not on the list (Oxford pdf 549). Tell it only as his memory. Any dash we add is ours, never the secret, and is never described as "a brown bottle".
- **Who first mixed it, and Williams's first name:** Oxford (Louis; Newman's keg) vs the 1934 *Tribune* (Williams mixed it at Crane's) vs Felten 2008 ("Gus") vs Cohasset sources ("Marshall"). Use "Williams", and frame the keg as Oxford's account.
