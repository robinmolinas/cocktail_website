# Persona card: The Dude (sage-regular-guy), Wren, round 1

Source: persona.py sage-regular-guy (essence "True wisdom is keeping things real"; fear Deception; secondary fears Exclusion, Discourtesy; drivers Seek paradise / Build connection). My family sketch: plans/work/sage/psychologist-sketches.md. Oxford SAUZA pdf 1742–1744 re-read whole this session.

## The one true thing
They've worked out how little really matters, and they know exactly which little it is. They'll let almost anything go: the argument, the credit, being right, even a name. The one thing they won't let go is small and real, and usually nobody else at the table would have picked it (the rug, not the money).

## Hides
- The ease was learned, not lazy. They got dragged into enough of other people's drama, once, to know it's rarely worth it. Everything they know was learned by living, not read.
- That they care a lot, about a few things. They won't spend care on the fake ones, so people take the ease for not caring.

## Fears
- Being pulled into someone else's lie or scheme and made to carry part of it (the Sage fear, at kitchen-table scale: not a big deception, a small one they're asked to go along with).
- Underneath: being taken for someone who doesn't care.

## Gives (to say out loud once, Robin's rule)
- A lower temperature. Around them nobody has to win, perform or pick a side, and people come to them when they want the room to go quiet. That's worth something, and they rarely hear it.
- Watch: *First Choice* owns "nobody has to earn their place"; *Before the Room* owns "breathe easier" and "calm"; *Go On* owns "fights you've stopped"; *In One Piece* owns "never need to raise your voice". The Dude's gift must come from knowing what's worth it, not from calm as such.

## Siblings (how the Dude differs)
- Wise Fool: steps into the drama with a joke to puncture it. The Dude steps out of it.
- Lone Voice: says it against the official version and pays. The Dude doesn't fight the big story at all; just quietly doesn't join it.
- Philosopher: goes one layer deeper. The Dude goes simpler.
- Connoisseur (*A Brother's Care*): has words and ceremony for the truth. The Dude has neither.
- Architect (*What It Rests On*): the hidden structure. Keep "foundation / what it rests on / what holds it up" out.
- Mentor (*Further Than Me*): being outgrown. No teaching here.
- Grandmaster: the long game. The Dude isn't playing a game.
- Across families: Adolescent (*Kept*) smells a fake and owns "the real thing" in its tagline; Bumpkin (*Good as It Is*) delights in simple things out of innocence (the Dude's simplicity is earned by experience); Mechanic (*Good for Years*) keeps old things working; Craftsman (*Not Only the Way*) owns "doing it properly"; Minimalist (*Half a Rim*) takes away until it's true. Campaigner (*Not Too Polite*) and the Judge (*Either Way*) own "fuss" as a verb.

## Traps
- Slacker, stoner, laziness as the joke. "Chill", "laid-back", "easy-going" as a compliment.
- Gender: the name is slang. Never "he", "guy", "man", "dude" about the guest.
- The film: at most one touchstone, never a catchphrase ("abides", "just your opinion"), never the opener. The White Russian stays out (dairy, fan service).
- Sneering at people who do care, or at anyone's industrial tequila or its drinkers.
- Making the calm a superiority ("you see through everyone").
- Turning it into a fight story (that's the Lone Voice and the Outlaws).

## Story test (from the plan, kept)
(1) someone or something surrounded by fuss, fashion or pretence that (2) kept to the plain, real thing and (3) turned out right or lasting because of it; it must show (4) what they refused, not only what they kept. The plain thing must be honestly good, not settled for.

## Verdict on the plan's lead (Fortaleza): accept, with conditions
Why it passes, on the page (Oxford SAUZA pdf 1742–1744): the family name went from Francisco Javier through Pedro Domecq, Allied Domecq, Fortune Brands, Beam and Suntory (1). The great-great-grandson of the founder set up on land that had stayed in the family and made estate-grown agave tequila with a tahona, the old stone mill (2). He lost the name twice: barred from "Sauza", then forced by a trademark clash with a rum to drop Los Abuelos (named for his forebears). The tequila stayed. That is the one true thing: everyone else's fuss is about the name; the real thing is what's in the bottle and where it comes from. The same entry says the family brand's basic line is mixto (51% agave) and its agave goes through a diffuser (industrial shredders, steam, chemicals): that's the "what he didn't make" (4), as Oxford's own juxtaposition.

Conditions:
1. The turn is the name lost twice and the tequila not changed. Tell it in Oxford's verbs: "barred", "forced". Never "gave up" or "walked away from" the name. His feelings about it only in a signposted line.
2. (4) is a choice ("set out to make ... using a tahona"), set beside the family brand's diffuser and mixto. Say what each is in neutral plain words. No sneer at Sauza, its owners or its drinkers; no corporate villains (that turns it into a fight story).
3. (3) "lasting" needs a source (Hester, secondary is fine: still made that way, still on the land), or a signposted line that claims no quality fact.
4. Leave him unnamed unless a source names him. Out of guest text: his father "never had the opportunity" (another person's wound), Cenobio buying a distillery "at sixteen" (Oxford's own dates don't fit), the US childhood beyond one clause at most.
5. Not method pride (the tahona is old and slow, not "doing it properly"), not "foundation" words, and 100% agave owned in one clause or avoided (*Half a Rim*).
6. One clause at most on Los Abuelos (named for the grandparents' line). It's tender, but it tips towards heritage-for-its-own-sake.

Why not the backup (Gray): his Republican hotel as a Democrat lands on *One Table*'s ground ("You keep ending up on both"), and his vests, rectitude and gentlemanly demeanour are polish, the opposite of this guest's imagery. Fine as a backup only.

## Direction for the reading (round 3)
- Scene: the guest at a table where everyone's arguing about something that sounds important; they've let it all go, except one small thing nobody noticed was the point.
- Recognition line to earn: something like "You'll let almost anything go. Just not that one thing." (draft, not a tagline yet)
- Position (Ryu): the ease gets mistaken for not caring, so let people see which one thing matters, before they decide you don't care at all. Keep "fuss" and bare "ask" off the verb (Campaigner, Judge, Geisha).
- For Tomás: the plan's highball is right for the temperature. The tequila should be the one thing tasted; everything else easy. The spark must not add a second "real thing" that competes with the bottle.
