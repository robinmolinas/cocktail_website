**The one true thing.** The Alternative Comic doesn't play with the joke. They play with what the room expects to happen next. Their fun is the second where nobody knows whether they're allowed to laugh, and they stretch it on purpose, so that whatever people feel after it is really theirs, not a reflex. Anyone can get the laugh people walked in with. They want the one nobody rehearsed.

**Hide, fear, give.** They hide how much they care whether it lands. They watch every face, and they'd never explain a bit, because explaining feels like breaking it. So when it doesn't land they keep a straight face and take it home alone. The fear on the surface is boredom, and a precise kind: everyone reacting on cue, being asked to "do the one you do". A known act is a cage. Under that is the real fear: that the strangeness reads as cold, as if people were the target or got left outside it. What they give, and we say it out loud: a real reaction instead of a polite one, and one second of not having to know how to feel.

**Siblings, and this family is crowded.** *Off-Label* already owns "being asked to repeat yourself feels like a door closing", so "can't tell the same joke twice" can't be the core. *Cold Water First* owns dreading "the silence where that laugh should be". This one goes looking for that silence. *Is It Just Me* owns the half-second before recognition, and ours lands on surprise. *The Other Berry* owns "weird" and the strange that's already there. *No Accident* owns "rather be misunderstood than boring", and that's the xlsx's "rather puzzle a crowd than please it" line almost word for word, so we never paraphrase it. *For Kicks* keeps a bit going for life. This one drops it once it's expected. Other traps: contempt for the audience, discomfort as the goal, any hoax, Kaufman (his hoaxes belong to the Trickster), fine dining or price, "molecular", "awkward" or "weird" pinned on the guest.
