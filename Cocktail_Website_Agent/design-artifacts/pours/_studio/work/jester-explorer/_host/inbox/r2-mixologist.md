🍸 **Tomás:** Wren, the page hands me the drink, and the home method is in its own words: "an Old-Fashioned suspended within a partially frozen orb of ice" (*Proper* p. 235). *Partially frozen* is something any freezer does. Put a plastic cup of water in the freezer and it freezes from the outside in. After about three or four hours (my craft call, unsourced: freezers vary, and I'll write how to check), break the soft top with a spoon handle and pour out the water that's still liquid. You've got an ordinary cloudy ice shell. Pour the cold Old-Fashioned into it and stand it in a rocks glass. At the table, one tap with the back of a spoon and the drink runs out over its own broken ice: in the rock, then on the rocks. That's a freezer, a cup and a spoon. No sling, no mould, no clear ice, nothing cut: it's cloudy ice, broken, the opposite of *Half a Rim*. The strange part is all prep, so the serve is one tap, not a show.

Inside is the classic rye Old-Fashioned people would want anyway: rye, demerara syrup, Angostura, and the orange peel squeezed over after the crack. No spark in the bottles. The *Flavor Matrix* is consulted and set aside, and I'll say so in Checks. The batch gets its water measured in a jug and goes in the freezer beside the shell. A drink that strong won't freeze at home (*LI* pdf 145), so the shell barely melts into it, and the dilution is a number I can write down rather than a guess.

Two I've killed. An ice lid laid over the glass is simpler, but a cover that comes off to reveal the drink is *Cold Water First*'s saucer, and nothing would flow out of the rock. Freezing the sugar and bitters into the ice gives a drink that changes as it melts, and that's *No Accident*'s cube. The closing line, when we get there, carries Joly's "a bartender joke and a chat": stay with them after the crack. No spec or numbers until you rule.
