# Tomás, staging (round 2): no spec, no balance until Wren rules the story

## The drink idea
- **From the page:** "an Old-Fashioned suspended within a partially frozen orb of ice sitting in a rocks glass… crack!—the cocktail flowed out into the glass, and what was once in the rock was now on the rocks" (*Proper* p. 235, pdf 249). The page names no spirit.
- **Home method (to prove):** a plastic cup of water in the freezer until a shell forms (about 3-4 hours, my craft call, unsourced; freezers vary). Break the soft top with a spoon handle and pour out the water that's still liquid: an ordinary cloudy ice shell. Pour the cold Old-Fashioned in, stand the shell in a rocks glass. At the table, the back of a spoon cracks it and the drink runs out over its own broken ice.
- **Kit:** freezer, plastic cup, spoon, jug, jigger, peeler. No sling, no mould, no carving, no clear ice.
- **Inside:** the classic rye Old-Fashioned: rye, demerara syrup, Angostura, orange peel squeezed over after the crack. Pre-diluted with measured water in a jug and chilled in the freezer beside the shell (a drink that strong won't freeze at home: LI pdf 145, blended drinks need the ABV down to freeze), so the shell barely melts into it and the dilution is a written number.
- **Spark:** *Flavor Matrix* consulted and set aside (Wren: strangeness in the serve, never the ingredients; Robin 2026-09-30, no spark is fine if the drink is interesting and tailored). Record in Checks.

## Rejected
- **Ice lid over the glass** (a thin disc frozen on a plate, cracked into the drink): simpler, but a cover over the glass that comes off is *Cold Water First*'s reveal ("the saucer coming off is the one reveal that's just for you"), and nothing flows out of the rock.
- **Water balloon orb:** truer to the shape, but a project: tie, freeze, cut, drain, refill.
- **Sugar-and-bitters frozen into the ice:** a drink that changes as the sugar melts is *No Accident*'s cube.

## To prove when the story is ruled
- Spec v1 to run (`old-fashioned` style, pre-diluted, no ice melt modelled at first): rye 60 ml (sweep 40-50%), 1:1 demerara syrup 7.5-10 ml, Angostura 2 dashes (count by hand, balance.py doesn't), water 20-25 ml.
- Worst case: shell melt after the crack (shards sit in the drink): run a +10 ml and +20 ml water case.
- Freezer: at ~30% ABV the batch shouldn't freeze at -18 °C (check LI p. 141/pdf 145 numbers; unsourced exact freezing point).
- Registry/plan near misses: Hero's Lovable Rogue (rye, rocks, one large cube) and Magician's Good Wizard (bourbon, cubed ice): apart by the broken shell. Half a Rim: its ice is clear and cut; ours is cloudy and broken. Own it in one clause.
- Allergens: rye, demerara, Angostura, orange: expected veto-free (run allergens.py).
- Closing line: the position is Joly's "a bartender joke and a chat": stay after the crack. Never "tell the story" (*Anyway*), never "lift" (*Cold Water First*).

## Round 3 updates (after Hester r2 and the host's note)
- **Who cracks it:** in 2011 the server cracked the ice (Hester, Sula, *Chicago Reader* 26 May 2011). So at home, **whoever made it cracks it at the table, in front of the person it's for**, and stays. Our move, never "as the Aviary did". This puts the cracking hand and Joly's chat in the same person.
- **Rye is ours:** no page names the spirit. Reason given as mine: whiskey is the Old-Fashioned's core in the Codex (p. 7, American whiskey); rye for its "distinct spiciness" (Codex p. 7; flavour, not heat: not the `spice` veto).
- **Why it stays liquid (sourced):** LI pdf 145: a drink has to come down below 15.5% (aim 14%) for a home freezer to freeze it. A pre-diluted Old-Fashioned sits around 30%, so the ice can hold it like a cup. Check the batch numbers against that.
- **Interest from the books, not from an odd bottle:** Codex p. 6, "Ask ten bartenders and you'll get ten different recipes… 'the one true way'"; the Old-Fashioned is "an exercise in restraint… precision is the name of the game". The expected drink, made exactly, in a form nobody expects. Name the drink inside a renowned classic in the reading.
