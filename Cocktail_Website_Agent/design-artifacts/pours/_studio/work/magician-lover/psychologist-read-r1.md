# The Siren (magician-lover): Wren's read, round 1

Sources: `persona.py magician-lover` (xlsx row); the family plan row (`_studio/plans/magician.md`); Oxford DUKES BAR (pdf 689) read whole; sibling readings read in full: *Worth Finding* (Charmer), *Up Close* (Magnetic Leader), *More Than One Head* (Apprentice), *Fair Measure* (caregiver-ruler); phrase greps across every pour (results below).

## The person

The sheet: "changes the air in a room"; people are drawn in "without quite knowing why"; the allure "is real and they know it, which is exactly why they handle it with care"; attraction "can transform someone or wreck them"; fear: "the spell that works too well, and the harm it does". Goal power/transformation; drivers leave a mark / build connection.

**The one true thing no sibling shares:** they feel responsible for an effect they never chose to use. Someone quits the job after one long dinner with them, leaves the relationship, books the flight, says the thing they'd been holding back for years. The Siren didn't ask for any of it, didn't push, maybe barely spoke. And afterwards they're the one lying awake asking: was that them, or was it me? The guilt is for things they never did.

**What they hide:** how much of themselves they keep back on purpose. They learned early that full strength lands hard on people, so they come in quieter than they are: they stand nearer the door, make the joke at their own expense, leave first, keep the intense question for later. People think that's simply their manner. It's a decision, made again every evening.

**What they fear:** not rejection. Being too much *for someone*: that a person near them loses their footing, follows the pull somewhere that isn't theirs, changes in a way the Siren never meant. (The sheet's "unintended results", in a person-to-person form, never in scale.)

**What they give (to be said out loud once, per Robin's caregiver edits):** people come away more themselves. Around this person, others find the nerve for the thing they were already half-ready to do. And because the Siren holds the pull so carefully, it's safe to be moved by them: nobody near them gets used.

**The position (Ryu's, earned last):** stop keeping yourself at half. The care was never in being less; it's in being plain about what you are. Show the whole of it, say honestly what it is, and trust people to choose how close they come. Their choices are theirs: you don't have to carry them. (Kind: a hard truth, then relief.)

## Traps

- **The guest is never the drink's strength.** No strong, potent, intoxicating, heady, dangerous, "too strong" about the person. The drink can be strong in y4 as fact; the likeness lives in honesty and wholeness, never in alcohol.
- **No drunkenness, ever.** The rule is never told as what happens after a third. "Could be ruinous" stays in the plan.
- **No siren-myth kit:** no sailors, songs, rocks, shipwrecks, mermaids, "irresistible", "spell". The personality name already carries it.
- **No seduction or romance plot**, no body, no gendered reading. The pull is presence, not sex appeal.
- **No flattery dressed as recognition:** "people are drawn to you" alone is a compliment and a horoscope. It only earns its place with the scene (the dinner, the decision, the lying awake).
- **Never a diagnosis** ("you feel guilty", "imposter"). Say what they do.
- **The rule's maker and motive aren't on the page.** Oxford: the strength "necessitated" the rule. Any reading of it as care, or as a choice not to weaken the drink, is signposted ("I like to think…").
- **"Two" is never the point** (*Fair Measure*'s two portions). The rule is named once, as Oxford gives it.

## My story test (for this person)

1. Something whose effect on people was real, and known to the ones who kept it.
2. They didn't weaken it. The care went somewhere else: into saying plainly what it is (a rule, a warning, a measure told out loud).
3. The care is as well known as the thing itself.
4. It did more good than harm, and people sought it out.
Out: absinthe and the louche, addiction and health, seduction plots, dilution as the care (*Fair Measure*), limits as "why the fun lasts".

## Verdict on the plan's lead: the Dukes Martini

**Accept, with the position changed.** I read DUKES BAR (pdf 689) whole. It passes all four limbs, better than the plan said:
1. "A Martini of unusual strength and concentration": the effect, known (1).
2. Gin poured frozen straight from the bottle, "without any shaking or stirring": nothing softens it. The care is the house rule, said out loud (2). That they *chose* not to weaken it is our reading, signposted.
3. "The bar's famous house rule": the care is famous in its own right (3).
4. Amis, 1988: "one of the best spots of its kind in London, if not the best of all"; stars, politicians and royals "sought refuge" in its quiet, wood-panelled room (4). The strongest Martini in town, in the place people go to feel safe: the Siren in one room. (Scope "sought refuge" to Oxford's words; never say they felt safe.)

**What changes from the plan:** the position is no longer "aim it on purpose" (that's *Up Close*'s). The story now argues the Siren's position on its own terms: the house never made the drink weaker to make it safer; it kept it whole and told you the truth about it. So: stop keeping yourself at half; be plain about what you are; trust people with their own choice.

**The fact that mirrors the hidden fear:** the bar made something whose effect worried even its makers, and its answer wasn't to dilute it. That's the exact thing this person has never let themselves do.
