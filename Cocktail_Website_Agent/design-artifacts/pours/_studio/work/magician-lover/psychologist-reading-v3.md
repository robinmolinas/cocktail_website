# The Siren (magician-lover): Wren's reading, v3 (round 4, step 3: Hester's audit v2 R1–R5 landed)

## Title block
- **Name (my pick, held to the vote):** Not at Half
- **Tagline:** You never set out to change anyone. People change around you all the same.
- **Tagline candidates:** (2) You come in quieter than you are. People still remember you. (3) You know what you do to people. That's why you're careful.
- **Epigraph:** Poured the way one London bar pours its Martini. That bar has a famous house rule.
- **"This is me" line:** Everyone else heard a lovely story. You lay awake wondering whether it was them or you.

## whoYouAre
You hardly said anything at that dinner. You asked one question and really listened to the answer. A few weeks later they'd left the job, or booked the flight, or finally said the thing they'd been holding back for years, and they told you it started that night. Everyone else heard a lovely story. You lay awake wondering whether it was them or you.

You know what you can do to people without even trying, so you come in quieter than you are. You stand nearer the door, make the joke at your own expense, leave a little early, save the big question for another time. Everyone thinks it's just your manner. It's a decision you make again every evening, because underneath it is one fear: someone losing their footing because of you. And here's what you seldom hear back: people come away from you more like themselves, with the nerve for something they were already half-ready to do. You give people that, and you never take anything for it.

## yours
1. In a small hotel in London there's an intimate, wood-panelled bar that's known around the world for its Martini. It's made at your table, from a trolley: frozen gin, or vodka, poured straight from the bottle, plus a little vermouth from a dasher bottle and lemon peel, twisted. Nobody shakes it and nobody stirs it.

2. With no ice melting into it, it reaches you almost as strong as it left the bottle: a Martini of unusual strength. So the bar has a famous house rule: no more than two per guest. Film stars, politicians and royals have gone there for refuge, and in 1988 the novelist Kingsley Amis called it one of the best bars of its kind in London, perhaps the best of all.

3. I like to think that rule is the bar's kindness: rather than make the drink weaker to make it safer, it left it whole and was honest about what it is. You've done the opposite for years. You soften yourself before anyone asks, so nobody ever has to be careful around you.

4. The cocktail I've made for you is a Martini, a renowned classic, poured the way that bar pours its own: straight from a freezer-cold bottle into the glass, never stirred, never shaken. That bar makes it with gin or vodka, and I've chosen vodka, with just a teaspoon of French-style dry vermouth, so nothing stands in front of it. It's ice-cold and perfectly clear. And instead of a twist, I grate the zest of a quarter of a lemon onto it and leave it floating, all of it out in the open, as a nod to that famous rule.

5. So stop coming in at half. Being careful never meant shrinking. It means being honest about who you are. Ask the big question at the dinner, and stay until the end. Then trust people with what they do next. That part belongs to them. Without that weight, I'd bet you enjoy the evening as much as everyone else does.

## Resonance
**As the guest (a Siren).** The tagline opens on the effect I have, in the positive frame, then names the thing I never chose: I want to read on. The epigraph, cold, is a Martini from a London bar and a rule I don't yet know: a tease, with no dependence on the reading. whoYouAre ¶1 is the scene and the "how did they know": the dinner where I barely spoke, the change I didn't ask for, the night I lay awake. ¶2 names the habit nobody sees (coming in quieter, the door, the joke, leaving early) as a decision, the fear in one plain clause, and the gift said out loud (people leave more like themselves, and I take nothing for it). The story mirrors me without asking me to be a bar: something whose effect is known, kept whole, with the care put into being plain. y3's signposted turn is the reveal, and the next two sentences are about me, not about the bar. The drink is proof: nothing added to tame it, the lemon all on show. The position is in my interest and gives something back (the evening, and the weight lifted).
**As a sceptic.** Horoscope marks, whoYouAre: S1 specific (scene), S2 specific, S3 specific, S4 specific, S5 specific (the line); ¶2: "you know what you can do to people without even trying" specific only with what follows (the quieter entrance), the list specific, "people take it for your manner" specific, the fear specific, the gift specific (the nerve for a half-ready thing), "you never take anything for it" borderline but needed against *By Design*. No more than one generic. Swap test: *Up Close* (Magnetic Leader) gives attention on purpose and fears it failing; here the effect is unchosen and the fear is it working. *Worth Finding* (Charmer) keeps a piece back to be found; this person keeps back intensity, to spare people, and nothing in the glass hides. *By Design* (Sex Symbol, lover-ruler) designed the effect and keeps the heat "safe" and people "more alive": I kept "safe" and "alive" out, and the gift here is nerve for one's own next step. *More Than One Head* owns runaway scale; this is one person at one dinner. *Their Night* and *On One Condition* ask "did I have the right?" about acts; here there was no act. *Good as It Is* owns "turn yourself down": not used. *Fair Measure*'s care was water and two portions; here nothing is diluted and the rule appears once. Traps: the guest is never the drink's strength ("strong" sits only on the drink in y2); no drunkenness; no sea, song or spell; no seduction; no gender. Rule check: the proposal points to the next dinner, not to the glass; the bar's motive is signposted in y3's one sentence; the twist is tied to the story in one clause (y4); the drink is "a renowned classic". Punch: the tagline is the weakest of the three; the whoYouAre opener and the y5 close both hold.
**Verdict:** ready for audit. **The line that lands hardest:** *Everyone else heard a lovely story. You lay awake wondering whether it was them or you.*
