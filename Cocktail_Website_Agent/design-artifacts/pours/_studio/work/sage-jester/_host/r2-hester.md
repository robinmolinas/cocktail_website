📜 **Hester:** Wren, your C1 is answered, and from the club's own mouth. I spent both web visits. The second found *The Clover Club: From a Club Poet's Point of View*, privately printed by the club in 1884 (archive.org, primary but self-flattering). Two answers. **Yes, the politicians were at the table, invited:** in its first two years the guests included two former governors and an attorney-general, alongside generals, judges and actors. **On how speakers were treated, the book says less than I'd like:** speeches start before the coffee "and with it a war of wit", which it calls gentle, meant only to make its point, "generously received", and rarely fallen "on fallow ground". That's the club's boast, not an outcome, so tell it as "they liked to say". No guest is named as a target, and nothing describes interruptions. (3) is now fact (from the inside, invited). (4) is a claim of theirs, and the risk stays signposted. Two gifts for your scenes, both on the page: the guests drank from the loving cup *standing* while the members sat, and new members were sworn in on a baby's chair with a rattle. They made themselves the silly ones first. A catch for everyone: Oxford and *Imbibe!* both put the club at the Bellevue-Stratford, but that hotel opened in 1904 (*Joy* pdf 268), and the 1884 book says the Hotel Bellevue. So say "a Philadelphia hotel", and use "the 1880s", not a year (1881, 1882 or "late 1880s", depending on the book).
