## The one true thing (no sibling has it)
They tell the truth to the person who most needs to hear it and can least afford to, usually someone with standing, at that person's own table, and they do it as a joke so the person can laugh and still take it home. **The joke is for the one it's about.** The laugh is a door left open: whoever's wrong can walk through it without losing face, and quietly change their mind on the way home. To make that possible, they'll be the silly one in the room, so someone else doesn't have to be the wrong one. They are cleverer than they let on, and the cleverness is in how carefully the silliness is aimed.

Why this and not the sheet's "slips past defences": *With the Bite In* (the Subverter) already owns the joke that gets a true thing past the people in charge, for the crowd's sake. The cleanest line between them: **the Subverter's audience is the crowd; the Wise Fool's audience is the king.** Lear's Fool talks to Lear.

## Hidden, feared, given
- **Hide:** how much it bothers them. The lie everyone's agreed to keep up sits with them all evening; the joke is the most serious thing they'll say. And how much thought went into it: what looks like a quip took them the whole meal.
- **Hide (the cost):** being "the funny one" has a price. When they're serious, about their own life, people wait for the punchline.
- **Fear (one fear, two faces):** the sheet's own: a world full of lies nobody dares to laugh at. The room where "you can't joke about that" is the rule, because that's the room where the lie is safe. The face they don't mention: the one it was for laughs, enjoys it, and changes nothing.
- **Give (say it out loud, Robin's rule):** the truth arrives without anyone having to lose. Someone gets to be wrong and keep their dignity. That's rarer than honesty, and kinder.
- **Tone:** funny is right and allowed (Robin: as far as human). Intelligent, never silly; warm, never cruel; aimed up, never down.

## Traps
- The horoscope: "you use humour to tell the truth." True of half the Jesters. The reading needs the *who it's for* (the king) and the *face kept*.
- Cruelty: the joke is never at the weak, never about a body, never a "roast" of the guest's friends.
- Politics: no party, no side, no living politician. A setting, never a stance.
- The word "fool": it's the personality's name, shown discreetly. Never call the guest a fool in the text.
- Gender: Lear's Fool says "boy" and "nuncle". Never.
- Lear as touchstone: allowed once (Robin's rule: one outside touchstone). It's the sheet's own example, so one clause at most, and Hester sources it.
- "Mocked, then proved right" about the guest: the drink's fall and return is the drink's story, never a verdict on the person (also my Devil's Advocate card's note).
- Drinking excess: no Yeats's three in a row.

## Story test (refined from the plan)
1. A truth told through humour (a name, a toast, a mocking drink).
2. Aimed up, at someone with standing or pomp.
3. **From the inside, with licence**: said in that person's presence and meant for them to hear, not smuggled past them for a crowd (the Subverter's).
4. The licence held: the target laughed along, or kept coming back, or the point stuck.
5. The risk: signposted only, never invented as an event.
Out: the merely silly, jokes at the weak, Prohibition outlaw romance, the Subverter's under-watch smuggling.

## Ruling on the plan's lead: **accept, with conditions** (Tomás builds v1 in round 2)
The Clover Club passes (1) and (2) on the page: "prominent journalists" in Philadelphia's grandest hotel, "dedicated to good eating, deep drinking, and giving politicians a hard time" (Oxford pdf 481); "rather riotous", "dedicated to raillery and refreshment", "the Friar's Club of its day" (*Imbibe!* pdf 239). It's insiders, at the grand table: that's the Fool's licence, not the Subverter's outsider song. And the drink is the club's own, which no other row can claim.

Conditions:
- **C1 (Hester, the one web visit if any):** the story needs limb (3). Find a period source on what the club's dinners actually were: were the politicians at the table as guests, and how were speakers treated? If one names a particular guest who took it in good part, (3) and (4) become fact. If nothing turns up, the reading stays at the sourced level ("giving politicians a hard time", "raillery") and leans on the drink. I'd rather a thin true story than a vivid invented one.
- **C2:** the risk is signposted only ("I like to think…"), never an event.
- **C3, the drink's fall:** one sentence, the drink's story. *Esquire*'s 1934 list also held the Bronx (first, Oxford pdf 358: *Anyway*'s drink) and the Alexander (pdf 92): say "one of", never "the worst". The return is on Oxford's page only through Julie Reiner's bar (Explorer ground): if the reading says it came back, it's one clause with no bar and no name, Hester's wording.
- **C4, out:** George Boldt (struck by Regular Guy), Reiner and the Brooklyn bar, Yeats, Lowe's half-vermouth build (Reiner's), Prohibition, any party or named politician unless the source makes it neutral. Wondrich's own jokes ("(ouch)", "the center might just hold") are his wit, not facts: never paraphrased as ours. The 1910 *Inquirer* columnist's pen-name, "A. Jin Rickki", is a fact and a lovely one; Hester's call whether it earns a clause.
- **C5, the bottle (Tomás):** Plymouth gin is *Just This Once*'s story bottle (the Scoundrel: "The man sent to sell the gin in this glass…"), and that pour is also Daiquiri-family. The triple differs (glass), but the plan didn't list it as a near miss. If you keep Plymouth, it's only "as the Oxford recipe prints it", never Plymouth's history; or go to the style, a soft, earthy dry gin. Your call; own it in one line.
- **C6, the drink's job from the person:** the mirror I'd build on is in the glass every time it's made: a frothy pink drink (Oxford's own words) over a full measure of gin. It looks like the least serious thing on the menu and isn't. That's my interpretation, signposted, never a fact. So the spark should be something found by tasting, not by looking: a seriousness under the pink. Never a joke ingredient, and never "pink for laughter" (plan guard). Egg white kept: your call, and the plan's; the family floor holds without this row.

**If C1 comes back empty and the room wants more than raillery:** the backup, Cyril Ray, carries (1)–(3) on the page ("direct and wry style… rendered the musty, mandarin pretensions… instantly obsolete", Oxford pdf 1613), and he too spoke from the inside, a wine writer laughing at wine writing. I'd still keep the Clover Club: the drink is the club's, and the person is in the glass.
