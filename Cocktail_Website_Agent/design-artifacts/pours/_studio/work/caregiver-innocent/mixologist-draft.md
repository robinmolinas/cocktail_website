# Mixologist draft — caregiver-innocent (rework v1, 2026-09-30)

## Recipe

glassware: flute or wide coupe, chilled
contains: veto-free

| amount | item | note (shown) |
|--------|------|--------------|
| 8–10 leaves | fresh sweet basil | muddle briefly in the cassis; fine-strain out before building |
| 20 ml | crème de cassis | a sweet blackcurrant liqueur; the basil-muddled cassis is what goes in the glass |
| 120 ml | Crémant de Bourgogne | sparkling wine from Burgundy, Kir's own region; or any dry sparkling wine; add last, over the cassis, down the side of the glass, no stir |

## Method

1. Put 8–10 fresh basil leaves in a small jug. Add the crème de cassis.
2. Press the leaves gently with a muddler or the back of a spoon for about 20 seconds — you want the oils, not pulp.
3. Fine-strain the basil cassis through a tea strainer into the chilled flute or wide coupe. Discard the leaves.
4. Pour the ice-cold Crémant slowly down the side of the glass. Do not stir.
5. Serve immediately.

## Checks

### Structure
Wine aperitif riff on the Kir Royale, which Oxford gives as "a variant made with crémant de Bourgogne, a local sparkling wine, or champagne" (KIR, pdf 1133). Oxford's Kir is 15 ml cassis to 100 ml wine; this is 20 ml to 120 ml, about the same proportion. Not a Codex-family cocktail: Regan's *Joy of Mixology* files the Kir under Orphans (pdf 315), cocktails with "no easily defined formulas" (pdf 213). Core: crème de cassis (the sweetening and flavouring element). Balance: Crémant de Bourgogne (dry sparkling wine, the diluting and acidic element). Seasoning: basil (aroma and a subtle herbal taste; Flavor Matrix pdf 52 gives basil as a surprise pairing for berries; extending it to blackcurrant is my step, see Pairings; contributes no modelled ABV, sugar or acid). Built in glass — no dilution beyond the pour.

### Balance
`balance.py` verdict: **freeform — no ranges applied.** Numbers:
- ABV: **12.6%** (nearest highball range 8–14%; well within)
- Sugar: **6.40 g/100 ml** (cassis at its legal floor; nearest styles fizz, highball, collins)
- Acid: **0.60%** (from the Crémant's tartaric; within reach of highball/fizz)
- Dilution: 0% (built, no shaking)

Justification: wine aperitif format (same as a Kir Royale). ABV is equivalent to a medium-strength sparkling wine with a liqueur sweetener. Sugar and acid are within reach of the highball range. Sound numbers for the format. 

**Values:** crème de cassis sugar at least 40 g/100 ml, French law's minimum of 400 g per litre (Oxford pdf 597), used at the floor; its ABV 16% is an unsourced standard value. Sweep: at 50 and 60 g/100 ml (sweeter cassis) sugar rises to 7.83 and 9.26 g/100 ml, strength and acid unchanged. Crémant de Bourgogne is in the library (Oxford KIR pdf 1133); the spec uses the `champagne_brut` row as its stand-in (brut sparkling wine, 12%; values unsourced).

### Pairings
Basil + blackcurrant (cassis): Flavor Matrix pdf 52 lists basil as a **surprise pairing** for berries (strawberry, blueberry, blackberry, raspberry, cranberry). Currants appear on that page only among the substitutes, so carrying the pairing over to blackcurrant is my own step, not the book's claim. The basil is muddled into the cassis and strained out. The Crémant's bubbles rise through the basil-scented cassis and carry the aroma up, so the basil reaches the nose before the first sip; on the palate the cassis leads and the basil sits underneath (my own knowledge of carbonation lifting aroma, unsourced). The only sourced claim is the Matrix pairing.

### Allergens
`allergens.py` verdict: **veto-free.** No nuts, no dairy, no gluten, no egg, no spice. Cassis: no allergens in the veto enum. Basil: herb, no relevant allergen flag. Crémant/sparkling wine: fining agents ignored unless a named bottle is labelled (STUDIO-RULES §4); no named bottle specified.

### Makeable
Built in the glass. Equipment: a small jug, a fine-mesh tea strainer (or muslin), the glass. No shaker. Crème de cassis is available in supermarkets and off-licences; Crémant de Bourgogne, the sparkling wine Oxford names for the Kir Royale (pdf 1133), is in most wine shops; any dry sparkling wine substitutes (Oxford itself allows champagne). Sweet (Genovese) basil is available year-round in most supermarkets. Muddle step takes about 20 seconds. No syrup prep, no overnight steps.

**Named bottle:** none. Crémant de Bourgogne is a regional style; substitute style is "any dry sparkling wine." Cassis values (unsourced) are general estimates; specific brands will vary slightly in sugar and ABV.

### Language
Plain language throughout. "Crème de cassis" explained as "a sweet blackcurrant liqueur" (Oxford pdf 597). "Crémant de Bourgogne" explained as "sparkling wine from Burgundy, Kir's own region." No bar jargon in the method. Wren's Resonance (v4 rework): whoYouAre and y5 restored word for word from Robin's kept text; y4 and epigraph ('Everyone expects the cassis. The basil gets there first.') true of the drink on the nose, as Pairings says.

## Image brief

A flute or wide coupe of deep violet-red Kir on a plain white or linen surface. Natural side light, no flash. The cassis forms a dark layer at the base; the Crémant rises above it in pale gold-rose — both halves visible in the glass. The bubbles climb the cassis slowly. One small cluster of fresh green basil leaves lies on the surface beside the glass, slightly pressed (the oils just released). No garnish in the glass. No crown, no formal backdrop. The feeling is a civic table after a long meeting — one glass left, the room already gone quiet.

## Names

1. **Before the Room** *(Wren's pick; I back it)* — the calm is already there when you arrive; Kir had the glass ready before the room had settled. Double reading in old formal use: "before the room" also means "in front of the assembled company," which is exactly where he stood.
2. **For the Room** — he poured it for every room; the Angel enters every room the same way.
3. **What He Brought** — the calm, the drink; what neither Kir nor the Angel took credit for.
4. **The Usual** — poured at every reception for years; the Angel brings the same calm every time.
5. **On His Watch** — the Resistance, then the mayor's years, then one glass, every reception.
