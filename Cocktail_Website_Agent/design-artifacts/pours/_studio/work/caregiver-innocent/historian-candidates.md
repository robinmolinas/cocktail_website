## Round 4 strikes (Hester's own words, not sources)
- **"the loudest bar in Boston"**: mine, struck. On record: "constant battles with Boston's many and vocal Prohibitionists"; "hands-on management" (Oxford pdf 353); the saloon "fitted up in a style of splendour unequalled in the Union", **his own advertisement** (same page).
- **"the care arranged quietly"**: mine, struck. Nothing in the library or the court snippets says the will was secret or that nobody knew his plan while he lived. The will was admitted to probate after his death (1877, per the court record via search summary, not read in full). Allowed: "the will was read after he died". Not allowed: "nobody knew", "quietly", "in secret".
- **Moral suasion**: the library's only gloss is Wondrich's, "basically, arguing people out of their thirst" (Imbibe! pdf 99), the temperance movement's tactic before legal prohibition. Brigham's drink was a jab *at* it, "a drink so seductive" it would make Jewett reconsider (same page). Using "moral suasion" as the Angel's method would invert the history onto him. Not usable as his method.
- Tomás v1d wording: "without adding any taste of its own" is not on record (angelica card C3/C4). Only "boosts the juniper".
