# The Wise Talker (jester-hero): Wren's read, round 1

Sources: persona.py jester-hero (essence, story, fears, drivers Build connection / LEAVE A MARK), the family plan row, the registry, and the readings of the near siblings and cousins (Worth the Trip, Anyway, For Kicks, Unrehearsed, Is It Just Me, Nothing to It, Night Light, Anyone Would Have; the plan rows for the Storyteller, the Warrior, the Expedition Leader, the Cynic).

## The one true thing
When it's bad and it *stays* bad, they keep being funny: the fortieth day as well as the first. The joke is a refusal. As long as someone in the room is still laughing, the bad thing hasn't got everything. Their method is to make the threat a little ridiculous (Guido turns the guards' orders into the rules of a game), because it's hard to be quite so afraid of something you've just laughed at. And everyone around them checks their face to know how worried to be.

## What they hide
- That it's work, not temperament. People say "nothing gets to you". It all gets to them. They've decided it doesn't get the last word.
- That each day's joke costs a little more than the last, and they're keeping count of what's left.
- (Not "I'm scared too": *Nothing to It* and *Night Light* own that. Not "I joke because it's serious": *Worth the Trip* owns it word for word.)

## What they fear
The morning nothing comes. Or worse, the joke comes and nobody laughs, because it's got too bad for laughing, and the people who've been reading their face see what they've been holding off. Not a flat party (the Morale Booster), not being thought cold (the Alternative Comic), not a table going quiet on a confession (the Stand-up).

## What they give (say it out loud once)
Dignity in the middle of it. If you can still laugh, you're still a person, not just someone something is happening to. And the mark: years later, when the people who went through it with them talk about the worst stretch of their lives, they mention the laughing first.

## Hidden need
Someone to laugh back: to join the refusal, not to rescue them (rescue is the True Friend's "allowed to be looked after").

## The position (for the story to test, not yet fixed)
It was never the joke holding everyone up. It was the refusal, and they've caught it from you. So on the day the joke won't come, let it not come, and let one of them make it. You'll hear your own line come back. What they gain: a rest, and proof of the mark. (Keep off *Anyone Would Have*'s "someone beside you already has" and *Next One's Mine*'s "next".)

## Traps
- **Denial.** This person isn't looking on the bright side. Never "look on the bright side", never "keep smiling".
- **Making the hardship the joke.** The reading laughs with them, never at what they went through. No deaths, no war as glory.
- **Life Is Beautiful stays out of guest text** (a camp). Dossier only.
- **Gender and roles.** Guido is a father; the reading never requires a child, a parent or a partner.
- **The compliment.** No "brave", no "strong". Recognise the refusal; don't praise it.
- **Too heavy.** This is a funny person. The reading should be allowed one real laugh, earned.
- **Medicine, cures and hangovers** (Caregiver ground and the Reassurer's), Prohibition (Outlaw), the sea and navy rations, flame (Magician), Irish Coffee at Foynes (*Everybody's*), the Café de Paris.

## The story test (for Hester to hunt against)
Must, all on a page:
1. A real hardship that **lasted** (days, weeks, a season), not a single bad moment.
2. Someone **in the middle of it**, sharing the cost: their own loss is on the record (their windows, their stock, their livelihood, their warmth).
3. **A joke made at the time**, in their own words or hand: a sign, a name, a menu line, a drink's name, a remark someone wrote down. Not a story told years later.
4. Aimed **at the hardship itself, for the people around them**, so they could keep going. Not at a lie (Cynic), not at the pious (Scandalmonger), not for an audience.
5. The **drink or ingredient** is on the same page (what they poured, named or joked about), so the story can be in the glass.
Bonus: the joke was repeated by others (the mark), or there's a day on record when it didn't work.

## Directions I'd accept
- **Person:** a publican, bartender or distiller who kept working through a long hardship and left a joke on the record for the people coming in.
- **Cocktail:** a drink whose name or recipe was a joke made by the people going through a shortage or a hard season, and it caught on. Guard: "stand-in" is *On One Condition*'s.
- **Ingredient:** an ingredient made do with, or kept going, in hard times, with a joke attached by the people doing it.

Leads from my memory, **unsourced**, for Hester to stand on a page or strike:
- The Blitz again, but not that sign: contemporary photo captions or diaries (Ministry of Information captions at the IWM, *Picture Post*, Mass-Observation) of a named pub or bar open with a chalked joke. A named person, a joke and a date and it leads. Café de Paris stays out.
- *The Wipers Times* (Ypres, 1916–18), the soldiers' paper of mock adverts. Anti-glory, which helps, but it's war: only if a drink is on its page and nothing about deaths, and I'd flag the tone for Robin.
- The library's nearest miss, to show the failing shape: Charles H. Baker Jr.'s Remember the Maine ("the Unpleasantness of 1933… Bombs Going off on the Prado", Oxford pdf 197). It's told afterwards, for the reader, and the joke is about bombs: fails 2, 3 and 4.
Ruled out in advance: Elephant Island (Explorer's Shackleton), pandemic drinks (medicine, deaths), *Whisky Galore* (sea, looting), Apollo 13 (no drink).

## For Tomás (no spec until the story is ruled)
The drink comes from what the story's people actually poured. Aim veto-free. Free slots in the family: Sidecar or Flip; tequila, mezcal, apple brandy and white rum unused as bases. Out: a toddy as comfort, navy rum, anything flamed, bitter-made-sweet as a metaphor (*Till Spring*). If a spark comes, I'd like it to say "refusal" or "still laughing", never "comfort".
