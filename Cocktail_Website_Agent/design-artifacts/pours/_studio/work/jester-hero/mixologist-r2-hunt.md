## Lead: the Texian Egg Nogg, Perote prison, April 21, 1843
Imbibe! pdf 129-130 (primary; Green's 1845 journal quoted), and pdf 127 ("a mezcal Egg Nogg on record, made by 'Texian' prisoners in Mexico").
- General Thomas Green and some 160 fellow Texians, prisoners of Santa Anna after a failed border raid; conditions at Perote "grim".
- To mark San Jacinto Day they bribed the guards to smuggle in ass's milk, sugar, eggs and mezcal, the mezcal carried in animal-gut tubes curled up under their shakos (tall soldiers' hats).
- They commandeered all the prison's cooking utensils; three officers whipped the eggs, another pounded the sugar, Green supervised.
- Green's words (journal, 1845): egg-nog "as never before was seen or drank under the nineteenth degree of north latitude"; "the juice of the agave inspired the soul" while "the ass's milk filled the stomach". The quantities: seven gallons of "vino mascal", as many of ass's milk, thirty dozen eggs, a large loaf of sugar.
- Wondrich's modern version (pdf 129-130): one bottle of good mezcal (he names Del Maguey Chichicapa: Brought Home's bottle, so not us), 3 cups cow's milk, 10 eggs, 1 cup sugar; yolks beaten with sugar, whites whipped and folded in. His Mexican chocolate is "not in the original".

### Against Wren's five
1. Lasted: PARTIAL. The page has them "being held prisoner" in grim conditions in 1843, after a raid; how long is not on this page (Hester: Green's journal).
2. In the middle, own loss on record: PASS. Green is one of the prisoners.
3. Joke made at the time: HALF. The deed is at the time (the gut tubes under the hats, the prison's pots commandeered, a party in a jail). The words are his 1845 journal: afterwards.
4. At the hardship, for the people around them: PASS for 160 fellow prisoners; RISK: the occasion is a battle's anniversary (war as glory), and Hester should check what else happened to these men in 1843 before Wren rules (my memory, unsourced: executions among the raid's prisoners that spring). Never in guest text either way.
5. Drink on the page: PASS (the recipe and quantities).
