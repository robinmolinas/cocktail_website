## Title block

**name:** The Wink (my pick) · others: Laugh or Swear · Still Funny · Tecolote (see alternatives file)

**tagline:** Things get bad. You get funnier.

**epigraph** (the cocktail, cold; 15 words)
*Mezcal, eggs and milk, once mixed in prisoners' water jars. Nobody stopped them.*

## whoYouAre

It's been bad for a while now. Not one bad day: weeks of it, the kind where everyone has run out of things to say. And you're still funny. Not loud, just there every morning with something, the way some people bring coffee. When it gets worse, people glance at you before they decide how worried to be.

People say nothing gets to you. It all gets to you. You've just decided it doesn't get the last word. What you don't mention is that each day the next joke takes a little more finding, and that you sometimes wonder about the morning nothing comes, when everyone looking at your face sees what you've been holding off. And what you give them is no small thing: while someone's still laughing, they're still themselves, not just people something is happening to.

## yours

1. In April 1843, prisoners from Texas were being held in the fortress prison at Perote, in Mexico, five months from home, with chains riveted round their ankles. They called the chains their jewellery. For a date that mattered to them, they got hold of mezcal, eggs, donkey's milk and a loaf of sugar, and made eggnog with every cooking utensil and water jar they had.

2. It got loud enough that the guards feared a riot. Their captain came round, was told it was how the prisoners kept their saints' days at home, and turned to go. That's when they gave the wink to a man called Trimble, who could do an owl. He rolled his eyes right back, twisted his head clean round and whooped. As one prisoner's journal tells it, the captain turned, frowned, and couldn't decide whether to laugh or swear. Then he said "Tecolote", screech-owl, and walked on, with the whole room roaring behind him. The name stuck to Trimble for good.

3. Everybody there knew whose face to turn to. The owl didn't make the chains any lighter. It let a roomful of prisoners be themselves for a minute, inside a fortress's walls, and even the captain who'd come to find out what the noise was ended up naming the joke. Later, at a roll call, it was Tecolote who covered for two of the others lying on the floor, sure of himself and kind with it, with a straight face. I like to think it was the same nerve the owl was made of. That's you: the laugh and the nerve, from the same place.

4. The cocktail I've made for you is that eggnog, a classic on record since 1788, made for one: joven mezcal, a whole egg, whole milk in place of the donkey's, and sugar, shaken until it's light and thick. The classic eggnog ends with nutmeg grated on top. This one doesn't, because there was none on their list: nothing goes in that wasn't on it. Crack the egg cleanly before you shake, keep half the shell, rinse it, and when the drink is poured, put a spoonful of mezcal in it. On another night at Perote, the prisoners kept the guard at their door in a good mood with a sip of mezcal passed through the bars in an eggshell, because nothing bigger would fit. I kept the shell as a nod to that: even the man guarding the door got a sip.

5. Some day the joke won't come. That isn't the day it wins. On the morning you've got nothing, let it not come. Go in anyway, unfunny, and just be there. My guess is it was never only the jokes they were counting on. It was you, turning up every single day, and I'd love you to see that on their faces.

## Resonance (v1, before the audit and before the drink is final)

**As the guest (a Wise Talker).** The tagline is my life in five words, and it's a little funny, which is right: "Things get bad. You get funnier." The epigraph stands cold (prisoners, water jars, "Nobody stopped them") and teases a story without needing it. whoYouAre ¶1 is the long middle, not one bad day: turning up every morning with something, and the glance people give me before they decide how worried to be. I know that glance. ¶2 has the line I'd quote: **"People say nothing gets to you. It all gets to you. You've just decided it doesn't get the last word."** Then it names what I don't mention (the next joke takes more finding, the morning nothing comes) and says plainly what I give. The story makes me laugh: an owl whooped at a prison captain's back, and the captain names it himself. The roll call shows the other half, the nerve with no joke in it. The drink is their own drink, with one sip for the man on the other side of the door. y5 doesn't tell me to stop being funny. It tells me the jokes were never the whole of it, and that I'm allowed a morning without one.

**As the sceptic.**
- *Horoscope:* ¶1 s1–s2 alone could fit anyone in a hard time, and s3–s5 narrow it (still funny, every morning, the glance). In ¶2, "It all gets to you" is borderline on its own but is carried by the next sentence. One borderline: pass.
- *Swap with the Jester siblings, all ten now read where authored:* **Reassurer** (*Worth the Trip*: one person, timing, "you joke because it is [serious]", the front): none of its frames here. **Morale Booster** (*Anyway*: a planned good night, the flop list): the party is setting only, in two clauses of y1. **Charismatic Leader** (*Who's In?*): its ending is "the first joke can be someone else's", and its whoYouAre has "years later, it's the night everyone brings up". **Both were my own first-draft ending and gift, so I cut them.** Our y5 is now the opposite move: no joke at all, just turning up. **Subverter** (*With the Bite In*): it owns laughing at whatever's in charge so a room "fears it less".
