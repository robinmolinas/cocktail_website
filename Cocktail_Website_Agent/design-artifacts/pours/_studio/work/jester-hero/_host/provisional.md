# Pour — The Wink · The Wise Talker (jester-hero)

## Cocktail

- **name:** The Wink
- **tagline:** Things get bad. You get funnier.
- **glassware:** a small plain earthenware cup (about 300 ml), chilled in the freezer; any small tumbler will do
- **contains:** `["egg-white", "dairy"]`

**recipe**

| amount | item | note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 60 ml (2 oz) | mezcal, joven (unaged) | any unaged mezcal of 40% or stronger; no particular village needed |
| 1 | whole egg, large and fresh | wash it before you crack it, because half its shell gets used |
| 30 ml (1 oz) | whole milk | theirs was donkey's milk. Use milk, not cream, so it stays light |
| 20 ml (4 teaspoons) | rich sugar syrup | for their loaf of sugar: two parts sugar to one of water, by weight, stirred until clear |
| 1 teaspoon (5 ml) | more mezcal, in half the eggshell | not in the drink: it's for someone else |

**method**
1. Put the cup in the freezer for ten minutes. Make the syrup ahead: stir two parts sugar into one part warm water, by weight, until it's clear, then let it cool.
2. Wash the egg. Crack it cleanly into the shaker, keeping one half of the shell whole, and rinse that half under the tap.
3. Add the mezcal, the milk and the syrup.
4. Shake hard without ice for about fifteen seconds. That beats the egg, which was the job three of them did by hand.
5. Add ice and shake again, hard and loud, for about twelve seconds.
6. Strain it into the cold cup. Nothing goes on top: no nutmeg, no cinnamon.
7. Stand the half-shell up beside the cup and pour a teaspoon of mezcal into it.

**closingLine:** *Fill the shell for whoever's on watch. On the days nothing's funny, show up all the same.*

## Reading

**epigraph**
*Mezcal, eggs and milk, once mixed in prisoners' water jars. Nobody stopped them.*

**whoYouAre**
It's been bad for a while now. Not one bad day: weeks of it, the kind where everyone has run out of things to say. And you're still funny. Not loud, just there every morning with something, the way some people bring coffee. When it gets worse, people glance at you before they decide how worried to be.

People say nothing gets to you. It all gets to you. You've just decided it doesn't get the last word. What you don't mention is that each day the next joke takes a little more finding, and that you sometimes wonder about the morning nothing comes, when everyone looking at your face sees what you've been holding off. And what you give them is no small thing: while someone's still laughing, they're still themselves, not just people something is happening to.

**yours**

1. In April 1843, prisoners from Texas were being held in the fortress prison at Perote, in Mexico, five months from home, with chains riveted round their ankles. They called the chains their jewellery. For a date that mattered to them, they got hold of mezcal, eggs, donkey's milk and a loaf of sugar, and made eggnog with every cooking utensil and water jar they had.

2. It got loud enough that the guards feared a riot. Their captain came round, was told it was how the prisoners kept their saints' days at home, and turned to go. That's when they gave the wink to a man called Trimble, who could do an owl. He rolled his eyes right back, twisted his head clean round and whooped. As one prisoner's journal tells it, the captain turned, frowned, and couldn't decide whether to laugh or swear. Then he said "Tecolote", screech-owl, and walked on, with the whole room roaring behind him. The name stuck to Trimble for good.

3. Everybody there knew whose face to turn to. The owl didn't make the chains any lighter. It let a roomful of prisoners be themselves for a minute, inside a fortress's walls, and even the captain who'd come to find out what the noise was ended up naming the joke. Later, at a roll call, it was Tecolote who covered for two of the others lying on the floor, sure of himself and kind with it, with a straight face. I like to think it was the same nerve the owl was made of. That's you: the laugh and the nerve, from the same place.

4. The cocktail I've made for you is that eggnog, made for one: joven mezcal, a whole egg, whole milk in place of the donkey's, and sugar, shaken until it's light and thick. Eggnog itself is a classic, on record since 1788, and the classic ends with nutmeg grated on top. This one doesn't, because there was none on their list: nothing goes in that wasn't on it. Crack the egg cleanly before you shake, keep half the shell, rinse it, and when the drink is poured, put a spoonful of mezcal in it. On another night at Perote, the prisoners kept the guard at their door in a good mood with a sip of mezcal passed through the bars in an eggshell, because nothing bigger would fit. I kept the shell as a nod to that: even the man guarding the door got a sip.

5. Some day the joke won't come. That isn't the day it wins. On the morning you've got nothing, let it not come. Go in anyway, unfunny, and just be there. My guess is it was never only the jokes they were counting on. It was you, turning up every single day, and I'd love you to see that on their faces.
