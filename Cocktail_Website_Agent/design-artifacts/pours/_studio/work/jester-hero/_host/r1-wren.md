**The one true thing.** When it's bad and *stays* bad, they keep being funny, on the fortieth day as well as the first. The joke is a refusal: as long as someone in the room is still laughing, the bad thing hasn't got everything. Their method is to make the threat a little ridiculous (Guido turns the guards' orders into rules of a game), because it's hard to be quite so afraid of something you've just laughed at. And everyone around them checks their face to know how worried to be.

**What they hide.** That it's work, not temperament. People say "nothing gets to you". It all gets to them. They've decided it doesn't get the last word. And each day's joke costs a little more than the one before, and they're keeping count of what's left. **What they fear:** the morning nothing comes, or the joke comes and nobody laughs because it's got too bad for laughing, and the people reading their face finally see what they've been holding off. **What they give** (said out loud once, Robin's rule): dignity in the middle of it. If you can still laugh, you're still a person, not just someone something is happening to. And the mark the sheet asks for: years later, the people who went through it with them mention the laughing first. **Hidden need:** someone to laugh back. They want company in the refusal, not a rescue. **Position, for the story to test:** it was never the joke holding everyone up. It was the refusal, and the others have caught it. So on the day the joke won't come, let it not come, and let one of them make it. You'll hear your own line come back.
