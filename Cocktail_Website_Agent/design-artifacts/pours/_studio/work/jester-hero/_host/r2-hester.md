📜 **Hester:** Tomás, your lead stands on the page, and Green's own journal is better than *Imbibe!*'s summary of it. I read Thomas Jefferson Green, *Journal of the Texian Expedition Against Mier* (1845, archive.org). Card: `fact-cards/perote-texian-egg-nog.md`. Anchors: `work/jester-hero/historian-anchors.md`.

**Your question: yes.** Seventeen of the Mier prisoners were shot at Salado on 25 March 1843 after drawing black beans, four weeks before the egg-nog (Green pp. 169–170, 283). Their captain, Ewen Cameron, was shot later on Santa Anna's order (pp. 284–285). Green dates it only "the 25th" (p. 376). The month isn't on any page I read. Trimble, our man below, later died "for the want of bread" (p. 261). All of that is dossier only.

**Two corrections to *Imbibe!* pdf 129.** Green **bought** the egg-nog with his "last doubloon" (pp. 258–259). The beef gut curled in a cap belongs to a Mexican soldier who sold brandy "per suck" on days it was banned (p. 277). It's a different day, and it was his cap, not their shakos. And "some 160" isn't on Green's egg-nog pages. Neither goes in.

**Wren, my lead is the person at that bowl, not Green.** The prisoners stood round it in rags and called their ankle chains their "jewelry" (p. 259). The noise brought the captain of the guard. They told him this was how their country kept its saints' days. Then they gave the nod to a prisoner called Trimble, who could do an owl. He twisted his head right round, rolled his eyes and whooped. As Green tells it, the captain didn't know whether to laugh or swear. He said "Tecolote" (screech-owl) and walked off, and the whole room roared (pp. 260–261). The nickname stayed with Trimble for the rest of his life. And on the escape night it was "Tecolote" who gave the cover line at the count, that two men lying on the floor were very sick. He said it "with an admirable mixture of confidence and sympathy", and the officer left (p. 315). On your five: (1) yes, five months from home, in chains (pp. 256–258). (2) Partly. His cost is the group's (rags, chains), and his own loss on the record is his death, which we can't use. Green's own cost is on the page: his last coin, his health, his irons. (3) Yes, a joke made at the time, told by a witness who wrote it down two years later, so "as Green tells it". (4) Yes, aimed at the guard sent to stop them, for the room, and the second time to cover two men. (5) Yes: the egg-nog is on the same page, and on the escape night the sentinel was kept sweet with mezcal passed through the bars in an eggshell (p. 316). Tomás, that eggshell is yours if you want it. Green's list has no nutmeg, so the vetoes stay egg and dairy.

**What you should weigh before ruling.** The occasion is a battle's anniversary. The men were a raiding party taken in Mexico. The toasts include "no more white flags" and a slur on Santa Anna's nation (p. 260). And Green himself was a planter who, by his son's account, died of grief over the Confederacy's defeats (TSHA *Handbook*). So Green can be the man who wrote it down, never the hero, and he's Thomas Jefferson Green, not the Confederate general Tom Green. **Backup:** none that passes independently. The library has nothing else on prisons, sieges or shortages with a joke on the page. That's both of my web visits spent. The only fallback is the escape night on its own, Trimble's line at the count and the eggshell, without the anniversary.
