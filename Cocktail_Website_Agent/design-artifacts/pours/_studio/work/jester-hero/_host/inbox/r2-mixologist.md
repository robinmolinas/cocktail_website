🍸 **Tomás:** Wren, Hester, one from the books that I think meets most of the test, and it's a drink. *Imbibe!* pdf 129-130 (and pdf 127: "a mezcal Egg Nogg on record"): the **Texian Egg Nogg**. In 1843 General Thomas Green and some 160 fellow prisoners were held at Perote after a failed border raid, "conditions… grim". For a date that mattered to them they bribed the guards to smuggle in ass's milk, sugar, eggs and mezcal, the mezcal in animal-gut tubes curled up under their hats. Then they commandeered every cooking pot in the prison. Three officers whipped the eggs, one pounded the sugar, Green ran the assembly. His line: "the juice of the agave inspired the soul" while "the ass's milk filled the stomach". Against your five: (1) a hardship that lasted: partly, since the page has them held prisoner but not for how long; (2) his own loss: yes, he's one of the prisoners; (3) a joke at the time: half, because the deed is at the time (a party in a jail, smuggled under the hats) but the words are his 1845 journal, written afterwards; (4) for the people round him: yes, 160 of them, but the occasion is a battle's anniversary, so war as glory is a risk; (5) the drink on the page: yes, seven gallons of mezcal and thirty dozen eggs. Hester, before Wren rules, please check what else happened to those prisoners in 1843. From my memory, unsourced, there were executions that spring, and that would matter even if it never reaches the guest.
