| story | In April 1843 the fortress of Perote, Mexico, held prisoners from Texas taken in the 1842 raids each way: about fifty Bexar men, captured when Woll's army seized San Antonio on 11 September 1842 (Green pp. 29–30, 39, 235; App. I p. 447; TSHA "Perote Prison"), Trimble among them (App. I p. 448); and Green with fifteen Mier men, taken after the Texan raid on Mier in December 1842 (pp. 74–82, 243; TSHA "Mier Expedition"). All hands came to the bowl (p. 259). They were months from home ("five months" is the Mier party's figure, p. 256), in chains, in cold quarters (pp. 256–258; F2–F3, C5). | A hardship that lasted: the long middle, not one bad day. They weren't innocents: men taken in raids both ways, told once and without a side. | one true thing: still funny on the fortieth day; limb 1 |
| story | They stood round their bowl in rags with chains riveted on their ankles, which they called their "jewelry" (Green p. 259, F7). | The room's own refusal, in one word. | refusal, not denial; limb 3 |
| story | For their celebration they bought mezcal ("vino mascal"), ass's milk, thirty dozen eggs and a loaf of sugar, and used all their cooking utensils and water jars to make egg-nog; three officers beat the eggs, another pounded the sugar (Green p. 259, F5–F6). | The drink is on the same page as the joke. | limb 5; the drink |
| story | Their shouts alarmed the guards, who feared a riot; the captain of the guard came round to know the cause, was answered, said "Bueno" and started to go (Green p. 260, F9). | The threat walks in on the one good evening. | fear: the bad thing showing through |
| story | Then they gave the wink to Trimble, who could mimic an owl: he rolled his eyes over, twisted his head "clean round" and whooped. The captain turned, frowned, hesitated whether to laugh or swear, said "Tecolote" (screech-owl) and moved on, to a roar of laughter from the room (Green pp. 260–261, F10). Told by a witness who wrote it down in 1845: "as he tells it". | Make the threat a little ridiculous; the threat itself names the joke. | one true thing; method: make it ridiculous; limb 3–4; everyone checks their face |

| lineage | Eggnog is on record from 1788 (Oxford EGGNOG pdf 702; *Imbibe!* pdf 127); the *Codex* calls it "the classic example" of egg with dairy (p. 256); the classic ends with nutmeg (*Codex* p. 256; Oxford pdf 703). A mezcal egg-nog is on record (*Imbibe!* pdf 127). Ours: joven mezcal, whole egg, cow's milk for the ass's milk, rich syrup for the sugar loaf, no nutmeg because none was on their list (p. 259). | A renowned classic, made from their list. Never "their recipe": the milk and syrup are ours. | drink: a classic worth having |
