📜 **Hester:** Wren, I read the lead on the page, and it holds. Both your corrections stand. The card is `_studio/fact-cards/shackleton-nimrod-mackinlays.md` (F1–F19, W1–W9, C1–C4). Your line numbers run two or three short, so I've used the file's own numbers below. The order: on 6 January, "at night I had to admit that this must be our last outward march" (3599); *then* the two days in the bags (3607); then 9 January, 88° 23′ (3633). On the miles: the book prints only "100 geographical miles" (3585). The 97 is our own sum, 90° minus 88° 23′, so 97 geographical miles, about 180 km, and it's labelled as ours. I have three corrections to my own row and to your read. **(1) "Consider the lives of those who were with me" (3538–42) is dated New Year's Day, five days before the turn.** After writing it he still depoted "and then to dash for the Pole" (3554). The same sentence also gives a second reason, that "all the results of our efforts would be lost to the world". At the actual turn the reason he gives is that nature and "our scanty supply of food had conquered us" (3604–05). So the reading can say that on New Year's Day he made himself think of the lives of the men with him. It can't say he turned back *because of* them. That's a motive on the record, but not at that moment, and "for their lives" stays our reading. **(2) "Every member of the Expedition was well" (4083) is news of the others, on 1 March, while Adams and Marshall were still out there.** The line that means everyone came home is "the entire expedition was safe on board" (6108), on 4 March. The book gives 1 a.m. (4095) and 2 a.m. (6108), so give the date, never the hour. Carry the counterweight openly too: Adams nearly went over the ice edge within sight of the ship (4097–4101). **(3) Your Bluff depot nod, Tomás, is better than a nod.** He writes that it was "the one which I had told Joyce to lay out" (3919–20). The plums and puddings are his own planning, put on the page by him. On the guards: the Emily letter is out. The book never says "whisky" (zero hits), so the tie is web-only, and nothing comes from shackleton.com or Whyte & Mackay's site. The Antarctic Heritage Trust (nzaht.org) is the spine: in January 2010 its conservators found three crates of Mackinlay's and two of brandy under the 1908 hut. The press says 2007, so there's no find year. One thing to know: the Trust gets money from the bottle sales. It's an interested party, though it isn't the brand. **For you, Tomás, on the bottles:** the Trust and CBC/AP (19 Jan 2013) support this. Whyte & Mackay owns the Mackinlay brand. Its master blender, Richard Paterson, recreated the whisky from a sample drawn by syringe through a cork, as a *limited edition of 50,000 bottles*, with a share going to the Trust. "Exact replica" is the Trust's word, and the April 2011 date is the Trust's too. The second edition is late 2012 or 2013 depending on the source, so no year. The relaunch is in *The Spirits Business* and on scotchwhisky.com (April 2017): a blended Scotch malt called **Shackleton**, built "upon" the recreation with other Highland malts, no age statement, 40% abv, about £40. **It is not the replica and not the whisky at the hut.** It can only be "the blended malt made in its memory", and anything we say about it being on sale today gets dated. The 50,000-bottle replica was limited, so as a recommended bottle the relaunch is the makeable one. Also out: "his favourite dram", the "25 hand-selected cases", "faced with certain death" and "first on the Polar Plateau". That's launch copy, relayed in trade press. So is whether the original was a single malt or a blend (scotchwhisky.com and AP disagree), so we name no type and no age. Story's ruled from my side.
