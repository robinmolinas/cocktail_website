- **The persona in one line (Wren):** the Expedition Leader takes everyone out there and is the same person who turns everyone round. They go as far as everyone can get back from, and not a step further. They hide how much they wanted the top, and the weight of counting heads. They fear being trapped somewhere they led people. What they give: people go further with them than they'd dare alone, and they come home.
- **The story chosen:** the plan's lead, on the primary page. Shackleton's Nimrod southern journey (1909), told in the book's order. At New Year he made himself consider the lives of the men with him, and kept going south for days afterwards. On 6 January he admitted it must be the last outward march, then they were pinned two days in the tent, and on 9 January he turned round about 180 km from the Pole, giving nature and food as his reason, "though failure was ours". The Bluff depot, laid on his own orders, got them back. He felt "the load… lifted from my shoulders", yet went straight back out for Adams and Marshall, and "the entire expedition was safe on board" on 4 March. **Runner-up:** Edgeworth David handing the lead to Mawson (unused: same expedition, a position, not a person).
