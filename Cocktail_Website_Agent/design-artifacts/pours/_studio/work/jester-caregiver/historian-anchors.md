## Drink rows (spec v1: 60 London dry gin 43%+ / 30 sweet vermouth / 5 dry / one thin orange wheel muddled with ¼ tsp cracked coriander, strained / 2 dashes Peychaud's; shaken; stemmed wine glass; 20.2%)

| kind | fact | meaning | speaksTo |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| lineage | The Bronx was among the drinks of the moment at her party (Oxford pdf 512), "the cocktail sensation of the 1900s and 1910s" (pdf 358), one of the two drinks whose citrus changed what a Cocktail was (pdf 491). Its old problem: enough orange to taste and it's weak; people tried juice, orange bitters and peel, and nothing settled it (*Imbibe!* pdf 237). The first recipe on record (Malloy, via Boothby 1908) used a squeeze of peel with about a barspoon of juice and orange bitters (pdf 238). Oxford's recipe muddles an orange wheel (pdf 358). Origin contested: no Waldorf. | Our try at an old argument, not the answer to it. We start from the muddled wheel. | position: one more try; let the drink sound worth having |
| riff | Coriander is one of the usual botanicals in London dry gin, "for its lemony notes" (Oxford pdf 894), and the *Flavor Matrix* lists coriander seeds among citrus's substitutes (pdf 80). ¼ tsp ("a pinch", never "a few"), cracked, muddled with the wheel and strained out. | What's ours: one small new thing added to an old argument. | tie the twist to the story |
| ingredient | Peychaud's is the Sazerac's bitters (Oxford pdf 1750), bright red (pdf 1502). Two dashes, in place of Oxford's Angostura. | For the guests who'd rather have had a Sazerac, her hardest ones to win over (F6). | gives: even the hardest person gets something |
