# Tomás, round 2: drink ideas for The Morale Booster (no spec, no balance yet)

Story not yet ruled, so nothing below has been through balance.py or allergens.py. These are directions for Wren and Hester.

## The drink's own history is a tally of tries (story lead)
- *Imbibe!* pdf 237 (Wondrich, BRONX COCKTAIL): the breakthrough came in 1907; "That didn't mean everybody was satisfied with it." Zoë Akins, 1913 play *Papa*: "He looks as weak as a Bronx cocktail." The trouble was the orange juice: put in enough to taste it and the drink is weak, leave it out and it's a Perfect Martini. "People tried everything in the way of dashes of juice, orange bitters, and orange peel to effect a compromise." Dissenters raved about "kiddie drinks and fruit punch".
- *Imbibe!* pdf 238: Boothby 1908 recipe (1 oz each Plymouth gin, French and Italian vermouth, 2 dashes orange bitters, about a barspoon of orange juice, orange peel); Solan's (1½ oz gin, ¾ oz OJ, 1 tsp each vermouth). Wondrich: "any other good London dry gin" makes it. Also: "When I want a stronger drink, I'll fix myself a Sazerac" (a coincidence with Walsh's menu: Hester to own it or leave it out).
- Oxford FROZEN DAIQUIRI pdf 855: the Bronx's orange juice "took the cocktail from the saloon into the home". Oxford COCKTAIL IN MODERN PARLANCE pdf 491: the Bronx and the Daiquiri changed what a cocktail was (citrus).
- Mirror (for Wren): the first drink on her menu was itself a crowd-pleaser everyone kept trying to fix. The riff's job is to be the try that takes, never to claim we "saved" the Bronx (it still fell: Oxford pdf 358, Esquire 1934).

## The fix is physics (the spark)
- *Flavor Matrix* pdf 80, CITRUS: flavour "is most prominent in the essential oils found in the outer colored layer of the rind", while the pulp "contains the sugars and acid". Substitutes for citrus: "Coriander seeds, cardamom, camomile…". (Surprise pairings sage and caraway are *A Brother's Care*'s and *Not Only the Way*'s; peanut and pecan are `nuts`. Cardamom is in *Off-Label*, camomile in *Till Spring* and *Everybody's*. Coriander is in no pour.)
- Coriander is a gin botanical "for its lemony notes" (Oxford GIN pdf 894; *Codex* p. 66) and in orange bitters (Oxford pdf 1447).
- So: take the orange out of the juice and into the peel. A thick strip of orange peel and a few cracked coriander seeds shaken hard with the ice (the ice breaks the oils out), fine-strained through a tea strainer, and only a little fresh juice. Enough orange to taste without the weakness. Household kit only.
- One gift for the hardest guest (Wren's): Peychaud's in place of the orange bitters, the Sazerac's bitters (Oxford SAZERAC COCKTAIL pdf 1750; PEYCHAUD'S pdf 1502, "bright-red"). A dash, not a cloud: no absinthe (*Rosetta*). Peychaud's is also in *No Accident* (3 dashes in a Seelbach): the same bottle, not the same gesture.
- Veto-free as far as I can see today (coriander isn't `spice`: heat only). To be proved.

## The shape clash
- Innocent's Little Prince now holds Martini · London dry gin · nick-and-nora. Gin's Martini glasses are taken: coupe (*Quite Alive*), nick-and-nora (Little Prince), small frozen cocktail glass (Magician's Siren), cocktail glass (Hero's Vesper fallback; *Instead* is Old Tom in a small stemmed cocktail glass).
- Keep the base (the story gives no gin; Wondrich pdf 238 says any good London dry). Move the glass: **a small stemmed wine glass, chilled**. Free for London dry (Explorer's small stemmed wine glass is rum). Craft reason, mine and unsourced: the fix lives in the scent of the peel and seed, and a bowl that narrows at the rim holds it.
- Not Plymouth: it's a single brand, and Boothby's was an advertiser (pdf 238). Not Old Tom: *Instead*'s ground.

## Round 3 update (after Hester's r2)
- **Correction, mine:** Oxford BRONX COCKTAIL pdf 358's recipe is an orange wheel cut in 8 and muddled in the shaker, 60 ml London dry gin, 15 ml each sweet and dry vermouth, 2 dashes **Angostura**, shaken, strained into a chilled cocktail glass. No juice, and no orange bitters (those are Boothby's, *Imbibe!* pdf 238). So Peychaud's replaces the page's Angostura, not orange bitters.
- The muddled wheel is already the Matrix's physics (pdf 80: flavour in the peel's oils, sugar and acid in the pulp). The riff builds on the page's recipe: cracked coriander seed muddled with the wheel (the spark), Peychaud's for Angostura (the gift). Fine-strain through a tea strainer for the seeds and pulp.
- Oxford's own verdict on the balanced Bronx: "a light and pleasant drink, not a spectacular one" (pdf 358). That's the riff's job: make it worth having.
- Oxford's "insipid" (too much juice), not "dull"; "at least eleven" claimants, so no Waldorf and no inventor anywhere.
- Oxford pdf 358: the Bronx took the cocktail "from the barroom to the sideboard", "helping to sell untold numbers of cocktail shakers". If Wren takes that clause, the drink stays shaken (the shaker is the Bronx's own tool).
- Glass stays as proposed: small stemmed wine glass, chilled (the page's cocktail glass is Siren's and the Vesper's shape with London dry).
