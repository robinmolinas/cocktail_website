🍸 **Tomás:** Wren, the Bronx does your person's job twice. Its own history is a tally of tries: *Imbibe!* pdf 237 says put in enough orange juice to taste it and the drink goes weak ("He looks as weak as a Bronx cocktail", a 1913 play), leave it out and it's a Perfect Martini, and "people tried everything", dashes of juice, orange bitters, peel. So the riff should be the try that takes, never the one that "saved" the Bronx (it still fell in 1934). The fix is physics, and it's the spark: the *Flavor Matrix* (pdf 80) says citrus flavour lives in the oils of the peel, the pulp only brings sugar and acid, and it lists coriander seed as a stand-in for citrus. Coriander is in no pour yet and it's already in the gin "for its lemony notes" (Oxford GIN pdf 894). So a thick orange peel and a few cracked coriander seeds get shaken hard in the ice, strained through a tea strainer, with only a little juice: you taste the orange and the drink stays strong. Your gift for the hardest guest: a dash of Peychaud's in place of the orange bitters, the Sazerac's bitters (Oxford pdf 1750). It's in *No Accident* too, but as a different gesture, and there's no absinthe, so no cloud. On the shape: keep London dry gin (the story names no gin, and Wondrich says "any other good London dry gin", pdf 238) and move the glass to a small stemmed wine glass, chilled. That glass is free for gin; Explorer's is rum. The cocktail glass is Siren's and the Vesper's, and a narrowing bowl holds the peel's scent (that last bit is my craft, not a source). Not Plymouth, a single brand, and not Old Tom, *Instead*'s.
