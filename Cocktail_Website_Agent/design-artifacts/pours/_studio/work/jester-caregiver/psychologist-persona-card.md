# Wren: persona card, The Morale Booster (jester-caregiver), round 1

Read fresh from `persona.py jester-caregiver` (Robin's rewritten sheet), my Jester sketch (`plans/work/jester/psychologist-sketches.md`), the family plan row (`plans/jester.md`) and the primary page (Oxford COCKTAIL PARTY, pdf 512, read by me this round; BRONX COCKTAIL, pdf 358).

## The one true thing (no sibling or cousin shares it)
They make the fun on purpose, for a group, and they give it a shape so everyone can step in: the daft game with rules, the invented Friday tradition, the theme, the "right, everyone, here's what we're doing". It looks made up on the spot. It almost never is. The sheet's driver is capitalised for a reason: PROVIDE STRUCTURE. Their fun is built, not found.

And the part nobody else carries: **they keep count of the ones that didn't take.** The quiz nobody got into, the evening that stayed flat however hard they pushed. Everyone else forgot it by Monday. They didn't.

## Hide, fear, give
- **Hide:** the planning, and the tally of flops. Also that it's work: they're thinking about the room while they're laughing in it.
- **Fear:** the gloom nobody can lift, and being the one who couldn't (the sheet's own words). Under that: that people think their fun is forced ("do we have to?").
- **Give:** a way in. People who would never start the fun themselves get to join it, and even the hardest person to win over gets something. Say it out loud once (Robin's rule): that's what their way gives other people, and it's worth something.

## Siblings (same primary) and cousins, and how this one differs
- **The Reassurer** (*Worth the Trip*, caregiver-jester, the near-twin): one person, the worst day, the timing of one line, "let the quiet happen". The Morale Booster is a room, an ordinary low week, a thing made with a shape, and they fill the quiet on purpose. The sheet's "they know exactly when a joke will help and when it would hurt" is the Reassurer's scene already: **not our recognition line.**
- **The Stand-up:** finds the funny already in the room. This one brings it in.
- **The Wacky One:** ridiculous for identity, for themselves. This one's fun is always for someone.
- **The Wise Talker:** high stakes, joking during the hardship, a shield. This one is everyday weather.
- **The Naïf, the Alternative Comic, the Tease, the Elf/Imp, the Scandalmonger, the Straight Man, the Cynic:** earnest, the puzzled reaction, teasing, mischief, the gasp, the deadpan, the jibe. None of them organises joy for a crowd.

## Traps
- Hospital clowns, any care job, medicine, cures, hangovers (the sheet's example; the Reassurer's and the Caregiver family's ground).
- "Clown", "comedian", "life and soul", "the fun one" as labels about the guest.
- The joke's timing (the Reassurer's).
- Wealth: the mansion and the Plaza stay out. A society hostess reads as the Geisha; keep the story on the idea, not the preparations.
- The reading is not about alcohol, though the story invents a drinks hour. The 1922 *New York Times* sneer is about "strong waters": if it's used at all, it's the sneer at fun made on purpose, never at drinking.
- "Flopped" overstates the page. Oxford (Felten) says the baby party "wasn't quite the enduring success" the cocktail party would be: say it **didn't catch on**. Laugh with it, never at her.
- "First" only as "so conceived and so called". The 1903 and 1906 parties that ended in deaths: never. Alec Waugh: never.
- Date: Oxford says "a Sunday afternoon sometime in the first few months of 1917"; April 3 is the *Beacon*'s article, not the party. No month.
- "For the hide-bound oldsters" is Felten's phrase, and the page serves Sazeracs **and Mint Juleps** to them. Name only the Sazeracs (the julep is Lover's), and any motive is "I like to think".
- No gloom on record at all: "heavy room" lives in whoYouAre's scene, never in the history.

## Story test
Someone who deliberately made joy for a group, put work and a shape into it (a plan, a theme, a format), and a time it didn't take is on record. Out: cures and medicine, one person, the front line and the taxi, a lone joke, a hangover, an entrance.

## Verdict on the plan's lead: Clara Bell Walsh, St. Louis, 1917 — accept
It passes all three limbs on one primary page: it was made on purpose for about fifty people; it had a shape (cocktails from noon to one, then dinner: the hour itself is the structure); and she "had tried out other party themes" first, one of which, guests dressed as babies drinking from bottles, didn't catch on. The one that did is still how people gather. That is the flop tally turned into the gift: nobody remembers the baby party; everyone knows the cocktail party.

Where the weight goes: on the trying (the themes before), the one that didn't take, and the one that did. Not on the house, the guest list or any sadness.

## Position I'm reaching for (Ryu's order: earned, never opened with)
The ones that didn't take are how you found the one that did. So throw the next one anyway, and let the one that falls flat be funny too. What they gain: the flop loses its grip. (Fallback, weaker because the True Friend is near it: let someone else make the fun for you once.)
