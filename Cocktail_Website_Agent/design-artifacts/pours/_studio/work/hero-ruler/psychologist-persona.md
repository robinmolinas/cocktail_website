Sources: `persona.py hero-ruler` (sheet story, goal Mastery, fear Weakness, drivers Leave a mark / PROVIDE STRUCTURE, example Hector); family plan `plans/hero.md`, hero-ruler row; *A Proper Drink* pp. 283–287 (printed), re-read whole by me this round; sibling readings re-read: *A Place Kept* (Noble Ruler), *The Long View* (Legend), *On Their Behalf* (Perfectionist), *Who's In?* (Charismatic Leader), *Anyone Would Have* (Firefighter), *Next One's Mine* (Saviour), *Nothing to It* (Pioneer), *Day Job* (Superhero).

## Who this is

The sheet: "stands in front so that others can stand behind", "keeps order when things fall apart because someone has to", "trusts discipline more than luck", and fears "not the fight but faltering in front of the people who depend on them."

In plain life: the week everything goes wrong (the redundancy, the illness in the family, the flat that floods), this is the person who still has everyone sit down to dinner at seven. Who still turns up on Monday shaved, ironed, on time, and expects the same of the others. Who says "we still do this properly" while everything else is loose. People find it a bit much. Some laugh. Some say "is this really the moment?"

## The one true thing (no sibling has it)

**When things fall apart, they don't loosen the rules. They hold them tighter, because a rule still kept is how everyone else can tell it isn't over.**

The standard isn't about quality (that's the Perfectionist) and it isn't about words (the Legend). It's a signal. When it goes wrong, people check this person's face and habits to find out how bad it is. They know it. So the ordinary rules keep running, visibly: that's the line the others stand behind.

## What they hide, fear, give

- **Hide:** the rules hold *them* up too. Keeping the table set at seven is how their own hands stay steady. And the doubters got to them: some nights they agreed with every person who said it was a terrible idea. Nobody saw those nights.
- **Fear:** being the first thing that slips, where the others can see it. Not failure: the visible wobble, because the others read the whole situation from it. One crack in the routine and the room would read "it's over".
- **Give:** the feeling, in the worst week, that something is still expected of you, so you still matter, and that it isn't over. Being held to a standard in a bad time is a form of respect. (Say it out loud once, Robin's caregiver rule: "that's how you tell people they're still worth the effort", or similar.)
- **Turned into the gift:** what looks like stiffness is the line everyone else can see.

## Traps

1. **War as glory.** The persona's imagery is battle lines and shields. The family guard says none. No Hector's death, no "front line" metaphors beyond, at most, one plain "in front". At most one of "hero / brave / fight".
2. **Diagnosis.** Never "controlling", "rigid", "stubborn" as a label. Say what they do ("you still set the table").
3. **The hurricane.** Named once, as the reason he came home. No toll, no images, no "storm" imagery elsewhere (my mass-death rule).
4. **The swear.** His "pretentious assholes" never reaches the guest. "Pretentious" is enough, attributed.
5. **"Cure".** Named plainly once. No wordplay (medicine is out of this family).
6. **Gender.** Warrior and Hector pull masculine. Second person only, scenes anyone lives (dinner at seven, Monday morning).
7. **The Pioneer's position.** No "tell them you were scared".
8. **The money.** The home bottles are a lovely detail but a Seed/Noble Ruler shape. One clause, warm, never the reveal.

## Story test (what the story must show)

A person responsible for a group goes first into something hard, and keeps order (a rule, a standard) while things have fallen apart around them; people doubt or mock the standard; it costs them; ideally the record shows doubt or strain the group didn't see. Out: battles, deaths as glory, military ranks.

## Verdict on the plan's lead: **accept Bodenheimer and Cure, re-centred on the dress code**

Re-read whole, pp. 283–287. It passes better than the plan says, because the strongest detail isn't the cupboard, it's the rule:

- **Went first, for a group:** he came home after Katrina meaning to be "one of the first" with a stand-alone cocktail bar; bought a building in lonely Freret, far from the French Quarter; "no one thought" it a good idea; ten to twenty people tried "interventions" (p. 285). He hired a staff and, on drinks, "ceded control to his employees" (p. 285).
- **Kept order when things had fallen apart:** the one rule he did set was a dress code: no shorts, no flip-flops, no baseball caps, which Cure "would never quite live down", and he spent nine months to a year convincing people they weren't just being pretentious (p. 285). His own words for the city: decaying, things washed away, "People started over" (p. 287). A dress code in a city starting over is exactly my one true thing: a rule kept so people can tell it isn't over. And it's funny, gently, which the reading can let it be.
- **Cost:** so short of cash that for the first two weeks the bartenders poured his own bottles from home; "We needed to be [successful], or we would have gone under" (p. 286). One clause.
- **Private doubt:** not on the page as an event. It's a motive, so it can be signposted ("I like to think there were nights he half agreed with them"), never claimed.

My reading of it, signposted: he handed his staff the part people would praise (the drinks) and kept for himself the one rule people would laugh at. That's "stands in front so that others can stand behind", in a bar.

## The drink (Tomás's call; my notes on fit)

The Crusta-shaped Sidecar fits the re-centred story better than it fitted the old one: **a drink that dresses up by definition**, from the city of the story, in a glass with its sugar rim and a lemon peel lining it like a collar. The tie I'd write, one clause: *it's a drink that dresses for the occasion, as a nod to the rule nobody let him forget.* Notes, all from my lessons: sugar on the outside of a rim never reaches the drink, so there's sugar inside too (Regan's "outside only" is our method, not the source's); no "his recipe" or "the original" for a riff; the collar and rim done properly every time is the standard itself, not a performance; never name Jerry Thomas. A spark is welcome if it's about the person (holding firm), not about the cupboard. Pantone 200 crimson is the image brief's, not the glass's, unless Tomás finds it on its own merits.
