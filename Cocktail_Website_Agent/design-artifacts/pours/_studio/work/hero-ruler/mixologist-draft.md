# Tomás: drink draft, hero-ruler (The Warrior), v1.3, round 4 step 3 (Hester r4 D1, D2 landed; closing line checked against W10: "doubters" → "a critic"; the drink is unchanged)

Spec: `_studio/specs/hero-ruler.json` (v1). A Brandy Crusta, stirred, on the Oxford Companion's recipe (pdf 610), with a little more curaçao and less lemon, dressed the Crusta's own way: fine sugar on the rim and half a lemon's peel lining the glass like a collar. The drink dresses up by definition, and the dress is what the Crusta is named for ("crusta" means crust, pdf 610). The spark: the cognac is steeped twenty minutes with ordinary black tea, so the drink's grip comes from tannin, not from a pour of lemon. The lemon is held to half a teaspoon, because a stirred drink this strong can't take more and stay in balance.

**Glassware:** small stemmed wine glass (about 200 ml), chilled, fine sugar on the outside of the rim, lined inside with a collar of lemon peel, no ice
**Contains:** veto-free

## Recipe

| amount | item | note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 60 ml | cognac steeped with black tea | 2 ordinary black-tea bags in 150 ml cognac (any VS or VSOP, 40% or stronger), 20 minutes, then lift the bags out; enough for two drinks |
| 5 ml (1 teaspoon) | rich simple syrup | 2 parts white sugar to 1 part water, by weight, warmed until clear |
| 5 ml (1 teaspoon) | dry orange curaçao | or a triple sec |
| 2.5 ml (½ teaspoon) | fresh lemon juice | measure it: a full teaspoon makes it too sharp for a stirred drink |
| 2 dashes | Angostura bitters | |
| half a lemon | lemon peel, pared in one long piece | the collar |
| a saucer | fine (caster) sugar | for the outside of the rim |

## Method

1. Put the two tea bags in the cognac in a jar and leave them 20 minutes. Taste from 15 minutes on and lift them out, without squeezing, when the tea shows as a dry edge behind the brandy. Left too long, black tea turns harsh.
2. Chill a small stemmed wine glass (step 3 does it).
3. About an hour ahead, run a cut lemon around the outside of the rim, roll the outside of the rim in fine sugar so none falls into the glass, and put the glass in the fridge: the sugar sets and won't run.
4. Pare half a lemon in one long strip, the way you'd peel an apple, yellow only. Curl it and fit it inside the glass so it lines the bowl like a collar.
5. In a mixing glass, put the tea cognac, syrup, curaçao, lemon juice and bitters. Fill with ice and stir for about 20 to 30 seconds, until the outside of the mixing glass is very cold.
6. Strain into the glass, inside the collar. No ice.

**closingLine:** *Fit the peel inside the glass, then take your hand away: it stays. Next time a critic has a point, grant it first.*
