# Persona read: The Giggler (innocent-jester)

Wren, round 1, 2026-10-04. Sources: `persona.py innocent-jester`, my family sketch (`_studio/plans/work/innocent/psychologist-sketches.md`), the Innocent plan row, the sibling readings (Jester- and Innocent-families, read today), *A Proper Drink* pp. 144, 145, 168, 169, 170, 200, 288, 313 (read on the page today).

## Core truth (the one thing no sibling shares)

**They don't make the joke. They're the one who can't stop laughing at it, and their laugh is what makes it funny for everyone else.**

Every Jester-side sibling *produces* the fun: the Wacky One keeps a running joke going, the Morale Booster plans the game, the Stand-up tells the story, the Reassurer times the line, the Wise Fool makes themselves the silly one on purpose, the Prankster sets something up. The Giggler produces nothing. They *find* things funny (more things, and sooner, than anyone around them), and it gets out before they can stop it. Nobody decides to follow them, but the room follows anyway. Laughter is how they check the world is safe: if a room can laugh, it's all right in there.

## Shows / hides

- **Shows:** an easy, frequent, real laugh; delight in other people's stories; the person who sets a table off.
- **Hides:** how many laughs they swallow (in meetings, on solemn occasions, with people who'd think less of them), and what it costs: a stifled laugh sits in the chest for the rest of the afternoon. They also hide the small hurt of the lonely laugh, the second when they're the only one laughing and the room doesn't follow. And the put-down they've heard all their life: "you'd laugh at anything". They wouldn't. They just find more of it funny.

## Hidden fear → hidden gift

- **Fear:** being told off for it (punishment is the primary fear) and, worse, learning to go solemn to stay welcome. The fear isn't the joke falling flat (that's the Morale Booster's list); it's being *corrected* for something that was only ever delight.
- **Gift (said out loud once, per Robin's caregiver-batch rule):** when they laugh at what someone said, that person feels funny, maybe for the rest of the night. Their laugh is a gift to whoever made it happen. And a laugh that hurts nobody tells a stiff room that nothing bad is going on here.

## The "real me" lines to aim for (drafts, not final)

1. "You're not the one telling the joke. You're the one who can't stop laughing at it, and that's usually when it gets funny."
2. "People say you'd laugh at anything. You wouldn't. You just notice more of it is funny." (watch: "notice" is the Surrealist's word in its tagline; may need "find")
3. "Nobody counts the laughs you swallow."
4. The gift line: "When you laugh at something someone said, they feel funny for the rest of the night. Very few people give that away so freely."

## Truth vs. legend

The true story, told warmly. They love a real thing that turned out delightful, and they'd be uneasy if the fun in the story had a victim or a lie under it. Legends allowed only signposted, and only kind ones.

## Tone

Genuinely light. A smile in the bartender's voice, one honestly funny moment if it comes naturally (the Toronto line can carry it), never a pun and never a "trying to be funny" reading. Warm, quick sentences. The fear is told gently and briefly: one paragraph at most, then back to the light.

## Distinct from siblings

- **The Reassurer (*Worth the Trip*)** waits, reads one person, times the line. The Giggler can't wait and can't time it, and it's never for one person's pain. Never "the wrong moment / the right one"; no grief.
- **The Wacky One (*For Kicks*)** authors the joke and keeps it going on purpose; owns "expected you to grow out of it", "any look across a room" and "bigger gift than being taken seriously". The Giggler authors nothing. So in the reading's own voice: no "taken seriously", no "grow out of it", no "look across the room".
- **The Wise Fool (*In Good Part*)** owns "the room that frightens you is the one where nobody's allowed to laugh" and ends on "be silly for the fun of it, and that somebody laughs with you". The Giggler's fear stays in the body (the swallowed laugh), never a frightening room.
- **The Mad Inventor (*Why Not?*)** owns "You laugh first, and loudest" and "the surprise is catching". So: never "laugh first", never "catching"/"contagious".
- **The Morale Booster (*Anyway*)** owns the planned fun and "even the one who swore they wouldn't ends up laughing".
- **Innocent siblings:** the Naive Artist (the made thing, the kind correction), the Free Spirit (the question), the Underdog (checking it was fair). Of the five "mocked" Innocents, this row owns **the laugh: the swallowed one, and the one that lets everyone else go**.
- **Elsewhere:** "breathe(s) easier/out" (Angel, Reassurer), "allowed" (Four Shares), "Someone had to… usually you" (Not Too Polite), "Every tease is a compliment" (the Flirt: the Giggler's laugh is a gift, never a tease).

## Traps

- Labels: clown, class clown, "the life of the party", "jester", Tickle puns, "giggle" as a pun on the name.
- Childhood: never call them childlike, young or innocent; no classroom scene (adult rooms: a meeting, a wedding speech, a train).
- A story whose lesson is "go quiet and you'll succeed" (it enacts the fear).
- Fun with a victim; alcohol as the fun; mockery of authority (Outlaw ground); humour in grief (the Reassurer).
- A reading that performs comedy. And a drink that's a gag the drinker is the butt of.

## My story test (refined from the family sketch)

The mirror must show (1) joy or fun that's **harmless and sincere, with nobody its butt**; (2) it **reached into a serious setting**; (3) it was **dismissed, frowned on or scolded for being unserious** (sourced); (4) **people loved it anyway, it spread further than anyone could explain, and it outlasted the frown**. Out: pranks with victims, drunken antics, grief, rebellion as the point, archetype puns, and any arc where the fun had to be given up for the success.

## Verdict on the plan's lead: Sam Ross, Ginger → the Paper Plane

**Accept, on conditions.** Re-read whole today (*Proper* pp. 144–145, 168–169, 170, 200, 288, 313).

Why it passes: Ginger is the persona's room: "very, very humorous", a menu that "read like a comedy script", orange plastic "like a psychedelic spaceship", and Jacob Briars's line that there was "no such thing as the idea anything might be wrong" (p. 144). That last line *is* the Giggler's safety. The frown is on the page and aimed at the fun: Ginger "wasn't taken seriously because it was so tongue in cheek… a credibility gap", and "Sam Ross felt that bias" (pp. 144–145). The ending is limb 4 told by its own facts: an equal-parts drink named after a song he had on while he worked it out, made for a bar where he never worked, and now, by his account, "essentially the official drink of Toronto", for reasons that remain a mystery to him (p. 288). Delight that spread further than anyone could explain. That's the laugh that the room follows.

Conditions (fresh eyes, beyond my plan review):
1. **The mirror is Ginger, the room, and the drink, not Ross's character.** The record's Ross is brasher than this guest: Pegu's "Puck" who rearranged a colleague's well and riled the waitresses (p. 169), "glad-handing, arrogant, likeable scamps" (p. 200), the ambassador quote (p. 304). None of it is told, and the reading never says he was like the guest.
2. **"I shut my mouth and watched" (p. 168) is out entirely**, not just softened. Told next to his success it teaches this guest to go solemn. And p. 169 shows he didn't: the mischief carried on. So the story jumps from Melbourne to Chicago honestly, without the claim either way.
3. **The reading's own voice never says "taken seriously"** (the Wacky One's whoYouAre). Use Reaburn's words with attribution ("tongue in cheek", "a credibility gap").
4. **The paper plane image is owned:** the drink is named after the song (sourced). Any picture of a paper plane sailing across a stiff room is "I like to think", one sentence, adult setting.
5. Carried from the plan: the divorce, the Pegu mischief, the crude Sydney quote, *Mohawk mullet*: out. No Milk & Honey, no Little Branch, no Petraske, no Attaboy (it's the Milk & Honey room), no Gold Rush. The Penicillin one clause at most ("the drink he's best known for"), or nothing. Ross is alive: date any present tense (*A Proper Drink*, 2016). The Ross sister and brother may be named as co-founders, never as a sibling-keeping story (A Brother's Care).

**Backup, the cocktail cherry:** hold, not run. It passes 1, 2 and 4 well (the bright red thing "everybody seems to have gone mad after", in the most serious glass, back after "Cherries are going out"), weak on 3 (fashion and price, not a scolding), costs the veto floor (`nuts`), and it carries a trap of its own: the guest must never read as the garnish, the decoration nobody needed.
