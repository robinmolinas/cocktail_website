## Fixes (old → new), all Wren's
| ID | old | new | why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| W1 | `and fell for the city's old drinking life` | `and was intrigued by the city's old drinking life` | Oxford's word is "Intrigued". "Fell for" gives a living man a feeling no page reports. |
| W2 | `He went through its old bars and early recipes` | `He researched its long-gone bars and early cocktails` | The bars were "defunct". "Went through" reads as a pub crawl through bars that no longer exist. |
| W3 | `long before that kind of history was fashionable` | `before that kind of history was fashionable` | Oxford says "before", not "long before". |
| W4 | `For years he was one of the few` | `Before Hurricane Katrina, he was one of the few` | *Proper* scopes it to "Prior to Katrina". "For years" is unscoped and could run to now. |
| W5 (optional) | `Around 2000, at the Ritz-Carlton's` | `Around 2000, he recalls, at the Ritz-Carlton's` | The date is his own recollection (ICE MALLET pdf 1066). It's optional because "around" already hedges it. |
| W6 | `People had been reading it out for decades before him.` | `It had been reprinted and quoted for decades before him.` | The page evidence is printings (1907, 1929, 1933, 1956) and one *fictional* recital (Cobb). That doesn't show "people … reading it out". The counterweight stays, on what's there. Pronoun: "It" = the ode, from y2. |
| W7 | `The cocktail I've made for you is that julep, the renowned classic,` | `The cocktail I've made for you is a Mint Julep, the renowned classic,` | "That julep" points back to McMillian's julep. The page gives his ice, not his recipe; ours is the *Codex*'s with the ode's syrup. |
| W8 | `McMillian's way, so it goes in dry and the cup frosts white all over.` | `McMillian's way, so it goes in dry. Churned, the cup frosts white all over.` | The canvas keeps the ice dry (*LI* p. 44); the frost comes from churning (*Codex* p. 31). This is Tomás's wording. |
| W9 | `The rest keeps to its steps, as a nod to you:` | `Otherwise it follows the ode's steps, as a nod to you:` | The pounded ice also departs from the ode's cracked ice, so "the rest" overclaims. The ice is already named as McMillian's. This is Tomás's wording. |
| W10 | `It's ice-cold, fragrant and quietly strong, and it wants sipping slowly.` | `It's ice-cold, fragrant and quietly strong, and it's made to last.` | "Sip it slowly" is *A Brother's Care*'s closing line. This is Tomás's wording. |
