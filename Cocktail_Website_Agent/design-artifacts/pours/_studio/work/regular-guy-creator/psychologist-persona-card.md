# Persona card: The Hidden Talent (regular-guy-creator), Wren, round 1

Sheet: essence "talent in a place you would never expect to find it; appearances can be deceptive". Goal belonging, fear exclusion. Primary fears: exclusion, discourtesy, *individuality*. Secondary fears: status quo, mediocrity, conformity. Example Billy Elliot. Brands IKEA, eBay, KitKat, VW (everyday things with more design in them than you'd guess).

## The one true thing
They've never quite believed they can have both: the thing they're good at, and the people they come from. So they keep the gift quiet in order to keep the people. The Creator half wants to make and be known for it. The Everyman half fears standing apart ("getting ideas", "who do you think you are"). Every sibling here wants to belong. Only this one carries something that could cost them the belonging.

## Hides
- The hours. It gets done where nobody who knows them is watching: late, alone, in another room, under another name, or "just messing about".
- How much it matters to them. If it came up at a family table, they'd change the subject.
- A quiet hope of being caught at it by the right person.

## Fears
- Exclusion from their own: that showing it would make them strange to their people, and their people strange to them. Not the spotlight itself (the Firefighter owns "being singled out feels like being left out").
- Never being known for it at all: dying with it in the drawer.

## Gives
- When it comes out, it changes what everyone around them thought "people like us" could do. They widen the room instead of leaving it.
- Their people get to be proud of something that's theirs.

## Siblings (what keeps each apart)
- Samaritan (*Whoever Comes In*): sees the overlooked others. HT *is* the overlooked one, and it's about their own gift. The Samaritan owns eyes and looking ("Your eyes stop"): no eye/looking motif here.
- Entrepreneur: builds a place. HT has a gift, not a venture.
- Have A Go Hero: untrained, takes something on. HT is the reverse: real skill, long practised, never mentioned.
- Bumpkin: the plain thing vindicated as plain. HT must never turn the plain into "secretly sophisticated": the gift sits inside the ordinary; the ordinary isn't redeemed by it.
- One Next Door: the familiar one, loved for being there. HT wants the gift seen without losing their place.
- Mechanic: a practical make-do fix, visible and useful. HT's gift is expressive and private.
- People's Champion: unnoticed skill called on in a crisis, then steps back. HT wants it seen, and fears it.
- Everyman: collective handed-down know-how. HT's gift is personal.
- Prankster, Scoundrel: far away.
- Creator family neighbour, the Photographer (*As It Was*): owns the shrug, "it's nothing", "making a fuss would feel like showing off". The Craftsman owns "stop saying it was nothing". The Muse owns "put your name on it". The Pioneer owns "tell the next one". Keep all of these out.

## Traps
1. The escape story: the gift as a ticket out of the ordinary. That insults the guest's people and is the reverse of this person.
2. "You're secretly a genius": flattery. Recognise the hiding and the cost, never the brilliance.
3. Condescension: "you'd never expect someone like you". The surprise lives in other people's assumptions, never in the guest's job, looks or background.
4. Imposter-syndrome talk: a diagnosis.
5. Modesty frames owned by siblings (above).
6. Billy Elliot himself: a film about leaving the village. Keep him out of guest text.

## Story test
1. The person was placed somewhere nobody linked to the gift (a job, place or background). On the page.
2. A real, documented gift: what they made, and that it was good. On the page.
3. A sourced moment it's seen. This is an event, so it must be on the page.
4. They kept both: they stayed with, or spoke up for, their own people or place. On the page, or the story isn't this guest's.
5. "Unseen, doubted or kept private at first" is a motive or state: it may be signposted if the page shows the long years without it.
Out: rags-to-riches as the point, genius myths, a gift for drinking, career-changers out of wealth (Joy pdf 86, Zac Doy), and stories where the ordinary job is the indignity (Lorincz and Beke as cleaners, *Proper* p. 220, whose page also carries "hungry" and the "mafia" joke). Coleman, Breaux, Ensslin and Bermejo stay out.

## Verdict on the plan's lead (Nichol, Oxford NICHOL pdf 1388, read whole)
Accept, and more strongly than the plan does. Limbs 1–3 pass: a distillery worker's son, a grain plant and liquid carbon dioxide, then the gin team, master distiller within a year; and after Tanqueray, two gins "to considerable acclaim", so the gift was his and not the brand's. Limb 4 is the heart: he was "a passionate, even irascible advocate" that large-scale distilling doesn't automatically mean compromised quality. He defended the place everyone looks down on. That's this person's essence said about a building: talent where you'd never expect it. And the page says he joined the gin team "there", at Cameronbridge: the gift came up where he already was.
Conditions: never "promoted" or "made good" as the point; never the grain plant as somewhere he escaped; never "genius"; keep "irascible"; keep "credited with"; No. Ten is never his; "nobody connected him with it" is as far as the reading goes (signposted). Asks for Hester: confirm on a page that Tanqueray was made at Cameronbridge in his time; and a page in his own words about the grain years or the move to gin, if one exists. Neither blocks the row.
