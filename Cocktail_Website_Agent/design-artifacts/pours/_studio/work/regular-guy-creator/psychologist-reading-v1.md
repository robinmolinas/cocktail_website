## whoYouAre

There's something you're good at that doesn't match the picture people have of you. A cake nobody believed you'd made. A song you only sing in the car. A drawing on the back of a work envelope. When someone finds out and says "you never said", you laugh and change the subject. Part of you has always believed you had to pick: be good at it, or stay one of them.

The truth is, you want both. And you've got one thing wrong: when it shows, nobody you love thinks you've got above yourself. They're proud it's one of theirs who did it. That's your gift to them, more than the cake or the song: it shows them what someone like them can do.

## yours

1. Tom Nichol grew up in Tullibody, in Scotland, near the distillery where his father worked. He started there at seventeen, screaming and shouting, he says. For over thirty years he did jobs nobody outside a distillery hears about: liquid carbon dioxide, then the plain grain spirit other drinks are built on. It gets little thanks, he once said, because it tastes of nothing and smells of nothing.

2. In 2005 he moved to the gin team at the same distillery, where Tanqueray is made, and within a year he was its master distiller, the person in charge of how it tastes. When he retired in 2015, the gin trade gave him a lifetime award. He called it completely unexpected, and said his father would have been very proud of him.

3. He lived about forty years a couple of miles from where he was born, and he was a passionate, even short-tempered champion of one idea: a big distillery doesn't have to make worse spirit than a small one. I like to think you'd recognise that: the talent turned up right where life had put him, and once it was noticed, he didn't leave the place behind; he stood up for it.

4. The cocktail I've made for you is a gin Old-Fashioned, the classic of spirit, sugar and bitters, built in an ordinary tumbler over ice from the freezer tray. The gin is Tanqueray London Dry, with nothing to cover it. It's said to be made with only four plants: juniper, coriander, angelica and liquorice, the one you notice last, as a hint at the finish. So the sugar comes as a liquorice root syrup, in honour of a talent that was in that distillery long before anyone named it. It adds nothing from outside the bottle; it brings forward what was already there.

5. So show it to the people who knew you first, before strangers. Make the cake for your own table. Sing with the window down on the way to your family's. You'll find out you never had to choose, and they'll get to be proud of something that's theirs too.

## Resonance

- **As the guest:** "Part of you has always believed you had to pick: be good at it, or stay one of them." This is the "how did they know" line. The private one is "you laugh and change the subject". The gift is said out loud ("it shows them what someone like them can do"). The position gives something back: their pride, and the end of the choice.
- **As the sceptic:** Is "something you're good at that doesn't match the picture" true of anyone? Half of it is, which is why the three examples are concrete and the line that lands is the choice, not the talent. No "genius", no flattery of the gift itself. The examples are mixed (a kitchen, a car, a work envelope), with no job or class named as lowly.
- **Siblings:** not *As It Was* (no shrug, no "it's nothing", no "showing off"); not *Not Only the Way* (no "stop saying it was nothing"); not *In Your Own Hand* (no "put your name on it"); not *Whoever Comes In* (no eyes or looking; "people noticed" is said once, about Nichol); not *Anyone Would Have* (no "singled out"). Not the Have A Go Hero (he had the training: thirty years of it).
- **History share:** y1–y3 about 172 of 351 words in yours (49%); y4 is the drink.
- **Name echo (for the room):** whoYouAre says "stay one of them" and "one of theirs"; the name *One of Us* turns that round. I read it as the answer, not a repeat, but say if it spends the line.
