🍸 **Tomás:** Wren, I'm with you on Nichol. Your guard gave me the spark, and Oxford has it on the page. TANQUERAY GORDON & CO. (pdf 1986) says Tanqueray "purportedly" uses just four botanicals: juniper, coriander, angelica and licorice. It describes the London Dry as "sharper with more pine", then "an earthier, richer finish with just a hint of licorice". So the Old-Fashioned's sugar becomes a **licorice root syrup**: a plain 1:1 syrup with 5 g of dried root simmered in it, then strained. That brings out the last thing you taste in the everyday bottle. Nothing is dressed up and nothing comes from outside the gin's own four. Coriander would have been the obvious one, but *Morale Booster* (the Bronx riff) already uses it as "the gin's own botanical", and angelica is *Before the Room*'s anchor. Licorice is free.

**Spec v0** (`_studio/specs/regular-guy-creator.json`):
- 60 ml Tanqueray London Dry, recommended (any juniper-led London dry gin)
- 10 ml licorice root syrup
- 2 dashes Angostura
- built in a plain kitchen tumbler on three or four ordinary freezer-tray cubes, with no garnish

That gets it off "rocks glass, one large cube", and the bottle has nowhere to hide. balance.py (built): **29.9% ABV, 7.00 g sugar, 0% acid. All in range.** The full sweep is in `work/regular-guy-creator/mixologist-sweep-r2.md`:
- At 40%: 27.8%, in range.
- At the US 47.3%: 32.8%, edge. The fix is 50 ml of gin, which lands at 31.9% and 8.1 g.

I kept the sugar under Arnold's 7.6 g on purpose. Licorice root tastes sweeter than its grams of sugar suggest, which is my own knowledge and unsourced.

**Allergens:** contains **nuts**. That's my safe-side call: licorice is a legume, kin to peanut, and dried roots are often packed alongside nuts. So the pour is no longer veto-free, and I'm saying so up front.
