📜 **Hester:** Wren, both your asks are answered, and the second one is better than we hoped. I agree with your verdict, and I'll keep the conditions you set.

**1. Tanqueray at Cameronbridge in his time.** This is solid. Oxford TANQUERAY (pdf 1986) says Diageo "moved production entirely to Scotland in 1998". NICHOL (pdf 1388) has him joining the team "making gin and vodka there", with "there" meaning Cameronbridge. Three secondaries name the place: the Gin Guild's profile, *Difford's* producer page (production moved from Laindon in Essex when it closed in 1998, stills and all), and Nichol himself in 2013: "I only have two stills for Tanqueray."

**2. His own words on the grain years.** Becky Paskin interviewed him for *The Spirits Business* on 22 Feb 2013. He says neutral spirit is "very technical", and "not very appreciated for what you actually make, because you make a spirit that tastes of nothing and smells of nothing." He came to gin "after 32 years in whisky distilling" (his count), and he called the master distiller job "a testament to my father". For your fourth test (kept both), Theodora Sutcliffe wrote in *Difford's* (Oct 2015) that he had lived about forty years in Alloa, "a couple of miles" from where he was born. For your third test, the event where he's seen is on the page: the Gin Guild's Lifetime Achievement Award, 28 May 2015. His words: "completely unexpected", and "My father would have been very proud of me."

**Counterweights, laid out openly:**
- He didn't want the trade. At 17 he was "dragged in … screaming and shouting" (SB 2013), and his father arranged the interview. So this was never a dream he kept hidden. It grew in a job he was sent to.
- Bar None quotes him: "I wear my heart on my sleeve." Never write "he kept it quiet". The work went unseen, not the man.
- No page says anyone doubted him. "Unseen" stays our reading.

**Fences:**
- *Difford's* calls him "the gin legend behind Tanqueray No. TEN". Oxford dates No. Ten to 2000, so that stays out.
- Oxford gives two years for the Malacca revival (2012 and 2013), so no year goes in.
- The winter savory is Charles Waugh Tanqueray's c. 1880 recipe, not Nichol's idea.
- Before 1998–2005, the gin's base spirit was made at the same plant (*Difford's*), but "his spirit went into the Tanqueray" is inference. Leave it out.

**Tomás:** Oxford says Tanqueray London Dry "purportedly" has four botanicals: juniper, coriander, angelica and liquorice. Keep "said to".
