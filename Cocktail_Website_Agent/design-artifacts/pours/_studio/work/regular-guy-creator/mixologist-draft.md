# Tomás: drink draft, regular-guy-creator (The Hidden Talent), v1, round 3

Spec: `_studio/specs/regular-guy-creator.json` (v0, unchanged in this draft). A gin Old-Fashioned built in a plain kitchen tumbler: Tanqueray London Dry, a licorice root syrup, Angostura. The syrup brings forward the licorice that's said to be one of the gin's four botanicals.

**Glassware:** plain kitchen tumbler (about 250 ml), three or four ordinary ice cubes from the freezer tray
**Contains:** nuts (the licorice root, on the safe side; see Allergens)

## Recipe

| amount | item | note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 60 ml | Tanqueray London Dry gin (recommended) | the gin Tom Nichol looked after; or any juniper-led London dry gin, 40% to 47% (at 47% or stronger, use 50 ml) |
| 10 ml (2 teaspoons) | licorice root syrup, homemade (below) | brings forward the hint of licorice that's already in the gin |
| 2 dashes | Angostura bitters | |
| | *for the syrup:* 100 g white sugar, 100 ml water, 5 g dried licorice root (about two finger-length pieces), chopped or snapped small | makes about 160 ml; keeps two weeks in a closed jar in the fridge. Dried licorice root is sold in health-food and tea shops. Check the pack for a nut warning |

## Method

1. Make the syrup: put the sugar, water and licorice root in a small pan and bring it just to a simmer, stirring until the sugar is gone. Let it simmer gently for 5 minutes, then take it off the heat and leave it for 30 minutes. Strain out the root and let the syrup cool.
2. Put the syrup and the bitters in a plain tumbler, then pour in the gin.
3. Add three or four ordinary ice cubes and stir for about 20 seconds, until the outside of the glass feels cold. Then stop: the cubes keep melting as you drink.
4. Nothing goes on top.

**closingLine:** *Make it where everyone is, not in the other room. Someone will ask about the finish.*

## Names

- ***Right Here*** (**my pick**). The gift came up where he already stood: the gin team "there", at the plant he'd worked at since 1993, and a home a couple of miles from where he was born (Hester A3, A6). It recognises the person, not the gin: the fear is that showing the gift costs you your place, and the name says you can keep it. It's easy to order ("Right Here, please"), and it's in no pour.
- ***Just a Hint***: Oxford's words for the licorice in the finish (pdf 1986), and a quiet nod to a talent that only shows at the end. But it captions the drink more than the person, and it leans towards the "it's nothing" shrug that *As It Was* owns.
- ***The Fourth One***: the fourth botanical, last on Oxford's list and last on the palate. Intriguing out loud, but it needs the reading to unlock it.
- ***Two Stills***: Nichol's own "I only have two stills for Tanqueray" (Hester, SB13). The big-distillery argument in two words, but it's about the plant, not the guest, and "still" puts a piece of kit in the title.
- *Set aside:* *Close to Home* ("Home" is in *Brought Home*); *Default* (Oxford's "seen by some as default", pdf 1987, is lovely in the reading but cold as a name).
