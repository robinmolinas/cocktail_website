# Persona card: The Makeover Artist (creator-magician)

Wren, round 1. Source: `persona.py creator-magician`. Siblings read against: the Creator ten (registry + drafts), and the Magician ten for the reverse and near pairings (the Genie, the Fairy Godmother, the Alchemist).

## Core truth
They can't look at something without seeing what it could be, and what they want at the end isn't "I'd never have recognised it". It's "that's so *them*". They change everything so a thing looks more like itself.

## Shows / hides
- **Shows:** the eye, the energy, the before-and-after. The friend who re-does your flat in a weekend, knows what haircut you should get, finds the chair on the pavement and makes it the best thing in the room. Bright, quick, generous with the vision.
- **Hides:** how much the reveal matters to them. The second when someone sees it and goes quiet is the whole reason. They'd rather be called "talented" than admit they did it for that face. They also hide the hours: it's supposed to look like magic, so nobody sees the sanding.

## Hidden fear → hidden gift
- **Fear:** that it doesn't hold. The room slides back, the person goes back to the old haircut, the thing goes back to how it was (Magician: inertia, unintended results; Creator: status quo). Underneath: that they changed something nobody asked them to change. Sometimes the other person heard "not yet good enough" where they meant "look what's in you".
- **Gift:** they look at people and things as if the good version is already in there and just needs getting out. Most people have never been looked at that way. After a makeover, the person is seen differently, by everyone, including themselves.

## The "real me" line to aim for
- A: *"You don't want to hear 'I'd never have recognised it.' You want to hear 'that's so you'."*
- B: *"You'll change everything about a room so it looks more like the people who live in it."* (scene)
- C: *"You've never seen a sad old chair. You've seen one that's waiting."* (lighter, maybe the tagline's ground)

## Truth vs. legend
The true story, with a before and an after both on the record. They love a reveal, but a real one: the before has to be real for the after to count. No myth, no disguise.

## Tone
Bright, quick, a bit delighted. Enchanting, not solemn (tangerine, sky blue, sunshine). Warmth with some sparkle. The hard truth, if we use it, told lightly and with affection.

## Distinct from siblings
- **The Photographer** (As It Was) keeps things as they are ("The colour was always there"; lost colour restored). The Makeover Artist *changes* everything. Guard: no restoration story, no "it was always there".
- **The Minimalist** (Half a Rim) takes away until it's true; its story is already "a bartender refused the mediocre Margarita". Guard: no "rescued a bad drink" shape; this one adds, bolds, transforms.
- **The Sculptor** (Quite Alive) makes for one person, quietly, and vanishes. The Makeover Artist wants to be there for the reveal.
- **The Surrealist** (The Other Berry) notices things are already strange. Guard: not "already are".
- **The Genie** (reverse pairing): instant, anything you wish, from nothing. The Makeover Artist works on what's there, and it takes hours that are hidden.
- **The Fairy Godmother** (magician-caregiver): a disguise that ends at midnight. The Makeover Artist's after is not a disguise; it's truer than the before.

## Traps
- The TV makeover cliché ("unrecognisable", "extreme", tears at the reveal) as the point.
- A colour-changing drink (butterfly pea, pH trick): a performance, and the obvious one. No drink you perform.
- "Hidden brilliance" / "potential" as praise: horoscope. Say what they *do*.
- Makeover as fixing people ("you improve everyone"): insults the guest's friends and misreads the guest.
- Sibling motifs: colour restored, "always there", subtraction, "smallest thing", sugar cube, "don't stir".
