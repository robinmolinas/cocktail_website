🍸 **Tomás:** Wren, before you give us the person, here's a lead from the glass side. A clarified milk punch. The makeover in it is chemistry, not a trick. You pour a good sour into milk and it curdles, so for an hour it looks ruined. Then it runs through a coffee filter overnight and comes out clear, and softer than what went in. The milk protein grabs the harsh, tannic stuff and leaves with the curds, and the whey stays behind and makes it silky (*Liquid Intelligence* pp. 263-267). It all happens in the kitchen the night before, so there's nothing to perform at the table. It's makeable with a jug, a sieve and a paper filter. For the spark, the *Flavor Matrix* gives tamarind as a surprise pairing for dairy (pdf 104). Brown sticky pulp, the least glamorous thing in the pantry, and its sour is part of what breaks the milk. First sketch, v0b: 60 aged rum / 20 tamarind water / 10 lime / 10 demerara / 15 cold black tea / 30 whole milk. `balance.py` on the freeform style reads 16.6% / 8.17 g / 0.97% before the wash. The tool can't model the milk breaking, so I worked out the after by hand: ~16.8% / 8.3 g / 0.81%. That puts it inside Arnold's sour ranges, at the sweet end, and it will taste drier than that because some of the sugar is lactose. Without the lime, tamarind alone lands flat (~0.67% acid), so the lime stays. `allergens.py`: dairy. The whey gets through the filter, so it isn't veto-free. I added a `tamarind_water` row, estimated at 20 g sugar and 4% acid, flagged as unsourced, and it touches no veto. Brown should come out amber, since the wash strips colour too, but that's my expectation, not proof. Hester, Franklin's 1763 milk-punch letter is on LI p. 267. Anything older or truer is yours.
