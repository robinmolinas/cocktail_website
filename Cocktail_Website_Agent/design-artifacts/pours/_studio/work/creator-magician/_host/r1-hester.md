📜 **Hester:** Wren, Tomás, I'll open with a hunt, since the Makeover Artist doesn't invent from nothing. They see what something could be and remake it. My pick is **Toby Cecchini and the Cosmopolitan**. A drink called the Cosmopolitan was already going round San Francisco. A 1987 *Chronicle* writer called it "a Kamikaze with cranberry juice". At the Odeon, most likely in 1988, Cecchini "didn't think much of it" and began tinkering. Oxford's word for what he did is that he *re-engineered* it: fresh lime instead of bottled Rose's, Cointreau instead of cheap triple sec, and the new Absolut Citron (Oxford, COSMOPOLITAN, pdf 585; *A Proper Drink* pp. 42–43). It was the staff drink. Then customers started asking for it, and he watched other bars make it "wrong and badly". His own summing-up is that he invented "the version everyone means when they order it" (Proper p. 43, from his 2004 memoir). A decade later the craft bars refused to stock cranberry juice so they'd never have to make it (Oxford pdf 586). So it's a before-and-after, and it also holds the fear on the record: the makeover got away from him and went mediocre in other people's hands. Three guards. "Invented" is never in our voice. The year is "most likely". And what was in the original is contested: the newspaper says cranberry, Cecchini says grenadine. So "he swapped in the cranberry" can only go in as his account, signposted. Tomás, the cranberry is also *Rosetta*'s, so it needs a reason or it needs to go. My runner-up is **clarified milk punch**. Scalding milk curdles the punch, which looks ruined, and strained back through its own curds it comes out clear and keeps for years (Oxford, MILK PUNCH, pdf 1305). Dave Arnold rediscovered it while rescuing a drink "we all agreed" wasn't very good (*Liquid Intelligence* p. 263). It's lovely, but there's no person in it, and clarity is already *Half a Rim*'s clear ice and *Rosetta*'s cloud.
