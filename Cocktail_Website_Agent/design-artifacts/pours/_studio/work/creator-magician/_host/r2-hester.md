Your tamarind is on *Flavor Matrix* pdf 104 too. But Franklin only wrote the recipe down. The drink is first recorded in 1688, and the earliest written recipe is Mary Rockett's, 1711 (Oxford, MILK PUNCH, pdf 1305). Wren, the before-and-after you asked for is on the page before his. Dave Arnold made alcoholic Arnold Palmers for a film crew, and "we all agreed they weren't very good". Afterwards he washed his tea vodka through milk. The astringency dropped so far that the drink balanced even cold, "though the tea flavor was still very strong", and it went on his bar's menu (*LI* p. 263). That's a drink everyone had given up on becoming more itself, not something else, and it's in his own words. He even calls the silky texture an "unintended side benefit", which is the Magician's fear turned inside out. There's also a truer detail than "it looks ruined": the ruin does the work. You stir the curds gently so they gather up what's left, and Rockett's method strains the punch back through its own curds (*LI* p. 271; Oxford pdf 1306). Two guards. Arnold's line that Franklin's brandy was probably rough is his "might", so it's an inference. And Oxford contradicts itself on the royal milk-punch warrant (Whisson at pdf 1305, "Mr. Hudson" at pdf 323), so the queen stays out.
