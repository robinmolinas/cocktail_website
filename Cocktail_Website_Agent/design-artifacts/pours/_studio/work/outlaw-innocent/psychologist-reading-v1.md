## whoYouAre

Most people assume you're against things: rules, habits, whatever everyone else has quietly agreed to call normal. That isn't quite it. You notice when something's put on, and once you've noticed, you can't unsee it.

Underneath sits a promise, made a long time ago, about the person you'd never turn into. You check on it more often than anyone would guess. Being told off has never scared you. What scares you is the small, reasonable yes that changes you a little, then a little more, until you're someone you once swore you'd never be.

You never got used to it, and the people near you are better off for it. When a room nods along to something that isn't true, you say so, and suddenly people remember what they meant before they stopped noticing. That counts for a great deal.

## yours

1. Chartreuse comes from the French Alps. It's a liqueur of herbs, made under the eye of the Carthusian monks, an order that lives in solitude and prayer. By around 2021 it had probably never been more popular. The new wave of cocktail bars had made it a favourite, and everyone wanted more.

2. So, quietly, the monks decided to make less. In January 2023 they told the people who sell it that from then on there would only be so much to go round. Their letter said they had no wish to make more than the order needed to live, and one reason they gave was to protect the life they'd chosen. Shelves emptied. Bartenders who'd built drinks around it had to find stand-ins.

3. If you're suspecting a clever way to make a bottle more wanted, I wondered that too, at first. But they put the reason in writing, to the very people who were asking for more. Nobody was wrong to love it. I like to think the monks simply wouldn't turn into a bigger version of themselves, one with no room left for the life they'd chosen. It's the same no I hear in you. It isn't aimed at anyone. It's for something.

4. So I built your cocktail the Old-Fashioned way, on Armagnac, the oldest of France's brandies, and the Chartreuse goes in by the spoonful, never more. That's my nod to the monks: it has exactly what it needs. Dark, dry brandy with a green, herbal edge, a drink that's sure of what it is. If the bottle is hard to find, that's the monks' doing, and it's the point.

5. Here's my side of it. Keep the promise. It's done more good than anyone's told you. But when you next say no, add what it's for, out loud, the way the monks did. People will always argue with a no. It's much harder to argue with what you're protecting. And then you're known for what you love, not only for what you refuse.
