# Persona card: The Adolescent (outlaw-innocent), Wren, round 1

## The one true thing
Their rebellion is a promise to themselves: *I will not turn into that.* The noise (the door, the hair, the "no") is the outside. Inside is a belief that life could be more honest than this, and a running check on whether they're still keeping the promise. No sibling's fight is with their own future self.

## Hidden / fear / gift
- **Hidden:** the hope. People see someone against everything; underneath, they still believe. The "no" protects a "yes" they rarely say out loud. Also hidden: the quiet check ("am I starting to become it?").
- **Fear:** being bought. Not punishment, not rules: the small yes that changes them until they "stop being what they were". Powerlessness = losing the one power they're sure of, their refusal.
- **Gift:** they never got used to it. They say "but that isn't true" when the room nods along, and remind the rest of us what we meant before we stopped noticing.
- **Position (draft):** the promise isn't only a no. Let people see what you're *for*, out loud, as loudly as what you refuse. (Gain: the power of being for something.)

## Siblings (kept apart)
- Rule Breaker (*Asked In*): did it before permission; quality, later recognised. Adolescent: refuses the offer; it's about the self, not the work.
- Maverick (*Can't Watch*): someone else's fight, injustice. Adolescent: their own integrity.
- Gangster (*Where You Stand*): knows how things get done, draws a line. Adolescent hasn't made peace with how things get done.
- Thrill Seeker (*Whoopee*): risk, own idea. Gentle Giant (*In One Piece*): restraint of power.
- Unauthored: Subverter (mockery), Fugitive (runs for love), Disrupter (overturns assumptions), Leading Edge (ahead first), Devil's Advocate (argues the other side).
- **Cross-family risk:** Perfectionist (*On Their Behalf*: turning things down, standards), Minimalist (*Half a Rim*: fewer things), Craftsman ("doing it properly"). Vogler's curation lives there. The reading uses his refusal to be bought, not his standards.

## Traps
- Never "young", "a phase", "grow out of it", "teenager". The guest may be fifty.
- No sneer at adults or at anyone who took the deal. The story is his promise, not their failure.
- Not purity as snobbery ("my way or the highway", "challenge you to like them").
- Imagery (hair dye, slammed doors) stays out of the reading.
- Registry: *Fair Measure* owns "never against"; *Not Only the Way* owns "doing it properly".

## Story test
1. At the start, refuses what the grown-ups (the establishment of their world) offered, because it was false.
2. Is right about something honest.
3. Pays for it.
4. The story shows whether the honesty held later.
Out: drugs, death, sneering at adults, punk and the King's Road.
