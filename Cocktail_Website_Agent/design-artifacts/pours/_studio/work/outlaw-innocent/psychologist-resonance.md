Read twice: as the guest (on a phone, no drink in hand), then as a sceptic holding the five authored Outlaw siblings (*In One Piece*, *Asked In*, *Where You Stand*, *Whoopee*, *Can't Watch*), read in full this step.

## 3. Swap test: four flags
1. **The opener frame is the family's.** "People assume you were born gentle" (*In One Piece*), "People think you're chasing the rush. It's plainer than that." (*Whoopee*), "People put it down to nerve… It's neither." (*Can't Watch*). My "Most people assume… That isn't quite it" is the fourth. **Fix:** open on a scene instead.
2. **The fear is *Asked In*'s.** "It's the yes you don't trust… as if being let in were the first step to being owned." My "small, reasonable yes that changes you" is the same shape. The Adolescent's fear is different: no one is letting them in; it's their own good reasons, over years, doing it. Keep apart from *Whoopee* too ("the life that gets planned for you a little more each year"): theirs is imposed, ours is self-made. **Fix:** no "yes", no "a little at a time", no one else doing it.
3. **The gift's last line is the family formula.** "That matters more than you'd guess" (*Where You Stand*), "that matters more than you let yourself think" (*Can't Watch*), "That's no small thing" (*Whoopee*), "it matters to far more people than ever say so" (*Asked In*). Mine is "That counts for a great deal." **Fix:** say the value concretely.
4. **y3's turn echoes *Asked In*'s y5** ("Make something that isn't against anything") with my "It isn't aimed at anyone." **Fix:** reword the turn. Also y5's "Keep the promise. It's done more good…" sits near *Whoopee*'s "I'd never tell you to stop jumping", and "out loud" near *Can't Watch*'s "where people can see": trim both.

Position check against siblings: *Where You Stand* ends on letting someone see your line (what you won't do). Mine ends on naming what the no protects (what you love). The turn, for rather than against, is distinct; v2 leans on it harder.
