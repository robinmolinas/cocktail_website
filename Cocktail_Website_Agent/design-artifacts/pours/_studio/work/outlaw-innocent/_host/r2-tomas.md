It isn't Bar Agricole, and it isn't Vogler's own words. Adkins, on the same page, says everything had to have "a sense of place and time and a link to agriculture".

So here's my lead: the Armagnac's own sweetener. Floc de Gascogne is Gascony's grape juice mixed with young Armagnac. The law says both have to come from the Armagnac area, "often coming from the same property", and the name comes from the Occitan for "bouquet of flowers" (Oxford pdf 818). It goes in as the one change to the Codex Old-Fashioned: a rich syrup made with white Floc in place of water. The drink stays dry, the way his drinks are (p. 179), and the little sweetness in it comes from the same grapes and the same place as the spirit. That's your hidden "yes", carried by the same grape as the "no".

I've only done the arithmetic by hand so far, no spec. On the built ranges, 60 ml Armagnac at 40% with 7.5 ml Floc syrup comes out at 29.5% ABV, 8.3-8.5 g sugar and 0.02% acid, which is in range but at the sweet top. At 43% the strength is on the edge, and at 46% it's OUT, so the spec will need a strength ceiling and a slightly smaller dose of syrup. Pouring the Floc in as a modifier fails: at 20 ml the acid goes OUT (0.095%), and as the only sweetener you'd need about 118 ml of it.
