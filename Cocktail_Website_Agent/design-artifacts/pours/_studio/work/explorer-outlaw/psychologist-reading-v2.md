name: Fine by Me
tagline: Most people hear "only if" as a no. You hear a way in.
epigraph: The bourbon in this glass owes a lot to someone's leftover bacon fat, and a joke.

## whoYouAre

Somebody once told you, "You can, if you do all of it yourself." They thought that would be the end of it. You said fine, and spent your own evenings on it without a word of complaint, because a hard yes is still a yes. You ask for odd things too, half as a joke, and you're often the only one who comes away with them.

You suspected the "only if" was meant to put you off. You took it anyway, in plain sight, with a grin. What frightens you comes later, when the thing you made becomes the thing you're known for, and everyone expects you to keep making it. But here's what your stubbornness gives people: because you carried it the first time, nobody after you has to. They get the yes for free. That's a generous thing to leave behind.

## yours

1. Don Lee came to bartending from a job in IT. Eben Freeman, a New York barman, told him about fat-washing: stir a melted fat into a spirit, freeze it, lift the fat away, and the flavour stays behind. You may have heard Lee invented it. He didn't.
2. A regular at PDT, the bar where Lee worked, had a lot of bacon fat and no idea what to do with it. Lee asked about it, as a joke at first, then asked for some. When he took the idea to Jim Meehan, who ran the bar, Meehan was wary, and fairly: infusions take staff time. Lee could put it on the menu if he did all the infusions himself. In Meehan's words, "in true Don Lee fashion, he did." Early on, Freeman tried Lee's version and said it was better than his own. It became the bar's best-selling drink, and it set off a whole run of fat-washed drinks, made with nuts, chorizo, even olive oil.
3. Whatever Meehan expected, I like to think Lee heard a yes, and got to work. So would you. The rest of it comes later. Lee is proud of the drink, but it nags at him too: he once said it's the one that will probably follow him around for the rest of his life. Years on, an environmental cocktail group even held it up as a drink that wastes nothing. He allowed you could see it that way, but said it was nowhere near what they'd been thinking. I'd guess you know that feeling, of watching something you started go places you never meant it to.
4. The cocktail I've made for you starts where his did: bourbon washed with bacon fat, the way his recipe is printed, with Benton's bacon from Tennessee and Four Roses bourbon if you can get them, or a smoky dry-cured bacon and any straight bourbon. You make it yourself, so the washing is yours to do: warm the fat, stir it into the bourbon, wait four hours, freeze it for two, lift the fat off and strain. A little maple, Angostura bitters and a twist of orange peel, as in his Old-Fashioned. Then I take it somewhere his recipe doesn't go: a tall glass packed with ice, topped with a crisp, dry sparkling cider, so the apple meets the smoke and the maple. I did that as a nod to everyone who took his idea their own way.
5. So let it follow you. Being known for something isn't a fence around you. It's the mark you meant to leave, even if you'd never say so, and it carries on without you holding it up. Walk on to the next odd thing whenever you like, and be glad, out loud, that you made this one.

## Resonance

- **As the guest (a Trailblazer).** The tagline intrigues me without a single "rule": it's the moment I'd recognise from my own life. The epigraph, read cold, is a small story about leftovers and a joke, and it makes me want the rest. whoYouAre's "a hard yes is still a yes" is the line that lands; "you suspected the 'only if' was meant to put you off" is the thing I'd never say. The fear (becoming the thing I'm known for) is mine, and it's named before the gift, which is said out loud: nobody after me has to carry it. In the story I see myself in two places, the hard yes taken (y2–y3) and the drink that went where its maker never meant (y3). The drink makes me do the washing, which is the story's hard yes, tied in words to everyone who took the idea their own way. y5 doesn't ask me to stop moving; it gives me the mark back as mine.
- **As a sceptic.** Barnum watch: "a hard yes is still a yes" alone could fit the Auteur (*Making the Calls*) or the Craftsman, so the joke-asking and "in plain sight, with a grin" hold it to this person. Sibling swaps: *Nothing to It* (no fear, no shrug, no "followed", no kept-inside-then-sold: Freeman's "never served it" is out); *Still Yours* (nothing about liking or dropping; y5 says walk on *and* be glad); *Beside the First* (no "change one thing"); *Left Standing* (no "there", no "take someone"); *Off-Label* / *Before the Room* (no door); *On One Condition* ("condition" appears nowhere in guest text); *Four Shares* (no "You're allowed to"). Fences held: no speakeasy, no "first", no "trail-blazing", no Meehan as jailer, Freeman credited, no "no stripes", no year, no drink in hand (y5 points to life). Every he/his in yours is Don Lee, Freeman or Meehan by name in the same sentence. Allergy: the drink contains spice (Benton's black-pepper cure) and pork; the reading names neither as a warning, the dossier does.

And when it starts following you around, let it.* It keeps Tomás's act and carries y5's position in Lee's own word (follow, p. 276), without "calling it after you" (the drink carries Benton's name, not Lee's) or "argue".
