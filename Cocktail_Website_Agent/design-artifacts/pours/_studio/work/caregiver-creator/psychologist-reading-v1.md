## whoYouAre

You have a way of doing things. How a shelf goes up, how a sauce is started, how the spare bed gets made for someone coming to stay. There's an order to it, and you keep to it when nobody's watching. Especially then. Whoever sleeps in that bed will never know about the corner you tucked twice. They'll just sleep well, and that's enough for you.

What you rarely say out loud is how proud you are of it. Pride feels like making it about you, so you call it "just doing it properly" and give the credit to the thing. And once or twice, explaining your way, you've watched someone hear "my way is better". You never meant that. You meant: this is the way I know how to look after you.

## yours

1. Kazuo Uyeda began working behind a bar in Tokyo in 1966, and in 1997 opened his own, Tender, in Ginza, a district where a drink can take a quarter of an hour to arrive. He spent years on a way of shaking that sends the ice round the shaker in a pattern instead of back and forth. He called it the hard shake: hard to master, not hard on the arm. He wrote that the West looks at results, while he and his colleagues "respect the process".

2. When he showed it in New York, what people remember him saying is "Nobody can do the hard shake but me", and they heard a boast. The man who brought him over says he meant something humbler: "I can't do your shake. I can do my shake." He told a colleague what it was for: fine bubbles that make a drink "mellow and easy to drink". And he added, "I have my way, but it is not only the way." Later, bartenders tested it and found that a good, vigorous shake of any style makes much the same drink.

3. I don't read that as the end of his story. I like to think it's the heart of it. The care was never only in the drink. It was in holding himself to his way for whoever sat down in front of him, and never asking anyone else to do the same. That's you. Proud of how you do it, and happy for everyone else to do it their way.

4. His bar's signature was a Gimlet, "velvety" and jade-green. Yours isn't his: it's gin and a lime cordial you make yourself. Lime cordial was first made to keep lime juice good on long sea voyages. Yours is fresh lime, its zest, sugar and a spoonful of caraway seeds, the ones in rye bread, warmed and left to steep for twenty minutes. Nobody will name the caraway. It's just why the lime tastes rounder. Made once, it waits in the fridge for the next six people. Then you shake it however you shake. Your way.

5. So I'll say the thing you'd rather I didn't. Next time someone thanks you for something you made and you hear yourself say "oh, it was nothing", stop. Tell them how you made it: the order, the part you did twice. It isn't boasting. It's the only part of the gift they'd never know to ask about.
