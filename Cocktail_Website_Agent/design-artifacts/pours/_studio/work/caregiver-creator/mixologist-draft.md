## Recipe

- **serves:** 1 (the cordial makes about 300 ml, enough for six drinks)
- **glassware:** a coupe (a stemmed glass with a rounded bowl), chilled
- **contains:** `[]`

| amount | item | note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 60 ml | London dry gin (40% or stronger) | |
| 45 ml | lime and caraway cordial, homemade (below) | the part nobody sees, and the whole of the balance |
| | *for the cordial:* 150 ml fresh lime juice, 90 g sugar, 90 ml water, the zest of 4 limes, 1 tsp caraway seeds | some London dry gin makers used caraway the same way: a little, to accent the flavour, not change it |

## Method
1. Make the cordial first, a day or an hour ahead. Peel the zest from 4 limes, just the green part. Squeeze the limes, and more if you need them, to get 150 ml of juice.
2. Put the sugar, the water, the zest and the caraway seeds in a small pan. Warm it gently, stirring, until the sugar has disappeared. Don't let it boil.
3. Take it off the heat, stir in the lime juice and leave it for 20 minutes.
4. Strain it through a fine sieve into a clean bottle and keep it in the fridge. It's best within a week.
5. When it's time, put a coupe in the freezer, or fill it with ice and water while you mix.
6. Pour the gin and the cordial into a shaker, fill it with ice and shake hard for about 10 seconds, whatever way your arms like to shake.
7. Empty the glass if you filled it, and strain the drink in. No garnish.

(Method note: adding the lime juice off the heat keeps it fresh-tasting; only the sugar, zest and seeds are warmed.)

## If the room lands on Sheridan (my case against, for the record)
- **The step Wren is counting on isn't his.** Warming the glass with hot water and pouring it away is every hot drink's first step. The *Codex* does it for its own coffee cocktail ("Warm a coffee or toddy mug by filling it with boiling water", p. 268). So the unseen care would be the genre's, not the Craftsman's.
- **The spark.** The Irish Coffee is a classic that only works one way: coffee, whiskey, sugar, and cream floated on top. A spark would have to change the one recipe the story says people are trying to get back to ("the way Sheridan did", Oxford pdf 1077). The story would be arguing with the drink.
- **Dairy.** That's fair on its own (*Down the Line* carries it), but with the other two it tips the balance.
- I'd build it if the room chooses it, and it would pass the hot ranges. I just don't think the glass would carry this person.
