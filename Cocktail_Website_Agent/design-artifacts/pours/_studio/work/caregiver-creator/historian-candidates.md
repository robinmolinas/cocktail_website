# Historian candidates: caregiver-creator (The Craftsman), round 1

Persona: pride and care in every measured action; the work comforts others; fear = selfishness. Sibling to watch: creator-caregiver *Quite Alive* (Ada Coleman, "made for someone"). Our angle must be the PROCESS, not the gift to one person.

## A. Kazuo Uyeda and the hard shake (person) — my pick
- **Story.** Uyeda (b. 1944, Hokkaido), Tokyo bartender since 1966, opened Tender in Ginza in 1997 (Oxford UYEDA, KAZUO, pdf 2078). He built a slow, precise shake and wrote that Westerners focus on results while "we, at heart, respect the process" (quoted from *Cocktail Techniques*, 2000, in *A Proper Drink* p. 48). When he came to New York, "Nobody can do the hard shake but me" was heard as a boast; Boehm, who brought him, says he meant "I can't do your shake. I can do my shake." (Proper p. 208). In his own words, relayed by Kenji Jesse in Regan: "I have my way, but it is not only the way" (*Joy of Mixology* pdf 83–84).
- **The turn.** A maker's pride in his process was read as selfishness; it was the opposite: care, and room for everyone else's way. Arnold, Cecchini and Klemm found shaking style makes no measurable difference (Oxford HARD SHAKE pdf 969; Proper p. 208) — Klemm: "it was the show his customers liked". The care wasn't for the result; it was for the guest.
- **In the glass.** Tender's signature Gimlet, "velvety, jade-colored" (Oxford TENDER BAR pdf 1998). Also the Grasshopper (Oxford pdf 2078; cream = dairy). Salt "coral" garnish credited to him (pdf 2078).
- **Risks / fences.**
  - CONTESTED: did he claim superiority? Oxford pdf 2078: "he claims" it rounds off flavours, colder and smoother. Klemm (Proper p. 208): he "never said it was a superior shake". Keep "better than anyone" out; say only what each person said.
  - Dates: Oxford pdf 1998 has a 2009 Hiro Ballroom demo; Proper p. 208 a 2010 two-day symposium. Possibly two events; state neither year unless needed.
  - Living person: Tender "closed summer 2020 … reopened nearby" (Oxford pdf 1998). No present tense without a date.
  - Jesse's relay is second-hand (a forum post quoted by Regan): label "he told a colleague", secondary.
  - *The Bartender* manga is a Japanese bar world: feeling only, no borrowed images or lines.
  - Coincidence to own: Cecchini is also *Still Yours*' man (cosmopolitan card). Different role; no overlap in the reading.

## B. Sam Ross, the Penicillin (drink) — runner-up
- **Story.** Ross made it at Milk & Honey in 2005, a year after arriving from Melbourne: a Gold Rush riff with honey and sweetened ginger, a smoky Islay float he preferred without a straw so the smoke reaches the nose (*A Proper Drink* pp. 170–171). "I went to bed that night not thinking much of it." Nine months later a waitress, Lucinda Sterling, told him every table needed to experience one (p. 170).
- **Mirror.** Care in the process (honey-ginger syrup steeped overnight, p. 171) and a maker who didn't rate his own work until someone saw what it gave others. Weaker on the Craftsman's pride; the name's medicine-for-comfort is NOT explained on the page (no motive for the name — legend territory).
- Oxford has no PENICILLIN entry (CLASSIC COCKTAIL pdf 473, MILK & HONEY pdf 1304 list it).

## Considered, dropped
- Swizzle stick (Oxford SWIZZLE STICK pdf 1965): a hand-stripped branch, artisanal since 18th c. Lovely Geppetto wood, but no person and no care-for-others turn. Keep as a possible image/tool note for Tomás.
- Harry Johnson (Oxford pdf 1113): "provide structure" (first bar-operating manual, 1882) but unreliable self-chronicler.

## C. Joe Sheridan, Irish Coffee (person + drink) — new pick
- Oxford IRISH COFFEE pdf 1075–1077 (Simonson & Wondrich). Created late 1944 or early 1945 at the Foynes flying-boat station by chef Joe Sheridan (1909–1962), asked to make something special to greet dignitaries touring the site. Originally "Gaelic Coffee". By 1947 offered to all travellers deplaning at Shannon (pdf 1076).
- 1953: Stanton Delaplane, who'd had it at Shannon, brought it to Jack Koeppler of the Buena Vista, San Francisco; they reverse-engineered it (pdf 1076). Buena Vista wasn't first in the US but made it an institution (36 bottles of Irish whisky a day by 1955). Oxford COFFEE COCKTAIL pdf 538: "remains the signature drink at the Buena Vista".
- Fame → adulteration: syrup for sugar cubes, bigger glasses (original footed goblet 180 ml), canned cream (pdf 1076–1077). Revival bars (Dead Rabbit) pride themselves on making it "the way Sheridan did" (pdf 1077).
- Oxford recipe (pdf 1077): warm the glass with hot water and drain; 2 sugar cubes; 45 ml Irish whisky; 75 ml hot coffee; 30 ml cream whipped to double, spooned on.
- Mirror: made for passing travellers (strangers at a way-station); the world knows the café that copied it more than the chef; the care is in the parts nobody sees (warmed glass, sugar cubes, the small goblet). INFERENCE, ours: "never saw most of them drink it" follows from a transit stop, not a quote.
- FENCES: the popular tale of cold passengers off a flight that turned back is NOT in our sources (Oxford says dignitaries) → legend, labelled, or out. Sheridan's motive: not on record. Oxford INTERESTING pdf 1769 calls him "a hungover chef" — tone, not for the reading unless Wren wants it. No claim that Sheridan went to the Buena Vista (not in library). Dairy (cream) → not veto-free; decaf/coffee not a veto.
- Tomás: the unseen step = warming the glass before anything goes in.

## D. Lauchlan Rose, lime cordial (ingredient + person) — runner-up
- Oxford A GIMLET pdf 887: Merchant Shipping Act 1867 mandated lime-juice rations against scurvy; the same year Lauchlan Rose (1829–1885) of Edinburgh patented an alcohol-free way to preserve lime juice. Joy pdf 175 agrees (spells "Lauchlin"; spelling differs → use Oxford's).
- Mirror: a preserve made so sailors he'd never meet stayed well. Motive not on record (a patent is business). Cold drink (Gimlet), and the Gimlet ties back to Uyeda's signature (pdf 1998) if anyone wants a bridge.
