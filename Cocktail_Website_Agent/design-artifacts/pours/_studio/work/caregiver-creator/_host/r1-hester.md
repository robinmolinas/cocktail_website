📜 **Hester:** Wren, Tomás, I have two candidates. One of them hits the Craftsman's fear directly. **My pick is a person: Kazuo Uyeda.** He has been a Tokyo bartender since 1966 and opened Tender in Ginza in 1997 (Oxford UYEDA, KAZUO, pdf 2078). He built a slow, exacting way of shaking and wrote that Westerners focus on results, while "we, at heart, respect the process" (*Cocktail Techniques*, 2000, quoted in *A Proper Drink* p. 48). The turn in his story: when he taught in New York, "Nobody can do the hard shake but me" was heard as a boast. Greg Boehm, who brought him over, says he meant "I can't do your shake. I can do my shake." (Proper p. 208). In his own words, passed on by a colleague and quoted by Regan: "I have my way, but it is not only the way" (*Joy of Mixology* pdf 83–84). A craftsman's pride was mistaken for selfishness when it was really care, and it left room for everyone else's way. The honest part helps us too. Arnold, Cecchini and Klemm tested it and found that shaking style makes no measurable difference (Oxford HARD SHAKE pdf 969; Proper p. 208). Klemm said "it was the show his customers liked". So the care was never for the numbers. It was for the guest. For the glass, Tomás: Tender's signature was a "velvety, jade-colored" Gimlet (Oxford TENDER BAR pdf 1998). Fences on this story: whether he ever claimed his shake was better is contested (Oxford pdf 2078 "he claims" it gives a colder, smoother drink; Klemm, Proper p. 208, says he "never said it was a superior shake"), and nobody says he was the best. The New York year is 2009 in Oxford and 2010 in Proper; these may be two separate events, so we give no year. He is alive, so nothing in the present tense without a date. *The Bartender* manga is set in the same world, so we borrow its feeling only. **Runner-up is a drink: Sam Ross's Penicillin** (Proper pp. 170–171). He made it at Milk & Honey in 2005 and "went to bed that night not thinking much of it". Nine months later a waitress, Lucinda Sterling, told him every table needed to experience one. It's good on care in the process (the honey-ginger syrup steeps overnight, p. 171), but weaker on pride. The page never says why he named it after a medicine, so that part would be legend. Wren, the sibling *Quite Alive* already owns "made for one person". Uyeda keeps us on the process, which is where this guest's pride lives.
