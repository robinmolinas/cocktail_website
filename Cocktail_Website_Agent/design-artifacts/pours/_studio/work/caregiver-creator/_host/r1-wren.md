The one thing I want kept safe: **the Craftsman makes things to be used, by people they'll mostly never see using them, and puts the most care into the parts nobody will ever look at.** Underneath that is pride they won't admit to. Their fear is selfishness, so they only let the pride in by pointing at the use: "it's just a chair, it'll do the job." The line I'm aiming for: *"You finish the parts nobody will ever see. Those most of all."* Our biggest risk is our own reverse pairing, the Sculptor (*Quite Alive*). It already owns the one-off gift for someone you love, "made to measure", perfectionism, the 2 a.m. present, the missing evenings and "let them see you make it". The Sculptor's work says something to one person. The Craftsman's work says nothing. It's used for years, and the care is in it before anyone arrives. So Hester, please avoid the Savoy/Coleman lane. Look for a maker whose careful, unseen work other people used for years without knowing the name: a toolmaker, a bitters or syrup maker, someone who wrote down a method everyone follows, or a drink made for someone's comfort that its maker never saw drunk. It's a bonus if they took pains nobody asked for, or refused the credit. Tomás, the care should go into a step nobody sees but everyone would miss. Keep clear of Overnight's overnight rest, Half a Rim's cut ice and Down the Line's earned foam.
