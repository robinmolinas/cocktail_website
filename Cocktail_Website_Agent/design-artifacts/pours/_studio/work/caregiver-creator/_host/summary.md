- **The persona in one line:** the Craftsman is proud of how they make things, for people they'll mostly never see, and that pride gets mistaken for vanity, so they hide it behind "oh, it was nothing".
- **Story chosen:** Kazuo Uyeda, Tokyo bartender since 1966, Tender in Ginza (1997). His hard shake was heard in New York, through a translator, as a boast; his relayed words were "I have my way, but it is not only the way", and the shake was for fine bubbles that make a drink "mellow and easy to drink" (Oxford pdf 2078, 969, 1998; *A Proper Drink* pp. 48, 208; Regan, *Joy* pdf 83–84, secondary). **Runner-up:** Joe Sheridan's Irish Coffee (Hester's second pitch).
- **What failed and why:** the story swapped three times. Round 1: Wren wanted unseen care, Hester brought Uyeda, Tomás a hot drink. Round 2: Wren took Uyeda as Hester dropped him for Sheridan and Tomás moved to Rose's cordial. Round 3: Wren took Sheridan as Hester and Tomás went back to Uyeda, each citing Wren's own "a warm drink belongs to the whole caregiver family". Hester broke the loop in round 4 by drafting sourced anchors for both. Tomás's first closing line ("If nobody asks what's in it, you got it right") argued for the habit, not the position, and was withdrawn. Hester's audit caught six fails ("He called it", "his colleagues", "what people remember him saying", "told a colleague", "never asking anyone", "His bar's signature") and four softs; the translator fact made y2 truer. Sibling echoes moved off *Rosetta*, *No Accident*, *Overnight*, *Down the Line* and *Making the Calls*.
- **"This is me":** *"Once or twice, explaining your way, you've watched someone hear 'my way is better'. You never meant that. You meant: this is the way I know how to look after you."* Position: when someone thanks you for something you made, stop saying "it was nothing".
- **Names:** *Not Only the Way* (pick, all three), *Tender*, *Keeps Well* (Hester), *Holds Up*, *Properly*, *The Back of the Drawer* (Tomás), *It'll Do* (rejected).
- **The drink:** a Gimlet in his honour, never his: 60 ml London dry gin and 45 ml hand-made lime-and-caraway cordial, shaken however you shake, no garnish, coupe. The cordial carries all the sugar and all the acid (the Codex's fresh Gimlet is OUT on acid at 1.04%). Spark: caraway, a *Flavor Matrix* surprise pairing for citrus (pdf 80), and what some London dry makers used "accenting the flavor, not changing it" (Oxford GIN pdf 894). 17.2% / 8.58 g / 0.84%, balanced; veto-free. Closing line: *"When someone says it's good, say caraway."*
- **Edges:** caraway is in the celery family (unsourced; celery isn't in the veto list), Robin's call; one edge at exactly 40% gin; Uyeda's words are relayed (secondary); the long closing line is recorded as an alternative.
