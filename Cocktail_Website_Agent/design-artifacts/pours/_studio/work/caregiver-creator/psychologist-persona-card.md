# Persona card: The Craftsman (caregiver-creator)

Wren, round 1. Source: persona.py (essence, story, Gepetto, goal Service, fear Selfishness, driver "Provide structure", brands Pampers / Johnson & Johnson / Vicks: everyday things people rely on without thinking).

## Core truth
They make things to be **used**, by people they'll mostly never see using them, and they put the most care into the parts nobody will ever look at. The care is for the user; the pride is theirs, and they'd never say so.

## Shows / hides
- **Shows:** patience, method, "doing it properly". Measured, calm, a bit old-fashioned about how things should be done. The one whose things hold up.
- **Hides:** the pride. They're proud of their work, quietly and a lot, and a little ashamed of it, because pride feels like making it about themselves. So they deflect every compliment onto the use: "It's just a chair. It'll do the job."

## Hidden fear → hidden gift
- **Fear:** selfishness. That the hours they spend getting it right are really for themselves, their standard, their name. So they only let the pride in through the back door: "it has to last, *for them*".
- **Gift:** things people can lean on without thinking. The best thing anyone can say about their work is nothing at all, years later, because it never let them down. Structure as care: they build what holds other people up.

## The "real me" line to aim for (drafts)
- "You finish the parts nobody will ever see. Those most of all."
- "You'll never meet most of the people your work looks after. You make it properly anyway."
- "You'd never call it pride. You call it doing it properly."
- Position candidate (earned, at the end): the pride is not the selfish part; let them sign it. (Gepetto's work gets up and walks away, into a life he isn't in. The Craftsman's work always leaves; let one thing carry their name.) Avoid "You're allowed to…" (Four Shares' frame).

## Truth vs. legend
The true story, and how it was actually made. They respect process and distrust a flourish. A legend is fine only if it's owned as one; the anchor must be a real maker's real method.

## Tone
Warm, plain, sturdy, unhurried. Like a good workshop: nothing showy, everything where it should be. A dry smile is fine (they know they redo things nobody would notice); no jokes at the work's expense.

## Distinct from siblings (and the reverse pairing)
- **The Sculptor (creator-caregiver, draft, Quite Alive) is the big risk.** It already owns: a one-off made for one person you love, "made to measure", perfectionism-that-isn't, "a shortcut says you weren't worth the time", 2 a.m. presents, vanishing to make it, "let them see you making it". The Sculptor's thing *says* something to one person. **The Craftsman's thing is used, by many, for years, and says nothing.** It's not a message; it's reliability. Not made to measure; made to last. Not "look at them before you pour"; care built in before anyone arrives.
- **The Nurse / Healer / Nanny:** they care for people directly, in person. The Craftsman cares *through the object*, and is usually absent when the care arrives.
- **The Old Master / Auteur (Creator family):** those are about authorship and being right. The Craftsman isn't after credit; it's the opposite problem, credit refused.
- **The Minimalist (Half a Rim):** takes away until it's true. The Craftsman adds the unseen work (the inside seam, the back of the drawer).

## Traps
- Anything the Sculptor owns (above), plus "by hand" as the whole point, "perfectionism", "hours", "late at night".
- Making it about craft-worship or "attention to detail" (horoscope: true of every Creator).
- A drink that's performed at the table (lesson so far). The care should be in a step done before the guest ever sees it, which the guest can't see but would miss.
- Overnight steps belong to the Makeover (Overnight); clear-ice cutting to Half a Rim; earned foam to Down the Line. Hester, Tomás: check the registry before settling the drink's gesture.
- Flattery ("you have golden hands"). Recognise what they do; don't praise it.
- Gepetto by name in the reading is optional and risky (a Disney reference can feel twee). The shape is the gift: he made something that walked away from him.

## Story brief for Hester
Look for a **maker whose careful, unseen work other people used for years without knowing their name**: a toolmaker, a bitters- or syrup-maker, the person who wrote the method everyone follows, a drink built for someone else's comfort that the maker never saw drunk. Bonus if there's a detail of care nobody needed to add, or if the maker was reluctant to take credit. Ingredient and person stories both count (Robin 2026-09-26). Avoid the Savoy/Coleman lane (the Sculptor's).
