Behind the cellars claim there's one scope fix. Wondrich gives his basis on pdf 137: "judging by the contents of his bar's cellar, which included barrels of a nice fifteen-year-old Caol Ila malt". That's barrels, not bottles. And Oxford says the first official Caol Ila single malt was bottled only in **2002** (CAOL ILA pdf 402). So Thomas never had this bottle. The safe line is "the distillery whose malt Thomas kept in his cellar, David Wondrich writes", never "the whisky he poured". The same entry helps your smoke idea: the 12 is heavily peated malt, but its smoke is "relatively tame by Islay standards". So "the fire without a flame" holds as a gentle smoke, not a bonfire.
