🪞 **Wren:** Here's the person. My full card is in `work/magician-regular-guy/psychologist-persona-card.md`.

**The Entertainer.** A pack of cards comes out at a bus stop, and for one minute the ordinary world goes strange. It's for anyone, strangers included. The hours of practice behind it stay private, and the flop is what sends them back to practise.

**The one true thing.** They make the wonder, and they're the one person in the circle who doesn't get it. At the reveal, everyone else gets to be a child for a minute. The Entertainer already knows how it's done, so they're watching faces: first the gasp, then the laugh, then two strangers turning to each other and suddenly not strangers. The gasp isn't what they practise for. They practise for that laugh passing between people who'd never have spoken. Working line: *"You don't practise so they'll be amazed. You practise so they'll laugh together."*

**Hide, fear, give.**
- **Hide:** how much that minute matters to them. The trick is the easiest way they know to reach a stranger. Never "shy", never "needs attention".
- **Fear:** the flop. Silence where the laugh should be, in front of strangers, with no friendship there to catch it.
- **Give:** for one minute, anyone is let in. We say that out loud once.

**Siblings.**

**The trap I'd put first.** The obvious reading is "it looks effortless, and it isn't". Other pours already own it three times:
- *Not Too Polite*: "People think it comes easily to you. It doesn't… you rehearsed it."
- *Nothing to It*: "Nobody's ever been able to tell", and "let them hear how close it came".
- *Half a Rim*: "looks like it took a minute". "A minute" is also in our own story, so we reword it or say plainly that it's a different minute.

*Anyway* owns "falls flat, laugh about it with them". That takes my own sketch's proposal, "let someone see the fumble", off the table. The hours of practice can appear as a fact about the person. They can't be the thing we recognise in them.

**Other traps.** Labels (card-trick nerd, party magician, showman). The coin behind the ear as the reading's image. Showing off as the motive. Magic puns. Making the watching sound sad: it's a cost the guest should recognise with a smile.

**Story test.** The plan's five tests stand: long private practice, an ordinary crowd, shared delight on the record, a fumble admitted, and joy that spread. I add a sixth, nice to have: where the maker's attention was while the crowd watched.
