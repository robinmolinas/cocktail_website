📜 **Hester:** Tomás, all four are on the page, and three of them carry a scope. I've added them to the card as F17–F20 and put three drink rows in the anchors.

- **"Glenlivet or Islay"** (*Imbibe!* pdf 140) is true, but it belongs to the **Skin**, in the **1887 edition**, not to the Blazer or to Thomas's 1862 text. His 1862 Skin is a small bar glass, one wine-glass of Scotch, "1 piece of lemon peel" and boiling water. "Long strip, no pith" is Wondrich's advice, and so is the demerara. Wondrich says neither Thomas nor the 1869 *Steward & Barkeeper's Manual* uses sugar. Your "merely a Scotch Whisky Skin" quote from pdf 141 is exact. It's Wondrich's judgment, so the reading credits it to him.
- **Scotch and Irish mixed** (pdf 142) is on the page, with a caveat: the 1863 book is lost (Oxford pdf 2019). What we have is its recipe as Campbell reprinted it in 1867, and Wondrich labels it "that is, Jerry Thomas, 1863". So say "his recipe from the following year", never "his 1862 book". "[1 oz each]" is Wondrich's bracket. No reason for mixing the two is given anywhere, so "two whiskies that wouldn't otherwise meet" is ours. Wren can build on it only as our reading.
- **"Cover with cup"** is exact, but read the sentence it comes from: "pour mixture into glass blazing, and cover with cup". The cup goes over a glass that is still on fire, so on the page it most likely snuffs the flame. That's my inference, and it matches Oxford's "Snuff each mug with the bottom of the other" (pdf 298). Your saucer keeping the lemon oil and smoke in is your craft reason, which is fine. It can't be told as why Thomas covered it. I'd tell it as "his recipe ends by covering the glass; I've kept the cover and given it a new job". That's sourced, owned as ours, and it keeps the fire in the story, not in the glass.
- **Caol Ila "the brand Thomas had in his cellars"** is Wondrich's aside on pdf 142, with no source given. Keep it attributed: "the historian David Wondrich writes…". Your cask-strength point is also his: Thomas "would have had to" use it because anything weaker barely lights. That makes a lower strength honest when unlit.

One more thing on the same Skin page, for Wren. Wondrich says the Skin "is surely Irish". That's a guess, and if it's used it's labelled his. The Bill Poole shooting and Lincoln's play on pdf 140 stay out.
