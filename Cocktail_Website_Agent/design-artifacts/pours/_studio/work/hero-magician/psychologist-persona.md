# Persona card: The Superhero (hero-magician) · Wren, round 1

Sources read: `persona.py hero-magician` (sheet story, goal Mastery, fear Weakness; Magician fears Unintended Results, Insignificance, Dullness, Inertia); my Hero sketch (`plans/work/hero/psychologist-sketches.md`); the family plan row; Oxford RIBALAIGUA Y VERT pdf 1655–1656 and FLORIDITA pdf 821, read whole; the whoYouAre and closing paragraph of every authored Hero sibling, plus the neighbours that share a piece of this person (*Right Here*, *Worth Finding*, *Cold Water First*, *Their Night*, *Worth the Trade*, *Down the Line*, *Off Duty*, *At the Time*, *In a Minute*).

## Core truth
They can do something most people around them can't, and they've learned to keep it put away: out for someone who needs it, back again the moment it would only be showing off. Underneath sits a question they never ask out loud: if the ability went, would anyone still call?

## The one thing no sibling shares
**They think of the ordinary self as the disguise, and the ability as the real them.** So when people come to them for what they can do, it feels like proof that the ability is the only lovable part. The reading's turn goes the other way: the ability is what they put on, like a work jacket; the person is the one who keeps choosing, every day, to put it on for somebody. Who they are lives in the choosing, not in the power.

## Shows / hides
- **Shows:** someone ordinary, quiet, sometimes a little awkward. Plays it down ("I'll have a look"). Never the one telling the story of what they did.
- **Hides:** the size of what they could do if they let it out. The upkeep: they can't bear to let it go rusty (fear of weakness). And the doubt that anyone much likes the plain version of them, the one in ordinary clothes.

## Hidden fear → hidden gift
- **Fear:** going weak (the day the ability isn't there when someone needs it), and the fear under that one: being wanted for the ability, not the person, and being nothing special without it.
- **Gift:** power used for other people and held back from display, so that nobody near them ever has to feel small. They could make a whole room feel slow, and they never have. They turn something extraordinary into a favour between friends. **The kindness to say out loud (Robin's caregiver rule):** you make people feel capable next to you, not smaller.

## The "real me" line to aim for (drafts, not final)
- *You've never quite known whether people come for you, or for what you can do.* (the fear, plainly)
- *What you can do is what you put on. Who you are is the one who keeps putting it on for someone.* (the turn; needs plainer words in v1)
- My bet for the line that lands hardest is the first. Robin's ledger says the fear named plainly is what lands ("What scares you isn't getting caught. It's being ignored.").

## Truth vs. legend
The plain true story. This person's power is real skill (Hero: mastery), not a trick, so no legend and no con. The Magician side wants one moment of wonder, and the true story already has it: a rich, famous man who still put on the white jacket every morning. That transformation, run backwards, is the wonder.

## Tone
Hero is assertive and empowering, Magician enchanting. For the bartender: warm and plain, a little wry about the disguise, never rousing ("go and change the world") and never awed. Admiration stays below praise: recognise what they *choose*, never how good they are.

## Distinct from siblings (Hero family)
- **The Firefighter** (*Anyone Would Have*): drill so the day feels ordinary; hides the hours. The Superhero's ability isn't rehearsal and the hidden thing isn't the hours: it's the size of the ability, and the doubt about the person under it. Keep practice-in-secret out of the guest text.
- **The Rescuer** (*Off Duty*, Caregiver family but the family's closest pour): runs toward the crisis and lives on the clarity. The Superhero has no rush; they're often the most reluctant to be seen at it.
- **The Dashing Hero** (*One Line*): hides a *feeling* behind cool. The Superhero hides an *ability* behind ordinariness. Its tagline ends "is for someone", so ours can't.
- **The Initiate** (*Work It Out*): sure there's more in them and can't prove it yet. The Superhero has the proof and keeps it out of sight: opposite problems. Its line "there's more in you than anyone has seen" is close in words: don't echo it.
- **The Saviour** (*Next One's Mine*): owing and credit. No bookkeeping here.
- **The Problem Solver** (unauthored): thinking under the clock with an audience. No double life.
- **The Warrior** (unauthored): falters alone beforehand. Not ours.

## Distinct from neighbours outside the family (the real risks)
- **The Hidden Talent** (*Right Here*), the closest risk: a talent that doesn't match the picture people have, hidden to stay "one of them"; "you never said"; position: show it. The Superhero's ability is *known* to whoever needs it and is used for them; it's muted in ordinary life out of decency, not to belong; and the fear is about identity (nothing without it), not about being thought above themselves. No "you never said", no "show it".
- **The Charmer** (*Worth Finding*): keeps something back so people stay; ends "They'll stay anyway. Let yourself find out." **My sketch's position ("notice that they stay") collides with it. Retired.**
- **The Genie** (*On One Condition*): summoned, hears the wish. No summoning, no wishes.
- **The Fairy Godparent** (*Their Night*): unasked help, at the back, slips out early. Keep "at the back" and leaving early out.
- **The Entertainer** (*Cold Water First*): practises alone for strangers' laughter; position: let one person watch you practise. Keep practice out of the proposal.
- **The Mechanic** (*Good for Years*): repairing things. Don't make the opening scene a repair.
- **Owned motif:** "looks effortless / it isn't" (*Not Too Polite*, *Nothing to It*, *Half a Rim*).
- **Taken proposal frames:** "when nothing's wrong" (*Off Duty*, *One Line*), "let someone look after you" (family-wide), "they'll stay" (*Worth Finding*).

## Traps
- **The compliment trap,** this family's biggest risk: any line praising the ability ("you're extraordinary") is cut. Recognise the choosing.
- Capes, "super", flying, saving the world, powers, kryptonite, phone boxes, and Clark Kent. The sheet's example is the obvious touchstone, but the persona's name is built on it, and the family's rationed words say never that word. Out.
- Fame. The guest isn't famous and doesn't want to be. The story's fame stays a setting, one clause.
- Hours of secret practice as the hidden part (Firefighter, Entertainer).
- Clinical words ("imposter syndrome"). Say it plainly.
- A performed drink: no throw (Innocent's Boadas row), no flame, no theatre. The ability in this glass should be the quiet kind.
- Gender: Constante is "he"; the guest never is.

## My story test (sharpened from the sketch)
1. A person with a skill well beyond their peers who kept an ordinary daily form (a working routine, a uniform, a quiet manner) when they could have traded it for the spotlight.
2. The skill used for other people, with the show held back.
3. Best if the record shows the ordinary side valued apart from the skill, or the skill's best work left unclaimed.
4. Out: fame as the point, performance, "looks effortless", hours alone.

## Verdict on the plan's lead: Constante Ribalaigua and the white jacket

**Accept, with conditions.** Re-reading pdf 1655–1656 and 821 whole changed four things:

1. **Correction (pdf 1656):** he told Sugrue he invented *a new drink every day* "as self discipline". It isn't "one drink a day", as the plan row and my own earlier note have it. Hester corrects the anchor. Usable in one clause, as his own words: it mirrors the fear of going weak (keeping the edge, daily). Never as hidden hours.
2. **The fame is bigger than the plan says:** "the presiding genius", "unquestionably the most influential mixologist of the twentieth century", celebrity regulars. My test puts fame stories out. It survives only because the reading turns on the jacket: wealth and celebrity, and still the white jacket every morning, behind the bar mixing. So: fame in one clause, no celebrity names (Hemingway, Dietrich, Stokowski out), "king" at most once and preferably not at all.
3. **He did take credit in print:** he bought the bar in 1935 and published souvenir recipe booklets from 1935 to 1948. "Without taking the credit" holds only for the Floridita cocktail, which "for reasons unknown" is in none of them (pdf 821). Motive is "I like to think" only.
4. **"Seemingly casual" (Woon, 1928) can't carry anything:** the looks-effortless turn is owned three times. "Unprepossessing, quiet and reserved" (Oxford's own words, paraphrased) is the ordinary side we need.

Test clauses: 1 passes (the jacket, every morning, after wealth); 2 passes (he mixed for others daily; the show held back is the reading's interpretation, signposted); 3 passes on its second half (the Floridita, unprinted, kept alive because someone else loved it), and its first half is unmet on the page. **Ask for Hester:** is there anything on the record (Sugrue 1935, Woon, a regular, his staff) of someone valuing Constante the man rather than his drinks? If not, the guest half carries that clause alone, and that's acceptable.

**Why the jacket is the mirror:** for most people the work jacket is the ordinary self. For him it was where the skill lived, and he chose it over the life of the famous man every morning. I like to think that's the Superhero's whole question in one garment: was the jacket the disguise, or was the famous man? The reading answers for the guest: the ability is what you put on; the person is whoever keeps choosing to put it on for somebody else.

**The Floridita, from the person's side:** right. It's the one he made and didn't put his name to, and it survived only because a visitor tasted it in 1938 and couldn't leave it behind. That's the answer to the guest's fear: the part you never put forward is what someone kept. Tomás's two flags (a Daiquiri variation; Trader Vic is the Storyteller's man) are Hester's to rule. My side: the guest text never needs the word "Daiquiri", and Vic in one clause is fine.

**Collision Tomás must know:** *In a Minute* (the Discoverer) pours the El Presidente: white Cuban-style rum, vermouth, orange curaçao, grenadine, an orange peel, credited to Ribalaigua in its dossier (Wondrich; Oxford "quite possible"), and called "a Cuban classic" in its guest text. The Floridita shares four of its six ingredients and the twist. Not a block (different family and glass, with lime and cacao, shaken), but the spark should pull it away, the El Presidente stays out of our guest text, and we never say "a Cuban classic".

**Drink guard from the person:** nothing performed. The hand-squeezed lime, fingers only (pdf 1655), is the jacket in the method: ordinary, physical, every time. The spark should be the quiet kind, something you'd only notice if you knew.

## Position direction (mine; replaces the sketch's)
Not "let them see you without it and notice they stay" (*Worth Finding*'s). Two candidates for v1:
- **(a) The introduction:** next time someone introduces you by what you can do, add one thing about you that has nothing to do with it.
- **(b) Going plain on purpose:** once, when someone calls for the ability, go round anyway and leave the ability at home; be company, not the answer.
I lean (a): concrete, new, and it puts the person's own words beside the ability, which is the turn of the whole reading. Neither uses "when nothing's wrong".
