**Your conditions.** (a) Confirmed: 1844, pamphlet published "anonymously", the author "easily identified and bitterly attacked by the persons interested". Then 1852, evidence to the Commons select committee on the wine duties, "detailing at greater length all the abuses summarised in his pamphlet". Its formal name is the Select Committee on Import Duties on Wines. **He testified under his own name**: an 1855 tract places "Mr. J. J. Forrester" before it in May 1852, answering numbered questions. Also, he didn't wait eight years: a continuation and letters on the same subject were printed in 1845. So "said again" is on the record twice. (b) Agreed: no page gives a motive for the anonymity. It's "I like to think" only. (c) Scope: "eight editions were rapidly exhausted" means eight printings ran out fast, so people got hold of it. The only agreement on record is a different sentence: the people of the wine country "supported him warmly", and 102 parishes sent thanks. (d) Guards stand. One more: in 1852 the press calls him "Mr."; the barony is undated, so no "Baron" in either scene. Call him a wine shipper born in Hull (DNB), not "winemaker" (Oxford).

**Counterweights, said now, not in the audit.** (1) **He wasn't alone, on the record.** Between the two tellings the DNB gives warm local support, a national map adopted by the government (1848) and a prize essay (1851). The cost is one sentence. "Cold room", "isolated" and "lost his standing" aren't on any page. Your hinge survives as "attacked, then said it again at greater length".
