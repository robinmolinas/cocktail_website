# Persona card: The Lone Voice (sage-outlaw)

Wren, round 1, 2026-10-02. Built from `persona.py sage-outlaw`, the Sage plan row (which I accepted at plan review after reading the DNB entry whole), and the readings of the nearest neighbours: *Not Too Polite* (Campaigner), *Hear Me Out* (Devil's Advocate), *Are You Quite Sure?* (Prodigy), *Kept* (Adolescent), *Plain to See* (Game-Changer), *Can't Watch* (Maverick), *Look Again* (Philosopher).

## Core truth
They found it out themselves, they said it, it cost them, and they **said it again**, at more length and in a bigger room. The first time is courage, and plenty of people have that. The Lone Voice is the second time: after the room has gone cold, they come back with more of the evidence, not less.

## Shows / hides
- **Shows:** composure, the facts in order, a calm "I've checked". People read it as stubbornness, or a liking for being the odd one out.
- **Hides:** they don't like being alone, at all. Part of them hopes they're wrong, because being wrong would let them back in. So they check again, mostly at night, and it still holds. They'd have liked the argument to win without them (Forrester published anonymously; the motive is ours, signposted). And when someone comes up afterwards and says "you're right, but I couldn't say so", it moves them and it stings at the same time.

## Hidden fear → hidden gift
- **Fear:** the comfortable version becoming the official one, and the record holding nobody's objection. Worse, the record holding *their* silence, because they got tired. (Outlaw half: the powerlessness of being made to take it back.) This is a fear about the record, outward. It is not the Adolescent's inward fear of drifting into someone they swore they'd never be.
- **Gift:** the truth is on the record before anyone's allowed to agree with it. Everyone who comes later has something to point to. The quiet ones in the room got to hear it said out loud. Say that plainly once (Robin's "say the kindness out loud"): their awkward evenings are other people's permission.

## The "real me" line to aim for (draft)
> "Part of you hopes you're wrong. It would make everything easier. So you go back over it one more time, and it's still there."

Backup: "You don't enjoy being the only one. You'd just rather be alone than agree to something that isn't true."

## Truth vs. legend
They want the true story, told evenly. They distrust a hero tale and they'll smell one. A vindication ending ("and history proved him right") reads to them as the very flattery they spend their lives refusing. Tell the cost and the second telling plainly, and leave the outcome at one clause.

## Tone
Sage: informed, thoughtful. Outlaw: direct, bold. Together: a bartender who talks to them as an equal and doesn't soften the facts, with warmth underneath, and no cheering. Serious, not grave. One dry line is welcome. No "brave", "hero" or "truth-teller" praise words: they'd flinch.

## Distinct from siblings and neighbours
- **Philosopher** (*Look Again*) keeps the question open. **This one has an answer** and pays for saying it.
- **Prodigy** (*Are You Quite Sure?*) is doubted for who's talking, and softens with "I think". **This one is attacked for what's said**, and doesn't soften. Their standing isn't the problem: the content is.
- **Game-Changer** (*Plain to See*) fears being followed into a mistake. **This one fears not being followed** while the mistake becomes official.
- **Connoisseur** (*A Brother's Care*) keeps a truth out of love. **This one publishes it**, at a cost.
- **Campaigner** (*Not Too Polite*, outside the family) speaks up for someone who isn't in the room, heart going. **This one speaks for what they found**, not for a person. The cause isn't the point; the evidence is.
- **Devil's Advocate** (*Hear Me Out*) argues the side they don't hold to test an idea. **This one holds it**, and wishes they didn't have to.
- **Adolescent** (*Kept*) can't stop noticing the fake at the table. **This one went and found out**, and wrote it down.
- **Maverick** (*Can't Watch*) breaks the rule where people can see. **This one isn't breaking anything**: they're stating what's so.

## Traps
1. **Vindication as payoff.** "Proved right in the end" turns recognition into flattery, and it's the plan's own "out".
2. **Martyrdom.** Forrester's drowning, Galileo's trial. Both stay out of guest text. **Galileo is the sheet's own example:** not even as a touchstone, because it pulls straight to trial and martyr.
3. **The crank reading.** A reading that says "everyone else is fooled and you see it" flatters every conspiracy theorist, and insults the guest's friends. The trait is **evidence they gathered and checked, against people who profited from the other version**, and the reading must leave them room to be wrong. Dignity both ways.
4. **Contrarian.** Disagreeing isn't the trait: that's the Devil's Advocate.
5. **Cause for others.** That's the Campaigner. The 102 parishes and "protector of the Douro" get one clause at most.
6. **Purity / honest wine.** Adulteration has three neighbours. Lead with the monopoly and the cost.
7. **"Sign it" endings.** *Can't Watch* owns "where people can see who did it", the Muse owns putting your name on it, *Left Standing* owns "don't go alone, take someone", the Campaigner owns "count". The anonymity limb invites all four.
8. **Phrases already owned:** "Someone had to say it" (Campaigner tagline), "say it/tell someone" closers, "the truth at the bottom of the glass" (Trickster), "Look again", "let them check" (Prodigy), "holds up on its own" (Rule Breaker).

## Story test
(1) Someone who stated what they had evidence for (2) against an official or popular version, (3) and paid for it in standing, company or credit; (4) the evidence is on the record. **New limb, the one no sibling has:** (5) **they said it again after the cost**, at more length, somewhere it counted. Vindication welcome but never the point. Out: a cause for others, no real cost, someone later proved wrong.

## Position direction (mine to settle in r3)
Not "sign it", not "say it". The hard truth in their interest: **the cold room isn't evidence, either way.** Being left alone doesn't prove they're wrong (on bad nights it feels like it does), and it doesn't prove they're right (which is the trap that turns into a pose). Go back to what they found, not to who's still sitting with them. The gain: they can stop taking the room's temperature as the measure of whether they were right. And the eight editions that were "rapidly exhausted" while those who profited attacked him suggest people were reading. Never "agreeing": the page doesn't say that. Their readers are usually quieter than their critics.
