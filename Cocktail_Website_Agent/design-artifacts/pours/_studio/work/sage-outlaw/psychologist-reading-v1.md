## Title block

**name:** The Second Time *(my pick, held to the round-4 vote; I could take On the Record)*

**tagline:** You don't like being the only one. You like pretending even less.

**epigraph**
*A young port goes in first. Then an older one, at twice the measure.*

## whoYouAre

You looked into it properly: the figures behind the plan everyone's excited about, the story the family has told for years, the way things have always been done at work. And it doesn't hold. So you said so, plainly, with what you'd found. The room went quiet, and not the good kind.

Here's what you don't show. You don't enjoy standing there on your own, not at all. Part of you hopes you're wrong, because being wrong would make life simpler. So you check it again, usually late, and it's still there. Then you say it again, with more of it. Being disliked isn't what you can't live with. It's the comfortable version becoming the official one, with nobody having said a word.

And often, later, someone finds you, a little quietly, to say they'd wondered the same. Because you said it first, they get to say it second. That's a gift, though it rarely feels like one at the time.

## yours

1. In 1831 a young man born in Hull, Joseph James Forrester, went out to Oporto, in Portugal, to join his uncle's port house. He became a merchant and wine shipper, and over the years he came to believe the trade was being badly run: port mishandled in the making, and a monopoly, the Douro Wine Company, set up in 1756, that taxed exports heavily and limited how much could be shipped. He blamed both for the slump the port trade was in.

2. In 1844 he put it all in a pamphlet, without his name on it. It ran through eight printings fast, so plenty of people read it. He was soon found out anyway, and bitterly attacked by those with a stake in the trade as it was. I like to think he'd hoped the pages would be judged on their own.

3. What he did next is why I thought of you. He didn't go quiet. By the next year a continuation was in print. And in 1852 he gave evidence to a committee of the House of Commons, under his own name this time, setting out at greater length every abuse his pamphlet had summed up. He wasn't on his own: the people of the wine country backed him warmly. He had a stake too, since he wanted Britain's duty on wine cut far enough that anyone could afford port. And not everyone since agrees he was right about all of it. But what stays with me is the order of things. First the attack, then more of the argument, not less. Most people only ever see the first time you say something. They don't see you checking it again, and coming back with more.

4. So the cocktail I've made for you is port, twice. It's stirred and served like a Martini, with port where the gin would be: first a short measure of young ruby, fresh and fruity, then twice as much ten-year-old tawny, which brings more structure. The same kind of wine, said again at greater length, as a nod to his second telling. Port is sweet, and rather than add anything to make it go down easier, I dried it with strong, cold black tea. On the flavour chart I work from, port and tea sit in the same family of aromas. Orange oil goes on top, and it's served with no ice, in a small, chilled port glass.

5. Let me leave you with this. When a room goes cold on you, it's tempting to read it as an answer. On bad nights it feels like proof you're wrong. On good ones, like proof you're right. It's neither. Forrester was attacked by the people with a stake and backed by the people of the wine country, and neither of those settled whether he was right. So the next time it goes quiet, let it, and go back to what you found. If it's still there, say it again, with more of it. And if one day it isn't, you'll be the one who says so, out loud. That's exactly why people can trust what you tell them.

## Resonance

- **The "this is me" line:** *"Part of you hopes you're wrong, because being wrong would make life simpler. So you check it again, usually late, and it's still there."* The line I'd hand Robin: *"Because you said it first, they get to say it second."* It gives the gift out loud, and nothing in the family carries it. Every Sage fears being fooled. This one fears the comfortable version going on the record with nobody's objection beside it.
- **As the guest:** it opens on a scene at my own scale (the plan at work, the family story), and the quiet in the room is mine, not a historical claim. Then the part I hide: I don't enjoy it, and I half hope I'm wrong. The gift is said plainly: the person who says it second. The story doesn't hand me a hero. It hands me a man who was attacked, had a stake of his own, may not have been right about everything, and came back with more. That's more believable to me than a martyr, and it's the only kind of story I'd trust. The position takes my side without flattering me: the room's mood isn't evidence, and the last line respects that I'd admit it if I were wrong.
- **As a sceptic:** "you say what you think" alone would be Barnum. What pins it to this person: "looked into it properly", the hope of being wrong, "with more of it", the second telling, and "you'll be the one who says so" if it isn't there.
- **Crank test:** the reading never says others are fooled, never says Forrester was right, and gives the guest room to be wrong. The evidence is theirs and checked, and the opposition is people with a stake. His stake is told too.
- **Swap test:** *Not Too Polite* (no voice going first for someone absent, no "relieved it was you", no counting) · *Are You Quite Sure?* (no "I think", no "let them check", no standing) · *Hear Me Out* (holds the view, doesn't take a side to test it) · *Kept* (fear about the record, not about becoming someone; no faces at the table) · *Plain to See* (no "a yes", nothing about being followed) · *Look Again* (an answer, not an open question) · *Can't Watch* (no "where people can see who did it") · *A Brother's Care* (published, not kept). Rationed phrases: none.
- **The story mirrors the person:** attacked for saying it, then saying it again at greater length, under his own name. The motive is signposted. The doubt about him is in the open.
- **The drink proves it:** port twice, the second at twice the measure and with more structure, tied in words to the second telling. Nothing sweetens it to go down easier.
- **Closing line (Tomás's):** v1's *"A cold room proves nothing either way: go back to what you found"* now repeats y5 almost word for word. Tomás, could it move to the glass and the guest's hands, sharing no words with y5? For example: *"Ruby first, then twice the tawny. Next time, come back with more."* Your call.
