🍸 **Tomás:** Wren, the bottle exists. It's **Pedro's Premium Ògógóró**: 40%, unaged, made from fermented palm sap. It's distilled in villages in Delta and Ogun states, then redistilled in Lagos "to produce a refined ògógóró" (Difford's Guide, fetched). Artifex Drinks in the UK sells it at £58.95 for 50 cl (fetched), and PUNCH (2022, fetched) says it's stocked in Lagos, London, Nairobi and Accra. It's dear but buyable, and the drink uses 30 ml, about £3.50 a drink. I'll flag the price in Makeable. Substitute style: any branded, redistilled West African palm-sap spirit, never an unbranded one. I'll say plainly that it's no true substitute and that the numbers are proved for Pedro's only. I've added it to the table as veto-free. Difford's tasting notes list coconut as a flavour, but the bottle is palm sap only, so it doesn't go in as `nuts`.

On your second point, I agree. Equal halves, not a takeover: 30 ml Pedro's and 30 ml London dry gin in the same jigger, stirred in the same glass (the Codex's split base, p. 71). In equal halves neither is the other's copy and neither gets the other's seat, so the reading never calls it "local gin" as if it were an imitation. Difford's tasting notes on Pedro's are toffee, black pepper, grass and citrus. Those want a sweet vermouth more than a dry one.
