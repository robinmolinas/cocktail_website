# Persona card: The Maverick (outlaw-hero) · Wren, round 1

Source: persona.py outlaw-hero (essence, story, goal Liberation/Revolution, fear Powerlessness, example Robin Hood; Outlaw fears Powerlessness/Dependence/Restrictions; Hero fears Weakness/Incompetence/Timidity).

## The one true thing (no sibling has it)
They can't watch. Told to stand aside while something unfair happens to someone else, they'd rather pay for doing something than live with having done nothing, and it's usually not even their fight. The break is never for their own share, and it has to *work*: a result, not a protest.

Candidate "this is me" lines (to test, not final):
- "It's rarely your fight. That's never once stopped you."
- "You'd rather pay for doing something than get away with doing nothing."

## What they hide
Some of it is for them. The unfairness matters, but what they truly can't bear is the helplessness: "there's nothing you can do" is the sentence that makes them move. They suspect this about themselves and never say it, because it sounds less noble than it looks. Second, quieter: their fixes are mostly workarounds, one person at a time, done out of sight. The rule stays standing, so they end up doing it again, alone. That's its own kind of powerlessness, and they know it.

## What they fear
Powerlessness: being made to watch. Not getting caught; being told to wait, go through channels, leave it to the people in charge, while it goes on happening.

## What they give
Things actually get fixed. With them around, "those are the rules" stops being the end of the matter, and other people see that a rule they thought was solid can be moved. (Say the kindness out loud once: what their way gives others is worth something.)

## Position (in their interest, earned)
Stop fixing it quietly, one case at a time. Once, break it where it can be seen, with your name on it, so it breaks for everyone and you don't have to do it again tomorrow. Not for the credit: for the rule. (Solarin's own shape: he got himself arrested on purpose, publicly, and the ban ended.)

## Siblings (Outlaw primary) and how this one differs
- **Gentle Giant (*In One Piece*, Stoumen):** also for others, also drinkers vs a licensing state. Giant = strength held back, staying, standing beside. Maverick = the rule broken on purpose by someone with nothing to gain, cost chosen. Keep off: rights to be served, "a right, not a favour", soft drinks, staying till morning, standing beside, "nobody has to be brave".
- **Rule Breaker (*Asked In*, Glenlivet):** an illicit spirit later legal, distrust of the yes. Ours is not the maker and never drank it; no "legally" epigraph, no "being let in".
- **Gangster (*Where You Stand*, Olmstead):** a code, a community, rule-writers who break rules, "line". Keep off "line", "where you stand", "procedure"; the word "code" at most once.
- **Thrill Seeker (*Whoopee*):** for the feeling; "tell people, send the invitations, no backing out". Our "where it can be seen" must stay about the rule, not committing to oneself.
- **Adolescent:** refusal of the false. **Subverter:** a joke at power. **Fugitive:** love. **Disrupter:** tears down the decaying thing (Spurrier). **Leading Edge:** the new before it has a name. **Devil's Advocate:** argues the other side. Maverick is none of these: an act, for others, that works.
- **Cross-family neighbour, the Campaigner (*Not Too Polite*, Heyrick):** the voice that goes first, shaking hands, pamphlet, "you don't stop to see what changed", counting wins. That spends the "vanishes before the thanks" gift: the Maverick reading can't use it as the gift or the position. Ours acts, never speaks or writes.

## Traps
- Speech, pamphlet, writing, "activist" framing: tell only what Solarin did (got himself arrested), never what he said or wrote.
- Robin Hood imagery in the reading (stealing, arrows, forest, masks): persona imagery only, at most one touchstone, and I lean against it (it announces the archetype).
- Teetotal as virtue: never moralise about drinking; he didn't drink, and that's the point about his stake, not about sobriety.
- Colonial history plain, never costume, never exotic. Fairness, not victory: Solarin's point was one rule for both, so the reading and the glass never cast the foreign bottle as beaten.
- Oxford's "popular history" of ogogoro's origins (Prohibition-evading moonshiners) stays out: contested on the same page, and Prohibition is the Gangster's ground.
- Methanol / "can be lethal": out (First Law; recipe names a rectified, branded bottle only).
- Causation at the page's strength: "That finally changed in 1968, when…". No stronger.
- Names: "Standing By" is a registry name, so not "stand by" as a phrase in the reading; "in the open" is *Fair Measure*'s; no "nobody has to be brave"; no "what changed".

## Story test (sharpened from the plan)
1. Breaks a rule or order themselves, on purpose, where it's seen.
2. For other people's fair result, with nothing in it for them.
3. It works: the unfair thing ends, on a page.
4. The cost is theirs and visible, and they take nothing from the result.
Out: speeches and pamphlets, crisis rescues, violence, stealing for gain, Prohibition and smuggling.

## Plan row: verdict
Accept Solarin. Every point is on Oxford OGOGORO pdf 1417, re-read whole. One correction to the plan's own shorthand: it says the 1919 treaty banned local distillation "while cheap imports came in". The page says the opposite about cheap imports: the treaty *limited* cheap foreign spirits and banned local distilling; what stayed allowed was foreign spirits (imported gin). Say "foreign spirits were allowed, the local one wasn't", never "cheap imports flowed in".
Needs from Hester: a second, fetched source on the 1968 arrest itself (where, what happened after, whether he was charged), so the reading has a scene and not one sentence; nothing about his school, other arrests or beliefs without a page. Needs from Tomás: a buyable rectified ogogoro on a page, or the drink fails "could I make it?"; and a thought: fairness in the glass (local and foreign side by side, equal), not the local in the foreign's seat.
