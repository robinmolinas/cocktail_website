# Persona card: The Devil's Advocate (outlaw-sage) · Wren, round 1

Sources: persona.py outlaw-sage (xlsx row: essence, story, goal Liberation/Revolution, fear Powerlessness; Sage secondary fears Deception, Conflict, Ambiguity; tone provocative, bold, direct). Every sibling and neighbour reading read in full this session.

## The one true thing (no sibling has it)
They argue against ideas they may well agree with. The side they take is the side nobody's on, and half the time it isn't theirs. An idea nobody has argued with hasn't been tested; it's only been agreed with. So they argue with it, including with their own beliefs, and they'd honestly be glad to lose to a good answer.

## Hidden, feared, given
- **Hide:** how often they agree. And that "I'm just playing devil's advocate" is also a shield: it lets them push without ever saying what they really think, so nobody can argue *their* view down.
- **Fear (one fear, two faces):** the persona's fear is a decision made before anyone was allowed to think (powerlessness, for someone whose one power is the question). The face they never mention: being written off as contrary ("there they go again"), because once their questions stop counting, they're back in exactly that room.
- **Give (say it out loud, Robin's rule):** an idea that's been through them holds up. When the real objection comes, from a boss, a buyer, a parent, the others already have the answer, because they were made to find it. People find them tiring at the time and thank them later.
- **Position (Ryu, in their interest):** once in a while, argue for something you actually love, and say plainly that it's yours. "No, really. I mean it." (The guest's half of the story: Morgenthaler's own words were "No, really. I'm serious.")
- **Tone:** funny is allowed and right. Provocative, bold, direct, never cruel.

## Story test
1. Argues the side nobody's on, on purpose and in public.
2. Argues it honestly and well: the strongest case, granting what the other side has right. Not a stunt.
3. Pays for it: a sneer, resentment, being thought contrary. At minimum, the scorn they argued against is on a page.
4. The argument left the thing better, won or lost. And what came after the page doesn't undo the person.
Out: causes for others (Campaigner), evidence against an official lie (Lone Voice), exposing a fake (Adolescent, Cynic), liked-first loyalty (Trend Setter), mocked-then-returned as the point (Wise Fool), jokes as the vehicle (Subverter).
