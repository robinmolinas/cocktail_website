# Persona card: The Stand-up (jester-regular-guy), Wren, round 1

## Who this is
Sheet: fun-loving, sees the funny side of everyday life; notices what everyone does and nobody mentions (keys checked three times, "five minutes" meaning forty); makes people feel normal by getting them to laugh at being human together; at the table, not at the head of it; torture = a dull evening where nobody says what they're thinking. Goal pleasure, fear boredom; secondary belonging/fairness, fears exclusion.

## The one true thing (no sibling has it)
They say out loud the small thing everyone does and nobody mentions, and the room finds out nobody was alone with it. The laugh is relief: "oh no, me too." What felt like your own oddity was everyone's all along.

## Hidden / fear / gift
- **Hides:** every observation starts as a confession. They're the one who checked the keys three times. They say "people do this" or "we all do this" when they mean "I do". The general version is safer.
- **Fear:** the polite evening: everyone acting more together than they are, nobody being real. That's what boredom means to them. Under it, gently: being the only odd one. They never risk it, because they make the oddity everyone's before anyone can pin it on them.
- **Gives (say it out loud, once):** people go home feeling less strange. Permission to be human, given to a whole table at once.
- **Hidden need:** to belong too. They bring everyone into the "us" but stay the one telling it, so they're always in the joke and seldom its subject. (Signpost: "sometimes".)

## Position (Ryu, earned last)
Go first with your own. Say "I do this", not "everyone does". The joke still lands, and this time they're in it. In their interest: they get known, not just enjoyed.

## Siblings and cousins (swap test)
- Surrealist (*The Other Berry*): notices that things are already *strange*. Stand-up goes the other way: shows the strange thing is *normal*. Keep "notice" out of the tagline.
- Photographer (*As It Was*): keeps the ordinary, quietly, for later ("Hold on, don't move"). Stand-up says it now, to the table, for the laugh. No keeping, recording, Tuesdays.
- Reassurer (*Worth the Trip*): one person, a bad moment, timing; y5 "the laugh is how you get in, don't let it become how you get out". Stand-up: a whole table, recognition, no rescue. Keep my position off "hiding behind the joke".
- Morale Booster (*Anyway*): plans the fun for a group. Stand-up plans nothing; it's what they see.
- Wacky One (*For Kicks*): invents absurdity that catches on. Stand-up invents nothing.
- True Friend (*Four Shares*): makes sure nobody is left out. Stand-up: nobody is left feeling odd.
- Cynic / Scandalmonger: aim at a lie or the powerful. The Stand-up aims at all of us, themselves first.
- Straight Man: the steady partner who feeds the joke. Not this one.
- Nurse (*Everybody's*), Rough Diamond (*Any Day*): own "everyone gets", "the usual", "a long shift".

## Traps
1. Mocking anyone: the 1896 man, the regulars, working people. The laugh is with all of us.
2. Labels: comedian, the funny one, stand-up, microphone, class clown, "working-class".
3. The persona sheet's Seinfeld and his "show about nothing": guests may already have read it on the sheet. Not the touchstone.
4. "Queer drinks" (1896 = odd) reads differently today. Dossier only, never guest text.
5. "Side by side" is *Till Spring*'s epigraph. Say it another way.
6. "His Helper" is the old name only: no gender, no sidekick insight (Straight Man's shape).
7. Registry taglines already open with "Everyone…" five times; "first" is in the Pioneer's and Trend Setter's taglines.
8. The drink must sound worth having: a renowned pairing back on menus, never "a shot and a beer"; no dropping, no bombs.
9. Let it be funny. A solemn reading of this person would be a misreading.

## Story test
Passes if: (1) the thing is ordinary and shared; (2) it was once treated as odd, or as not worth saying; (3) someone looked at it fondly, said it, and people saw themselves; the joke is on all of us, never on one person or class; (4) the drink is that ordinary thing, made worth having. Fails: fame, invention, a stage, a mocked person, the strange-in-the-ordinary (Surrealist), keeping it (Photographer).

## Verdict on the plan's lead (Boilermaker): accept, with the mirror moved
Read Oxford pdf 306–307 whole. The plan rests the mirror on working people naming it after their jobs. That's the weak limb: nobody is named as the namer, "and His Helper" is gendered and the Straight Man's shape, and a job label is a social label. The page gives a better arc, and it's the Stand-up's exactly:
- 1896: a man orders whiskey and beer together and the bartender and regulars are thrown; the *New York Herald* writes it up as comedy, a piece titled "A False Alarm" (April 5, 1896, p. 4). A whole bar in uproar over nothing.
- Yet the Scots and the Irish had drunk them together all along (an 1846 visitor found it remarkable). The "odd" order was only odd in that room.
- By the 1930s–40s it was the plainest order in the bar and had a name; by 1948 a columnist still had to explain it; today it's back on menus as pairings.
So the reveal: what looks like one person's oddity is everyone's, once someone says it. The 1896 man is one of the crowd; the joke lands on the fuss.
