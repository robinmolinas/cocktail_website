# Tomás: drink leads, round 2 (no spec, no balance run until Wren rules)

## Base pairing (from the page)
- Oxford BOILERMAKER pdf 307: "Fortunately, some of those still involve a shot of rye and a glass of lager beer." Billy Rose 1948: "straight rye with a beer chaser". Modern menus: "spirits and beers paired in various and creative ways".
- Oxford pdf 306: the assimilating pair was "(German) beer and (Irish/American) whisky".
- So: straight rye (no brand; table key `rye`, 50%) + a cold German-style pilsner (table key `beer`, 5%, gluten, unsourced values). Root: Highball (spirit + long fizzy partner), served apart, so `freeform`.

## Lead 1 (my pick): apricot in the rye
- Flavor Matrix STONE FRUIT, pdf 236: surprise pairings "Beer, sage, soy sauce"; best pairings include brandy, corn, citrus.
- The Matrix ties stone fruit to beer, not to rye: rye + apricot is my craft call, unsourced.
- Method idea: dried apricots steeped in the rye overnight, strained; served in a small glass beside the pilsner. Nothing dropped in, nothing mixed.
- To run once ruled: sweep the steep (apricot sugar leaching into 45-60 ml rye; dried apricot unsourced ~50-60 g sugar/100 g), strength drop negligible. New ingredient `apricot_steeped_rye` or `dried_apricot` via add_ingredient.py, safe side (sulphites aren't a veto class; check the veto list).

## Lead 2: the brim-full first sip (Wren to rule; "no drink you perform" lesson)
- Oxford KOPSTOOT pdf 1143: chilled genever, small tulip glass "filled to the very top", first sip bending from the waist, hands behind the back; Dutch name since at least the 1940s.
- Oxford HERRENGEDECK pdf 1002: German korn with a beer chaser, "gentleman's setup".
- Story use (Hester/Wren): every country had its own name for the pair, so the 1896 order was odd only in that room.
- Drink use: fill the rye to the brim; first sip without lifting it. Physics: a brimming glass can't be lifted without spilling.
