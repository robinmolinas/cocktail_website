# The Problem Solver (hero-sage): Wren's read, round 1

Sources: `persona.py hero-sage`; sibling and cousin readings as they stand today (the ten Hero pours, plus *The Long View*, *At the Time*, *Note to Self*, *Show Your Working*, *Good for Years*, *For Good*, *Off Duty*, *Before the Room*); Oxford DAIQUIRI pdf 621 and 623, re-opened whole by me this round (pdf 622 is an image page, no text).

## Core truth (the one thing no sibling carries)

**The bit they love and the bit they dread are the same few seconds.** The plan breaks, everyone turns to look, and there's a moment where nothing has come yet. That moment is the whole thrill for them, and it's also their nightmare. A riddle with no chance of having no answer isn't a riddle, so they can't have the pleasure without the risk, and they've never told anyone they enjoy it.

The question they ask isn't "what went wrong?" but "what have we got?" While everyone else is listing what's missing, they're already counting what's left.

## Shows / hides

- **Shows:** a level face when the plan falls apart. Quick, practical questions ("what do we know, what do we have, what happens if we try this?"). A fix nobody would have thought of, assembled from whatever was in the room.
- **Hides, first:** that part of them is enjoying it. It feels unkind to enjoy someone else's emergency, so the delight is kept behind the level face. A quiet, almost guilty disappointment when everything goes to plan.
- **Hides, second:** the blank. The second where nothing has come yet, with everyone looking. The level face is partly how they get through that second without anyone seeing it. Sometimes they say the first answer before they're sure of it, because a silence in front of people feels worse than a wrong guess. And they keep a private worry that the fix won't go down well, even as they serve it up.

## Hidden fear → hidden gift

- **Fear:** standing in front of a problem, with everyone watching, and having nothing. It's a fear of weakness on show, not of failing in private (the Hero's fear of weakness, sharpened by the Sage's fear of not knowing).
- **Gift:** when they ask "right, what have we got?", the room stops panicking and starts looking. Panic turns into a job other people can do too: fetch the limes, check the cupboard. They hand the people around them a next step, and with it some of their own nerve. **Said plainly (Robin's caregiver rule):** "That's what you give people: a way out of the panic, and something to do with their hands."

## The "real me" line to aim for

- *"The part you love and the part you dread are the same few seconds."* (draft, whoYouAre)
- Backup: *"You'd never wish the oven dead an hour before twelve people arrive. But when it goes, something in you sits up."*

## Truth vs. legend

They want the true story, contested bits included, provided they're resolved cleanly. Sage fears ambiguity: a loose end in the history would bother this guest more than any other Hero. The family story and Lyman's crew version are both on the page. Say which is which in one clause each, and never leave it hanging.

## Tone

Brisk, warm, a little amused. This one can be funny about the guilty pleasure ("something in you sits up"). Never solemn, never "brave". The bartender sounds like someone who's been handed a broken evening and enjoyed it too.

## Traps

1. **Calm as the point.** The Rescuer, the Angel and the Legend own it. Calm may appear once, as what the room sees, never as the recognition.
2. **"The worse it gets…"** (the Legend's tagline) and "never calmer than when" (the Rescuer's).
3. **Preparation or rehearsal** (the Firefighter), **crew credit** (the Firefighter). Lyman's "group creation" gets one honest clause and is never the point.
4. **"Nobody even knew anything went wrong"** (the Innovator's tagline shape).
5. **"Whatever's there / to hand / make do"** (the Mechanic, and "to hand" is in five pours). Say it fresh: "what's left", "what have we got".
6. **"I don't know"** as the position. The Free Spirit owns "makes 'I don't know' safe", the Prodigy's closing line is "leave off the 'I think'", the Genius owns "Show Your Working", and the Campaigner's closing line owns "count the ones you've already won". The position has to avoid all four.
7. **"Having nothing to show"** (the Initiate). Say "nothing comes" or "the blank".
8. **Flattery:** "brilliant", "genius", "sharp mind". The persona's example is Apple's Genius Bar: a scene is welcome, never the label ("the one everyone calls when the wifi dies" is a role, so at most a passing scene).
9. **"By accident."** Oxford's "fluke birth" is Oxford's word. He improvised.
10. **Leaving it open** (Sage fears ambiguity): the reading ends on something definite.
11. **Rationed words:** "brave", "hero", "fight": one at most, better none.

## Story test (for any lead)

A person solved a practical problem **on the spot**, **for people who were waiting**, from **what was left** (a shortage, a broken plan, a missing thing); the source shows the audience and, ideally, a **private worry** the waiting people couldn't see. Best if the stopgap outlived the emergency. **Out:** slow research, a fix designed for next time (the Innovator), luck or "accident", rehearsed drills (the Firefighter).

## Verdict on the plan's lead: Jennings Cox and the first Daiquiri (1896): ACCEPT, with conditions

I re-opened Oxford DAIQUIRI pdf 621 and 623 whole. It passes my test better than the plan said:

- **Shortage, audience, what was left:** out of gin, American visitors there, rum he had, limes from a tree in his own garden, sugar, a pitcher, shaved ice (pdf 621).
- **The private worry is on the page**, and it's the persona's nightmare in small: he had the rum "but he feared that it would not be to the taste of the Americans". The plan quoted that. What it didn't quote is the next clause: the limes and sugar went in **"to disguise the rum somewhat."** So the drink that became a renowned classic was a fix for two problems at once: the missing gin, and his own worry about the replacement. The balance of the Daiquiri was born of a worry nobody at the table saw. That's this guest exactly: the answer served with a level face and a doubt behind it.
- **Outcome on the page:** "It was an immediate success."
- **The stopgap outlived the emergency (pdf 623):** newspaper accounts say that for twelve years he never thought to name it and just called it his "rum sour". He'd moved on to the next thing. That's the second mirror (the Problem Solver doesn't stop to keep what they made in a hurry), and it could carry the position (see below).

**Conditions (mine, on top of Hester's):**
1. "The family story goes" for the gin scene (Puig to Wolfe, 1970). Lyman's 1935 "group creation" in one clause, granted plainly, never the point.
2. "Made, or at least supervised" (Oxford). Never "invented", never "by accident", never "father".
3. **Who named it is contested** (Linthicum: a San Carlos Club bartender, 1908; Woon: Cox himself). The reading never says who named it. "Twelve years as his rum sour" is on the page as newspaper accounts' claim, so Hester rules on it.
4. The pleasure isn't on the record. It lives in the guest's whoYouAre only, never as a claim about Cox. His motive beyond "feared… to disguise" is "I like to think".
5. Keep out Constante, Hemingway, Kennedy, the frozen Daiquiri, the navy doctors (pdf 623) and Bacardi as a recipe bottle (as the plan says).
6. Coincidence watch: none that I can see. Daiquirí the town gave the drink its name. That's a fact for Hester, not a coincidence.

**Backup (Bourbon & Branch's staggered reservations):** keep it only as a backup. The clock and the crowd are on the page, but the fix came after two weeks of "a complete disaster", and it was a system so the problem wouldn't happen again. That leans to the Innovator and the Ruler family, not to a riddle solved in the moment.

## Position: two candidates for the room (not locked)

- **A (lead): keep the next one.** He called it his "rum sour" for twelve years and never thought to name it. The Problem Solver forgets their best answers the moment the riddle's over. So the next time the blank comes, they have nothing to remind them that the answer has always come before. Proposal: when the next fix works, stop long enough to give it a name. Gain: the blank gets smaller, because you'll remember how often it filled. (Guard: never "count", which is the Campaigner's.)
- **B: let one person see the second before the answer**, so the fun is shared and the blank stops being something to hide. Risky against the Free Spirit, the Prodigy and the Genius (trap 6), and it asks a Hero to show the very weakness they fear. That makes it a hard truth, earned only if the reading builds the trust first.

## Drink direction (Tomás's), my read for the person

The pitcher, swizzled with shaved ice, poured into small tumblers for everyone waiting: **right for this person.** It's the fix for a whole room at once, and nobody waits longer than anyone else. Conditions from the person and the registry:
- **The spark should come from "what's left":** a kitchen-cupboard or garden thing, makeable tonight, that tastes like a choice rather than a compromise. Ideally it echoes "to disguise the rum somewhat": a touch that solves a problem, not a garnish. Never a specialty bottle the guest would have to go out for.
- ***Off the Tour* (the Naïf) owns the palm-spun swizzle and "until the glass goes white"**, also with white rum. The swizzle can stay in the method (it's on the page), but the closing line and the epigraph can't be about spinning the stick or frost.
- ***Four Shares*** owns the shared vessel "never poured for one". The epigraph mustn't lean on sharing.
- The drink must sound worth having: "a renowned classic", in its first form.
- Exact numbers in the pitcher. Sage fears ambiguity, so "to taste" belongs nowhere in this method.
