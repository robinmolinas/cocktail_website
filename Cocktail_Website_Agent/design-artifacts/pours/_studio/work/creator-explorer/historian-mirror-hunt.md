# Mirror hunt: The Experimentalist (creator-explorer)

Hester, round 1. Persona: artists who find new ways of being creative; embrace chaos; goal innovation; fear status quo / mediocrity (Creator) and entrapment / subordination (Explorer). The turn I hunted for: **someone who used a thing against its rules, and it worked.**

## A (my pick): Angostura poured by the ounce. Ingredient + the Trinidad Sour

**The story in three sentences.** Angostura began as a surgeon's stomach medicine in 1820s Venezuela, and bartenders took it "off-label" into drinks, where it has been measured in dashes ever since. Around 2008–09 bartenders turned that upside down: Valentino Bolognese won Angostura's own European competition with 30 ml of it in one drink, and in Brooklyn Giuseppe Gonzalez pushed it to 45 ml as the *base* of a sour, with a little rye as the accent. Oxford files it among the "crazy stews": absurd on the page, it works in the glass, and it's now served around the world.

**Why it mirrors them.** The Experimentalist doesn't add a new ingredient; they take the one everyone knows and refuse its rule (the accent becomes the base). The fear (mediocrity, the status quo) is answered by a thing that "shouldn't work" and does. The ingredient itself was never meant for drinks at all: medicine used off-label.

**Sources.**
- Oxford Companion, ANGOSTURA BITTERS (pdf 111): Siegert, 1820/1824, medicine, "off-label use as a drink enhancer", most recipes a few dashes. Primary.
- Oxford, ANGOSTURA BITTERS (pdf 112): the oversized label began as a mistake (two brothers designed bottle and label separately); lost a competition, but a judge said it helped identity. Primary.
- Oxford, ANGOSTURA BITTERS (pdf 113): Bolognese's Trinidad Especial, 2008, 30 ml ("most cocktails use less than a tenth of that"); Gonzalez's Trinidad Sour, 45 ml, "not an immediate sensation", later on craft menus with many variations. Primary.
- Oxford, pdf 503 (cocktail-design entry by Conigliaro and Saunders): Trinidad Sour as the example of a drink that "seems absurd on the page" but works. Primary.
- *A Proper Drink*, p. 315: Gonzalez's own account (Clover Club; made for a StarChefs competition he didn't win; Reiner: "Nobody's going to drink it"; he reprinted the menu while she was away; Gertsen spread it). Reliable secondary; his words on the record.

**Risks.**
- **Year conflict (C1):** Oxford 2009, *A Proper Drink* 2008. Reading gives no year.
- **Origin conflict (C2):** Oxford says Bolognese's drink inspired Gonzalez; Gonzalez's own account (Proper) doesn't mention Bolognese. Say "within a year of each other", or tell only Gonzalez's; never "he took it from".
- **The menu story is disputed (C3):** Reiner doesn't remember it and doesn't recall the drink on Clover Club's menu (Proper p. 315). It's the best "this is me" beat, and it can only be told as *his* account ("the way he tells it"), or not at all. Wren's call; I'd keep it out of the reading, or signpost it hard.
- Orgeat = almonds (Tomás: veto).

## B (runner-up): Don Lee and fat-washing. Person + technique

**The story in three sentences.** At PDT in 2007–08, Don Lee was a regular turned bartender "with no stripes" (Madrusan), tinkering with chefs' techniques: popcorn-infused rum after tasting a popcorn soup, cherries he tried to carbonate until the bottle blew up. Fat-washing came from a pastry chef (Sam Mason) through a bartender (Eben Freeman) who tried bacon in bourbon but never served it; Lee put it on the menu ("We didn't know any better at PDT. So we just put it on the menu"), and the Benton's Old-Fashioned sold thousands. Now it's the drink that follows him, and he says it kills him that it probably will, to the grave.

**Why it mirrors them.** Freedom to fail in public (exploding cherries), borrowing from other arts (kitchen, pastry; Oxford traces fat-washing to perfumers' *enfleurage*), and the Explorer's trap: your one famous experiment becoming the cage.

**Sources.** *A Proper Drink* p. 231 (Freeman: bacon-in-bourbon idea born at WD-50, never served), p. 271 (popcorn, exploding cherries, "you came to PDT for the experimental"), p. 275–276 (Lee's words; "haunted"). Oxford, FAT WASHING (pdf 762): enfleurage; Benton's the most widely known fat-washed drink. Primary + reliable secondary.

**Risks.** Year conflict (Proper 2007, Oxford 2008). Benton's is a bourbon Old-Fashioned (same family and base as No Accident) and bacon (veto); butter-washed popcorn rum is dairy. Lee "did not invent fat-washing" (Proper p. 275): never "he invented". PDT's own origin story is "tangled and confused" (Proper p. 272).

## Pick
**A.** Its turn is the cleanest mirror of this persona (the rule itself broken, not a new toy), its core facts sit in Oxford, and it's niche in the way Robin likes. **B** has the stronger ache (the experiment that becomes the cage) and Wren may want it; if so, Tomás needs a fat-wash that isn't bacon or butter, and the reading must not credit Lee with inventing the technique.
