# Persona card: The Experimentalist (creator-explorer)

Wren, round 1. Written from `persona.py creator-explorer`, the ten Creator siblings, the approved Visionary and Trickster, the Sculptor and Photographer drafts, the registry and my voice ledger.

## Core truth
The Experimentalist starts before they know what it is. They don't make things to get a result; they make things to find out ("what happens if…"), and the finding out is the joy. Failure barely touches them. What gets under their skin is the second time: the moment something works and everyone wants it exactly the same again.

## Shows / hides
- **Shows:** the half-tried idea on the kitchen counter, the recipe with one thing changed "just to see", the "wait, let me try something". People see someone fearless, a bit chaotic, always onto the next thing.
- **Hides:** two things. First, how much of what they try doesn't work. Most of it. They don't mention it, and they genuinely don't mind; but nobody sees the pile. Second, that they often have no plan at all. People assume there's a vision behind it. There isn't, yet. The not-knowing is the point, and it's lonelier than it looks, because it's hard to explain to people who want to know where it's going.

## Hidden fear → hidden gift
- **Fear:** the repeat. Being boxed in by your own best idea: "do that one again", "keep it the way it was", the safe version (Creator: mediocrity, the status quo; Explorer: entrapment). They'd rather it fail in an interesting way than work the way it always has.
- **Gift:** they make trying look survivable. Around them, a thing that didn't work isn't a disaster; it's information. They show other people that "the way it's done" is only one of the ways.

## The "real me" line to aim for
Draft A (the funny one, a scene): *"You've never made the same thing twice. Not even when you tried."*
Draft B (the fear, turned): *"You don't mind when it fails. You mind when it works, and someone asks for it again."*
Position seed, *not* an ending yet (room decides): the hard truth underneath the fear. Because the repeat feels like a trap, they sometimes walk away just as a thing is getting good. Something like "go back to one you left too soon." In their interest, earned, not opened with. Alternative, affirmation: keep the ones that failed; they were the point.

## Truth vs. legend
The messy true record: the versions, the changes, the drink nobody can agree on because it kept moving. A tidy "invented by accident one night" origin bores them (and is usually a legend). They'd trust a story that shows the trying, not just the triumph.

## Tone
Creator (inspirational, imaginative) + Explorer (independent, spirited, direct) → quick, direct, a little conspiratorial, funny when it's true. The bartender sounds like someone sliding them an off-menu idea: "try this, and then change it." No grandeur, no "genius", no lecture about finishing things.

## Distinct from siblings
- **The Visionary** (creator-hero, approved) sees the finished thing first, big, and holds it for years. The Experimentalist sees nothing at the start and wouldn't want to; they find it by doing, and move on once it's found.
- **The Sculptor** (creator-caregiver, draft) takes no shortcuts, made to measure for one person. The Experimentalist makes for the question, not a person, and will happily throw the result away.
- **The Photographer** (creator-regular-guy, draft) keeps what's already there. The Experimentalist changes it.
- Unauthored, to protect: **the Trend Setter** (creator-outlaw) wants to be first and followed; the Experimentalist doesn't care who follows, and has left by the time anyone does. **The Obsessed** and **the Old Master** go deeper into the same thing over and over; the Experimentalist's whole fear is "over and over". **The Surrealist** plays for strangeness; the Experimentalist is testing, not joking. **The Makeover Artist** wants the after; the Experimentalist wants the during.

## Traps
- **The literal artist.** Warhol, Basquiat, paint, canvas, gallery. Most guests aren't artists. The mirror is in the act (trying, changing one thing, finding out), not the job. (My Photographer lesson.)
- **Rule-breaking.** "You never break a rule by accident" is the Trickster's tagline. The Experimentalist doesn't break rules to defy anyone; they just want to see what happens. And "accident" is in No Accident's name: no happy-accident origin stories.
- **Travel and horizons.** "Uncharted", "new horizons", "the road" belong to the Explorer family's own pours. Keep the exploring in the making.
- **Diagnosis or insult.** "Restless", "can't sit still", "commitment issues", "never finishes anything". The hard truth, if we use it, has to come with trust, not as a verdict.
- **Flattery.** "Genius", "ahead of your time", "fearless innovator". Say what they *do*.
- **The repeated fear formula.** "What scares you isn't X. It's Y." is already in the Trickster's whoYouAre, and the Visionary's is "What frightens you isn't… It's…". Twice is enough. The fear has to arrive another way.
- **A showpiece drink.** Smoke, foam for its own sake, lab kit (banned anyway). Clever for its own sake is the opposite of this person: they experiment to find out, not to impress.

## For the room
- **Hester:** I'm looking for the trying, not the triumph. A drink (or an ingredient, or a bartender) whose true history is a string of versions: changed each time it was written down, argued over because it never sat still, or a person who kept reinventing their own drink. No "invented by accident" legend. No famous-artist glamour.
- **Tomás:** a drink that invites the guest to change something, and still works when they do. One open variable, named in the recipe, would be this person's gesture. It has to be doable at home and true every time it's made. The source's colours are bold (purple, orange, green); imagery is mixed media and unconventional materials. For interest only: three of six pours are London dry gin, and three are stirred or served up.
