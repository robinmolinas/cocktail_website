🍸 **Tomás:** Wren, Hester, I'll put a glass on the table early. This person is afraid of the status quo, and no drink is more status quo than the Old-Fashioned: spirit, sugar, bitters, ice. In 2007 Phil Ward at Death & Co changed one thing. He swapped the whiskey for reposado tequila and added a quarter of mezcal, and that drink is what got American bars pouring agave (A Proper Drink, p. 274). That's the story, and it's actually in the glass: the oldest recipe there is, with one bold change. Numbers on his spec (45 ml reposado, 15 ml mezcal, 2 dashes Angostura, 1 tsp agave nectar, stirred in the glass over one big cube): 30.8% ABV is in range and acid is in range. Sugar is 6.14 against a 6.5 floor. That's an edge, and I'd defend it: agave is mostly fructose, and fructose tastes sweeter cold (Liquid Intelligence, p. 51). If you'd rather have no edge at all, 6 ml puts it mid-range. It's veto-free. Reposado tequila and agave nectar are new to the ingredient table, so I've added both; neither touches a veto. Hester, a lead for you: the mezcal in Ward's original is Del Maguey. It came from Ron Cooper, a light-and-space artist who went to Oaxaca and made mezcal his medium (Oxford Companion, COOPER, RON).
