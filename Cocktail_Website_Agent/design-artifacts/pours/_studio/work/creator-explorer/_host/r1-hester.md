📜 **Hester:** Wren, Tomás, two candidates for you. They're in `work/creator-explorer/historian-mirror-hunt.md`. What I hunted for was someone who broke a thing's own rule and made it work.

**A (my pick): Angostura poured by the ounce.** It started as a surgeon's stomach tincture: Siegert, Venezuela, first batch in 1824. Drinks were never its intended use. Oxford calls its move into cocktails "off-label", and says most recipes use it by the dash (Oxford Companion, ANGOSTURA BITTERS, pdf 111). Even its oversized label came from a mistake. The brothers designed the bottle and the label separately, the sizes didn't match, and a judge said the mismatch was good for the brand (pdf 112). Then, around 2008–09, two bartenders reversed the rule. Bolognese won Angostura's own competition with 30 ml in one drink, where most cocktails use under a tenth of that. Gonzalez at Clover Club went to 45 ml and made it the *base* of a sour, with the rye as the accent (pdf 113; *A Proper Drink*, p. 315). Oxford's design entry uses it as its example of a drink that looks absurd on paper and works in the glass (pdf 503). That's the mirror: the Experimentalist keeps the familiar ingredient and breaks its rule.

**B: Don Lee and fat-washing at PDT.** Fat-washing passed from a pastry chef to Eben Freeman, who tried bacon in bourbon but never served it. Lee, a regular "with no stripes", put it on the menu because "we didn't know any better." Along the way there was popcorn rum, and cherries he tried to carbonate until the bottle exploded (*Proper*, pp. 231, 271, 275). It hurts more. He says it kills him that the Benton's will probably follow him to the grave. But it's a bourbon Old-Fashioned, which No Accident already is, and it's bacon.

Three guards on A are on the new card, `fact-cards/angostura-trinidad-sour.md`. First, no year: Oxford says 2009, *Proper* says 2008. Second, never "he took it from Bolognese", because Gonzalez's own account doesn't mention him. Third, Gonzalez's story about reprinting the menu behind Reiner's back is the best beat, but Reiner doesn't remember it. It can only be told as *his* telling, or not at all.
