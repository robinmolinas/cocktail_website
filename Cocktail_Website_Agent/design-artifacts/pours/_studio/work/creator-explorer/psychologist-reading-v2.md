## whoYouAre

Hand you instructions and you'll follow them, right up to the moment you don't. You start things before you know what they'll be. That isn't a hole in the plan. It's the part you like best: finding out.

Most of what you try doesn't work, and you honestly don't mind. You rarely mention those ones, and there are more of them than anyone guesses. What gets under your skin is the opposite: something works, and everyone wants it exactly like last time. Being asked to repeat yourself feels like a door closing. Stand next to you long enough and people start saying what you say: "let me try something." You've shown them that something that didn't work isn't a disaster. It's just the next thing to change.

## yours

1. You've probably seen the bottle behind a bar. Almost nobody pours more than a few dashes of it. It's Angostura, and it began in the 1820s as a medicine: a surgeon in Venezuela made it for soldiers' stomachs. It was never meant for drinks. Bartenders borrowed it anyway, and in their hands it stayed a seasoning, never the main thing.

2. Then a bartender in Brooklyn, Giuseppe Gonzalez, made a sour (spirit, citrus and sugar, shaken) with an ounce and a half of it. The bitters became the base. The whiskey, just half an ounce, became the accent. The way he tells it, the drink didn't win the competition he made it for, and he was told nobody would drink it. It wasn't an immediate hit. But it spread, and one of the great reference books on drinks now uses it as its example of a recipe that looks absurd on paper and works in the glass.

3. I like to think he wasn't trying to be clever. He just wanted to know what would happen. That's the question you ask about everything, and like him, you'd rather find out than be told. And it didn't stay his. Bartenders went on to make their own versions of it, which is exactly how you'd want something of yours to be treated.

4. So yours keeps his ounce and a half of bitters and changes the rest. His almond syrup is gone. In its place is a syrup of lemongrass, which sits beautifully beside warm spice and citrus. Then lemon, shaken, and poured into a wide, shallow stemmed glass. And his half ounce of whiskey is now yours to choose: any unsweetened spirit you have open. Gin, mezcal, rum, a smoky whisky. Every one works, and every one makes a different drink.

5. But it didn't win, and he didn't drop it. Somewhere you've got something you left half-tried: a recipe, a plan, an idea you walked away from just as it started working. Go back to it. Not to make it the way it was. To find out what it turns into when you stay.
