# Persona card: The Pure Heart (lover-innocent) · Wren, round 1

Sheet (persona.py): loves without games, without strategy, without keeping score, without pretending to care less than they do. Falls completely. Tender, trusting, easy to hurt. Would rather be hurt than become guarded. Fear: being alone in a world with no one to love that simply. Goal: intimacy/connection. Example: Romeo and Juliet (never shown). Imagery: soft daylight, white fabric, handwritten notes, "sweetness without kitsch".

## The one true thing
Everyone assumes they love this openly because they've never been hurt. They have. Openness isn't their innocence. It's a decision they make again after every hurt, against all the advice to "be more careful". People read them as naive. They saw the risk as clearly as anyone. They just decided that being hurt costs less than being careful.

## What they hide
The bruises. They don't show what it cost, partly because showing it brings the advice ("you need to protect yourself") that they've already decided not to take. So the hurt is the private part. The love is all on show.

## What they fear
Not being hurt again. Becoming guarded: the day they catch themselves playing it cool, waiting an hour to reply, saying "it's fine" when it isn't, and loving with conditions like everyone else. If that happens, the world really has no one in it who loves simply (the sheet's fear, from the inside).

## What they give (said out loud, Robin's 2026-09-30 rule)
Next to them, people stop pretending to care less than they do. They say it back. Being loved without conditions, and without having to earn it, is rare, and they give it to everyone they love.

## Traps
- Naivety or innocence as the trait (the Naive Artist's). Their openness is chosen, after hurt.
- Victimhood: "people take advantage of you" (it's in my test's out-list, and it's the misread they hate most).
- An injustice as the mirror (my Regular Guy ruling): someone wronged is never the picture of a guest's virtue.
- Sugar and kitsch: no hearts, no pink (the Flirt's), no "pure", "innocent", "sweet soul". Tone: tender, plain, a little brave.
- Advising them to protect themselves. Never. The position can't be "be more careful", and it can't be "show the cost" either: that proposal is already used too often (*Night Light*, *Nothing to It*, *Cold Water First*, *A Place Kept*).
- Requiring a partner. The reading covers friends, family, anyone they love. Romeo and Juliet stays on the sheet.
- Tragedy as the payoff, or trust rewarded as a fable (that flatters instead of seeing them).
- Registry words: "first" (Pioneer, Prodigy), "let it show" (One Next Door), "ask" (Geisha), "allowed to" (Four Shares), "pretending" (Lone Voice tagline), "bring" (Knight), "land/unpack" (Fugitive), "Say what you loved" (Bumpkin).

## Story test (sharpened from the plan's)
1. **Love given whole, on the page** (to a person, people or a place), not rumour.
2. **A real letdown on the record after it**, and not an injustice or a debt unpaid.
3. **They didn't take it back:** stayed, kept loving, on the record.
4. **It holds:** it was worth it, without tragedy as the payoff and without reward-for-virtue.
Out: rumour as the core trait; journeys (Knight), elopement and family objection (Fugitive), hosting (Geisha), money lent, health, death as the ending.

## Verdict on the plan's lead (Cato Alexander): replace
Read whole (Oxford ALEXANDER, CATO pdf 90–91):
- **The trait is a rumour.** The only open-handed act is "lending incautiously to his patrons, rumor had it". The recognition would rest on what people said.
- **The hurt is the out-list's.** If the lending is the trait, the mechanism is patrons who didn't repay a man born enslaved. Unsaid, it's still "taken advantage of", and it's an injustice used as a mirror. Said, it's worse.
- **"Stayed open" is a pun.** The 1852 oyster house was a living, not a heart reopened; it lasted barely a year, and "he disappeared from the public eye". Limb 3 isn't on the page.
- **No love in it.** It's hospitality and credit, which is the Geisha's ground. And Cato is Willard's "only rival" on the same Oxford page. Two Lover pours about 1830s New York hosts will read as twins side by side, whatever we keep each from naming.
- Cipriani (backup): still trust rewarded with no hurt (the fable). Out.

## Proposed lead (for Hester to source): Rita Cowan Taketsuru
Oxford TAKETSURU, MASATAKA (pdf 1975), primary, read whole: in Scotland he "found time to woo and marry his landlady's daughter, Rita Cowan". In 1920 "the couple returned to Japan to discover" the whisky plans were on hold (limb 2, on the page). "Taketsuru and Rita lived in Hokkaido for the rest of their lives" (limb 3). Nikka grew to Japan's second-largest whisky maker (NIKKA pdf 1391), and in 2014 NHK broadcast a morning drama "based around Masataka and Rita's love story" (limb 4, Oxford's own words: a love story).
- **Mirror:** she went all in, the life she'd come for wasn't there when she arrived, and she didn't take any of it back. A country made a love story of it ninety years on.
- **Conditions:** centre the arrival and the staying, never the voyage (the Knight) or family objection (the Fugitive; *Rent-Free*'s parents objected to "a stranger"). "Returned" is his word, not hers. His 1934 slight is his and stays out. Wartime suspicion of her, if Hester finds it, stays out (an injustice). Her death (1961) and childlessness stay out. She's the subject, never "the wife who followed". Hester finds one sourced detail of her openness after the letdown (limb 3 at full strength). Dates conflict across Oxford entries (Explorer's plan noted it), so use only the dates that agree.
- **Claims for the host:** Taketsuru was the Explorer's unused Pioneer backup (that batch is done). The Innocent plan holds him as the Dreamer's *conditional* lead ("the hunch, never Rita"), and its Lark lead is running. Settle it before round 3.
- **If it fails:** Hester hunts against the four limbs above (web, secondary). Cato isn't the fallback.
