**Ceilings (every row, and the reading):**
- **Rita has no words and no reasons on record** in anything read. Her love is shown by what she did; anything about what she felt or meant is "I like to think…".
- **Strike the shorthand now:** "she went all in" and "the life she'd come for wasn't there" (r1) aren't on any page. The page's letdown is the firm's: the distillery plan he'd been sent to learn for was on hold when they arrived. Say that.
- **No years** for the voyage, the arrival or the move north (Oxford contradicts itself: C1, C2). The only safe years: the marriage, January 1920 (Itoh), and NHK's 2014 drama.
- "Returned" is his; for her, "arrived" or "went with him".
- "For the rest of her life" (F11) is the sourced form; never "she never went back" or "never left".
- F9 is one secondary fragment: "she taught English and piano" and nothing more (no school, no "to support them", no "immersed herself in Japanese life").
- Never "the mother of Japanese whisky" (L1), never a scene from *Massan* (L2).
- "His company" for Nikka; "they moved north together". Not "she founded" (pdf 1391's "he and his Scottish wife moved … and founded" is one compound sentence; the same entry calls him "its founder", pdf 1975 says "His company", pdf 2156 "founded the firm Nikka": his, three times). Never "beside the distillery": the pages give Hokkaido and the town of Yoichi, not where she lived in it.
- Nothing on any page about what Rita cooked, baked, kept or poured (r3, Tomás's lead 1): no ingredient may be "hers".
- Wren's exclusions: family objection, the secret wedding, the 1934 slight, wartime, her death, childlessness, the voyage.
