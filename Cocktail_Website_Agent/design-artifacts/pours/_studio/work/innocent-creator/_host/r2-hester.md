- **Oxford, MIXOLOGY, THE HISTORY OF (pdf 1332), F6:** the Calimotxo is cross-referenced among the new canon's drinks. The entry says those drinks had "no baggage and no rules", and many felt that too much had been given up for them: complexity, balance, elegance, contemplation. So the people who know the rules frowned at it in print. That frown covers the whole canon, not this drink by name, and its "1960s" dates no drink.
- **Rosie Schaap, who wrote Oxford's entry, *NYT*, 20 May 2013 (F9):** she got "more than a few skeptical glances" ordering it in New York bars, and felt no contrition at all.
- **Simon Creasey, *Guardian*, 30 July 2012 (F10):** a Granada barman saw cola going into a glass of the good local red. He pointed at the house wine in the barrel as the one you mix, and at the bottle as the one you don't. That's your whoYouAre line happening in a real bar: someone explaining how it's usually done, not banning it. Tell the rule, not the telling-off.

Now the point I'd put first. The fuller origin is worse than Oxford's. The Getxo town council's account, as EFE reported it (*Noticias de Gipuzkoa*, 12 August 2022, F11), says the Antzarrak were running the festival and had bought 2,000 litres of wine that had turned. They tried mixes until cola hid the taste and settled on equal parts so they could sell 4,000 litres. Someone even suggested not saying what was in it. In that account the name also joins a friend's nickname to a Basque word it glosses as "ugly", a joke about his looks. Oxford says the word means "short", so the name is contested (C1). "They made the wine good to drink" would contradict the only detailed record. So I'd leave the rescue out of the reading entirely, and give the name only as "after a friend's nickname". Your mirror still stands without it. On the council's account, the name had spread across Euskadi and Navarre by the early 1980s, then across Spain, and in 2022 the festival honoured the friends at fifty.
