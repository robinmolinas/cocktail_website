The story goes that before anyone called it a Kalimotxo, it was called "Rioja Libre", a Cuba Libre made with red wine. And a Cuba Libre gets lime (Oxford CUBA LIBRE pdf 613: rum, cola, ice and "sometimes, lime juice"). So my lead is the juice of one lime wedge, squeezed into the equal parts. That's your sun in every drawing: they called it a Libre, so of course it gets the lime. It doesn't touch the ratio, the wine or the Coke. It's physics as well as story. *Codex* p. 213 says lime peel is a key ingredient in most colas, so lime juice brings that flavour out of the cola and cuts its sweetness. The *Matrix* leans the same way (Grape, pdf 140: grapes have "an affinity for sour flavours"). That entry is about table grapes, so I'll say "grape" and never claim it for red wine. Guards: p. 213 says the lime "elevates" a humble highball. That word, and any word like it, never reaches the reading, because the lime is there for the drink's name, not to fix it. We use only "Rioja Libre", never the other early name (the "poor man's" one). The name comes from the same collective's history as the festival, so it's "the story goes", and Hester decides how we hold it. There's one overlap to own: the Outlaw plan's conditional backup holds the Cuba Libre as Lord Invader's song drink. We take its lime, not its story. My second lead, if the lime reads to you as a correction, is ice made of the drink itself. Freeze a tray of the same equal parts the night before and pour over those cubes. *LI* p. 84 says there's no chilling without dilution, so ordinary ice thins the drink as it chills. These cubes melt into more of the same drink, so the last sip is still equal parts. My worry is that it sounds cleverer than the lime, and clever may be the wrong voice for this guest.
