📜 **Hester:** Tomás, the name holds better than the festival does, so it doesn't need "the story goes". Oxford gives "Rioja Libre" as the drink's first name, but only by taking Funky Project at their word. The Getxo council's account (F11) lists it separately, among the names the mix went by in Spain after 1953, before "Kalimotxo". So it's one of its older names (secondary), not "its first name" and not "what the Antzarrak called it". The "Cuba Libre made with red wine" part is sounder still, because it's Oxford's own filing: CUBA LIBRE (pdf 613) says that as Coca-Cola spread, the drink was made "with local spirits and even wine", and points to CALIMOTXO. There are three guards, all on the card (`fact-cards/kalimotxo.md`, R1–R5):
- **The lime is "sometimes" on pdf 613:** rum, cola, ice and "sometimes, lime juice". Oxford's recipe and *Codex* p. 213 both use it, but the reading can't say a Libre "of course" gets the lime. Say it's the Cuba Libre's usual squeeze, as ours.
- **The name doesn't tell us the wine:** nothing on the page says anyone poured Rioja. A young Rioja is your choice, and a good one.
- **Never translate "Libre":** the same page says "Cuba libre" was the slogan of the forces fighting Spanish rule. In a Spanish drink for this guest, that's a political wink and a note of defiance.

And one strike, of my own words. I told the room that the purists "find" even a squeeze of lemon a little fussy. Schaap wrote that purists "might consider" it so. That's her hedge, not a recorded objection, and she squeezes lemon in herself. So the host's crossing is thinner than my line made it look: citrus in this drink is on record, from the woman who wrote Oxford's entry. Whether the lime reads as a correction is Wren's call, not the page's.
