Wren, round 1, 2026-10-04. Sources read: `persona.py magician-innocent`, my Magician sketch (`plans/work/magician/psychologist-sketches.md`), the plan row (`plans/magician.md`), Oxford FLAIR BARTENDING (pdf 797–798) and TGI FRIDAY'S (pdf 2014) whole, Oxford JOHNNIE WALKER (pdf 1111–1112) whole, WHISKY (pdf 2136 snippet), CAOL ILA (pdf 402 snippet).

## The one true thing

They never worry that it won't work. They worry that it will, and then keep going faster than they can follow.

They start things on excitement, a step ahead of being ready, because the excitement is the point. And the things they start take. That's the part no sibling carries: their fear isn't failure, a wrong result, being found out or being ignored. It's success with its own legs: the idea that doubles, the thing everyone picks up, the name that ends up meaning something they never chose.

## Hide / fear / give

- **They hide the "almost".** Everyone sees the delight. What they don't say is the small, guilty wish that it would slow down, just for a week, because it's more than they can hold. They keep it quiet because it sounds ungrateful: who complains that something's working? (This is where the second half of them lives: the wish to stay good and safe, and the unease of a dilemma with no wrong party in it.)
- **They fear unintended results** (the sheet's own word). Not a disaster: a mess. The thing growing in a direction they didn't pick, carrying their name, or someone else's they care about.
- **They give the start.** Things begin because of them. Their excitement is catching, and plenty of what exists around them exists because they couldn't wait. Underneath, a plain hope that what they start might change something for the better. (Say this kindness out loud once in the reading: Robin's rule from batch caregiver.)

## Traps

- **Age.** "Young", "fresh-faced", "new kid": out. The guest may be sixty and new at something. "New" means new to *this*.
- **Mickey, Fantasia, brooms, buckets, hats, spells**: family trap; no "magic" puns. The image brief inherits this.
- **Labels:** "apprentice", "trainee", "founder", "entrepreneur", "boss".
- **Shame or a lecture** ("power grows faster than wisdom" as a moral). The mishap is funny and theirs, never a scolding.
- **Casting the guest as the story's man.** Alexander Walker is "steely" and "forceful" on the page; the guest isn't asked to be either. The mirror is his one letter, nothing more.
- **Brand worship.** The story is a sentence a man wrote and a nickname nobody asked for, not a sales history.

## Story test (for this row)

1. Someone took up a thing they'd been handed or had just begun, and set it going with energy. (Soft: the person carries "before you're ready"; never claim it of the story.)
2. It ran away in scale, faster than they could steer, ideally in their own words.
3. An elder is in the story, and the runaway touches the elder (their shop, their name, their method).
4. The cost is a mess or an overload, never harm.
5. The joy survived, and nothing was shut down.

Out: injury, disgrace, prodigy stories, happy-accident payoffs, invented feelings.

## Verdict on the plan's lead (Mescall): replace

Mescall, read whole on Oxford pdf 797–798 and TGI FRIDAY'S pdf 2014:

- The room's lesson is that the story has to be in the glass, and Tomás's own note says the page names no drink for him: there's nothing to bridge to.

**Replacement lead: Alexander Walker and the doubling (Oxford, JOHNNIE WALKER, pdf 1111–1112, primary).** Unclaimed: no plan or pour uses it (grepped plans, pours, rooms; Explorer's Compass Box backup only mentions Glaser as a former Johnnie Walker marketer).

- 1820: John Walker bought a grocery, wine and spirits shop in Kilmarnock and began selling whisky. His son Alexander turned that whisky into a true brand (trademarked Old Highland Whisky by the mid-1860s).
- After the London office (~1871) the business grew fourfold 1865–1880, then "from 1880 it exploded". In 1885 Alexander wrote that his business seemed to double every year, and that he could "almost wish it would double itself back", because it was "more than one head can carry". (Test 2, in his own words; test 4, an overload, no harm.)
- He refused to advertise ("Quality will stand on its merit"). Oxford: into the vacuum that left, in the 1880s and 1890s, stepped the public's nickname "Johnny Walker", which the Walker family disdained as disrespectful to the founder. (Test 3: the runaway took the elder's name. Fear: an unintended result, grown from his own principle.)
- 1886: a limited company brought long-standing employees into the business with his sons. (Test 5: it was shared, not shut down.)

**Passes 2, 3, 4, 5 on the page. 1 is soft:** nothing says Alexander was unready; the reading carries that from the person.

## Position direction (to rule on in round 3)

The hidden thing is the "almost": the wish it would slow down, never said. So the position is to say it while it's still funny, and get a second head on it before it's more than one can carry. Not "pass it on" (*Down the Line*), not "tell them you were scared" (*Nothing to It*), not "slow down" (that takes the joy). The joy has to survive the advice: keep starting things, and say "this is a lot" out loud early.
