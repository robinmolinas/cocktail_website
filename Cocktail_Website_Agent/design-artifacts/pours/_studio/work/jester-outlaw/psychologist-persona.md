# Persona card: The Scandalmonger (jester-outlaw) · Wren, r1, 2026-10-04

Source: persona.py jester-outlaw (essence, story, goal Pleasure, fear Boredom, drivers Build connection / LEAVE A MARK). The persona's named example is never referenced, nodded to or echoed.

## The one true thing (no sibling or cousin has it)
They say at the table what everyone else only says in the corridor afterwards. They'd rather be blamed for the gasp in the room than agreed with in private. The outrageous line comes out like a full stop, but it's meant as a question: *well? who else thinks so?*

## What they hide
- That they enjoy it. The gasp is a real pleasure, and they don't apologise for it. And some nights it's only for the gasp, and they know exactly which ones. (Honest, gentle: the persona itself says "sometimes it is".)
- That the line is a dare, not a verdict. They're waiting for one person to say it back, in front of everyone. The gasp is the opening move, not the point.
- That going too far is often deliberate: said that far, the honest opinion everyone actually holds suddenly sounds mild, and someone dares to say it.

## What they fear
Good manners as a way of swallowing things: the gasp absorbed, someone asking who'd like more potatoes, and the agreement saved for the corridor. Being shocked at in public and nodded at in private. (Distinct from the Trickster's "ignored", the Devil's Advocate's "there they go again", the Stand-up's "polite evening where nobody admits anything".)

## What they give (said out loud in the reading, once)
They take the blame for the first line so the honest conversation can happen in the room, now, not later in whispers. They turn "how dare you" into "hang on, is it true?" That's worth something, and they rarely get thanked for it.

## Siblings (Jester) and how this one differs
- Morale Booster (*Anyway*): makes fun on purpose for a group. Here: makes trouble on purpose, for the truth or the gasp.
- Wacky One (*For Kicks*): absurd on purpose. Here: offensive on purpose.
- Stand-up (*Is It Just Me*): says what everybody does, so nobody's alone. Here: says what nobody will, and stands alone in the gasp. **Owns** the polite-evening fear and the "half a second" pause.
- Alternative Comic (*Unrehearsed*): owns "the second before" the laugh, "watching every face", "a real reaction instead of a polite one". Keep all three out.
- Wise Talker (*The Wink*): humour in hardship. Naïf: sincere, laughed at. Tease: timing. Elf/Imp: harmless, small, rules. Straight Man and Cynic: unauthored (the Cynic will take the preachers' lie; this one keeps the gasp aimed at the powerful).
## Cousins outside the family (closest risks)
- Subverter (*With the Bite In*, outlaw-jester): hides the truth inside a joke so it gets past. This one doesn't disguise it: said straight, on purpose, for the gasp. Owns "aim it higher", "taken with the point gone", and its own New York court case (authorship): no court-and-money arc.
- Wise Fool (*In Good Part*): honours the powerful first, goes first as the silly one, protects face. Owns "a story everyone has agreed to believe". The Scandalmonger gives no rattle and saves nobody's face.
- Lone Voice (*On the Record*): printed attack, came back with more, the quiet later ("they get to say it second", "didn't go quiet"). No printed-again arc here; no "second".
- Trickster (*No Accident*): "rather shake a room than disappear", "being ignored". The persona's "rather be hated than ignored" stays out.
- Devil's Advocate (*Hear Me Out*): tests ideas from the other side; position "say plainly what you think". Campaigner (*Not Too Polite*): "someone had to say it", for the absent. Bad Influence (*Accomplices*): "drop the act". Hidden Talent: "change the subject" (phrase owned).

## Traps
- Cruelty: the target is the powerful or the pompous, never the weak, never faith itself (a religious guest must feel safe).
- Excusing it: never "you're secretly brave", never a cause. Never "you're just misunderstood".
- Flattery: "you're the only honest one" is a compliment. Recognition is "you enjoy the gasp, and you're waiting for someone to answer it".
- Libel read as lying: the reading never implies the guest says false things, and never says Croswell's claim was true or false.
- Gendered or tabloid language; the persona example in any form.

## Story test
A person who put the unsayable out loud (or in print) on purpose, aimed up at the powerful or the pompous, with the reaction on record and a price paid, and whose line outlived the price. Out: secrets, hoaxes, cruelty to the weak, a cause, deaths.

## Position (candidates, for the reading)
- Next time the room swallows the gasp and moves on, don't top it with a bigger line. Ask the question your line was hiding, out loud, and wait. (Gains: the answer they actually wanted, in the room.)
- Keep the ones you'd stand behind if asked "is it true, and why say it?"; enjoy the rest without pretending they're principled. (Croswell/Hamilton: "truth, with good motives".)
