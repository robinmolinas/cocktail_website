# Anchors: lover-caregiver (The Geisha)

Hester, v2, round 5 (2026-09-30), with audit v1. Story ruled by Wren in round 3: Orsamus Willard of the City Hotel, with guards. Card: `_studio/fact-cards/orsamus-willard.md` (F1–F17, C1–C3). Drink ruled round 4: **cold, made after Willard's Apple-toddy** (sugar cut: the one owned change). The hot row is struck.

**Ceilings (every row, and the reading):**
- The ten-year guest is "the story people told about him", never a dated event.
- The only date is "until 1848". Nothing else is dated, including the doors.
- Always "a British army captain", never "Alexander" on his own (the Pure Heart tells Cato Alexander's story).
- Nobody is named as the one who asked him back ("lured", no agent).
- The captain's motive isn't on record. "Repaid the favor" is Wondrich's joke, and "the care came back" is our reading.
- Only the Apple-toddy is Willard's on the captain's page (C3).
- Never "his toddy" for a hot glass, and never "his recipe" for changed proportions.
- Never "this recipe" or "its maker" for Willard and our glass. Willard told the captain the recipe; he didn't invent the Apple Toddy (earliest citation 1792, *Imbibe!* pdf 138).
- Never "one person wrote him down": several people wrote about him (Morning Post 1829, Murray 1839, a Buffalo reporter 1851). He wrote nothing himself.
- The Apple Toddy was "something of a signifier of Americanness" only early on, before the julep and the cocktail took the role (*Imbibe!* pdf 138). Never "stood for the country".
- No photographic memory, no titles ("most famous", "first celebrity"), no death or grave, no julep, no ice pioneering.

## Not anchors (dossier only)
- The unused first name ("nobody ever used it", *Imbibe!* pdf 20; Wondrich's "perhaps because it was Orsamus"): out of the reading (Wren r3), kept as the one-way-attention fact.
- The retirement and return: retired once, "lured out of retirement" in 1843 when the hotel had failed, stayed until 1848 (Oxford pdf 2194). Not in Wren's beats. If it comes back, "until 1848" is the only date, and nobody is named as the one who asked.
- Oxford calls the things he did for guests "eccentricities" (pdf 2193), and he "would do anything possible to accommodate a guest's desires". That's the servility trap, so it stays out.
- The dates conflict (card C1): the start year, the julep year, and whether he went home in 1848 or 1849.
- Wondrich's "half an apple per drink" and 30–45 minutes at 350°F are modern notes. Willard gives no time ("till thoroughly roasted and quite soft").
- On the captain's page, "fourth part" doesn't say whether it's by weight or volume, and nothing says the apple is strained. Changed proportions = "after Willard's".
- The running head of the captain's p. 300 is "Receipts for Topers" (heavy drinkers). Keep it out.
