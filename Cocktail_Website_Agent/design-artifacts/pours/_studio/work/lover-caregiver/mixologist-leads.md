## Read on the page this round
- *Imbibe!* pdf 139: Thomas 1862 Apple Toddy (small bar-glass; 1 tbsp fine white sugar; 1 wine-glass [2 oz] cider brandy; half a baked apple; glass two-thirds full of boiling water; nutmeg). Notes: "the great Willard, whose iced version is one of four of his recipes to survive, preferred plain grape brandy"; Wondrich: "listen to Willard (preferably with a nice VSOP cognac)"; apples peeled, cored, "wrap them in wet brown paper as Willard suggests", 350°F for 30–45 min, or "as Willard suggests, roast them in the embers"; "Half an apple per drink should do" (Wondrich); 1 tbsp sugar, "no place to skimp on the sweetness"; Steward & Barkeeper's Manual 1869: "only drank in cold weather", "a little strong", 4 oz spirit. Execution: sugar + splash of boiling water in a heated mug, add spirit and apple, stir until the pulp dissolves, 1–2 oz more boiling water.
- *Imbibe!* pdf 138: the Toddy's water, "about one part spirit to one and a half to two parts water", as hot as possible; Wondrich favours Demerara.
- *Imbibe!* pdf 175: Captain Alexander "repaid the favor by recording four of the Great One's recipes"; the one printed there is the Cock-Tail (1833). **Willard's iced Apple Toddy is not printed on any page I read.**
- Codex p. 38: a toddy is an Old-Fashioned with hot water; 4 oz water to 2 oz spirit baseline; heat raises the perceived alcohol, so toddies need a bit more sweetener.
- Oxford TODDY pdf 2034–2035: nutmeg a traditional but optional fillip; Apple Toddy with roasted apple and whisky, rum or brandy.
- Matrix, Pome Fruit (pdf 196): best pairings include brandy and wine; surprising: basil, crab, sage, olive, peas. Basil is *Before the Room*'s, sage is *A Brother's Care*'s leaf; olive and peas don't belong in a hot toddy. Consulted and set aside.

## Direction
- Hot, Thomas's build, **made the way Willard liked it**: grape brandy (a VSOP cognac, style only, no named bottle), apples wrapped in wet brown paper and baked (a wetted paper bag at home). One owning clause: "Thomas made it with apple brandy; I followed Willard, who preferred grape brandy."
- Why hot: the only recipe on the page is Thomas's hot one; Willard's iced one is named, not printed; ice is fenced out of Willard's story (plan); the Apple Toddy is a cold-weather drink.
- Nutmeg off (`nuts`), so veto-free. No replacement spice needed.
- Glass: small heatproof glass, warmed with hot water first (plan's shape; not *Off Duty*'s teacup).

## Story leads that are physics
1. The bake is 30–45 minutes: about as long as it takes someone to get to you. Put the apples in when you make the call.
2. The count: half an apple per drink (Wondrich). Bake a half for someone you haven't asked yet, then ask them.
3. The grape brandy is attention going back to Willard: his preference, kept, by the guest who makes it.
