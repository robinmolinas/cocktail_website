## Still standing

| # | where | as written in v2 | verdict and fix |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A1 | epigraph | "The captain who wrote this recipe down said its maker never forgot a face." | **Fail.** Two problems, and Tomás's "toddy" fixes only the first. (1) "This recipe": our sugar isn't Willard's. **"This toddy" passes**, because it names the drink, not our proportions. (2) "Its maker" still fails. Willard didn't invent the Apple Toddy: its earliest mention is a 1792 comedy (*Imbibe!* pdf 138), the year he was born. The captain gives the recipe as "says Mr. Willard… is thus made", so Willard told him how it was made. Wren's call on words, as long as the man is the one who **made it for him or told him**, never "its maker". For example: *"The captain who wrote this toddy down said the barman who made it never forgot a face."* (Wondrich has Willard mixing for the captain, pdf 175.) |
| A2 | y2 s1 | "He almost never left the hotel, and he'd do anything he could to make a guest comfortable." | **Fail (signpost).** Oxford lists both among the stories told of him (pdf 2193, "no shortage of stories of his eccentricities"). y1's signpost doesn't carry into a new paragraph. Fix: "They said he almost never left the hotel…" (or similar). |
| A3 | y4 s1 | "One person he served did write him down." | **Fail.** Several people wrote about him (the *London Morning Post* 1829, Murray 1839, a Buffalo reporter 1851, "one patron recalled": Oxford pdf 2192–94; *Imbibe!* pdf 20–22). After "no account of his own life", this reads as the only one. What's true: he wrote nothing himself, and a guest wrote down his recipe. Fix: "A guest did write down one thing of his." Or drop the sentence and start with "A British army captain who drank at his bar…". |
