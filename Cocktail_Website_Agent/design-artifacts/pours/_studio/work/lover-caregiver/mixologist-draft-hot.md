# Tomás: the hot alternative (use it if the ruling is hot)

Spec: `mixologist-spec-hot.json` (style `hot`). If this wins, I'll copy it to `_studio/specs/lover-caregiver.json` and swap these sections into `mixologist-draft.md`.

**Glassware:** a small heatproof glass (about 250 ml), warmed with hot water first
**Contains:** veto-free

## Recipe
| amount | item | note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| about 55 g | roasted apple (half a medium apple, peeled and cored) | wrapped in wet brown paper and roasted soft, the way Willard wrote his |
| 60 ml | brandy (grape brandy: a VSOP cognac, or any aged grape brandy of about 40%) | Jerry Thomas used apple brandy; I used the grape brandy Willard preferred (Wondrich) |
| 2½ tsp (about 10 g) | sugar | Thomas's full tablespoon runs too sweet with the apple's own sugar |
| 100 ml | boiling water | |

## Method
1. Peel and core the apples, half an apple for each drink. Wrap each one in wet brown paper and roast at 180°C (350°F) for 30 to 45 minutes, until completely soft.
2. Warm the glass with hot water and pour it out. Put in the sugar and a splash of boiling water, and stir.
3. Add the brandy and half a hot roasted apple, and stir until the apple has broken down into the drink.
4. Top up with the rest of the boiling water, stir, and serve with a spoon.

**closingLine:** *Put the apples in, then call the one you're hoping will come.*

## Checks (the parts that differ)
- **Structure:** Old-Fashioned family, hot toddy (Codex p. 38). This is Thomas's 1862 build (Imbibe pdf 139) with Willard's brandy and his wet-paper roasting, so the copy never calls it "his": the heat is Thomas's.
- **Balance:** `balance.py`: **11.1% ABV, 7.25 g sugar/100 ml, 0.10% acid, balanced** (hot 10–13 / 5.5–7.5 / 0–0.3). With 120 ml of water: 10.2%, 6.65 g, balanced. Thomas's full tablespoon (12.5 g) reads **OUT: 8.32 g**, so 2½ tsp. A very sweet apple (13%) would put it at 7.75 g, just over.
- **Allergens:** contains [], veto-free. Nutmeg off.
- **Registry:** a second hot toddy after *Off Duty* (Irish whiskey, teacup, "Nobody makes this one for themselves. And it won't wait."). The base, glass and gesture all differ, but the neighbour is close.
