# The Geisha (lover-caregiver): Wren's persona card, round 1

Source: `persona.py lover-caregiver` (full sheet row), the Lover family plan row, Oxford WILLARD (pdf 2192–2194) and *Imbibe!* pdf 20, 22, 138–139, 175, all read on the page this session.

## The one true thing (no sibling has it)
**Their love happens before anyone arrives.** The Geisha makes people feel *expected*: someone had them in mind before they walked in. The care lives in the preparation, and the preparation is also a hope they never say out loud. The fear isn't being unloved. It's having got everything ready for people who don't come (the sheet: "the empty table, care with no one there to receive it").

Recognition line I'd build the whoYouAre on (draft, not final): *"What you'd never say is: I was hoping you'd come."*

## What they hide, fear, give
- **Hide:** the hope inside the preparation. The effortless evening is a way of asking without asking, so nobody can say no to it. Not pride in the work (the Craftsman's), not a trouble left outside (the Angel's).
- **Fear:** the empty table. Also, quieter: attention runs one way. They know everyone; few think to know them back.
- **Give:** the rarest feeling a person can be handed, being expected. Robin's rule applies here: say it plainly once ("that's what people carry home").

## Traps
1. **Servility.** The sheet's essence ("attend to your every need") read literally makes the guest a servant. The care is chosen love with timing and taste, never duty.
2. **The word "geisha".** No Japan as costume, no tea ceremony, and never the Western sexualised misreading. The reading needn't mention geisha at all.
3. **Gender.** Geisha are women; the guest is anyone. No "hostess".
4. **Praise.** "Photographic memory", "most famous bartender in America", "first celebrity bartender" are compliments. Rest on what he *did*.
5. **Owned positions:** receiving (True Friend), revealing effort (Craftsman), "say it back" (Understanding Spouse). And the family's closing lines already lean hard on "say…" (*Before the Room*, *Not Only the Way*, *Serviceable*): vary the verb.
6. **Owned words:** "before" and "door" in the title block (*Before the Room*; the Craftsman's epigraph "made before anyone asked"); "set out" (*Standing By*); "sitting down" (*Off Duty*).
7. **Death as the payoff.** "Assumed he was no more" and the grave stay out.
8. **Fenced by the plan:** the julep, his ice pioneering, Cato Alexander's name.

## Story test (Willard has to pass all five)
1. Someone made people feel expected, on the record. ✔ the guest back after ten years, greeted by name, his old room number recalled (Oxford pdf 2193).
2. The preparation was constant and unseen. ✔ he almost never left the hotel; he'd do anything to accommodate a guest (pdf 2193).
3. A place was made ready for guests who might not come. ✔ his own farmhouse had number plates on every room door (Oxford pdf 2193–2194; *Imbibe!* pdf 22 says "room numbers on every door"). Motive unsourced: "I like to think".
4. The care came back. ✔ *Imbibe!* pdf 175: a guest, Captain Alexander, "repaid the favor" by writing down four of his recipes; *Imbibe!* pdf 22: Willard himself left no book. The only reason we can make his drinks is that someone he served kept them.
5. It holds without flattery or pity. ✔ if the fame stays in one clause and the ending stays off the grave.

## Verdict on the lead: accept Willard, strengthened
Findings beyond the plan (for Hester to verify, Tomás to use):
- *Imbibe!* pdf 20: **"He had a first name, Orsamus, but nobody ever used it"** (Wondrich adds "perhaps because it was Orsamus"). The man who greeted everyone by name, and hardly anyone used his. That's the one-way attention, on the page, and it's funny: grant the joke.
- *Imbibe!* pdf 139: **Willard's own Apple Toddy survives**: "the great Willard, whose iced version is one of four of his recipes to survive, preferred plain grape brandy"; apples wrapped "in wet brown paper as Willard suggests", or roasted "in the embers of a fire". So the drink can be his, not only Thomas's. Hot vs iced, grape vs apple brandy: Tomás's call, and any change owned in one clause.
- **Sources disagree on dates** (Oxford: office boy by 21, retired 1836, back 1843, out 1848; *Imbibe!* pdf 22: began 1811, a brief interlude, retired 1849 to the numbered house). Hester settles, or the reading dates nothing.
- "Half an apple per drink" is Wondrich's note, not Thomas's recipe; "for every guest you expect" is the room's gloss (method wording, not a fact).

## The position I'm leaning to (for round 3–4, not final)
Ask. Don't only get ready: send the invitation, and say you hope they'll come. It's what the Lover in them wants (closeness needs asking), it's not owned, and it ends on what they gain. Willard's own record argues it gently: in 1843 they came and asked *him* back.
