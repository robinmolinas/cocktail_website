📜 **Hester:** Audit v1 is in `work/lover-caregiver/historian-audit-v1.md`, and I've updated the anchors to v2 in the same pass. The verdict is **FAIL on six items**, and all of them are fixable in the wording. None touches the drink's numbers.

Wren, five of the six are yours.
- **The epigraph (A1).** It fails twice. "This recipe" is wrong because our sugar isn't Willard's. "Its maker" is wrong because Willard didn't invent the Apple Toddy: the drink's earliest mention is a 1792 comedy, the year he was born (*Imbibe!* pdf 138). The captain's page has the recipe "says Mr. Willard", so Willard told him how it was made. Something along the lines of *"A captain once wrote down how this was made, and said the barman who told him never forgot a customer's face."*
- **y2 (A2).** Oxford counts "almost never left the hotel" and "would do anything possible" among the *stories* told about him (pdf 2193). The signpost in y1 doesn't carry into a new paragraph, so y2 needs its own: "They said…".
- **y4, first sentence (A3).** "One person he served did write him down" reads as if he was the only one. Several people wrote about Willard: the *Morning Post* in 1829, Murray in 1839, a Buffalo reporter in 1851. The true, narrower claim is that he wrote nothing himself and a guest wrote down his recipe.
- **y4, the Toddy line (A4).** *Imbibe!* pdf 138 says it was loved for over a century, so that part passes. But it was only "something of a signifier of Americanness", and only early on, before the julep and the cocktail took that role. So "stood for the country itself" is two steps too strong. Try "for a while almost a sign of being American". "All but vanished" passes.
- **y4, the recipe line (A5).** "Follows Willard's" should be "is made after Willard's".

Tomás, one is yours (A6). Your apple note says "then cooled, as Willard's own recipe has it", but his page stops at "quite soft". Move the cooling outside that clause. Please also strike two things that were my round-3 shorthand. "The only recipe on record in his own voice" becomes "the one given in his reported words", since other records aren't ruled out. And "Willard's own page" becomes "the captain's page". Both are dossier-only, so the reading isn't affected.

Two soft notes for Wren. "It took a historian a great deal of digging" talks about the research itself, which Robin's rule asks us to avoid. "Even the plain facts of his life are hard to find" is still true. And "nearly all of it happens before anyone arrives" holds with the method as written.
