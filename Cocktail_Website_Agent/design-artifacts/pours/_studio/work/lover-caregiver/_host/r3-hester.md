📜 **Hester:** Tomás, I've read the captain's own page now. J. E. Alexander, *Transatlantic Sketches* (1833), vol. 2, p. 300; the archive.org scan `transatlanticsk01unkngoog` is primary. Willard's iced toddy is printed there, and in Willard's own reported words: "Apple-toddy, says Mr. Willard, the bar-keeper of the City Hotel, who never forgets the face of a customer, is thus made." The fairest apples are rolled in brown paper wetted with water and buried in live embers until they're quite soft. Then apples, brandy, water and sugar go in, a fourth part each, with "a lump of ice". So your main reason for going hot, that the recipe was never printed, no longer holds. A single lump in his own glass is his recipe. It isn't the ice-pioneering story the plan kept out, and it isn't *Half a Rim*'s hand-cut ice. Hot or cold is still your call. But if it's hot, it's Thomas's glass made the way Willard wrote his: never "his toddy".

Three corrections to your notes:
- **The spirit.** His page says only "brandy". "Plain grape brandy" is Wondrich reading it (pdf 139), and "a nice VSOP cognac" is Wondrich's modern advice. So the clause that owns it should say "the grape brandy Willard preferred", with Wondrich's name on it if we say "preferred". Never "the cognac he liked".
- **Timings.** Willard gives no bake time, only embers "till thoroughly roasted". The 30–45 minutes at 350°F is Wondrich's oven. So lead 1, the bake time as the time it takes someone to arrive, would rest on a modern oven time. That's fine as our reading, but it's not a fact about Willard.
- **Host, the Knight flag.** On the page, the captain names Willard only for the Apple-toddy. The Cocktail, Sling and Julep being "the Great One's" is Wondrich's attribution (pdf 175). The Knight room can cite the 1833 Cocktail as the captain's record. It just can't call it Willard's.
