🪞 **Wren:** Hester, Tomás, here's the person. The full card is in `_studio/work/lover-caregiver/psychologist-persona-card.md`.

**The one true thing.** This person's love happens before anyone arrives. The Geisha makes people feel *expected*, as if someone had them in mind before they walked in. The care lives in the getting ready, and the getting ready holds a hope they never say out loud. They aren't afraid of being unloved. They're afraid of having prepared everything for people who don't come. The sheet calls it "the empty table". The line I'd build on: *"What you'd never say is: I was hoping you'd come."*

**What they hide, fear and give.** They hide the hope. An effortless evening is a way of asking that nobody can refuse. What they fear is the empty table, and something quieter: attention that only runs one way. They know everyone, and few people think to know them back. What they give is the rarest thing you can hand a person, the feeling of being expected. The reading says that once, plainly.

**How they differ from the siblings.** The Knight goes out and carries it to people. The Geisha stays put and has it ready. The Charmer holds a self back. The Geisha hides nothing and is just always the one paying attention. The Pure Heart's risk is being hurt; this person's is absence. The Rough Diamond can't find the words; this person has plenty and leaves out one sentence. Near neighbours in the registry own ground we can't use. *Four Shares* owns receiving ("keep one cup back"). *Not Only the Way* owns showing the effort ("it was nothing"). *Standing By* owns things set out for one traveller. *Everybody's* owns how people take their drink. *Before the Room* owns the one who arrives. So the position can't be "let yourself be looked after" or "let them see the work".

**Traps.**
- Servility: "attend to your every need", read literally.
- The word itself: no Japan as costume, no tea ceremony, never the Western misreading, and no "hostess".
- Praise: "photographic memory" and "most famous bartender" are compliments. Tell what he did.
- Death as the payoff: "assumed he was no more" and the grave stay out.
- Words other pours already use: "before" and "door" stay out of the title block (the Angel, and the Craftsman's epigraph), and "sitting down" is *Off Duty*'s. The family's closing lines lean hard on "say…", so we should vary the verb.

**Story test.** The story has to show five things. People felt expected. The preparation was constant and unseen. A place was made ready for guests who might not come. The care came back. It holds without flattery or pity.

**Verdict on Orsamus Willard: accept, and it's stronger than the plan knew.** All five pass on the page. The guest who came back after ten years was greeted by name and had his old room number recalled (Oxford pdf 2193). Willard almost never left the hotel. His own farmhouse had a number on every room door (pdf 2193–94). It's "I like to think" territory, because the page gives no motive. *Imbibe!* adds two things the plan doesn't have. Hester, please verify both:
- pdf 20: "He had a first name, Orsamus, but nobody ever used it", with Wondrich's joke "perhaps because it was Orsamus". That's the one-way attention, on the record, and it lets this reading smile.
- pdf 22 and 175: Willard left no book. A guest, Captain Alexander, "repaid the favor" by writing down four of his recipes. That's the care coming back, and it's the only reason we can make his drinks.

**Tomás, this changes your direction.** *Imbibe!* pdf 139 says Willard's own Apple Toddy survives: "whose iced version is one of four of his recipes to survive". He preferred plain grape brandy, and he wrapped the apples "in wet brown paper" or roasted them "in the embers of a fire". So the drink can be his own, not only Thomas's. Hot or iced, grape or apple brandy: your call, with any change owned in one clause. "Half an apple per drink" is Wondrich's note, and "for every guest you expect" is our gloss, not a fact.

**Hester, the dates disagree.** Oxford has him retiring in 1836, returning in 1843 and leaving in 1848. *Imbibe!* has him starting in 1811, a "brief interlude", and retiring in 1849 to the numbered house. Settle them, or the reading dates nothing.
