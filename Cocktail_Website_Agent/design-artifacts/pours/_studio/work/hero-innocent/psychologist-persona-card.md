## The persona, as the sheet gives it
At the very start of becoming someone. Sure there's more to them than this, "without a shred of proof yet". Brave the way beginners are: unsure and willing. Each trial survived makes them more themselves. Trusts mentors, "sometimes too quickly". Dreads the moment it counts, and turning out not to be up to it. Goal mastery; fear weakness (Hero), punishment and danger (Innocent). Sunrise yellow.

## The one true thing (no sibling shares it)
**Every other hero here has already proved something. The Initiate hasn't, and knows it.** So they hand the question "am I any good?" to someone they look up to, and wait for the verdict. What they don't see yet: a teacher can tell you something's wrong. The answer that makes you yourself is the one you find on your own, and it's still yours years later.

- **Hidden:** a private certainty that there's more in them, which they'd be embarrassed to say out loud because there's no proof. And how long they carry a teacher's offhand remark: a "not quite" from the right person can sit in them all week.
- **Feared:** being asked for it for real, in front of the very person they wanted to impress, and having nothing. Underneath: being found out ("a good job he knew he wasn't qualified for", *Proper* p. 10) and being replaced ("he'd just find someone who could", p. 12).
- **Given:** they listen to people who know more as if it matters, and they take a hard word home and turn it over instead of arguing. People like teaching them; it's been a while since anyone listened like that. (Say this kindness out loud once, per Robin's caregiver edits.)
- **The turn (Ryu's position, in their interest):** stop waiting for someone above you to hand you the answer, or to say you're good enough. Go and find it, wherever it turns up, and bring it back. When they say "OK", it'll be yours.

## Traps
- **"Ready"** is the persona sheet's own word and it's taken (Firefighter y5, Geisha). Use "up to it", "good enough", "there yet".
- **"Young", "first day", "beginner's luck"** (never "young": DeGroff was in his mid-thirties; *Proper* calls him "the younger man" relative to Baum: keep it out).
- **The hero words** ("hero", "brave", "fight"): one at most, family-wide guard.
- **Luke Skywalker** is the sheet's example: no Star Wars touchstone.
- **Baum** is never kind and never a trickster (card guards; DeGroff calls him a son of a bitch in the same breath). The reading doesn't explain his silence; it lets it stand.
- **Bloody Bull vs Bullshot; lemon-and-lime out vs Worcestershire out; 1984 vs 1985:** "his beef-broth drink", "orange", no year (card C1-C3).
- **The out-of-print book** (the purest "trusts too quickly" beat on p. 11) is out: it's Jerry Thomas (*Beside the First*), and a bookshop hunt is *Anyone Would Have*'s y1. Dossier only.
- **The Bel-Air colleague's "you're pretty good at it"** (p. 10) is someone else seeing it first: the Underdog's beat. Dossier only.
- **The acting dream** that "died hard" (p. 10): it would imply the guest's dream is the wrong one. Dossier only.
- **The menu "disaster"** (p. 13): a strong line, but a fourth beat pushes history past half. One clause at most, or dossier.
- **"Get them on their toes" and the consommé:** a habit, not a finding (F7). Out.
- **The orange peel twisted over the top** is in *The First Guess*'s y4: 4-gram the drink paragraph.
- DeGroff is living: date any present-tense claim.

## My story test (as in the plan, sharpened)
1. A person at the start of **becoming someone** (not necessarily a first job), with a teacher or someone they looked up to.
2. A real test that came before they felt up to it, on the record.
3. **New:** the answer is their own, found away from the teacher, and it lasted.
4. The teacher stays as hard as the record makes him (no disguised kindness: that's the Rogue's).
5. Trusting too quickly may show, gently. Never "young". Out: prodigies born finished, stories told only once someone was famous.

## Verdict on the plan's lead (DeGroff and Baum)
**Accept, with conditions.** It passes all five on the page:
1. Years behind a bar, teaching himself "out of a sense of self-preservation" to keep "a good job he knew he wasn't qualified for", and "the world did not" notice (*Proper* p. 10). That's the "more to them than this, no proof yet" in someone else's words. Then Baum auditions him (p. 11).
2. "There's something wrong with it. Fix it." and "I couldn't figure it out" (p. 11). And the safety net he asked for: "What if it got busy? Shouldn't they keep some sour mix on hand?", answered with "he'd just find someone who could". "The bartender decided to work it out" (p. 12). That's the fear (weakness, replacement) on the record, in his own hesitation.
3. The answer came from a Chinese menu, not from Baum ("Joe never told me anything"). Baum said OK. The orange is still in the recipe DeGroff prints decades later (Oxford pdf 375). The fix outlived the test.
4. Baum stays hard. Nothing on the page says he believed in DeGroff, and the reading doesn't claim it.
5. Trust shows gently: he takes an instruction with no reason and goes looking.

**Spine I'd want (history ≤ half):** p. 10 in one clause → the audition and "Fix it" → the orange beef menu → Baum's OK → the sour-mix hesitation and "decided to work it out" → the Rainbow Room in one clause (Oxford: "headquarters of a revolution", attributed to the book). The reveal: the answer was never Baum's to give.

**Drink (Tomás's call, my fences):** the plan's direction fits: built on the recipe DeGroff prints, with the orange as the point. Keep both orange touches and **no lemon or lime anywhere in the glass** (that's what was wrong). We leave out the Tabasco and pepper, so we own that ("built on", never "his recipe"). A spark is welcome only if it doesn't read as a second fix competing with the orange. The reading must make a beef-broth drink sound worth having (Robin's rule): Oxford gives "a favorite among ad men, movie stars" (pdf 374) and the Wondrich piece title "no joke", which Hester checks before anyone leans on it.
