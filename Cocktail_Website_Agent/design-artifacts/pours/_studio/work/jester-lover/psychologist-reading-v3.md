## whoYouAre

You've had the best news of your week since lunchtime, and you haven't said a word. It can wait until the plates are cleared. The present comes out last, after a decoy that gets a groan. Someone asks why you're smiling, you say "I'll tell you later", and they lean closer. The real pleasure is watching it reach their faces.

You choose the moment with care, and you light up when it comes. It isn't a no that you dread. It's a flat "just tell me" from someone who's stopped playing along, or the day your little delays become what everyone expects. And there's one seat in the game you never take: the one where you're the one waiting.

So here it is, out loud: you give people something to look forward to. With you, an ordinary evening gets a reason to linger, and a tease is an invitation into the game.

## yours

1. In nineteenth-century France, cafés served a plain little drink: coffee and a spirit, simply mixed. New Orleans took that café drink and turned it into Café Brûlot, a coffee prepared at the table over several careful steps. It's said Jules Alciatore first made it at Antoine's, the restaurant his father founded, which still serves on St Louis Street. Galatoire's and Commander's Palace make it too, and to many people it's part of eating in New Orleans.

2. One classic recipe soaks cinnamon and peels of lemon and orange for hours in cognac and kirsch, a clear cherry brandy. Then the warm brandy runs down a long spiral of orange peel studded with cloves. Only then, in the classic recipe, comes the coffee, the very thing everyone ordered, and pouring it in puts out the brandy's flame.

3. Nobody is fooled. Everyone there knows the coffee is coming. And I like to think the person carrying it out enjoys every face while choosing the moment. That's you, teasing, exactly. Coffee after dinner is about the most ordinary thing there is, and New Orleans gave it a build-up. You do that to an ordinary evening.

4. The cocktail I've made for you follows the classic recipe's order, in my own measures, for two little coffee cups, and it's never lit. Cognac and kirsch sit for a few hours with the peels and cinnamon. Then they're warmed gently with syrup made from demerara sugar, and run down a clove-studded orange spiral into the cups. It's warm, dark and spiced, with the cherry as the surprise. The coffee, made with chicory the New Orleans way, comes in at the end. And one more change, just for you: someone else pours the coffee, whenever they choose.

5. So, one evening soon, let someone else decide when. Let them keep their news until the plates are cleared, or hand you the present after the decoy. Sit in their seat for once and enjoy not knowing. You'll feel what you've been giving people all along, and you'll find the game is even better with two. I hope someone keeps you guessing.

## Resonance

**"This is me" line:** *"And there's one seat in the game you never take: the one where you're the one waiting."* y5 answers it, and Tomás's closing line puts it in the guest's hands ("let the timing be theirs").

**On the drink as built (v1.1).** It does what the reading claims, every time it's made: the spirits are steeped and warmed, the clove spiral is the build-up, and the coffee, the thing everyone came for, comes in at the end, poured by someone else whenever they choose. So the drink acts out the position, not the wound: the guest is the one kept guessing. Nothing in it is a show the guest must perform (never lit, no bowl, no ladle). It sounds worth having: a renowned New Orleans classic, warm, dark and spiced, with the cherry as the surprise. 11.3% / 6.67 g in the hot range; two cups, not a bowl (*Four Shares*). It's `nuts` (kirsch), not veto-free as the plan hoped: I accept it, because the cherry is the one surprise in the cup and cherry red is the persona's own colour.

**As the guest (the Tease).** Recognised, not flattered: an everyday scene (news held till the plates are cleared, a decoy present), nothing crude, no partner or stage needed. The hidden part (the moment chosen with care, the lighting up) is said warmly; the fears are the persona's own (the flat room as "just tell me", the spell turned routine as "what everyone expects"). The gift is said out loud. The position is in their interest and ends on a gain (better with two) and a wish.

**As the sceptic.** Barnum: "you love surprises" fits anyone; holding the timing, and the one seat never taken, don't. Siblings and cousins kept out: hidden compliments and "plainly" (*Only Half Joking*), the second before a laugh (*Unrehearsed*), attention on purpose (*Up Close*), getting a room ready (*Hoping You'd Come*), endings (*Curtain Call*), "they felt it too" (*Rosetta*), "make them wait" (*Wide Open*). *Whoever Comes In* (brandy and coffee, part made ahead): no line rests on "ahead, then later" alone; the wait is tied to who chooses the moment (y3, y4, y5, closing line). Every paragraph 4-grammed against all pours; hits cleared ("being teased by you", "what you rarely hear", "with you around people", "that's your kind of", "the one who brings", "in the recipe I", "everyone at the table" and more).

**Lint (host r4):** whoYouAre trimmed to 152 words. "his" in y1 is Alciatore's father, a historical person, never the guest: accepted. "one people call it" and "it's a gift people" are gone.

## Names (Wren)
