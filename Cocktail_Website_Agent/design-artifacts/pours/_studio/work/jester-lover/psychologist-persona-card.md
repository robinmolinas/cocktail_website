# Persona card: The Tease (jester-lover), Wren, round 1

Source: persona.py jester-lover (essence, story, goal Pleasure, fear Boredom, Lover: intimacy, fears coldness; example Dita Von Teese, never named to the guest).

## The one true thing (no sibling has it)
The Tease decides **when** people get the good part, and makes ordinary things worth waiting for by choosing the moment. The pleasure is the wanting they create in someone else, and watching it happen. Not a hidden compliment (the Flirt), not the uncertain second before a laugh (the Alternative Comic), not attention given on purpose (the Magnetic Leader), not getting a room ready (the Geisha), not ending on a high (the Pleasure Seeker), not watching for "they felt it too" (the Obsessed).

Scene that carries it: the best news of the week, kept until the plates are cleared; a present handed over last, after a decoy; "I'll tell you after", and they mean it, and they're smiling. Small, everyday, nothing crude, nothing sexual by default (a guest of any age or situation must see themselves).

## What they hide
- How much they enjoy the faces. They pass it off as just being playful; it's the best part of the evening for them.
- That they like holding the timing. Being the one who waits (someone else's surprise, someone else's "not yet") makes them restless: it's the one seat in the game they don't take.

## What they fear
- The shrug: "just tell me", the person who stops playing along. The flat room.
- Routine: the tease that's become predictable ("here they go again"). The spell turning into habit is the worst version of boredom, because it's their own magic gone ordinary.

## What they give (say it out loud, Robin's rule)
People get to look forward to something. An ordinary evening, an ordinary coffee, an ordinary Tuesday becomes an occasion because they made someone wait for it, with a grin. Being teased by them is being let in on a game. That's worth a lot, and they rarely hear it.

## Position (Ryu: earned last)
Let someone make you wait, once. Be on the other side of a "not yet" and enjoy it. They'll feel exactly what they give people, and the game becomes shared (the Lover half: connection, not just effect). It ends on a gain: the pleasure they hand out, received.
- Guard against *Up Close* y5 ("let someone else carry the conversation, and let them show you"): mine is about the timing and the wanting, never about leading the talk.
- Rejected: "give it plainly once" (*Only Half Joking*'s position); "end it on a high" (*Curtain Call*'s); "ask" (*Hoping You'd Come*).

## Traps
- Seduction/burlesque as the scene: the essence invites it; it reads gendered and sexual. Tasteful cheekiness only; the burlesque cues stay in the image brief.
- Withholding as coldness or manipulation ("keeps people on a string"). Never. It's play, and everyone's smiling.
- Echoes: "wait for the X" (*Rosetta*'s closing line), "the second before it" (*Unrehearsed*), "best part" (*Curtain Call* whoYouAre), "make them wait" (*Wide Open*, as the advice the Pure Heart refuses: fine, opposite pole, but don't borrow its words), "worth the ..." (*Worth the Trip*), "waits with you" (*Off Duty*), "never poured for one" (*Four Shares*), overnight waits (*Overnight*).
- Fire as the point (Magician's Blue Blazer; *Cold Water First* ran it). Fire is not the Tease.
- Coffee drinks already in the registry: *Everybody's* (Irish-coffee shape, cream, goblet), *For Kicks* (beans as flies), a coffee-ice block in sage-hero. Ours: no cream, no beans, demitasse.

## Story test
1. Something held back on purpose, with style, so the wanting makes it.
2. The thing held back is ordinary, or at least the thing everyone came for, and the build makes it an occasion.
3. The maker's enjoyment of the faces is on the page, or signposted.
4. Nobody is fooled: everyone knows it's coming. No trick, no truth at the bottom.

## Verdict on the plan's lead: Café Brûlot at Antoine's (Oxford pdf 381-382)
Accept, with conditions. Read the entry whole: it passes all four. (1) Spices and peels steep for several hours first, then the warm spirits go down the studded peel; (2) the thing held back is coffee, the most routine thing at the end of a meal, poured last and turning the show into what everyone drinks; it becomes "an essential part of experiencing New Orleans" (Oxford's words, dossier only); (3) enjoyment signposted ("I like to think whoever brings it to the table enjoys every face"); (4) everyone at the table knows the coffee is coming.
