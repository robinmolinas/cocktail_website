## Dossier only
- C1: Oxford's description says a lemon spiral, its recipe a clove-studded orange spiral; Commander's and frenchquarter.com side with the recipe. Write "orange", never "lemon spiral".
- C2: frenchquarter.com puts the coffee in before the lighting. So "in the classic recipe", never "always".
- C3: frenchquarter.com dates Antoine's claim to "the 1890s"; Tucker (2009, OCR cut off) notes it isn't on a 1910-era Antoine's menu. No decade in the reading.
- F9's "a wonderful light show for your guests" is the recipe writer's line about fire: dossier only (Wren's guard: the flame appears once, as what the coffee puts out).
- F6: the silver-lined copper bowl and brass cradle; F13: conversation stops at nearby tables, lights dimmed, second cup preferred (unsigned).
- L1 Prohibition "coffee as cover" (out: legend-grade, the Trickster's shape). L2 dimmed lights. L3 devil cups.
- Spanish Coffee (Oxford pdf 538 calls Brûlot "much older") is Magician's backup: never named. The Blue Blazer: never named (Wren).
- Antoine's 1840 opening: *Till Spring* (caregiver-magician) already has "the way they did in 1840". Keep the year out of the reading, or own it.
- **Sibling shape (audit r3, D6):** *Whoever Comes In* (regular-guy-caregiver) is brandy and coffee, part made days ahead, with the espresso pulled only when someone's there (its epigraph). Our wait anchor is fine as a fact, but no line may rest on 'part ahead, the rest later' alone. Tie the wait to who holds the timing.
- **Proportions (audit r3):** our drink follows the classic recipe's order in Tomás's own measures, for two and unlit. Never write "Antoine's recipe", "the original" or "twelve cloves".
- **Struck (audit r4, R1–R2), my shorthand:** "plain café habit" / "Paris café drink" (my r2 turn and A1) became y1's "everyday habit" and "Paris … after a meal". Oxford says a nineteenth-century *French café drink*; Paris is only the unread *Le Droit* citation.
