## whoYouAre

You've had the best news of your week since lunchtime, and you haven't said a word. It can wait until the plates are cleared. The present comes out last, after a small decoy that gets a groan. Someone asks why you're smiling, you say "I'll tell you later", and they lean closer. Nobody minds. The real pleasure is watching it reach their faces, and you never miss one.

People call it playful, and it is. The part they miss is how carefully you choose the moment, and how you light up when it comes. It isn't a no that you dread. It's a flat "just tell me" from someone who's stopped playing along. Or worse, the day your little delays turn into something everyone expects. And there's one seat in the game you never take: the one where you're the one waiting.

You rarely hear what all this does for people, so I'll say it. You give them something to look forward to. An ordinary evening gets a reason to linger, and a tease from you is an invitation into the game. It's a gift people remember long after the evening.

## yours

1. In nineteenth-century Paris, cafés served a plain little drink after a meal: coffee and a spirit, simply mixed. New Orleans took that everyday habit and turned it into Café Brûlot, a coffee prepared at the table over several careful steps. It's said Jules Alciatore first made it at Antoine's, the restaurant his father founded, which still serves on St Louis Street. Galatoire's and Commander's Palace make it too, and to many people it's part of eating in New Orleans.

2. The recipe I follow starts the waiting hours before anyone sits down: cinnamon and peels of lemon and orange soak in cognac and kirsch, a clear cherry brandy. Then the warm brandy runs down a long spiral of orange peel studded with cloves. Only then, in the classic recipe, comes the coffee, the very thing everyone ordered, and pouring it in puts the flame out.

3. Nobody is fooled. Everyone can see the coffee pot. And I like to think the person carrying it out enjoys every face along the way. That's you, teasing, exactly. Coffee after dinner is about the most ordinary thing there is, and New Orleans gave it a build-up. You do that to an ordinary evening.

4. The cocktail I've made for you follows that order, made small for two little coffee cups, and it's never lit. Cognac and kirsch sit for a few hours with the peels and cinnamon. Then they're warmed gently with syrup made from dark brown sugar, and run down a clove-studded orange spiral into the cups. It's warm, dark and spiced, with the cherry as the surprise. The coffee, made with chicory the New Orleans way, comes in at the end. I've moved one thing, and it's for you: someone else pours the coffee, whenever they choose.

5. So, one evening soon, let someone else decide when. Let them keep their news until the plates are cleared, or hand you the present after the decoy. Sit in their seat for once and enjoy not knowing. You'll feel what you've been giving people all along, and you'll find the game is even better with two. I hope someone keeps you guessing.

## Resonance

**"This is me" line:** *"And there's one seat in the game you never take: the one where you're kept waiting."* The position in y5 answers it directly.

**As the guest (the Tease).** Recognised, not flattered: the scene is everyday (news held till the plates are cleared, a decoy present), nothing crude, nothing that needs a partner or a stage. The hidden part (how carefully the moment is chosen, how much they love it) is said warmly; the fears are the persona's own (the flat room as "just tell me", the spell turned routine as "something everyone expects"). The gift is said out loud (something to look forward to). The position is in their interest and ends on a gain (the game is better with two) and a wish.

**As the sceptic.** Barnum check: "you love surprises" would fit anyone; the timing as the thing they hold, and the seat they never take, don't. Siblings and cousins: no hidden compliment or "plainly" (*Only Half Joking*), no uncertain second before a laugh (*Unrehearsed*), no attention given on purpose or "let them carry the conversation" (*Up Close*), no getting a room ready (*Hoping You'd Come*), no endings (*Curtain Call*), no "they felt it too" (*Rosetta*), no "make them wait" (*Wide Open*). Words Hester flagged kept out: "occasion", "the good part", "best part", "not yet", "1840", "poured last". y5 says "decide when" so it doesn't spend Tomás's closing line ("let the timing be theirs").

**History share:** y1–y2 are 147 of 363 words in yours (40%), under half.

**Watch:** the flame is named once, in y2, as what the coffee puts out; never in the drink or the ending. The name's words recur once in whoYouAre (the scene) and nowhere else. The kirsch makes the pour `nuts`, not veto-free as the plan aimed: Tomás's call, and I accept it, because the cherry is the one surprise in the cup ("where nobody expects it") and the cherry red is the persona's own colour.

**Verdict:** draft. Ready for Hester's r3 audit.

## Names (Wren)
