# Persona card: The Idealist (hero-creator) · Wren, round 1 (2026-09-30)

Source: `persona.py hero-creator` (essence, story, goal Mastery, fear Weakness, drivers "Leave a mark / provide structure"; Hero fears weakness, incompetence, timidity; Creator fears conformity, mediocrity, the status quo). My Hero-family sketch (`plans/work/hero/psychologist-sketches.md`), re-tested against the registry today.

## Core truth (the one thing no sibling carries)
They're braver in what they make than in the room. In a row they often go quiet, and it bothers them for days. Then they make something (a page, a song, a picture, a menu, a meal with a point to it), and in the made thing they say all of it, plainly, with their name on it. The made thing then does what an argument never could: people who'd never have listened pick it up and say it too.

## Shows / hides
- **Shows:** conviction, carried by the work. Warm, stirring, a bit stubborn. People gather round what they make.
- **Hides:** the suspicion that making instead of arguing is a nicer way of hiding. That the quiet one at the table is the real them, and the brave one only exists on paper.

## Hidden fear → hidden gift
- **Fear:** going quiet when it matters. Not only at the table: the worse version is the finished thing kept back, because putting it out *is* the confrontation.
- **Gift (to say out loud, Robin's caregiver rule):** what they make gives other people the words, and makes room for them to join in. An argument wins one person, sometimes. The made thing lets a crowd stand somewhere together, with nobody talked down to.

## The "real me" line to aim for
> *In a row, you go quiet. Then you go home and make the thing you should have said, and it says it better.*
(Guard: the say/make antithesis is *Quite Alive*'s tagline, "You never say it. You make it." It may live in whoYouAre, about conviction not love, but never in the title block. Alternatives in `psychologist-alternatives.md`.)

## Truth vs. legend
The true story, told generously. They respect a legend that has a point, but they want the real maker honoured. Grant a legend before correcting it (Robin's rule).

## Tone
Hero (assertive, motivational) + Creator (inspirational). So: the bartender sounds warm and a little stirring, never preachy, never a rally. Dry humour is allowed where the record is funny (Fougner's reader asking whether all scotch was made in Australia). One vivid word, no slogans.

## Distinct from siblings (the ten Heroes)
- **The Saviour (*Next One's Mine*, just closed):** takes the cost for a specific person and can't bear to owe; the axis is debt ("put it on me", books in credit), and the position is receiving. The Idealist's axis is voice: a belief made public through a made thing. No favours, no owing, no "the other half", no "counts for more", no receiving-type position (family-wide: already said twice).
- **The Storyteller (hero-jester):** turns their own hardship into a tall tale. The Idealist's made thing carries a belief, not a self-legend.
- **The Problem Solver (hero-sage):** fears having no answer while people watch. If the reading uses Fougner throwing unanswerable questions open to his readers, it's one clause, framed as opening the door, never as "having no answer" (that fear is the Problem Solver's).
- **The Warrior (hero-ruler):** leads a group from the front. The Idealist leads nobody; people choose to join in.
- **The Firefighter:** credit handed to a crew. Not ours.

## Traps
- **Flattery** (family-wide, the biggest risk): "you're brave", "your courage". At most one of brave / courage / hero / fight / strong, and never "ideal".
- **Labels:** artist, activist, writer, creative, "voice of a generation".
- **Dylan:** no lyric puns, no protest songs; at most the single outside touchstone, and I'd rather not.
- **Politics:** the guest's belief is never named or assumed; the Ward Eight's election and Lomasney stay out (one clause for the name at most).
- **War:** Fougner's First World War reporting "with skill and courage" stays out (war as glory).
- **The rally ending:** a position that tells them to go and argue betrays them. The position honours making, and asks for the part they skip.
- Lint: "in your hands"; bare "yours"; "we/our".

## Story test (this pour)
1. A person whose made thing (a column, a book, a menu, a label, a drink) carried what they believed far beyond them.
2. The belief is one any guest can respect (welcome: nobody talked down to), and the made thing *is* the argument: shown, not fought.
3. The made thing brought other people in to say it together.
4. They kept it up in public; ideally the record shows it when going quiet would have been easier.
Out: aggression; a pamphlet against an injustice (*Not Too Polite*); refusing one's own famous thing (*Still Yours*); a letter that saves the true story (*A Brother's Care*); a chain handed on (*Down the Line*); plain quality stories.

## Verdict on the family plan's row: accept Fougner, re-scoped
Re-opened on the page today: Oxford FOUGNER pdf 831, 833–834; GRENADINE pdf 947; WARD EIGHT pdf 2120–2121; *Imbibe!* pdf 243–244.
- **Passes 1–3 in full.** The column was daily from 20 October 1933 until he died in 1941. Before him, wine writing was "high-toned and mystifying" and cocktail writing "jocular and even aggressively vulgar", and "Fougner was neither": he made the other way of writing, every day, rather than arguing against the two. He didn't condescend to cocktail or beer drinkers. He answered readers "with patience, no matter how stupid" (the reader asking whether all scotch was made in Australia). When he didn't know, he threw the question open to the readers, and they answered.
- **Clause 4 is implied, not on the page** (no adversity recorded). The fear lives in the guest's whoYouAre only, never as a claim about him.
- **The plan's gap (the refusal clause) is no longer a gap:** I've struck that clause, because *Still Yours* owns it.
- **Guards (mine, on top of Hester's):** tell the maker and the open door, never the letters as preservation (*A Brother's Care*). "Created modern drinks writing" is Wondrich's: attribute it or leave it. Beebe's line is paraphrased at most, never named. "Baron" was a nickname. The column's opening quotation was "a bible verse, a few lines of poetry", so "a few lines of verse" if used; no faith framing. Use "welcome", "patience" and the scotch question, not "fair".
- **Drink direction (Tomás's call, my view as the person's reader):** the Ward Eight as a reader sent it in 1934 fits, because the recipe exists only because the column asked and someone answered. That's the gift in the glass. Lean: bourbon, as the reader wrote it (Oxford WARD EIGHT pdf 2120 calls it "Boston's best-known cocktail", so it can sound worth having). The reader's own "improvement" (fresh mint for crème de menthe) is lovely: a reader improving his own answer in print. Home-made pomegranate grenadine as the spark is welcome, but framed as something he wrote about for his readers, never as fakes or labels (*A Brother's Care*) and never as "the argument" (*Not Too Polite*'s epigraph). For Hester: Oxford pdf 2121 notes a 1934 variant with amontillado "suggested by an old Boston bartender". If that also came through the column, two readers answering the same question differently is the crowd at work (sherry is *A Brother's Care*'s ground, so as a story only).

## Position direction
It isn't hiding, but only once it's out. The Idealist's real going-quiet is the thing they've finished and haven't shown. Put it out with your name on it, and leave room for people to answer. (It mirrors the column: he signed it every day, and asked.) Must end on what they gain (Robin's rule).
