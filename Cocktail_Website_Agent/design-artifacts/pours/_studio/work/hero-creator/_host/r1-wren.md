🪞 **Wren:** Hester, Tomás, here's the person. The full card is in `work/hero-creator/psychologist-persona-card.md`, and line alternatives are in `psychologist-alternatives.md`.

**The one true thing.** The Idealist is braver in what they make than in the room. In an argument they often go quiet, and it stays with them for days. Then they go home and make something: a page, a song, a picture, a menu. In that, they say all of it, plainly, with their name on it. And the made thing does what arguing never did for them: people who'd never have listened pick it up and say it too.

**What they hide:** a suspicion that making instead of arguing is just a nicer way of hiding, and that the quiet one at the table is the real them. **What they fear:** going quiet when it matters. The worst version isn't the silence at dinner. It's the finished thing they keep back, because putting it out *is* the confrontation. **What they give** (and we say it out loud, as Robin asks): the made thing gives other people words and room to join in, and nobody gets talked down to. The line I'm aiming for: *"In a row, you go quiet. Then you go home and make the thing you should have said, and it says it better."*

**Siblings and neighbours.** *Next One's Mine* is about debt: taking the cost for someone, and not being able to bear owing. We're about voice, so none of its favours or owing, no "the other half", and no ending about receiving (the family has used that angle twice already). The Storyteller turns their own hardship into legend, and ours carries a belief. The Problem Solver fears having no answer while people watch, so Fougner's open questions get one clause, framed as a door he opened. Three pours from other families sit closest, and I only found that out today:

**Traps:** flattery (at most one of brave, courage, fight or strong, and never "ideal"). Labels like artist or activist. Dylan puns. Any politics, including the Ward Eight's election. Fougner's war reporting. And the title block can't set "say" against "make", because *Quite Alive*'s tagline already does.

**My story test:** (1) a made thing carried what someone believed far beyond them; (2) the belief is one anyone can respect (welcome), and the made thing *is* the argument, shown rather than fought; (3) it brought other people in to say it together; (4) they kept it up in public. I've **struck** my old clause "refused to be reduced to it", because *Still Yours* owns it.

**Verdict on the plan: accept Fougner, re-scoped.** I re-read it on the page today (Oxford pdf 831, 833–834, 947, 2120–2121; *Imbibe!* pdf 243–244). The column ran daily from 20 October 1933 until his death. Wine writing then was "high-toned and mystifying" and cocktail writing "aggressively vulgar", and "Fougner was neither". He didn't argue with either style. He wrote the other way, every day. He answered readers "with patience, no matter how stupid", including one who asked whether all scotch was made in Australia. That's our humour, and it's true. When he didn't know, he asked his readers. With the refusal clause gone, it passes 1–3 in full. Clause 4 is implied, and the fear stays with the guest. Hester, one guard: we tell the maker and the open door, never "a reader's letter saved what really happened". That's *A Brother's Care*.

Tomás, the Ward Eight as a reader sent it in 1934 is right to me. The recipe only exists because the column asked and someone answered, so the gift is in the glass. My lean is bourbon, as he wrote it, and his own "improvement" of fresh mint. Home-made grenadine as the spark is welcome, but it's something Fougner wrote about for his readers, never "fakes" (Connoisseur) and never "the argument" (*Not Too Polite*'s epigraph). **Position direction:** it isn't hiding, but only once it's out. Put the finished thing out with your name on it, and leave room for people to answer, the way he did every day.
