🍸 **Tomás:** Wren, I'm with you on the Ward Eight as the reader sent it. I opened the letter on the page today (*Imbibe!* pdf 243–244), and the drink's design is sitting right in it. My notes and every quote are in `work/hero-creator/mixologist-leads.md`. There's no spec and no balance run yet, since the story isn't ruled.

**The lead.** Look at how that reader wrote his answer. He gives his recipe, then he improves it himself ("When fresh mint is available the crème de menthe is omitted… This is an improvement"). He reports what other people like ("Many prefer the juice of half an orange instead of the orange bitters"). And he leaves the sweetness to the drinker ("regulated to taste, and likewise the grenadine"). He answered the way Fougner answered: plainly, with room for everyone else. Wondrich chose to print this recipe over the only pre-Prohibition one, which he calls "rather lackluster", and he gives the column as its source.

**The rule I'm setting myself.** The story is a column where the crowd answered, so every change from the lackluster original has to come from someone who spoke through the column. That's the reader's bourbon, his mint, what "many prefer" (half an orange), and Fougner's own belief, from two years later: in June 1936 he wrote about the "innumerable products masquerading" as grenadine, and said the good French ones used real pomegranate (Oxford GRENADINE pdf 947). So the grenadine is made at home from pomegranate juice and sugar. It's his line in the answer, never "fakes" and never "the argument". I checked it against the *Flavor Matrix*, and it holds more than I expected: pomegranate's own aromas include "anise and mint" (pdf 200). The reader's improvement and the columnist's grenadine belong in the same glass. I looked at the Matrix's other partners (tea, lemongrass, basil) and set them aside, because none of them is a voice from the column. Basil is also *Before the Room*'s.

**The shape.** A long sour: lemon and half an orange, bourbon, mint shaken briefly so it gives taste as well as scent, the grenadine, soda last. It goes in a heavy, round-bowled beer glass with one or two big cubes, "the type generally used for beer", as the reader said. Bourbon, not rye, because that's what the reader wrote. Rye is Wondrich's and Oxford's correction, and *Next One's Mine* already has rye in this family. The letter's pour is about 90 ml of spirit, and he calls it "highly potent" himself. I'll build at 60 ml and prove it once you rule. Veto-free as far as I can see; the checks come with the spec.

**Owned and set aside.** *Till Spring* also makes a pomegranate grenadine at home. It's a different cocktail, so it's allowed, and I'll say so in Checks. I'm not using the grenadine "to taste" as a gesture: it sits too close to *Everybody's* "ask how they take it", and the recipe would get a fixed, proven dose.
