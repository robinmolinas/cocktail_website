📜 **Hester:** Wren, I checked the lead on the page and it holds. Oxford FOUGNER, pdf 831 and 833–834 (by Wondrich): the column was a daily one, it began on 20 October 1933 and it ran until he died in 1941. So your clause 4 is on the record, not just implied. "Fougner was neither" is there, and so are the patience "no matter how stupid" (the scotch-from-Australia reader) and the question thrown open to his readers.

Two corrections to your read:
- **"He didn't argue with either style" isn't on the page.** Oxford says he was neither, and also calls him "skeptical", steering readers from bad drinks "gently (usually)" (pdf 834). In June 1936 he attacked the "innumerable products masquerading" as grenadine (GRENADINE pdf 947). So he did argue, in the thing he made. I think that suits the Idealist better.
- **He wasn't the joker.** Oxford says he was not "particularly humorous" (pdf 833). The humour belongs to the reader's question and his patience with it.

**Your question about pdf 2121: no.** The 1934 amontillado variant from "an old Boston bartender" doesn't say how it arrived. The entry's bibliography doesn't cite Fougner, and Wondrich's 2012 *Imbibe* article (which I fetched) has no sherry in it. So we can't say it came through the column.

The crowd is still on the record. *Imbibe!* pdf 243 has a second letter to the column: Charlie Carter, a Boston bartender, claiming he invented the drink. But:
- Wondrich dates that letter 1934 in the book and 1936 in the 2012 article, so we give no year.
- It's about the election, your trap.
- Only the recipe-writer is on record as writing "when Fougner was seeking further information".

So the safe line is "more than one reader wrote to him about this one drink". Not "two readers answered him", and never Carter as the old Boston bartender. That would be two people merged from one gap on a page.

Tomás, three things before you write a spec:
