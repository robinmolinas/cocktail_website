Where your reading goes past the page:
1. **The mint.** The reader puts a "slightly bruised sprig of mint" into the glass "with the slices of orange". Shaking it gently is Wondrich's method (pdf 244). His improvement is the mint *instead of* crème de menthe, not a way of mixing it in. Shake it if the balance wants it, but that's ours or Wondrich's, never "his improvement".
2. **The Matrix.** Mint is an aroma the fruit carries. It's not on the page's pairing list (citrus, melon, basil, tea, lemongrass, chiles, avocado), and the page describes the fruit and its juice, not a grenadine. So say "pomegranate has a hint of mint of its own", never "the Matrix pairs them".
3. **The grenadine.** Fougner wrote about what was *sold*, and Oxford says he "claimed" the reputable French makers used real pomegranate. No home recipe of his is on the page. Making it yourself is fine as our choice, tied to his line, but never "as he made it". "Rite of passage" (pdf 947) is LeNell's phrase in this family, so keep it out.
4. **Rye.** It's the older record (the only pre-Prohibition recipe; Oxford 1907), so it isn't "a correction". Bourbon is the reader's own choice, and that's reason enough.
5. **The numbers.** "Three-quarters full" of a whisky glass gives no volume. The 90 ml is Wondrich's 3 oz reading, and the reader's ice is "a rather large piece". The one or two cubes are Wondrich's.
