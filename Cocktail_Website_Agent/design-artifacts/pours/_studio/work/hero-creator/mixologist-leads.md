## Sources opened on the page
- *Imbibe!* pdf 243: Charlie Carter's "circumstantially detailed 1934 letter" to Fougner's column claiming he invented the Ward Eight (Puritan Club, 1903). The only pre-Prohibition recipe is "rather lackluster", so Wondrich prints the one "another reader sent to the Sun in 1934, when Fougner was seeking further information on the drink". The reader: the Ward 8's basis "was a whisky sour", "distinctly a warm weather drink", "always served in a large, heavy glass of the type generally used for beer—that is, with a large round bowl."
- *Imbibe!* pdf 244: the letter's recipe: juice of one lemon, a barspoon of powdered sugar, the whisky glass three-quarters full of **Bourbon**, a large piece of ice, 3–4 dashes orange bitters, 3 dashes crème de menthe, half a jigger grenadine, filled with plain water or seltzer, two half slices orange, pineapple, one or two cherries. "When fresh mint is available the crème de menthe is omitted… This is an improvement." "Many prefer the juice of half an orange instead of the orange bitters." "The amount of sugar should be regulated to taste, and likewise the grenadine." "very pleasant, although highly potent." SOURCE line: Fougner, "Along the Wine Trail", 1934. Wondrich: always considered a rye drink; use fresh mint and orange juice; his build: 1 lemon, ½ orange, 1 barspoon sugar, 3 oz rye, mint sprig, shake gently "so as not to brutalize the mint", beer goblet with 1–2 large cubes, ½ oz grenadine, chilled seltzer.
- Oxford GRENADINE SYRUP pdf 947: June 1936, Fougner wrote of "innumerable products masquerading under that name" and said reputable French producers used real pomegranate juice; home-made grenadine is now "a minor rite of passage".
- Oxford WARD EIGHT pdf 2121: 1907 recipe (20 lemon, 5 sugar, 7 grenadine, 60 rye, 30 soda, stemmed goblet); amontillado split "suggested by an old Boston bartender in 1934" (Hester: via the column or not?).
- *Flavor Matrix* pdf 200: pomegranate "characterized by sharper aromas such as anise and mint"; best pairings citrus, melon, basil, tea, lemongrass, chiles, avocado; surprising: mushroom, basil.
- *Codex* p. 129: grenadine from pomegranate juice "spiked with orange oil" is "both juicy and tangy"; p. 47 House Grenadine (acids and enzyme: not for home; *Till Spring* adapted it without acids).
