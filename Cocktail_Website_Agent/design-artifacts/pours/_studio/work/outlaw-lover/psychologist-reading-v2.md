## epigraph
*Its wine spends a night in the cold, as two newlyweds kept theirs: in a mine.*

## tagline candidates
1. You love like it's a getaway. Even where you stop is a hideout.
2. For you, falling for someone and breaking free were always one thing.
3. Nobody ever made you settle. You'd rather pick the place yourself.

## whoYouAre
A one-way ticket. A job left on a Friday. A family dinner where you finally told them, and the table went quiet. You've chosen people, places and whole lives like that: all at once, and usually against advice. And every time, choosing and getting free felt like the same thing. Whoever you fell for was also the way out.

You'd never tell anyone how much you'd like to land somewhere. It feels like giving in. And the road running out has never scared you. Being parked does: someone else deciding where you stop, and the life you ran towards slowly picking up the rules of the one you ran from.

And here's what that wildness gives whoever you choose: they don't have to guess. They know they were picked over everything else. Next to you, love looks like courage, and anyone watching starts asking what they'd leave for theirs. Not many people can give that.

## yours
1. In 1879 a young Scottish writer, Robert Louis Stevenson, crossed the Atlantic and then the whole of America to reach Fanny, an American, in California. The next spring, they married. His parents had objected, because she was "an American and a stranger", and when they saw his mind was made up, they gave in completely.

2. After the wedding, the two didn't rent a house. They moved into one: an abandoned miners' bunkhouse high on a mountain above the Napa Valley, with Fanny's son and their dog. What had tempted him was the idea of moving in rent-free. He crowned the two of them "the King and Queen of Silverado", and his first job, to take possession, was cutting out the poison oak that had grown up through the floor. They kept their wine in the old mine tunnel, the one place on that hot hillside that stayed cool.

3. His book about that summer ends at night, with a single candle stuck in the neck of a bottle. From inside, he wrote, the old cracked house seemed to burst with light, and anyone out on the far hills would have stopped to watch. I like to think that's what stopping looks like for you: not being caught, but choosing the place, and making it as wild as the road. Years later, in Samoa, they bought land, cleared it themselves and built a house they called Vailima. Fanny's sister wrote that it was there "home" first came to mean something to them, and he was still bringing her each morning's writing to judge.

4. The cocktail I've made for you is a wine highball: white wine and soda, one of the long, light drinks the highball is traced back to. In a Napa cellar he tasted a "Hock", a Californian white sold under the old German name, so here it's a crisp, dry California white. The evening before, one bay leaf goes into the bottle, and the bottle goes into the coldest, darkest corner of the fridge, as a nod to the tunnel, and to a canyon he wrote smelled of sweet bay at sunrise and late at night. Then it's poured over ice into a tall stemmed wine glass, and topped with soda straight from the fridge.

5. So my proposal is this. When the pull to go comes again, ask yourself what you'd be leaving. If it's someone deciding for you, go. If it isn't, stop, on purpose, in a place of your choosing, and make that the adventure. Being caught means someone else chose where you'd end up. Stopping means you did.

## Resonance test (v2)
- **As the guest (a Fugitive).** Tagline (1) catches me with a grin: getaway, yes; hideout, yes, that's how I'd describe the flat I love. ¶1's one-way ticket and the quiet table are mine. ¶2's "Being parked does" is the line I'd quote: nobody has said it like that. ¶3 makes me feel valued without being flattered: what I give the one I chose. The story surprises me (Stevenson as a squatter, crowned in a ruin), and y3's candle is the image I'd keep. The proposal doesn't tell me to settle down, which I'd resent: it tells me to pick the place. "Stopping means you did." Ready.
- **As the sceptic.** Horoscope check: "chosen against advice", "getting free" and "being parked" would not fit a Caregiver or a Ruler; "love looks like courage" alone is generic but is held by "picked over everything else". Swap test against siblings: Thrill Seeker (*Whoopee*: the drop, alive) no; Rule Breaker (*Asked In*: being let in, "good only because not allowed") no, the parents are one clause in y1 and the ending is a place chosen; Adolescent (*Kept*: drift) no; Pleasure Seeker (*Curtain Call*: when it ends) no, ours is where. Partner guard: "whoever you fell for", "whoever you choose": a guest who chose a city or a life still fits. No gendered word about the guest.
- **Punch check.** The "this is me" line: "the road running out has never scared you. Being parked does". Name *Rent-Free* doesn't repeat it.
- **Rule check.** One voice ("I" only), no bare "yours" for the drink, no archetype names, no drink in hand, signpost covers one sentence, history ≈ 47% of the reading (y1–y3 history plus the y4 Hock/Spritzer/bay clauses, over 580 words).
- **Verdict:** ready, pending Hester's audit of v2. Closing line accepted: *Take it somewhere you picked yourself. Then stay as long as you like.*
