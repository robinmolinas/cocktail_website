# Persona card: The Fugitive (outlaw-lover), Wren, round 1 (2026-10-01)

## The one true thing (no sibling has it)
For the Fugitive, choosing someone and getting free are the same move. Love arrives as an escape, and escape as love. Every sibling rebels *against* something; this one rebels *towards* someone, and takes them along. Freedom for two.

## Hide / fear / give
- **Hides:** how much they'd like to land. Out loud it sounds like giving in, so it stays unsaid. Also a quiet doubt: whether they know how to love on a night when nobody's against it.
- **Fears:** someone else deciding where they stop. Being caught isn't the police. It's the life they ran to slowly getting the rules of the life they ran from: the car parked for them, the love tamed by other people's expectations. (Outlaw: powerlessness; Lover: the love going cold.)
- **Gives:** the people they love always know they were chosen over everything else. Around them, love looks like courage, and others remember they're allowed to choose their own life.

## Position (seed)
Stopping isn't being caught. Being caught is when someone else picks the place; stopping is when you do. So stop somewhere of your own choosing, and make it as wild as the road (Stevenson's squatters' honeymoon: a ruined bunkhouse, rent-free, "King and Queen of Silverado", one candle).

## Siblings and traps (registry-checked)
- Thrill Seeker (*Whoopee*): the drop for the feeling, "your idea", "the one kind of helpless you choose". Fugitive: not the thrill, the person; no way back, a life, not a jump.
- Rule Breaker (*Asked In*): "It's the yes you don't trust", "being let in = owned", "is it good, or only good because it isn't allowed?", "holds up on its own". So: the parents' yielding is never the ending, and no "is it love or only the running?" question.
- Adolescent (*Kept*): the gentle drift into who you swore not to be. Fugitive's fear is a place and a person being pinned, not a self drifting.
- Pleasure Seeker (*Curtain Call*): endings, "choose the night's ending yourself". Fugitive's is where, not when; never "choose the ending".
- Sense Seeker (*Straight Back*): "let it take you", "somewhere else". Avoid "swept/carried away".
- Quest Seeker (*Someone Else's Map*): "Far doesn't scare you. Finished does." Avoid far/finished.
- Gentle Giant "People assume…", Thrill Seeker "People think…", Maverick "People put it down to…": no "People …" opener for whoYouAre.
- Lover family (*Standing By*, *Out of Your Way*): the reading never needs a partner; "whoever or whatever you chose".
- 4-grams: "nobody gets to tell" (Asked In, Still Yours), "on your own terms" (On One Condition), "the people you love" (x3), "what you're really afraid of" (Good as It Is): all out.

## Story test
1. Chooses a love or a life against the people who expected otherwise (a family, a settled path).
2. Goes for it, with a crossing or a leaving that costs.
3. The running stops, and the story shows the stopping chosen by them, on their own ground, with the love still there.
4. It holds past the page (the curious guest who looks it up finds it lasted).
Out: crime, violence, death, ruin, affairs, secrets, illness as theme.

## Plan row verdict (Stevenson and Fanny): accept, with corrections
- **Steerage is wrong.** Stevenson, *The Amateur Emigrant* (Essays of Travel, Gutenberg #627, "The Second Cabin"): "I was not, in truth, a steerage passenger"; advised to go second cabin. Never "steerage".
- **The mountain's reason is medical** (Sanchez: freedom from the fog "which we dreaded for the sick man"): no motive for the mountain in guest text.
- **Parents:** only Sanchez's beat (objected to "an American and a stranger"; when his mind was "irrevocably made up", yielded, allowance, welcome). One clause, never the ending (Asked In).
- **Never quote the Gosse letter** ("loved for three years and a half" dates the love to her marriage).
- **Kelmar chapter: never.** Its antisemitic portrait rules out the whole chapter in guest text.
- **The stopping, on the page:** "the King and Queen of Silverado" (four squatters: him, Fanny, her son Sam, the dog Chuchu); "rent-free, into a ready-made house"; first improvement cutting poison oak "by which we took possession"; wine kept "in the tunnel that once rang with picks"; one candle "in the neck of a pint bottle", "and yet the old cracked house seemed literally bursting with the light" (g516 ~l.2440).
- **Stevenson's own epigraph** (Cicero, *De Officiis* I.xx): "…ne cui parerent, libertate uterentur: cujus proprium est sic vivere ut velis" (to obey no one, to use their liberty, whose mark is to live as you please). Translation needs a sourced English, or it's ours, signposted.
- **Past the page:** married 19 May 1880; Colvin: his father's help "always amply and ungrudgingly given". The room fetches a page for "together until he died" if the reading claims it lasted; his illness and death never named.
