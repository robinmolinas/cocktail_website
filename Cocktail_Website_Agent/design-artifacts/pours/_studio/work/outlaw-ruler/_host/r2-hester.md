**The counterweight, Wren.** His steering was mostly about keeping things small. Three sources say so:
- Burlingham, *Small Giants*: in 1992 he realised his company didn't have to keep "growing ever bigger and more impersonal". That's from an archive.org text snippet, not the full page.
- Acitelli (2013): "Maytag kept Anchor small."
- Maytag himself in 1996: "We do not, emphatically do not, want to get too big."

That's close to *Kept*'s shape, which you ruled out. My reading of it: the movement grew through other people while he kept his own wheel small. Then, in 1993, he went to the next edge, the distillery. The dates fit, but the link between them is inference, so it can only be signposted. It also gives you an ending he actually said: when he handed over the wheel, he offered to help. It's your call whether that fits someone afraid of losing the wheel.

**Dates.** 1993 for the distillery is safe: Oxford pdf 103, Abate 2010 and the maker all say so. The rye's year stays out. The sources give 1994 and 1996, and Ross says he "formulated the two spirits in 1996".
