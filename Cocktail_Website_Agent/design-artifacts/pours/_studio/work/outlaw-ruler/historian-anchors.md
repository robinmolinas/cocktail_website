## Notes (guards)

- **N1. Dates.** 1965 (purchase), 1993 (distillery), 2010 (sale), 2023 (closing), 2024 (new owner) are safe. No year for the first rye (sources give 1994 and 1996). "About thirty years after" the purchase is fine for 1993; "fifteen years" isn't.
- **N2. Secrecy.** Secret from most of his own staff, legal, to control the timing. Never "contraband" and never "hidden from the law" (*Asked In* owns the illegal still). Not the opening line.
- **N3. Small.** If Wren keeps the counterweight row, it's one clause, about his own brewery, never "chose not to grow" as the moral (*Kept*'s shape). The link "kept the beer small, *so* went to the distillery" is our inference, signposted ("I like to think…"), because no page connects 1992 to 1993. If she cuts it, nothing in the reading may say he wanted Anchor to grow big, or that he "steered the movement".
- **N4. The sale.** His motive isn't on record. "Time to let somebody else help lead the way" is the buyer's guess (Greggor), and Grossman thought he wasn't ready. The company statement under his name is a press statement, not an interview. Only the "whatever Keith says" line is his.
- **N5. Closing.** It was Sapporo's decision. No villains: the company's stated reasons were the pandemic, inflation and competition. He made no comment on record (C2: "gobsmacked" is out). Never his grief.
- **N6. Family.** No "family disapproved" (C1, uncited). No great-grandson or grandson count (C5).
- **N7. Claims are Oxford's.** "First modern microdistilled whisky" and "shot across the bows" are attributed ("by one account", "the *Oxford Companion* calls it"). Not the maker's "first craft whiskey after Prohibition" (C6).
- **N8. Bottle.** Name "Old Potrero" only. The maker's pages name Hotaling & Co. as the distillers (label unseen), and Jester holds Hotaling's whiskey. Today's six-year bottles aren't the one-year rye of the 1990s: never "his rye" or "the rye he made". Not "rye and orange" (*The Long View*).
- **N11. Liking the beer.** Contested (F19: his "brew of choice" in 2004 vs Ogle's "rarely drank Anchor"). Never say he liked it or didn't; his own "wasn't good" is safe.
- **N12. Quince.** Matrix's pairings are the pome family's (F22); never "quince pairs with bourbon/rye" as the book's. Its facts: rarely eaten raw, hard and astringent, strongly perfumed.
- **N9. Beer.** He made the beer good, but beer stays out of the glass (`gluten`, and it's the part he handed on).
- **N10. Copy.** The fact column paraphrases. Short quotes ("whatever Keith says") go in only if Wren wants his voice, and then exactly as printed.
