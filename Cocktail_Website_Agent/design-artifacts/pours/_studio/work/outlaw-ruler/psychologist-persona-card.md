# Persona card: The Leading Edge (outlaw-ruler), Wren, round 1

## The one true thing
The rebel who steers. They're there before the new thing has a name, and they're the one who gives it a shape other people can join. What only they carry: they want the wheel, and among people who hate being told what to do, wanting the wheel is the one thing they can't say.

## Hides / fears / gives
- **Hides:** the plan. People see the wild part; nobody sees how much is worked out, or how long it was kept behind a door until it was right. Underneath: they want to be the one steering, and say it out loud and it sounds like the thing they all rebelled against.
- **Fears:** not that it won't catch on. That it catches on and turns into something else, run by people who never understood it, smoothed out for everyone, with their hands nowhere near it. The day it doesn't need them.
- **Gives:** new things mostly die young: talked out of it, copied badly, bought up. Theirs tend to grow up, because someone was steering while it was fragile. Name it once, plainly (Robin's caregiver rule).

## Story test (four points)
1. Spots something new before it had a name.
2. Gives it a shape (a product, a name, a place, a rule set) that others then follow: a movement, not only a company.
3. Steers it as it grows, and it costs them something.
4. Faces the moment it outgrew them or got tamed, and the story shows what they did then. The ending must hold past the page (what came after, to today), and the person's own words must fit this person.
Out: punk / King's Road / McLaren; "made for the staff, then it spread"; a single masterpiece; a chain handed on; money as glamour.

## Verdict on the plan's lead (Fritz Maytag and Anchor): accept, with a centre moved
Read on the page this round: Oxford ANCHOR DISTILLING (pdf 103, whole entry); Wikipedia, Fritz Maytag and Anchor Brewing Company (raw, fetched; leads only); Stett Holbrook, "Of grape and grain", *San Francisco Chronicle*, 29 Jan 2004 (fetched, whole article); SF Standard, 31 May 2025 (fetched); search results only for SF Chronicle 12 May 2026 and americancraftbeer.com 19 Aug 2026 (not fetched).

- Point 1: 1965, a dying brewery, factory beer ruling (Chronicle 2004: "when factory beers like Budweiser and Pabst ruled").
- Point 2, now whole: Chronicle 2004: Anchor "produced the first drops of what would become a tsunami of artisan brews"; Lew Bryson: "Everybody outside the (major brewers) owes him a debt." For the rye: "the competition now follows" (Chronicle), and Oxford's "signaling the coming of a new generation".
- Point 3: ten years to profit; "He paid his help but not himself" (Chronicle). The family "did not initially meet with enthusiasm" (Wikipedia, needs a better page).
- Point 4, and the centre: when the beer had become a wave that didn't need him, he went to the edge again: a distillery hidden in a corner of the brewery's warehouse, a secret "to all but a few employees" until he knew it was good, the stills called "the gadget" (Chronicle 2004). Oxford: "an early—perhaps too early—shot across the bows". That's the strategist half visible, and it gives the position: the edge was him, not the brewery. The 2010 sale is his letting go; the 2023 closing is the fear realised after he'd gone, one sentence.
- After the page: Maytag is living (born 1937). Anchor closed July 2023; bought by Hamdi Ulukaya May 2024; idle a year on (SF Standard, 2025); search results in 2026 say a comeback with the old labels is under way (not fetched). The ending must not rest on the closing as final, nor on the comeback as done.
- His own words fit: "I wanted out of there. I wanted to move on and do my own thing" (the rebel); a friend: "potentially stubborn", "the lord of Langtry" (the ruler). Quotes with "we" ("simply 30, 40 years ahead of our time"; "starting a rye whiskey revival") are paraphrase only.

### Guards (new)
- **The gadget vs *Asked In*:** a secret distillery sits near Glenlivet's illegal one. Ours was legal and secret from his own staff, for timing, not from the law. One clause, never the word "illicit" or "contraband", never the opening.
- **Limits vs *Kept*:** "not wanting to sacrifice the small size" (Wikipedia, uncited) and Inc's "for setting limits" (403, not fetched) are *Kept*'s shape. Out.
- **Privilege owned or left out:** "for the price of a used car", an heir's inheritance. If money appears, it's plain and once; never "rich rebel" glamour, never the family firm as the thing he rebelled against beyond his own words.
- **Release year contested:** Oxford: production began 1994; Chronicle: first bottling 1996. State neither, or "in the 1990s".
- **"First modern microdistilled whisky"** is Oxford's claim: attribute it.
- Fred Kuh's "set-up" is charming colour; dossier only unless the room needs the scene.
- No Sapporo villainy; no labour story (the union drive is not his).
