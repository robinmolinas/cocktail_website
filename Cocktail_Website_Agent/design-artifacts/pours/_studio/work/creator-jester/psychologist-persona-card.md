# Persona card: The Surrealist (creator-jester)

Wren, round 1. Written from `persona.py creator-jester`, the ten Creator siblings, the Jester-secondary list (22 personalities, incl. the reverse pairing jester-creator, *The Wacky One*), the approved Trickster and Visionary, the four Creator batch drafts, the registry and my voice ledger.

## Core truth
The Surrealist doesn't try to be strange. Their mind puts two things together that don't belong, and to them the connection is simply *there*, the way a face is there in a cloud once you've seen it. When they say the odd thing, they aren't performing. They're reporting. The laugh that follows is a side effect, not the aim.

## Shows / hides
- **Shows:** the sideways remark, the "what if it were…", the idea that makes a meeting go quiet and then laugh. People call them funny, creative, "a bit mad". They seem to have fun with everything.
- **Hides:** two things. First, that they meant it: the odd idea was serious, and being laughed *at* instead of *with* stings more than they show. Second, how much they edit themselves. The strangest things they see, they keep back. The little apology in front ("this is going to sound weird, but…") is the part of them that's been told to be sensible.

## Hidden fear → hidden gift
- **Fear:** being sanded down. The polite smile, then everyone goes with the usual option. "Be serious for once." A life of days that all look the same (Creator: mediocrity, conformity; Jester: boredom, monotony). Underneath: being taken as a joke when they were telling the truth.
- **Gift:** they make ordinary things worth looking at again. After an afternoon with them, people notice what they walked past a thousand times. They give others permission to say the odd thing too.

## The "real me" line to aim for
- Draft A (the scene, funny, gift inside): *"You've started a hundred sentences with 'this is going to sound weird, but…'. It usually stops sounding weird about a minute later."*
- Draft B (the core): *"You don't try to be strange. You say exactly what you saw."*
- Draft C (the hidden sting): *"When people laugh, you laugh too. You still meant it."*
- **Position seed** (earned, Ryu's order; the room decides): drop the apology. Say the second picture straight and let people catch up. It's in their interest, it's kind, and it's far from the other endings (Trickster: "stay long enough to show them how it was done" is close to the *opposite*, good; Experimentalist: "whether you like it is the experiment"). A drink-shaped closing line might be: *"Don't explain it first. Let them taste it."* (checked against the registry: nothing close).

## Truth vs. legend
The true story that sounds made up. Not a hoax (that's the Trickster's ground: "The story was a trick"), not a legend to be unmasked. A fact stranger than fiction, told plainly, would delight them. They'd enjoy a real "wait, that actually happened?".

## Tone
Creator (inspirational) + Jester (witty) → this can be the funniest reading in the set. Robin's rule: as far as it's human and relevant. The form can be the recognition, as the Minimalist's shortness was: **one real sideways leap in the writing**, a sentence that turns somewhere unexpected and lands true. One, not a string of them. Clever for its own sake would betray them (Robin's list) and would be exactly the "performance" they aren't doing.

## Distinct from siblings
- **The Wacky One** (jester-creator, reverse pairing, unauthored) must be protected: for them the laugh *is* the point, and they make things to be funny. For the Surrealist, the seeing is the point and the laugh is what happens to other people. Never write "you love making people laugh".
- **The Trickster** (magician-outlaw, approved) sees the strings, has a plan three moves ahead, says truths as jokes to slip past guards, fears being ignored. The Surrealist has no plan and no agenda; the joke isn't a vehicle for the truth, the strange image *is* the truth. Don't use "you see the strings", "as jokes", "three moves ahead", "ignored", or "Where other people see… you see…".
- **The Experimentalist** (creator-explorer) tries versions to find out; the fun is the process ("let me try something"). The Surrealist doesn't iterate: it arrives in one flash, already joined up ("doesn't that look like…").
- **The Mad Inventor** (explorer-jester, unauthored) builds contraptions. The Surrealist builds nothing new; they put two things that already exist side by side.
- **The Makeover Artist** (creator-magician, unauthored): transforms the thing. The Surrealist leaves the thing alone and changes how it's seen. Careful with the source's "turning the ordinary into the extraordinary", which is Makeover ground.
- **The Photographer** (creator-regular-guy) keeps the ordinary as it was ("the Tuesdays"). The Surrealist sees what else it could be. Don't touch Tuesdays.
- **The Visionary** (creator-hero, approved) sees the big finished thing in the future. The Surrealist sees the thing in front of them, now, sideways.

## Traps
- **Dalí as the mirror.** The source names him. The guest isn't Dalí: no moustache, no melting clocks, no lobster telephone as décor, no "genius". A sourced Dalí fact can serve the story, but the guest must never be told they're like him.
- **Labels:** "quirky", "random", "weird", "eccentric", "the creative one", "the life of the party" (the Jester cliché, and a social label Robin rejects).
- **Praise:** "brilliant imagination", "a mind like no other". They've heard it, and it treats the seeing as a talent to admire rather than a way of being to recognise.
- **Dreams as intoxication.** No absinthe hallucination legend (false, and a drug frame), nothing about altered states. The guest isn't drinking while reading.
- **The fear formula** "What scares you isn't X. It's Y." (already in the set; and the Trickster's "being ignored" is taken).
- **A gimmick drink.** Colour change, smoke, glitter, a trick glass: theatre, not a person, and a Trickster/Makeover move. The Surrealist's strangeness must taste *right* the moment you drink it.

## For the room
- **Hester:** a true story that sounds made up, about two things that were never meant to meet and did. A drink or ingredient from one world ending up in another; a pairing everyone said was wrong that stuck. Surrealist Paris is fair game if it's sourced (the movement's famous image, Lautréamont's "chance meeting of a sewing machine and an umbrella on a dissecting table", is the exact idea, if you can source it and it can live in the dossier or an epigraph about the drink). No hoax, no legend-to-debunk (Trickster), no absinthe hallucinations.
- **Tomás:** Robin's "spark" note fits this personality better than any so far: the Flavor Matrix pairing *is* the person. Two things that don't belong on paper and taste obvious in the glass. Delicious first, strange second. True every time, easy at home, no theatre. For interest only: gin is three of eight; nothing here needs it.
