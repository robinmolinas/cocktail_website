# Pour — The Other Berry · The Surrealist (creator-jester)

## Cocktail

- **name:** The Other Berry
- **tagline:** You don't make things strange. You notice they already are.
- **glassware:** coupe, chilled
- **contains:** `[]`

**recipe**

| amount | item | note (shown) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 60 ml | light, dry white rum | |
| 22.5 ml | fresh lime juice | |
| 20 ml | cherry-tomato and vanilla syrup (below) | made the way you'd make a strawberry syrup |
| 1 | small ripe cherry tomato, for the rim | where the strawberry would sit |

**Cherry-tomato and vanilla syrup** (a day ahead; makes enough for a dozen drinks or more): put 250 g ripe cherry tomatoes and 250 g white sugar in a blender and blend until completely smooth and the sugar has dissolved. Don't heat it. Push it through a fine sieve into a jar, pressing the pulp to get all the liquid out, and throw the skins and seeds away. Split a vanilla pod lengthways, scrape the seeds into the jar and drop the pod in. Leave it in the fridge overnight, then take the pod out. It keeps up to two weeks in the fridge. Taste it before you use it, and if it fizzes at all, throw it out. (No pod? Half a teaspoon of vanilla extract instead.)

**method**
1. A day ahead, make the syrup (above).
2. Put the coupe in the freezer for ten minutes, or fill it with ice and water while you mix.
3. Pour the rum, the lime juice and the syrup into a shaker.
4. Fill the shaker with ice and shake hard for about ten seconds, until the outside is too cold to hold.
5. Empty the glass if it's full of ice water. Strain the drink into it through a small sieve, so no bits of tomato get through.
6. Cut a small slit in the bottom of a cherry tomato and sit it on the rim, where the strawberry would go.

**closingLine:** *Tell them it's tomato before they taste it.*

## Reading

**epigraph**
*Made like a strawberry daiquiri. There isn't a strawberry in it.*

**whoYouAre**
You've started a hundred sentences with "this is going to sound weird, but…". Then you say what you saw: the thing that looks like something else, two ideas nobody thought belonged together. There's a pause, then a laugh. About a minute later, it doesn't sound weird at all.

None of it is a performance. You're just saying what's there. You laugh along, but you meant it, and it stings a little when it's taken as only a joke. So the strangest things you see, you mostly don't say. What you can't stand is the sensible version: an idea sanded down until it fits in. What you give people is a second look at things they thought they knew.

**yours**

1. In 1886, four men named Nix brought tomatoes into New York from the West Indies. They paid the tax on vegetables, under protest, because fruit came in free, and sued to get the money back. At the trial, both sides read dictionaries aloud. The importers looked up "fruit", "vegetables" and "tomato"; the other side looked up "pea", "cucumber" and "squash". Two men who'd sold produce for thirty years gave evidence.

2. The case reached the US Supreme Court, and in 1893 it answered. Botanically, the Court said, the tomato is the fruit of a vine. But the people who buy and sell food call it a vegetable, because it's served with the soup, the fish or the meat, not as dessert. So for the tariff, it was a vegetable. And botany goes further than the Court did: the tomato is, botanically, a berry. The strawberry isn't.

3. My guess is you'd have skipped the dictionaries. You'd have looked at it, red, its seeds kept on the inside the way a berry keeps them, and served it for dessert. It's what you do with everything: you go by what a thing is, not by where it usually sits.

4. So this is a daiquiri (rum, lime and sugar, shaken), made the way you'd make a strawberry one, with the tomato in the strawberry's seat. The syrup is made the way a strawberry syrup is: ripe cherry tomatoes blended cold with sugar, plus a vanilla pod, because vanilla and tomato, surprisingly, go well together. It tastes of ripe red fruit and vanilla first, and only then of an idea. There's no trick in it. It's the tomato, served where it looked like it belonged.

5. You apologise in advance for things you'll turn out to be right about. Drop the "this is going to sound weird". It gives people a way to laugh it off before they've looked. Say what you saw, plainly, the first time. Then let them catch up. They usually do, about a minute later.
