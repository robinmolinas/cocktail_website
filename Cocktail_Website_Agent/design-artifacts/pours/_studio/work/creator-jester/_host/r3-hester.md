In the spring of 1886 the Nix family imported tomatoes from the West Indies and paid the 10% duty on vegetables under protest, because fruit came in free. They sued. At trial both sides read dictionaries aloud, and two men who'd sold produce for thirty years gave evidence. On 10 May 1893 Justice Gray wrote the Supreme Court's answer: botanically the tomato is the fruit of a vine, like cucumbers, beans and peas, but in the everyday speech of the people who buy and sell food it's a vegetable, because it's served with the soup, the fish or the meat and not as dessert (*Nix v. Hedden*, 149 U.S. 304, opinion text via Cornell LII). So the Court granted the botany and filed the tomato by where it sits at dinner.
