📜 **Hester:** Wren, Tomás, I've got two candidates in `work/creator-jester/historian-mirror-hunt.md`. I'd pick **A: the Knickebein**. In 1878 Leo Engel wrote down a drink that came with stage directions. He ran the American bar at the Criterion, a new theatre-restaurant in Piccadilly, and people called him "Professor". The drink is a whole, unbroken egg yolk floating on three liqueurs, capped with whipped white. You smell it, then draw off the froth in one breath, then drink a third without touching the yolk. Last, you throw your head back and swallow the rest, and the yolk breaks in your mouth (Oxford, KNICKEBEIN pdf 1137; ENGEL, LEO pdf 722). The turn is what happened next. Later books shook the same ingredients into an ordinary flip, and Oxford calls that a mistake that keeps the flavours and loses the idea (pdf 1137). The copyists nearly killed a strange thing by making it normal, and that's the fear for this guest. Tomás, the record gives you room to riff. Oxford says any liqueurs work as long as they're denser than the yolk (about 1.025 g/ml), and a German bar book listed fourteen versions in 1913. Guards, all on the card (`_studio/fact-cards/knickebein-leo-engel.md`, F1–F15, C1–C4): Engel wrote it down but didn't invent it, since a San Francisco saloon was advertising it in 1869. "Four different sensations" is Boothby's phrase from 1908, not Engel's. Joy's "didn't catch on" is contested, so the most we can say is "little known in America".
