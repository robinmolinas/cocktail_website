# Wren's read, round 1: The Bad Influence (lover-outlaw)

Sources: `persona.py lover-outlaw`; plan row (`plans/lover.md`); *A Proper Drink* pp. 155–157 re-read whole this round; Oxford EMPLOYEES ONLY (pdf 721) and COCKTAIL VARIATION (pdf 533); sibling and Outlaw-side pours grepped (`lover-*.md`, `*-outlaw.md`, `ruler-jester.md`).

## Who this is

The sheet: the reason the evening got interesting. They suggest the thing nobody planned, and somehow everyone says yes. Loud and raw about what they feel. "The rebellion isn't cruelty. It is their way of saying: be real with me." Fear: being tamed into someone safe, then left for being boring. Goal: closeness. Driver: leave a mark.

**The one true thing (no sibling shares it):** politeness feels like distance to them, and a rule broken together is the quickest way past it. They don't want an audience and they don't want followers: they want accomplices. Halfway through the thing nobody should be doing, people stop performing and say what they actually think, and that's the person the Bad Influence was trying to reach all along. The dare is a question in disguise: *will you be real with me?*

- **What they hide:** that the wildness is a way of asking to be close. Under the "come on" is "please come". They'd never say the second out loud, because asking plainly feels like the safe, tame version of themselves.
- **What they fear:** being kept only for the noise. Two exits and both are lonely: stay the wild one and never be known, or get tamed into someone safe and watch people drift off because the fun has gone. (The sheet's own words. Not the end of the night: that's the Pleasure Seeker's. Not losing the wheel: that's the Charismatic Leader's.)
- **What they give (said out loud, per Robin's caregiver edits):** around them, people drop the act. They say the true thing, the funny thing, the thing they've never told anyone, usually while doing something they shouldn't. The Bad Influence makes people more themselves, not worse. (Never "people go further than they would alone, and come back glad": that's *Who's In?*'s gift.)
- **Recognition line I'm building toward:** *You don't want an audience. You want accomplices.* (Grepped: "accomplice" is in no pour.)
- **Position (Ryu, earned last):** they don't have to keep raising the stakes to keep people. Proposal, pointing to life: ask someone to do something completely ordinary with you, and see who says yes to that too. The ones who are really with you will. Ends on what they gain: being kept for themselves.

## Traps

- Drinking as the fun: no "one more", no party-bar frame, no drinking harder, nothing about hangovers or mornings.
- Courtney Love and the stage: no examples, no rock clichés in the words (the image brief can have grit).
- Cruelty, recklessness, danger as the thrill: the persona's line is "the rebellion isn't cruelty". Nothing risky to anyone else's safety, so no driving, no water at night.
- "Tamed" can read as being in a couple. The reading never requires a partner.
- On the page and out: "a bar whose patrons got laid"; "Women came and… men followed"; "overwhelmingly male"; "cult of personality… primarily Dushan"; "they had no other option" (the staff quote's back half); "cried all the way to the bank" / highest-grossing (gloating isn't the hold); "merry prankster" (the chapter title too: the Trickster's); the speakeasy façade; free pouring as rebellion (LAB-adjacent).
- The soup's motive isn't on the page. P. 155 says Zaric wanted *food* because at Pravda hungry customers left. That sits next to the soup but is about food in general, and it's partly business. If used: "food", plainly, partly business, and any warmer reading of the soup is signposted.

## Story test (what the record must show)

1. **A rule broken together:** they walked out of Schiller's together, early 2004, and took the bar manager with them; the owner "was not happy with us" (Kosmas, p. 155). ✓
2. **Dismissed for the noise:** the cocktail people decided they couldn't be taking their drinks seriously "because people are having too much fun there" (p. 157). ✓ This is the cost limb, on the record, and it's this person's fear: wanted for the fun, not taken for real.
3. **The quiet part, under the noise:** from the first day, homemade chicken soup for anyone still at the bar at closing time (pp. 155–156; Oxford pdf 721: free, each night). ✓
4. **People stayed for more than the noise:** nobody ever left EO; some bartenders have worked nowhere else; Zaric: they stay "because they feel they had a hand in creating this" (p. 156). ✓ **This is the mirror the plan missed:** people stay where they're accomplices, not audience. It's the one true thing, said by Zaric about his own staff.

All four limbs pass, better than the plan thought: limb 4 now has its reason on the page.
