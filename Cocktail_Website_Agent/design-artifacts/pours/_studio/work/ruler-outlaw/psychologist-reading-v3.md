## whoYouAre

You were the one who said the old way was finished. People laughed. Then they let you try, it worked, and the new way was yours to keep going: the rota, the budget, the wet Tuesday when nobody remembers why anything changed. Most people who start a fight are gone by then. You stayed.

You'd rarely admit it, but you never wanted a free-for-all. You wanted it run properly, by you, and you had the plan for afterwards before the argument was over. The day you dread is someone new standing where you stood, saying what you said, and you hearing yourself answer the way they once answered you. But staying is the hard part, and hardly anyone thanks you for it. You make the better way real, and keep it real, so the people who wanted it actually get to live in it.

## yours

Toby Maloney came from the club world, where he could turn out seven vodka drinks in fifteen seconds. A tiny New York bar where one round took fifteen minutes struck him as laughable. He took a night a week behind its bar anyway, thinking he might learn something.

In 2007, The Violet Hour opened with him behind the bar, in Chicago, then mostly a city of beer and whiskey. Later, one bartender said the city's bartending family trees ran back to two people, and he was one.

He has since said he cringes at some of it, like refusing to make a vodka Martini because he thought it was beneath him. He also said he doesn't think any of this would exist without that seriousness. He kept both halves. A few years on, one of his protégés co-wrote a small book of radical recipes, a reaction, his co-author said, to how boring a lot of cocktail people were. Maloney contributed to it.

That's you in one life: the newcomer who laughed, the strict one who held the line, the old hand who gave something to whoever came next. You know the cringe at how hard you once had to push, and you'd still never say it was wrong to push. And one day, years in, someone younger looks at what you built the way you once looked at the old way.

The cocktail I've made for you starts from the Pimm's Cup that one of that book's recipes began with, a British summer cooler: muddled strawberry and cucumber, the Pimm's left in, a little lemon, and soda. Then one spoonful of the bright red bitters their book swapped in for the Pimm's. The measures, the lemon and one more touch are my own: a sprig of mint, clapped once between the palms, a trick Maloney pioneered, as a nod to what you bring to whatever the next one makes. Your order is still the glass. Their change is in it.

When someone you've taught comes back and tells you it's all got a bit boring, don't defend it first. Ask what they'd do instead, and give them something towards it. You had your answer ready once. So might they, and whatever they build will carry some of you in it.

## Resonance

**"This is me" line:** *"The day you dread is someone new standing where you stood, saying what you said, and you hearing yourself answer the way they once answered you."* The persona's own irony (they fear the next upheaval), turned into a fear of becoming, never an accusation.

**As the guest.** The tagline names the after, the one true thing, in the positive frame. ¶1 is a scene anyone who has changed something will know, with no label. ¶2 says the hidden thing (order was the point, run by them), the fear, and the gift aloud. The drink tells the irony kindly: the guest's own order is still the glass, and the next one's change is in it, a spoonful, welcomed rather than fought. The proposal gives back ("whatever they build will carry some of you").

**As the sceptic.** "Said the old way was finished" alone fits any Outlaw, so it's finished by "You stayed", and all of ¶2 is about the after. Swap test: *Beside the First* questions its own work first, while here the cringe is Maloney's and kept, and the challenger is someone else. *Asked In* distrusts the yes; this one took it. *Still Yours* goes off it when everyone arrives. *Who's In?* hides enjoying "your hand on the wheel", so the hidden thing here is order and the plan for afterwards. *Not the Same* loves the old version. *Off-Label* owns bitters as the drink's foundation: its epigraph says "built on them" and "by the dash", and neither phrase appears here. The bitters are told as a swap for the Pimm's, which is this story's own fact.

**The mint (Tomás asked).** Kind, not a stamp, the way I've written it: it's what the old hand brings to the next one's drink, the "give them something towards it" of y6 made physical. It never claims Maloney gave this, or anything specific, to the book (A5): he "contributed", and the mint is our nod.
