## whoYouAre

You were the one who said the old way was finished. People laughed. Then they let you try, it worked, and the new way was yours to keep going: the rota, the budget, the wet Tuesday when nobody remembers why anything changed. Most people who start a fight are gone by then. You stayed.

You'd rarely admit it, but you never wanted a free-for-all. You wanted it run properly, by you, and you had the plan for afterwards before the argument was over. The day you dread is someone new standing where you stood, saying what you said, and you hearing yourself answer the way they once answered you. But staying is the hard part, and hardly anyone thanks you for it. You make the better way real, and keep it real, so the people who wanted it actually get to live in it.

## yours

Toby Maloney came from loud club bars, where he could turn out seven vodka drinks in fifteen seconds. A tiny New York bar where one round took fifteen minutes struck him as laughable. He went anyway, to learn.

In 2007, The Violet Hour opened with him behind the bar, in Chicago, then a city of beer and whiskey. Later, one bartender said the city's bartending family trees ran back to two people, and he was one.

He has since said he cringes at some of it, like refusing to make a vodka Martini because he thought it was beneath him. He also said that without that seriousness, they wouldn't have what they have. He kept both halves. Then one of his protégés co-wrote a small book of radical recipes, a reaction, his co-author said, to how boring a lot of cocktail people were. Maloney contributed to it. I like to think he knew exactly what it was.

That's you in one life: the newcomer who laughed, the strict one who stayed, the old hand who gave something to whoever came next. You know the cringe at how hard you once had to push, and you'd still never say it was wrong to push. And one day, years in, someone younger looks at what you built the way you once looked at the old way.

The cocktail I've made for you is the next one's answer. That book took The Violet Hour's Pimm's Cup, kept its muddled strawberry and cucumber, and poured bright red bitters where the Pimm's had been. It's fresh, a little bitter, and better than it sounds, which is roughly how your own idea went. The measures here are my own, as is one addition: a sprig of mint, clapped once between the palms, a trick Maloney pioneered, as a nod to what you bring to whatever the next one makes. Your old order is still in the glass, under their change.

So when someone you've taught comes back and tells you it's all got a bit boring, don't defend it first. Ask what they'd do instead, and give them something towards it. You had your answer ready once. So might they, and whatever they build will carry some of you in it.

## Resonance

**"This is me" line:** *"The day you dread is someone new standing where you stood, saying what you said, and you hearing yourself answer the way they once answered you."* The persona's own irony (they fear the next upheaval), turned into a fear of becoming, never an accusation.

**As the guest.** The tagline names the after, the one true thing, in the positive frame. ¶1 is a scene anyone who has changed something will know, with no label. ¶2 says the hidden thing (order was the point, run by them), the fear, and the gift aloud. The drink now *is* the position: the next one's answer, grown out of the guest's own template, and the proposal gives back ("whatever they build will carry some of you"). The glass proves that line.

**As the sceptic.** "Said the old way was finished" alone fits any Outlaw, so it's finished by "You stayed", and all of ¶2 is about the after. Swap test: *Beside the First* questions its own work first, while here the cringe is Maloney's and kept, and the challenger is someone else. *Asked In* distrusts the yes; this one took it. *Still Yours* goes off it when everyone arrives. *Who's In?* hides enjoying "your hand on the wheel", so the hidden thing here is order and the plan for afterwards. *Not the Same* loves the old version. *Off-Label* owns bitters as the drink's foundation: its epigraph says "built on them" and "by the dash", and neither phrase appears here. The bitters are told as a swap for the Pimm's, which is this story's own fact.

**The mint (Tomás asked).** Kind, not a stamp, the way I've written it: it's what the old hand brings to the next one's drink, the "give them something towards it" of y6 made physical. It never claims Maloney gave this, or anything specific, to the book (A5): he "contributed", and the mint is our nod.
