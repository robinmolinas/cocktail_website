| lineage | He came from the club world, where he could turn out seven vodka drinks in fifteen seconds. On his first visit to a slow New York craft bar he was handed a Daiquiri served up, and he'd never had anything like it. Yet, in Simonson's telling, he didn't think the place would amount to much: a quarter of an hour for one round seemed laughable. He offered to bartend there one night a week anyway, to give Petraske a break, thinking he might learn something (*Proper* pp. 92–93; F3–F4). | Won over before he won anyone over. | overthrow | In 2007 The Violet Hour opened with him behind the bar, in a city Simonson sums up as beer, whiskey and nothing fancy. It had no vodka, no olives, small coupes, house rules of manners, and in his words "everything fresh, everything measured right". A month later the Drawing Room opened, and between them Chicago suddenly had a cocktail scene (*Proper* pp. 280–282; Oxford pdf 522; F6–F11). | A real overturning of how a city drank, and not done alone. | the fight, and the win |
