# Persona card: The Revolutionary (ruler-outlaw)

Wren, round 1, 2026-10-01. Source: `persona.py ruler-outlaw` (xlsx row), sibling readings read today, family plan row, the plan lead's pages read whole (Imbibe pdf 74-75; Oxford pdf 1331, 1579, 1580, 517).

## The row, in plain words
- Essence: leading the way in challenging how things are. Goal: control. Fear: revolution. Drivers: provide structure, leave a mark.
- The xlsx story gives the irony: they tear down the old order, and once they've won they fear the next upheaval as much as any king did. "Order was what they wanted all along, as long as it was theirs." The example (Guevara, the banknotes signed "Che") stays out of everything: politics and violence. But the image behind it is the key: **the rebel who ends up running the bank, and still signs with the rebel's name.**

## The one true thing (no sibling has it)
**This is the only personality whose story starts after the win.** Every other rebel in the set is about the fight. This one fought, won, and stayed for the dull part: the rota, the budget, the new way of doing it that now has to work on a wet Tuesday. They were never against order. They were against an order that didn't deserve to be followed, and they had the replacement ready before they'd finished the argument.

## What they hide
- That they like being in charge, and always did. It sounds like the people they fought, so they don't say it.
- That they had the plan for afterwards long before anyone asked. The noise was the visible part; the plan was the point.
- That holding it is harder than taking it was, and nobody gives you credit for the holding.

## What they fear
**The day someone stands where they once stood, saying what they once said, and they hear themselves answer like the people they beat.** Not losing. Becoming the thing they replaced. (Draft "this is me" line, frame checked: not the Trickster's "What scares you isn't… It's…", not the Old Master's "What you dread isn't…".)

## What they give (said out loud, once)
They make change real. Plenty of people can say what's wrong; this one is still there on Monday making the new way work, so the people who wanted it actually get to live in it. Things are better, and stay better, because they didn't walk away after the shouting.

## The position (Ryu's, earned last)
When the next one comes to tell them it's all wrong, ask what they'd put in its place, and listen to the answer the way they once wanted to be listened to. They had an answer ready, back then. So might the next one. (Gain: the thing they built gets to keep living, in someone else's hands. Never "step aside", never "you've become them".)

Outlaw neighbours (shared secondary), the real danger zone:
- **Old Master** (*Beside the First*): fears "settling into being right", questions their own work first. Closest fear in the set. Keep apart: the Old Master's challenger is themself; the Revolutionary's is someone young with their old fire. The position must never be "question it yourself first".
- **Rule Breaker** (*Asked In*): distrusts the yes, being let in = being owned. The Revolutionary is the opposite: they took the yes and run it.
- **Trend Setter** (*Still Yours*): liked it first, goes off it when everyone arrives. The Revolutionary wants everyone to arrive.
- **Maverick** (*Can't Watch*): owns "those are the rules", bending them one person at a time. **Campaigner** (*Not Too Polite*): owns "Someone had to say it".

## Traps
- **The hypocrite reading.** "As long as it was theirs" can't become an accusation. The irony is owned in one kind clause, never as a gotcha.
- **Politics, violence, uniforms, flags, fists**, anything from the xlsx imagery. The guest's revolution is a team, a family business, a club, a kitchen.
- **Ruler puns:** no rule/reign/crown/kingdom/throne wordplay (the plan already guards Wondrich's "Kingdom of Mixed Drinks").
- **Barnum:** "you challenge the way things are" fits every Outlaw. The reading lives in the after, or it isn't finished.
- **Owned frames:** "You were never against…" (*Fair Measure* tagline), "Someone had to say it" (*Not Too Polite*), "those are the rules" (*Can't Watch*), "settling into being right" (*Beside the First*), "What scares you isn't…" (*No Accident*).
- **Downfall stories**, comebacks, and nostalgia revivals.
- **Gradual erosion passed off as a revolution** (see verdict below).

## Story test
**Must hold:** (1) a person, or a drink with a person behind it, who overturned how something was done, with a reason of their own on the page (a conviction, quoted or reported, not a historian's list of causes); (2) they won, and then ran the new way themselves: opened the place, set the standard, trained the next ones, did the dull part; (3) the irony told in one kind clause.
**Can be waived as story fact:** the next challenger. If the page has it, it's a gift; if not, it moves into the guest's half (the fear and the proposal), never invented as an event.
**Out:** violence, politics, a ban broken for profit (Outlaw ground), a story ending in their fall, a reading that calls them a hypocrite, an erosion by many small causes, a comeback, a plain revival.

## Verdict on the plan's lead (the single drink that "overthrew" the punch bowl): replace
Read whole, the pages turn it.
1. **No conviction.** Imbibe pdf 74-75 calls the bowl's fall "a plethora of subtle and incremental strains", "like all long-running social institutions": industrialisation, busyness, better-aged spirits, a global economy, central heating, and democracy as one item on the list. The plan quotes the one item as "the conviction behind the fall". It's erosion, the opposite of a revolution.
2. **No overthrow.** Punch didn't lose: "We didn't stop drinking Punch when the bowls went up on the shelf… having it quick and on the spot." The bowl was "promoted" to ceremony. And the step from bowl to glass on that page is Willard's punch "one glass at a time", Lover's reserved lead.
3. **No next challenger.** The 2010s bowl returns "as an efficient way of getting a well-mixed drink into the hands of a crowd" (Oxford pdf 1579): a convenience, not a challenge, and nobody on the page minds.
4. **Nobody in it.** With no person and no conviction, every limb needs "I like to think", and the guest has no one to see themselves in. And the deposed order would be *Four Shares*' bowl.
5. The backup (the 1960s canon deposing the Dry Martini) was already weak on conviction ("silly, sweet, simple, colorful"); same erosion problem. Not it either.

## Hunting brief (for Hester)
A person who overturned a bar's, a trade's or a drink's way of doing things, with their reason on the record, and then had to run what replaced it. Best if the page also shows them meeting someone who came at them the way they once came at the old order. A pointer I saw in passing and ruled out: *Proper* p. 169 (Sam Ross ignoring Pegu's "house rules or hierarchy"), which has the shape from the challenger's side, but Saunders and Pegu are the Auteur's (*Making the Calls*). Living people dated. No Prohibition law-breaking, no Tales of the Cocktail (its founder's 2017 exit is a downfall).
