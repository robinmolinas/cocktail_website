## whoYouAre

You were the one who said the old way was finished. At work, in the family business, at the club where nothing had changed in twenty years. People laughed. Then they let you try, it worked, and suddenly the new way was yours to keep going: the rota, the budget, the wet Tuesday when nobody remembers why anything changed. Most people who start a fight are long gone by then. You stayed.

You'd rarely admit it, but you never wanted a free-for-all. You wanted it to run properly, and you wanted to be the one running it. You had the plan for afterwards long before the argument was over. That sounds a little too much like the people you replaced, so you leave it unsaid. The day you dread is someone new standing where you stood, saying what you said, and you hearing yourself answer the way they once answered you. But staying is the hard part, and hardly anyone thanks you for it. Plenty of people can say what's wrong. You make the better way real, and keep it real, so the ones who wanted it actually get to live in it. It counts for more than all the shouting.

## yours

Toby Maloney came from loud club bars, where he could turn out seven vodka drinks in fifteen seconds. A tiny New York bar where one round took fifteen minutes struck him as laughable. He stayed to learn.

In 2007 he opened The Violet Hour in Chicago, then a beer-and-whiskey town: no vodka, small coupes, everything measured. Another serious bar opened a month later, and suddenly the city had a cocktail scene. Before long, its bartenders traced their family trees to two teachers. He was one.

He has since said he cringes at some of it, like refusing to make a vodka Martini because he thought it beneath him, and that without that seriousness none of this would exist. He stood by it. Then one of the bartenders he'd trained co-wrote a small book of radical recipes, a reaction, his co-author said, to how boring a lot of cocktail people had got. Maloney contributed to it. I like to think he knew exactly what that book was, and helped anyway.

That's you in one life: the newcomer who laughed, the strict one who stayed, the teacher who gave something to whoever came next. You know the cringe at how hard you once had to push, and you'd still never say it was wrong to push. The cocktail I've made for you starts from The Violet Hour's Pimm's Cup, with its muddled strawberry and cucumber, the very drink that book took as its starting point. I've kept it whole and added one measured spoon of the bitters the book poured in its place, as a nod to the one who came after. Your order and their change share one tall, cold glass, and it's better for both.

So when someone you taught comes back and tells you it's all got a bit boring, don't defend it first. Ask what they'd put in its place, and give them something towards it. You had your answer ready once. So might they, and whatever they build will carry some of you in it.

## Resonance

**"This is me" line:** *"The day you dread is someone new standing where you stood, saying what you said, and you hearing yourself answer the way they once answered you."* It's the persona's own irony (they fear the next upheaval), turned so it's a fear of becoming, never an accusation.

**As the guest.** The tagline names the after, which is the one true thing, with the positive frame first. ¶1 is a scene anyone who has changed something will recognise, without a job title (work, family business, a club are three doors, not a label). ¶2 says the hidden thing (order was the point; I want to run it), the fear, and the gift out loud, and leaves them valued. The proposal gives back: what they built keeps going, in someone else's hands.

**As the sceptic.** Horoscope check: "you said the old way was finished" alone would fit any Outlaw; it's finished by "You stayed", and every line in ¶2 is about the after. Swap test: *Beside the First* questions its own work first, but here the cringe is Maloney's, kept and not taken back, and the challenger is someone else. *Asked In* distrusts the yes; this one took it. *Still Yours* goes off it when everyone arrives; this one wants everyone in. *Who's In?* hides enjoying "your hand on the wheel", so I kept the hidden thing on order and the plan for afterwards, not on enjoying control. *Not the Same* loves the old version; here the old one had to go. No "You were never against…", "Someone had to say it", "those are the rules", "What scares you isn't…", "look what", "Here's the part nobody guesses".

**Fences kept (from Hester's card).** Never "his bar" (Alexander owned it): "he opened". The scene is two bars, told so. The rules aren't attributed to him. The book is aimed at "a lot of cocktail people", never at him, and what he contributed isn't stated. "Cosmos" becomes "vodka drinks" (*Still Yours*). No Milk & Honey, Pegu, Cure, New Orleans, Katrina, Joly by name, and no "militant", "seminaries" or "our way or the highway". "Looking back" carries no year. One signposted sentence ("I like to think…").
