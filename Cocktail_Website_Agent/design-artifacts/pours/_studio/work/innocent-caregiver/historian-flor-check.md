## Wren's two questions
**Does it form on its own?** Yes. The Consejo Regulador (the sherry DO's own body; Oxford cites its site in WINES, FORTIFIED, pdf 2201) describes the film as formed "by specific indigenous ambient yeasts". Luyten: in Andalucía flor starts "almost immediately after fermentation"; the yeasts are in the air. Secondary; the Consejo is the closest to the original.

**How long does fino age under it?** No fixed figure for fino on any page I read. What is sourced: **all sherry** must age at least two years (sherry.wine, citing the Pliego de Condiciones; this scope is all sherry, not fino in particular); the Cádiz study sampled a fino "5 years old"; for amontillado the flor "dies off after a few years" (Luyten). Reading-safe: "for years". The search-summary figures ("4 to 7 years", "three to eight") come from an unattributed wiki site: never cite them.

## New, sourced, and useful
- **It breathes the air in first.** sherry.wine: the oxygen that gets in "is rapidly consumed by the yeasts' breathing", and the wine stays protected beneath the film. Luyten: flor protects the wine from oxygen "while also consuming most of it". This is the strongest mirror line on any page: what would spoil the young wine reaches the flor first.
- **The wine carries its taste.** *Molecules* 2022: acetaldehyde "comes mainly from the metabolic action" of the flor and gives the fino its green-apple and almond aromas. Clause 3's "carrying something of the watcher" is on the page. Taste words only, never recipe claims.
- Seasons: winemakers report it thicker in spring and autumn (Luyten, reported, so "winemakers say").

## Against it (my honest weighing)
1. **Clause 4 is the weak one.** No cost to the flor is on any page except its ending: for amontillado the veil "disappears" (dies naturally, or the wine is fortified until it can't live, 16% or more per *Molecules*, 17% per Luyten). That's the watcher ended so the wine can go on: self-erasure, and close to "dying", both out in Wren's test. What it takes is the wine's (it eats over 90% of the glycerol in the oldest fino, *Molecules*): a cost to the watched, not the watcher. For fino there's no stated cost at all.
2. **The Free Spirit echo is structural, not one word.** Both are microbes that live on a drink in its cellar, named, and depend on the cellar's air (Oxford pdf 346 ties flor to the same humid bodegas as evaporation). A fence on words alone won't stop the shape repeating (my Angel pitfall). Ownable only if the two tellings share no word (walls, cellar air, evaporation, "lives on what escapes") and the image briefs stay apart (inside the cask, the white film, against their black walls).

## Traps, if it runs
- **"Seen as a defect"** (Luyten: it occurred naturally "but it was seen as a defect"): the scorned-then-useful shape Wren struck from rye as the Underdog's. Out.
- **Replanting:** the Cádiz cellar replanted weak casks with flor from the casks next to them. Tempting, but it leads straight to the True Friend's ending ("allowed to be looked after too"). Out of the reading, or Wren rules.
- **The solera:** sherry.wine calls criaderas y soleras "the only viable method" for biological ageing, so it sits right next to flor. It's A Brother's Care's ground: never named.
- **"Green apple":** one apple in the family (Thoreau's, family guard). The reading says almond or bread (Codex p. 246 "fresh bread"), never apple. Almond is a taste word, not the `nuts` veto.
- **"Angels' share", "evaporation":** Free Spirit and Lover ground.
