# Fact audit v1: innocent-caregiver, reading v1 (round 4, 2026-09-30)
Audited: `psychologist-reading-v1.md` (title block, whoYouAre, y1–y5). Card: `fact-cards/flor.md` (updated this round: F5 scoped, F13–F16 added, C1 widened). Sources re-read on screen this round: *Codex* pp. 91, 245–247; sherry.wine "Ageing" (full page); *Molecules* 2022; Luyten.

**Verdict: FAIL, eight fixes, all small.** The story holds. One of them is mine: see E1.

## E1, struck first: my own shorthand
"The air that gets in is rapidly consumed by the yeasts' breathing" was my r2–r3 pitch. Read in full, the Consejo's sentence is about **oxygen dissolved into the wine when it's moved between barrels** (the solera's transfers), and that oxygen *stimulates the flor's regrowth* (sherry.wine). It isn't a general statement about air creeping in. The mirror survives on other sentences from the same page: the butts leave "two fists" of air at the top; "the wood is permeable to oxygen"; the flor protects the wine "by preventing direct contact between the liquid and the air contained within the butt"; and Luyten says flor protects the wine from oxygen "while also consuming most of it". So the facts are: there's air in the barrel, and more comes through the wood. The flor sits between it and the wine and uses up most of the oxygen. Clause 4's "effort" still stands: the breathing is real. Only its scope was wrong.
