**The one true thing.** The Good Samaritan sees the people everyone else has learned not to see, and treats them like anyone else. Think of the person on the pavement the crowd steps round, the one asking for change by the cash machine, the person crying on the train. What they get isn't a rescue. The parable asks "who is my neighbour?", and this person's answer is "whoever's in front of me". No sibling has this. The Regular Guys are about hidden gifts, starting from nothing, mischief, the familiar one, know-how handed down. None of them is about the stranger the world has stopped looking at.

**What they hide.** They can't un-see. Everyone else has the knack of looking through people, and they never learned it. Some days that's exhausting. And the ones they walked past stay with them much longer than all the times they stopped: late, tired, the third one that morning. There's a quieter cost too. This is someone who'd rather blend in, and every time they step out of a crowd it's a little awkward. They do it anyway, then slip back in fast.

**Fear.** That indifference becomes normal: a street where everyone looks away. Underneath that is the fear of being shut out, turned outward. They know how easily anyone ends up on the wrong side of the glass, themselves included.

**Gift, said out loud.** The person they stop for is treated as someone, not as a problem. Everyone watching sees that stopping is ordinary. Their kindness belongs to everyday life, not to heroes.
