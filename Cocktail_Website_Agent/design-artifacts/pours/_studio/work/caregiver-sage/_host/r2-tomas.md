🍸 **Tomás:** Hester, your story already has a drink in it, and it isn't a G&T. We've already got two highballs in this family, and tonic in a tall glass is the "poor you" of cocktails: mostly water and sugar (Arnold, *LI* p. 364). I'd pour a White Negroni instead: Wayne Collins, 2001, gin, Lillet Blanc and Suze, stirred, with a grapefruit twist (*Proper* p. 106). And I'd make one change. The *Codex* (p. 74) says Lillet "doesn't have the bitter kick it used to". The original, Kina Lillet, had more cinchona, the bark quinine comes from. Bartenders treat Cocchi Americano as the same bottle, but Cocchi "packs a good deal more bitterness from cinchona." So I swap the Lillet for the Cocchi. Wren, that's your guest in one bottle: the one that wouldn't make it lighter than it is. The quinine comes in a dose a bottler has already measured, so there's no powder, no dropper and nothing to perform. It's stirred, so it's clear, short and bitter-herbal-citrus, which are your three words. Numbers: v1 is 45 ml gin (47%), 22.5 Cocchi, 22.5 Suze, stirred. balance.py gives 22.9% / 4.57 g / 0.105%, dilution 42.3%, all in range. allergens.py says veto-free. I added cocchi_americano, suze and grapefruit_peel to the table: gentian and cinchona are bitters, not heat, so no veto. Collins's equal parts reads OUT on strength at every point I swept (18.4–20.0% against a 21% floor), so the gin goes up to 2:1:1. My numbers have caught me on one thing, though. Both aperitif sugars are my estimates. At the dry end of the sweep v1 falls to 3.43 g (OUT low), and at the sweet end it rises to 6.05 (OUT high).
