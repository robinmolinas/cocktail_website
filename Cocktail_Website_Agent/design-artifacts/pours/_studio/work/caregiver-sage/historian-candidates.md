# Hester, mirror hunt: caregiver-sage (The Doctor), round 1

Persona: knowledge and intelligence used to care; goal service; fears selfishness, and (secondary) deception, ambiguity; drivers "provide structure". Example Dr. House.

## Chosen to pitch: the doctor who wouldn't write it (card: fact-cards/dave-arnold-quinine.md)
Arnold's mother, a doctor, refused her own son a quinine prescription for his tonic, without hesitation, because strangers would drink it (LI p. 365). The easy kindness was to help her boy; the real one was for people she'd never meet. He got it elsewhere, and then wrote the sternest safety box in the book (p. 366); the link between the two is our reading, signposted.
Mirror: care that says no; knowledge used for the absent patient; no ambiguity ("without hesitation"); fear of selfishness inverted (she refused to do the self-serving favour). History ≤ half; lineage: tonic was a medicine, now isn't (LI p. 365; Oxford pdf 898, 2043–2044).
Glass: a G&T riff (highball). Spark for Tomás: the dose, the bitterness as the care. Bottled tonic/tonic syrup only, never powder.
Risks: Arnold named in *Overnight* (own it or keep him the channel); she's unnamed and everything is her son's account.

## Runner-up: Prescription Julep (Harper's 1857, "Quackenboss, M.D."; Imbibe pdf 152–153)
A comic doctor writing a julep as a prescription in Latin. Fiction; the doctor who says yes. Good foil, weaker mirror.

## Rejected
- Gimlet / Surgeon Gimlette: sourced only to the Royal Navy website via *Joy* pdf 289; Oxford calls it "a simple cocktail with a difficult explanation" (pdf 887); lime cordial is already *Not Only the Way*'s lineage.
- Penicillin (Proper pp. 170–171): a drug's name, no doctor in the story.
- Navy doctors who carried the Daiquiri to Washington, 1909 (Oxford DAIQUIRI pdf 623): carriers, not carers.
