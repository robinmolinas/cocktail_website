## Ingredient: Old Tom (the lineage)

| kind | fact | meaning | speaksTo |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| lineage | In the 1800s publicans bought gin unsweetened and sweetened it themselves ("cordial gin"). Old Tom was the grade that began from the stronger gin and got the least added sugar and water, advertised as "the strongest allowed to be sold": well over 40% when sweetened gins averaged about 37%. Sugar was around 35 g per litre (Oxford OLD TOM GIN pdf 1436). | Of all the sweetened gins, the one watered and sweetened the least: the gin that wasn't made gentler for the customer (Wren's and Tomás's mirror; the tagline's "you don't say 'poor you'"). GUARDS: it was a retailer's grade, not a distiller's recipe. It was sweetened (never "unsweetened"). These are 19th-century figures, NOT Ransom's: no source gives Ransom's sugar or proof (family ≠ bottle). "Strongest" refers to a price grade: don't make the reading about alcohol. | Bitter · Controlled · Honesty |
| lineage | First in print in 1810. By the 1880s it had a name in the US and went into new drinks including the Martini and the Gin Fizz. Taste turned drier, and by the late 20th century it was essentially unavailable (Oxford pdf 1438). | A gin that disappeared because people wanted something drier, until someone who knew brought it back. ## Glass: the Martinez (Tomás's)

| kind | fact | meaning | speaksTo |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| drink | The Martinez is an early name for the Martini, first in print in 1884. Modern recipes call for Old Tom, sweet vermouth, maraschino and bitters (Oxford MARTINEZ pdf 1243). The *Codex* says it is "believed" to have been made with Old Tom originally (p. 68). Simonson names it as one of Old Tom's drinks in the same sentence as the advice (*Proper* p. 241). | The bottle IS the advice, so there's no bridge. Keep "believed" for the Old Tom origin, and never "the original recipe" (Oxford: "subject to interpretation"). | Tomás |
| method basis | Regan prints the Martinez in older proportions (2 vermouth : 1 Old Tom) and suggests reversing them for modern taste, 2 Old Tom : 1 vermouth, "preferably Ransom" (*Joy* pdf 336). | Ours is that reversal with the maraschino left out and not replaced. Say "the way Regan suggests, without the cherry liqueur". NEVER "Regan's recipe", "the classic" or "the original". A riff changes what the facts may claim. | Tomás |
