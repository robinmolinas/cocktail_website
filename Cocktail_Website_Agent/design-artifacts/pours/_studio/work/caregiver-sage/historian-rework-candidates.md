## (Superseded, round 1) Chosen candidate: The Chartreuse monks, 1903

### The story

In 1903, the French state expelled the Carthusian order from their monastery in the Chartreuse Mountains — a conflict between the Church and the French Republic's new secularization laws. Only a handful of monks knew the complete 130-botanical formula for Chartreuse (Oxford Companion, CHARTREUSE, pdf 439). The monks took the recipe to a new distillery in Tarragona, Spain (Oxford, pdf 439). The French government sold the brand name to secular businessmen, who formed a commercial company and produced their own version of Chartreuse without the recipe. The product was so inferior it was nicknamed "liquidatreuse" (chartreuse.fr, secondary; also wikiedia.org/wiki/Chartreuse_(liqueur), secondary). The company went bankrupt in 1929. The monks eventually recovered the trademark and returned to France.

**The "honest no that protects":** The monks did not leave the recipe. They could not legally resist the expulsion, but they could — and did — walk out with the one thing the government could not compel: the knowledge. Their refusal to surrender the formula was the protection. Without it, the imitation failed, and everyone who has since drunk real Chartreuse owes that moment. The "no" held.

**Mirror for The Doctor:**
- The monks had knowledge others couldn't replicate. They knew what was in it — the government did not. That's "since when?": since 1605, actually, and without the knowledge you can't fake the answer.
- The care was for the long term, not the easy short term (surrendering the recipe might have spared the monks trouble; they didn't).
- The "honest no" protected strangers — future generations of drinkers — not just themselves. That's the Doctor's fear of selfishness turned inside out.

**Guard:** The monks are not named individually in Oxford. The "liquidatreuse" nickname and the bankruptcy come from secondary sources and must be signposted as such if they enter the reading. The expulsion, the Tarragona relaunching, and the secret-formula constraint are all in Oxford (primary).

## Proposed drink: The Last Word

**Source:** Oxford Companion, LAST WORD, pdf 1166.

Recipe: 30 ml each dry gin, green Chartreuse, maraschino liqueur, fresh lime juice — shaken, strained into a stemmed cocktail glass.

- **Family:** Daiquiri (shaken, citrus spirit/modifier/citrus)
- **Why it flows from the story:** Green Chartreuse is the ingredient the story belongs to. The Last Word puts it in as one of four equal shares — not a background note, but an equal vote. The drink refuses to soften the Chartreuse's intensity, just as the monks refused to soften their answer. Also: the drink was itself "largely forgotten" until Murray Stenson found it in 2003 (Oxford, pdf 1166). Two things that refused to be lost.
- **Why it fits The Doctor:** "Pleasing four-part symmetry" and "unexpected harmony of its assertive ingredients" (Oxford). Four equal parts is precision: no cheating, no inflating, no ingredient made lighter than it is. The acid (lime) is honest. The bitterness (Chartreuse) is not dressed up. The drink makes no apologies.
- **No health claims:** Nothing about medicine or quinine. Chartreuse's "elixir" origin is brand lore (Oxford: "according to brand lore"), not a medicinal claim.

**Fact card to write:** `_studio/fact-cards/chartreuse-monks-last-word.md` — monastic story (1605 to 1932), 1903 expulsion, Last Word provenance.

## Runner-up drink: The Bijou (Harry Johnson, 1900)

**Source:** Oxford Companion, BIJOU COCKTAIL, pdf 266.

Recipe: 30 ml each Plymouth gin, Italian vermouth, green Chartreuse + 2 dashes orange bitters — stirred, strained, lemon twist.

- **Family:** Martini (stirred, no citrus)
- **Why it could work:** Johnson replaced Grand Marnier with Chartreuse in 1900 — choosing the harder ingredient three years before the 1903 expulsion. Also "woken from hibernation" in the 2000s (Oxford). Stirred = more controlled, ceremonial.
- **Why I prefer the Last Word:** The Bijou is also in the martini family, same as the original retired pour (White Negroni). The Last Word (daiquiri family) gives caregiver-sage a different shape. And equal parts is a cleaner echo of The Doctor's precision.
