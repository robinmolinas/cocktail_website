# The Doctor's no: the test a rework story has to pass (Wren, rework round 2, 2026-09-30)

The person doesn't change. whoYouAre (kept, with Robin's edit) already names the no:
"when a friend comes to you hoping you'll say the plan is fine... Yes would be so much easier,
and for an evening they'd like you more for it. You say no anyway."

So the story's no has to have three things:

1. **Said to someone who wanted the yes, someone the refuser had reason to please** (a guest, a friend, an employer, family). Yes would have been easier and better liked. The cost is the point.
2. **From knowing something the asker didn't**: examined, tasted, measured. Not policy handed down, not instinct.
3. **It held, and the person asking (or the people they served) was better off**, and a source says so, not us. The motive can be signposted, but the protection itself can't rest on our "I like to think".

4. **(added round 3) The no is for the sake of the person asking, not the work.** A no that keeps a drink, a menu or a product up to standard belongs to the Craftsman or the Auteur. The Doctor's no stands between someone and a thing they want that would cost them. What it costs them has to be something other than their health: money, a livelihood, their name, a friendship, a bad plan. Blanket limits for everyone are the Nanny's shape.

Checked in the library and not usable: Regan's bartender who steered him away from the bore (*Joy* pdf 112) is kind and protective, but it's done through a white lie, and this guest fears being deceived; it's also unnamed and has no drink. Freeman talking Mason into absinthe over Sambuca (*Proper* p. 232) is about quality, not the person. The Only William and the Kaiser's "hangover" (Oxford pdf 1760) takes us back to health.

Round 4, also dropped: Joe Rickey's handwritten card, "Dont Drink too Many" (Oxford RICKEY pdf 1662). The entry frames the drink with a physician, "less intoxicating" and "healthful" (pdf 1663), which is health, and a warning about quantity is the Nanny's shape. Simon Ford's three-month deal (*Proper* p. 111) is a yes, not a no.

**What I'd accept (round 4).** The one refused can be a friend, a colleague, a client, a boss or a guest. The protected one can be the asker or someone in the refuser's care (staff, family), as long as what's spared is money, a livelihood, a name or a plan, and not health or quality. The person can be obscure, as long as they're named. Web sources are fine, marked by tier and as close to the original as we can get. Test 3 is met by the refused person's own later words, or by the outcome on record. Not accepted: nos about quality, blanket limits, keeping a secret, anything medical.
