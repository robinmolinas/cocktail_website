# Persona card: The Doctor (caregiver-sage) · Wren, round 1

## Core truth
They care by finding out. Where other people comfort, they ask the next question, and they won't say "it's probably nothing" until they know it's nothing.

## Shows / hides
- **Shows:** calm, questions, facts, a slightly dry manner. The friend who asks "since when?" and "have you had it looked at?", who reads the leaflet, who remembers the dose. Can come across as cool, even blunt.
- **Hides:** how much they worry. The problem goes home with them. They're still turning your symptom, your leak, your bad week over in their head three days later, and then a link arrives. And a quieter thing: they enjoy the puzzle, and they're a little ashamed of that.

## Hidden fear -> hidden gift
- **Fear (Caregiver: selfishness):** that they're in it for the puzzle, not the person. That their curiosity is a kind of selfishness, and that people feel examined rather than cared for. (Sage adds: being wrong, and being lied to or lying.)
- **Gift:** they don't stop until they know, and they tell you the truth. Their refusal to reassure falsely IS the care. Hope that isn't grounded feels to them like a small cruelty. The curiosity is how the care keeps going after everyone else has said "poor you" and moved on.

## The "real me" line to aim for
- *"You don't say 'poor you'. You ask 'since when?'"* (tagline candidate: intriguing, positive frame)
- whoYouAre heart: *"You'd never tell someone it's probably nothing. Not until you know it's nothing."*
- the hidden part: *"Part of you enjoys the puzzle, and you've wondered if that makes you cold. It doesn't. It's why you're still thinking about it on Thursday."*

## Truth vs legend
The true story, with the real cause found. A diagnosis, not a myth: they want the legend named as a legend and the actual reason given. Ambiguity (a Sage fear) is the thing the pour must never leave them with.

## Tone
Dry, precise, warm underneath. Allowed to be a little funny (the example is Dr House), never flippant about illness. Short sentences. No sentiment poured over them: they'd distrust it.

## Distinct from siblings
- **Craftsman** (*Not Only the Way*): pride in how it's made, the method. The Doctor's pride isn't in the method, it's in being right about what's wrong. Keep "the order you do it in" away.
- **Nurse** (regular-guy): presence, the hand held, the everyday. The Doctor isn't the one who stays by the bed; they're the one who figured out why.
- **Reassurer** (jester): makes it lighter so it hurts less. The Doctor refuses to make it lighter than it is.
- **Healer** (magician): transformation, the mysterious cure. The Doctor distrusts anything that works without a reason.
- **Researcher** (explorer): drawn to the unknown for its own sake. The Doctor's curiosity always has a patient at the end of it.

## Traps
- **No profession label.** Most guests aren't doctors. No "you're the doctor of the group", no white coat, no "patients". Everyday scenes only (a friend's cough, the leaflet, the noise the car makes, the child's temperature).
- No diagnosis of the guest (irony, and my own rule): recognise, don't examine them.
- No false comfort anywhere in the reading or the drink: no placebo, no "secret cure" legend passed off as fact.
- Don't make them cold. House is the example, not the person: the rudeness is not the gift.
- No drink you perform: no dropper, pipette, syringe, test tube, "prescription" gimmick.
- Medicine-in-a-bottle motifs must not tip into "alcohol is good for you".
- Craftsman already owns the lime cordial against scurvy (lineage clause): avoid scurvy/Gimlet.

## Story directions (for Hester, not facts)
The mirror should be someone who cared by finding out, or bitter medicine made takeable without pretending it isn't bitter. Candidates to check: Dr Siegert and Angostura bitters (a surgeon's remedy for soldiers); quinine and tonic (the bitter bark made drinkable); Peychaud the apothecary. Avoid the Gimlet/scurvy line (Craftsman's).
