# The Cynic (jester-sage): Wren's read, round 1 (2026-10-04)

## The one true thing
The Cynic is a disappointed idealist who never stopped hoping, and makes the joke so nobody catches them at it. The eye-roll is out in the open. The hope is kept out of sight. Each sharp line about how things are only works because they still have a clear picture of how things *should* be, and they mind the gap more than anyone at the table. The laugh is how they look straight at what's absurd without despairing of it.

Source anchors (xlsx): "laughs because the alternative is despair"; "under the eye-rolls is someone who cares a great deal about what is true"; "being bored by lies that everyone pretends to believe"; goal Pleasure, fear Boredom; drivers *seek paradise*. The paradise driver is the key: the cynic still has a picture of how it could be.

## Hide · fear · give
- **Hides:** the hope. They'd sooner be caught being kind than being caught believing in something. Whatever they do about what they hope for, they do quietly, with no face on it, so the hope can't be laughed at the way they laugh at everything else.
- **Fears:** two layers. On the surface, boredom: pretence is *dull* to them, not just wrong (the persona says bored, not outraged, and that's the Jester half). Deeper down: being taken for someone who believes in nothing, and the day the joke stops being funny and turns into the despair it was keeping out.
- **Gives (said out loud, once, Robin's rule):** after the bad news, theirs is the line that lets everyone breathe out and nod. People can face the absurd thing because someone made it laughable and didn't make it hopeless. They keep a room from despair *and* from pretending, both at once.

## Siblings and cousins (who owns what, so we stay off it)
- **Wise Fool** (sage-jester, *In Good Part*): owns "a story everyone has agreed to believe", the kind, careful joke aimed at one person's self-story, and "people are still waiting for the funny part". **This is our nearest cousin and our biggest trap.** The persona's own "lies everyone pretends to believe" is the Wise Fool's whoYouAre sentence. Our reading can't rest on that line: it rests on the hope underneath.
- **Wise Talker** (*The Wink*): owns humour through hardship, "every morning with something", "things get bad, you get funnier". So 1906 can't be told as joking through the ruins, and *Cheerio* can never be a daily morning presence.
- **Subverter** (outlaw-jester): owns the joke as a way to get the truth past power. **Scandalmonger** (unauthored, Croswell): owns scandalising the pious and powerful. **Lone Voice** (sage-outlaw): owns saying it plainly, and the comfortable version becoming the official one. **Devil's Advocate**: owns "settled". **Stand-up**: owns "everybody" vs "I". **Morale Booster**: owns cheering a room up on purpose. **Trend Setter** (*Still Yours*): owns eye-rolling.
- **What's left only to the Cynic:** the hope hidden under the jibe. The others hide fear, cost or confession. This one hides belief.

## Traps
1. Bitterness or sneering read as the person ("you see through everyone"). That's the mask, never the person.
2. Flattery for being clever ("you always see the joke"). Recognise what it's *for*.
3. Politics, the news by name, or any side taken. Colbert is never named.
4. Mocking faith. A religious guest must feel safe: we're on the side of people being blamed, never against belief.
5. The Wise Fool's "story everyone believes" and the Subverter's "smuggle the truth": keep both out.
6. Despair said too heavily. The title block intrigues: the tagline sits on the hope, not the dark.
7. "Anonymous giving, no thanks owed" is the Healer's gift (Regular Guy ruling). For us anonymity is *hiding the hope*, never modesty.
8. No "eye-roll" (*Still Yours*), no "morning" ritual (*The Wink*), no "put your name on it" (taken nine times in the registry; *In Your Own Hand*'s closing line).

## Story test
A claim people were asked to swallow, turned over in public with wit by someone who plainly cared about the truth, **and** the hope under the jibe on record: an act of idealism, ideally done quietly. Out: the Seelbach hoax, a legend corrected as the whole point, bitterness with no care under it.

## Verdict on the plan's lead (Field, 1906 verse + *Cheerio*): accept, with conditions
It passes every limb, and it has one I didn't ask for: **the anonymity.** Field signed the jibe and later kept his face and name off the hope ("no press pictures were permitted, and his fee was donated to charity", OAC). One life, both halves of the Cynic: the joke in public, the idealist in hiding. That's the "how did they know?" mirror, and it gives the position on a plate: *you put your name on the joke and kept it off the hope; once, try it the other way round* (wording to be found, off the registry's "your name on").

Conditions:
1. The lie is the blame laid on a burned city ("If, as they say…"): the reading takes the city's side against being blamed, never against faith. The verse is quoted once, only in Hester's single fetched version, and the bartender adds no jibe of their own.
2. The quake's toll, images of the ruins and the fire: never. One plain clause of setting.
3. *Cheerio* is idealism kept (poetry, a voice with no face, the fee given away), never "cheering people up" and never "every morning".
4. "Famous at once" / "the whole city repeated it" only if Hester stands it on a page (SF Heritage was a search result, not fetched). Otherwise "people repeated it".
5. Whoever saved the warehouse (staff, firemen, the Navy's hoses) is at most one clause, and it's Hester's to source. If it stands, I like it: it was people, not providence.
6. Whiskey stays the punchline of the verse, never "the drink that survived" as a miracle.

Backup (DeVoto) fails my test on the lie limb, as the plan says. Keep it in the drawer.
