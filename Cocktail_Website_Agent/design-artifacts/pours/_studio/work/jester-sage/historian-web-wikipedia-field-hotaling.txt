The '''Hotaling Building''' is a historic building located at 451 [[Jackson Street (San Francisco)|Jackson Street]] in [[Jackson Square, San Francisco|Jackson Square]], [[San Francisco]], [[California]]. It is a [[San Francisco Designated Landmark]] (no. 12) since 1969, and is part of the [[Jackson Square Historic District]].<ref name="Noehill.com">{{Cite web |title=San Francisco Landmark #12: Hotaling Building |url=https://noehill.com/sf/landmarks/sf012.asp |access-date= |website=Noehill.com}}</ref>

The '''Hotaling Annex East''' is the next door building at 445 Jackson Street which used to serve as the horse stables for the Hotaling Building, and it is listed separately as a San Francisco Designated Landmark (no. 13) since 1969.<ref>{{Cite web |title=San Francisco Landmark #13: Hotaling Annex East |url=https://noehill.com/sf/landmarks/sf013.asp |access-date= |website=Noehill.com}}</ref> The '''Hotaling Stables Building''' is in the alley next door at 32–42 Hotaling Place, and it also served as the stables, and it is also listed separately as a San Francisco Designated Landmark (no. 11) since 1969.<ref>{{Cite web |title=San Francisco Landmark #11: Hotaling Stables |url=https://noehill.com/sf/landmarks/sf011.asp |access-date= |website=Noehill.com}}</ref>

==History==
It was built in 1866 by [[Anson Parsons Hotaling]] to originally be a hotel. However, Hotaling later moved to the whiskey business. It housed the Hotaling & Co. which imported whiskey from Kentucky to create its own branded bourbon blends (J.H. Cutter and Old Kirk) for local distribution.<ref name="Young-2025">{{Cite web |last=Young |first=Kerri |date=2025-06-13 |title=Fun Friday: Hotaling's Whisky |url=https://www.sfheritage.org/heritage-in-the-neighborhoods/hotalings-whisky/ |access-date=2025-11-22 |website=San Francisco Heritage |language=en-US}}</ref>

It is located along Hotaling Place (formerly Jones Alley) is the street adjacent to the building that runs along the old city shoreline.<ref name="Chamings-2024" /><ref name="Young-2025" />

The building façade is Italianate with cast iron and cast iron shutters, a fire-resistant type of construction.<ref name="Noehill.com" /> It was also one of the few surviving buildings after the [[1906 San Francisco earthquake|1906 Earthquake and Fire]], thanks to a mile long fire hose that stretched through [[Fisherman's Wharf, San Francisco|Fisherman's Wharf]] and [[Telegraph Hill, San Francisco|Telegraph Hill]]. As the California Powder Works was blowing up buildings to create fire breaks, the employees of Hotaling & Co. rallied to move between 3,000 and 5,000 barrels from the Hotaling Building to a safer area, an empty lot where the new [[United States Customhouse (San Francisco)|customhouse]] was under construction. It remained one of the only alcohol stocks not destroyed by the authorities after the disaster.<ref name="Young-2025" />

Because of the saving of the building, [[Charles K. Field]]<ref name="field" /> once stated famously, "If, as they say, God spanked the town for being over-frisky, why did He burn His churches down and spare Hotaling's whiskey?" Hotaling became one of the rare distributors of liquor in San Francisco still standing after the earthquake.<ref name="Chamings-2024">{{Cite news |last=Chamings |first=Andrew |title=The history of San Francisco's oldest alley |url=https://www.sfgate.com/obscuresf/article/the-secrets-of-san-francisco-s-oldest-alley-16745881.php |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240130180106/https://www.sfgate.com/obscuresf/article/the-secrets-of-san-francisco-s-oldest-alley-16745881.php |archive-date=2024-01-30 |access-date=2025-11-22 |work=SFGATE |language=en}}</ref>

== Closure and legacy ==
Hotaling & Co. left the building in 1943 and closed. The company remained an active player of the local alcohol industry through a resurrection by the [[Anchor Brewing Company]], which was sold to [[Sapporo Breweries|Sapporo]] in 2017.<ref name="Young-2025" /><ref>{{Cite web |last=Gurevich |first=Natalia |date=April 18, 2025 |title=Homegrown SF whiskey honors 1906 'quake resilience |url=https://www.sfexaminer.com/culture/food-and-drink/hotaling-old-potrero-whiskey-honors-sf-earthquake-resilience/article_d5d088e4-dbb2-4558-b600-2eb90e3f3ecf.html |access-date= |website=[[San Francisco Examiner]] |language=en}}</ref>

The building was revived in 1952 when Dorothy Kneedler Lawenda and Harry Lawenda of Kneedler-Fauchere purchased it and made it a center for their wholesale interior decorative design elements firm. The name Jackson Square was adopted, many buildings were renovated and the street became the interior design center for San Francisco for decades.

##### Charles K. Field
{{short description|American journalist and poet}}
[[File:Charles Kellogg Field.jpg|thumb]]
'''Charles Kellogg Field''' (September 4, 1873, in [[Montpelier, Vermont]]<ref name=Kent>[https://archive.org/details/onethousandmen00kent/page/74/mode/2up MONTPELIER], in ''One Thousand Men'', by Dorman B. E. Kent; published 1915 by the Vermont Historical Society; via [[archive.org]]</ref>– 1948) was an American journalist and poet.

He served as editor of ''[[Sunset (magazine)|Sunset]]'' from about 1914 to 1920, after buying the magazine from the [[Southern Pacific Railroad]] along with his colleagues. He was a member of the pioneer class of [[Stanford University]] in 1895.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.oac.cdlib.org/findaid/ark:/13030/kt5870397n/|title=Guide to the Charles K. Field Collection|website=oac.cdlib.org}}</ref>

In 1914, Field was indicted under the [[Defense Secrets Act of 1911]] for publishing photographs of the [[Panama Canal]], then under construction, and its fortifications, along with [[wikisource:en:Sunset (magazine)/Volume 32/Can the Panama Canal be destroyed from the air?|an article]] by Lieutenant Riley Scott suggesting that the canal was vulnerable to an attack by air.<ref>[[wikisource:en:Morning Press/1914/July/11/Editor and aviator are arrested for disclosing military secrets|Editor and aviator are arrested for disclosing military secrets]], Associated Press, July 11, 1914.</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.nytimes.com/1914/09/20/archives/held-for-panama-photos-californians-arrested-for-publishing.html|title=HELD FOR PANAMA PHOTOS.; Californians Arrested for Publishing Pictures of the Fortifications.|date=20 September 1914|work=The New York Times}}</ref><ref>{{cite book|title=Pacific Pharmacist|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=Ttc5AQAAMAAJ&pg=PA127|year=1915|publisher=Searby Memorial Fund|page=127}}</ref><ref>{{cite book|title=The Bookseller, Newsdealer and Stationer|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=yKlUAAAAYAAJ&pg=PA72|year=1914|publisher=Excelsior Publishing House|page=72}}</ref>

From 1927 to 1940 Field hosted a radio program on NBC called ''Cheerio''.<ref>{{cite book |title=The A to Z of Old Time Radio |date=2010 |publisher=[[Scarecrow Press]] |page=57 |isbn=9781461672074 |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=5uzfQjhlUXoC&pg=PA57 |access-date=18 March 2022}}</ref> He also adopted this as a [[pseudonym]], writing ''The Story of Cheerio, by Himself'' in 1936.
