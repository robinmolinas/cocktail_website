🪞 **Wren:** Hester, Tomás: my read is in `work/jester-sage/psychologist-read-r1.md`. The short version:

**The one true thing.** The Cynic is a disappointed idealist who never stopped hoping, and makes the joke so nobody catches them at it. The eye-roll is out in the open. The hope is kept out of sight. Every sharp line about how things are depends on a clear picture of how things *should* be, and they mind the gap more than anyone at the table. The persona's driver is *seek paradise*, and that's the key: they still have the picture.

**Hide, fear, give.** They hide the hope: they'd sooner be caught being kind than caught believing in something, so whatever they do about it, they do quietly, with no face on it. They fear boredom first (pretence is *dull* to them, not just wrong), and under that, being taken for someone who believes in nothing, and the day the joke stops being funny and turns into the despair it was holding off. What they give, said out loud once: after the bad news, theirs is the line that lets everyone breathe out. People can look at the absurd thing because someone made it laughable without making it hopeless.

**Siblings, and the trap.** Our nearest cousin is the Wise Fool (*In Good Part*), which already owns "a story everyone has agreed to believe". That's almost the persona sheet's own line about lies everyone pretends to believe, so this reading can't rest on it. It rests on the hope underneath. *The Wink* owns humour through hardship and "every morning with something". The Subverter owns getting the truth past power, the Scandalmonger owns scandalising the pious, the Lone Voice owns saying it plainly, and *Still Yours* owns eye-rolls. What only the Cynic has: the others hide fear, cost or a confession; this one hides belief.

**Traps.** Sneering read as the person; praising them for being clever; politics or any side taken (no Colbert); mocking faith; despair said too heavily (the tagline sits on the hope); anonymity told as modesty (the Healer owns "no thanks owed"); "put your name on it" (nine pours have it).

**Story test.** Something people were asked to swallow, turned over in public with wit by someone who plainly cared about the truth, **and** the hope under the jibe on record, ideally done quietly.
