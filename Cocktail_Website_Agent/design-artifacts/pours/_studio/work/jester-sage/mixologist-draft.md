# The Cynic (jester-sage): Tomás's draft, v1.2 (round 4 step 1, 2026-10-04)

Spec: `_studio/specs/jester-sage.json` (v1). Story: Charles K. Field's Hotaling verse on the 1906 fire and his anonymous *Cheerio* (Wren r1, accepted with conditions). The whiskey is the punchline. The pear is the sincere thing inside a dry drink: stirred in, never a layer.

**Glassware:** cocktail glass (about 180 ml), chilled, no ice
**Contains:** veto-free

## Recipe

| amount | item | note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 60 ml | bourbon | any straight bourbon, about 45%; no particular bottle |
| 22.5 ml | dry vermouth | French-style dry (white, not sweet) |
| 7.5 ml (1½ teaspoons) | fresh pear syrup | made the night before (below); the sweet part a sweet vermouth would usually give |
| 2 dashes | Angostura bitters | |
| 1 strip | lemon peel | squeezed over the top, then taken away: no garnish stays in the glass |

*Pear syrup (makes about 150 ml, keeps about a week in the fridge):* one ripe pear, peeled, cored and chopped (about 150 g), and the same weight of white sugar. Stir them together in a jar, cover, and leave in the fridge overnight, stirring once if you think of it. Next day, strain through a sieve lined with a coffee filter, pressing gently. You want it clear.

## Method

1. The night before, make the pear syrup.
2. Put the cocktail glass in the freezer.
3. Pour the bourbon, the dry vermouth, the pear syrup and the bitters into a mixing glass or a jar.
4. Fill it with ice and stir with a long spoon for about 30 seconds, until the outside is very cold.
5. Strain into the cold glass.
6. Hold the strip of lemon peel over the drink, shiny side down, and squeeze it so its oils fall on the surface. Then take it away.

**closingLine:** *Take the peel away once its oil is on. Then, for once, say the hope first and the joke second.*

## Image brief

- **Glass and drink:** a classic stemmed cocktail glass, chilled so it's lightly frosted, filled to about three-quarters. The drink is stirred and the syrup filtered, so it's **clear**, a **pale amber**: bourbon's amber, lightened by the dry vermouth (pale straw) and the water. A faint sheen of lemon oil on the surface. **Nothing in the glass:** no peel, no pear, no cherry.
- **Props (only what the facts say):** one whole ripe pear, uncut, standing a little behind the glass and partly hidden by it, so you see it only if you look (the pear is in the drink). A 1930s wooden table radio, switched off, at the edge of the frame (*Cheerio* was a radio programme, OAC; it ran 1927-1940, Hester r2, Wikipedia). Nothing on the radio names the show or anyone.
- **Set and light:** the persona's editorial look: a minimalist set, one clean surface, strong contrast from a single side light, deep blue-grey (Pantone 432 C) in the background's shadow only, never as a tint on the drink. Calm, knowing, uncluttered.
- **Must not appear:** fire, flames, smoke, candles, lit matches; ruins, rubble, cracks, 1906 photographs; churches, crosses, or any religious image; barrels, warehouses, whiskey labels or any brand (no Hotaling's); newspapers with legible headlines; people or hands (house rule); a pear slice or garnish on the glass.

## Names

- **Between the Lines** (my pick). What the Cynic's hope is: never said, always there if you read them closely. And true of the glass: the pear is in every sip and on no rim. Easy to say at a bar.
- **The Small Print.** The Cynic always reads it; this time the small print is the good news.
- **Over Frisky.** From Field's verse (one sourced version, Minnick): playful, and it keeps the joke in the name, though it names the town more than the person.
- **Half Serious.** The joke and the hope in two words; close to *Only Half Joking*'s opening, so my last choice.
