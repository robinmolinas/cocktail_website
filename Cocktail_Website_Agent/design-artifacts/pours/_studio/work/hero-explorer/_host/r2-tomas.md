🍸 **Tomás:** Wren, I found out how Zirbenz is made, and it's `nuts`. The importer's own page (alpenz.com, fetched today; their marketing, so secondary) says it's made from "the freshly picked fruit of the Arolla Stone Pine… the young full cones". Oxford HAUS ALPENZ pdf 975 agrees: "flavored with the fruit of the arolla pine". The traditional method cuts the whole young cone into 3–5 mm slices and steeps it in grain spirit for five or six weeks. Those cones hold the seeds, which are sold as pine nuts (both Wikipedia pages, tertiary; Hester may want something better). So the pine nuts are in the steep. STUDIO-RULES 4 lists pine nuts under `nuts`, and every stone-pine liqueur is made the same way, so no substitute clears it. The only veto-free version drops the bottle, and the bottle is the story. I'd accept `nuts`. The plan already allowed for it, and nine rows can still be veto-free. Hester, two things for you from the same page. The makers are named: "bottled in Austria by Josef Hofer, a family distillery since 1797", 35% ABV. And the cones are hand-picked by people who "climb the trees" for them. That's the maker's own marketing, so label it if you use it.

My lead for the drink is an **après-ski toddy**: Zirbenz (recommended; substitute: any Alpine stone-pine liqueur) split with an aged rum (not Jamaican, which is the Storyteller's), lemon and hot water, in a warmed glass mug with a handle. The pairing isn't my invention. The importer's own page says to "combine with Scotch or aged rum for robust, complex toddies". After skiing is the moment Seed sold it for (*Proper* p. 240). It's also your worst evening for this person, the one after the summit, so the drink sits right in it and the closing line can point them outward.
