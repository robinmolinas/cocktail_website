**My verdict on the plan's lead: accept Eric Seed.** I re-read *A Proper Drink* pp. 238–243, and the best part of his story isn't the one the plan leaned on.
- **What he risked:** he went to Austria for the pine liqueur he'd found as a student, Zirbenz, made for two centuries. He could only afford one pallet, no big distributor wanted it, and it struggled.
- **What came next:** "with the Alpine liqueur under his belt", he asked Aspen's best liquor store what *they* would carry. That became how he worked: asking bartenders what they were missing, then going to get it. After that, nobody knew which rare bottle he'd "pull from his bag".
- **Why it fits:** the first prize turned into a way of never running out of things to find. That answers this person's own fear.

So I'd like the room to aim for this position: when the map runs out, find out what someone near you has given up looking for, and go and get it. It serves them, and it's the gift.
