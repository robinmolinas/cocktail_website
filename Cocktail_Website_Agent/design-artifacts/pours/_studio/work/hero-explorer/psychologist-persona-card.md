# The Quest Seeker (hero-explorer): Wren's persona card, round 1 (2026-10-01)

Source: persona.py brief (essence, story, goal Mastery, fear Weakness, Indiana Jones, drivers Leave a mark / Seek paradise). Plan row: `_studio/plans/hero.md`. Page re-read in full: *A Proper Drink* printed pp. 238–243 and 60–61.

## The one true thing
Distance doesn't scare them. Being finished does. They need a named thing out there (a summit, a lost record, an exam, a recipe), and the worst day isn't the hard one on the way. It's the quiet one after they've arrived, with nothing left on the map. The prize is real, but it's mostly how they find out what they're made of. Once they've found out, they need something else to find out about.

**Why this and not my sketch's "scared the whole way, goes anyway":** *Nothing to It* (the Pioneer, explorer-hero) now owns that whole idea: "You're scared every time", "Nobody's ever been able to tell", and the position "tell them you were terrified, and that you went". The persona sheet's snake-fear can get one clause ("you've never claimed to be fearless"), never the centre, and never the position.

## Hides / fears / gives
- **Hides:** the flat feeling the evening after (the medal still on, the photo posted, and they're already reading up on the next one), and that the prize matters a little less to them than they say. People take it for restlessness or never being satisfied. It's closer to this: without something to go after, they don't feel like themselves.
- **Fears (their kind of weakness):** going soft, a comfortable life that never asks anything of them, a map with no blank spaces left on it.
- **Gives:** they come back with things nobody near them could have got, and they make far away look like a place you can actually get to. Say it out loud once: the people around them have more in their lives because this person went and got it.

## Siblings and close pours (swap test)
- **Pioneer / *Nothing to It*:** going first while people watch, hiding the fear, the shrug so others follow. Out: scared/terrified, "nobody could tell", "went first", shrug, making it look easy.
- **Firefighter / *Anyone Would Have*:** practising for the day. The Quest Seeker goes after something; it doesn't wait for anything. Dionysos is used in that pour, so he's out here completely.
- **Saviour / *Next One's Mine*:** owing. **Idealist / *The Long Answer*:** staying quiet, making things. Its y5 is "ask people what they think": my position must not use that wording.
- **Problem Solver (unauthored):** having no answer while everyone watches. Keep "answer", "riddle" and "under pressure" out of this reading, even though the sheet's story says "a hard answer".
- **Expedition Leader (explorer-ruler, unauthored), "discovering the new and mastering it":** the closest unwritten one. Theirs is about leading people and mastering a place. This one is a personal hunger for the next target. Leave leading and team words alone.
- **Tourist / *Brought Home*:** owns "brought home" and bringing back places. The page's chapter title is "Bringing It All Home", so avoid home/brought-back phrasing in the name and the closing line.
- **Conservationist / *Left Standing*:** would rather know it's there than have it. This one wants to have it. It's a useful contrast, but don't spell it out.
- **Experimentalist / *Off-Label*:** never the same thing twice (that's variety in making). Here it's a named target.

## Traps
- Flattery ("bold, daring, fearless"). Use at most one rationed word (brave/courage/hero) in the whole reading.
- Indiana Jones puns, whips, temples, "adventurer" as a label. Relics taken from other people (no looting, no colonial "discovery").
- "Never satisfied" as a diagnosis or a scolding. It's an appetite, and the reading respects it.
- The position must not be restraint ("stay put", "enjoy what you have") unless it also gives something back (Robin's rule: end on what the guest gains).

## Story test (revised)
1. A person went after one specific, hard-to-get thing and got it.
2. A real risk on the way is on the page. (A fear in their own words isn't required: the fear in the reading belongs to the guest.)
3. **The getting didn't end it:** the source shows what they went after next, ideally because the first prize showed them how to keep finding.
4. Out: taking without credit, the prize as the whole point, a fear claimed for them that isn't on the record.

## Verdict on the plan row: accept Eric Seed, re-read on the page, for a better reason than the plan gave
*Proper* p. 240: he discovered the liqueur as a student in Austria, "went to Austria to find an example", and brought back Zirbenz, made for two centuries. He could afford only one pallet (sixty cases). No big distributor wanted it, a one-man wine outfit distributed it, and it struggled. Then, "with the Alpine liqueur under his belt", he asked Aspen's best liquor store what *they* would carry, and that became his way of working (pp. 240–241): he asked bartenders what to bring over (Wondrich's funky Jamaican rum, Meehan's Batavia Arrack, crème de violette). Every visit he made to a bar was an occasion, because "one never knew what arcane spirit he would pull from his bag". Kenyon: "Who is this bookworm? He has amazing things."
- Clauses 1, 2 and 4 pass. Clause 3 passes strongly, and it's the heart of the pour: the first prize turned into a way of never running out, by asking other people what they were missing. That answers this person's own fear.
- **Position direction:** when the map runs out, find out what someone near you has given up looking for, and go and get that. It's in their interest (they'll never run out of things to find) and it's the gift. It's distinct from the Pioneer (tell), the Firefighter (trust), the Saviour (accept) and the Idealist (send, then ask what they think).
- **Guards:** never say Seed was afraid. "Mild-mannered", "nothing safe or boring" and "bookworm" are Simonson's and Kenyon's words, so attribute them. He's living, so date anything in the present tense (the book is from 2016). Credit the Austrian makers plainly. LeNell's scolding (p. 238) belongs to the Rogue row and stays out, even though it's the only feeling of his on record. Crème de violette is *As It Was*'s, and that pour's bottle note already names Haus Alpenz: the guest text here should mention the company in one clause at most, or not at all. Old Tom and Wondrich's distilling, p. 241, belong to *Instead*: we can name Wondrich only as someone who asked for a rum.
- **Weakness:** it's quiet. No temple and no cliff. I think that's a strength for recognition (the guest's own quests are ordinary ones), but Tomás's drink and the scene in whoYouAre have to carry the sense of going a long way.
