# Backup hunt: hero-explorer. Hester, round 2

Wren's test: one specific hard-to-get thing, got; a real risk on the page; what came next, ideally because the first prize taught them how to keep finding. Out: taking without credit, the prize as the whole point, a fear we'd claim.

## Backup: Greg Boehm, re-read on the page (*Proper* pp. 60–61). Passes more than it first looked; still partial.
- B1 Simonson, of all the old-book collectors (not Boehm alone): "It was hard work. It was a quest." (p. 60)
- B2 Boehm collected "about six hundred titles before he got bored and packed them away" (p. 60). **This is the persona's flat evening, on the record:** the collection finished, boredom, boxes.
- B3 "A couple years later, in 2005, he took them out of the boxes. 'The world had changed in those two years'… 'These books that had been museum pieces were now working manuals.'" He used them every day to make drinks (p. 60).
- B4 Risk: from 2007 he published reprints, "Uncertain of the market for these, he began with six carefully chosen volumes", sold at first only on the website (p. 61).
- B5 The one specific thing: Embury's *The Fine Art of Mixing Drinks* (1948) was still in copyright, so "he tracked down Embury's surviving daughter and purchased the copyright" (p. 61).
- B6 What came next: "Suddenly, anybody interested in classic cocktails could acquire the books" others had dug for (p. 61).
- **Fails / partial:** the risk is mild (a publishing family: grandfather founded Sterling, father published too, p. 60); the "one thing" is a side detail inside a collection. **Guards:** p. 61's "Dionysus and Doudoroff" (Firefighter's man; the Superhero's backup) are never named; Saunders (*Making the Calls*) and Calabrese (Dashing Hero backup) stay out; the reprinted titles that are Jerry Thomas's stay unnamed. Living: date him (2016).
