# The Little Prince (innocent-ruler): Wren's persona read, round 1

Source: `persona.py innocent-ruler` (sheet story, essence, goals and fears of both archetypes). Archetype names never reach the guest.

## Who this is

Someone with one small world, taken completely seriously. It might be a window box, a friendship that goes back to school, the corner of the office nobody else keeps, the old dog, the family recipe made properly every Sunday. To anyone else it looks small. To them it's the whole thing, and they tend it every day: the small jobs done before they turn into big ones (the sheet's baobab shoots, pulled each morning while they're still shoots). Grown-up measures (how big, how many, what it's worth, who's important) don't impress them. They don't argue with them. They just don't register.

## The one true thing (no sibling has it)

**They don't measure what they love by its size. They measure it by whether it's all right today.** Smallness isn't a sacrifice and it isn't a statement. It's simply where all of their attention goes.

## What they hide
How much it frightens them. They shrug it off ("it's only my little…") because they've learned that other people find the seriousness funny, or sweet. Inside, one missed day feels like it could undo everything.

## What they fear
Failing the one thing in their care (the sheet's deepest fear). Under it, the second archetype's fear: that the small mess left for a day grows until it can't be pulled up (chaos). And the first archetype's: being the one to blame (punishment).

## What they give
Whatever is in their care is safe. And everyone near them learns, without being told, that a small thing taken seriously is worth more than a big thing taken lightly. With them, nothing small is ever only small. (Robin's caregiver rule: say this kindness out loud once in the reading.)

## Traps
1. **Retelling the book.** The name already is the book. No rose, fox, planet, "tamed", baobab, "essential is invisible", stars, grown-ups in guest text. (Robin's one-touchstone allowance isn't needed: the name spends it.)
2. **Childish or twee.** "The clarity of youth" read as naivety. The guest is serious, not small.
3. **Royal or ruling words** (prince, rule, reign, helm, crown, kingdom): archetype pun.
4. **Perfectionism and control** (the Perfectionist's, the Auteur's). The tending is care, not standards.
5. **Making for one person** (the Sculptor's).
6. **Small as a moral stance** (the Outsider's "enough"), or anti-numbers snobbery. They aren't against numbers; numbers just aren't how they see.
7. **Inheritance as memory** (A Brother's Care).
8. **Deaths** (the book's ending, the story's people).

## Story test (what the story must hold)
1. One small place or thing tended by hand over a long time (decades).
2. Its size never became the point: on the page, it stayed small, or ignored the world's measure (crowds, growth, numbers).
3. The tending is active and daily, not the preserving of a memory.
4. It came through because of them, and is stronger now than one keeper (the answer to the fear).

## Verdict on the plan's lead: Boadas, accept on conditions

Read on the page this round: Oxford BOADAS COCKTAILS pdf 303 (whole entry), BOADAS, MIGUEL pdf 301, sources pdf 302 and 304.

**Why it holds.** The mirror is better on the page than in the plan. Oxford says Boadas was even smaller when it opened in 1933: a long corridor with a bar attached, and only after the civil war did it gain "a few precious meters" and become today's triangle. Eighty years, a few metres. It sits a few steps from the city's most crowded street, and Oxford says it makes you feel in a different universe from that street: a small world of its own beside the crowd. "The complete opposite of a tourist trap." And its people "shun jiggers": numbers, the measure everyone else uses, simply aren't how they work. Maria Dolors took it over in 1967, kept her father's eye for detail and his warm hospitality, and with Pep Maruenda kept the Cuban throw going after it had fallen out of use (pdf 301). Test: 1 yes (decades, one tiny room); 2 yes (corridor to triangle, tourist trap, jiggers); 3 yes, if told as the throw and the room, not as the father; 4 yes, and better than the plan says: pdf 303 says the throw "still can be seen in use today at many of the city's other bars", and an apprentice who started there in 1970, Jerónimo Vaquero, now owns it. What they tended outgrew them. That's the answer to this guest's fear, and it may carry the position.

**Weaknesses, said plainly.**
- Maria Dolors herself gets three sentences on the page. Unless Hester finds more (her own book, *Los cóctels del Boadas Cocktail Bar*, 1990, or the bar's history page: a good use of one of her two web visits), the story is the room and the throw, with her as its keeper, not a portrait of her.
- "Kept the throw alive" is "he and his successors" on pdf 301: collective, Miquel included. Never "she alone".
- "Fifty years" is arithmetic from 1967 to shortly before her death; the death is out, so Hester rules the wording ("from 1967, for decades" is safe).
- Inheritance pulls toward A Brother's Care. The daily tending carries it, never the father's memory.

**Day Job: neighbour, not a collision, if four lines hold.** Different person, bar, city and drink; the Hero room left the throw to us on purpose (its image brief bans "a drink poured between two glasses (Innocent's throw)"). The risk is adjacency, and it's sharper than "Constante in one clause":
1. **No Floridita, no Constante, no Havana apprenticeship in guest text.** Day Job's y1 is a boy whose father brings him into bartending at the Floridita; Miquel's is the same shape (pdf 301: his father put him to work there in 1908). Two pours would open alike. At most "brought from Cuba".
2. **No "every morning behind the bar" and no jacket.** That's Day Job's lead image. We tell the room and the gesture, not a person going in each day.
3. **Barcelona** is one clause in Day Job: nothing to own.
4. **The glass.** Day Job is a nick-and-nora, and *Can't Watch* (outlaw-hero) is Martini · London dry gin · Nick & Nora (about 150 ml): **exactly the plan's shape for this row**, registered after the plan was checked. Tomás moves it. Gin Martinis are crowded besides (*Quite Alive*, *Worth Finding*, *Say So*, *Can't Watch*, *Tried and True*). Spark or base change welcome; if Hester finds what Boadas throws, follow it.

**Other guards:** the throw as care for the drink (cold, diluted, a little air), never a show (the Giggler's) and never practice advice (*Cold Water First*); no "helm" (Oxford's word) or any ruling word; Josephine Baker, Miró and the bartenders' club: out (status and fame, the opposite of the truth); no copy of Oxford's runs ("different universe", "opposite of a tourist trap" go in our words or attributed); Vaquero is living: date the present tense; the recipe is measured even though they shun jiggers, said kindly.
