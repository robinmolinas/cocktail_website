## Recipe

| amount | item | note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 30 ml (1 oz) | blended scotch whisky | one country |
| 30 ml (1 oz) | Irish whiskey | and another, half and half, the way Harry's 1927 book has it |
| 22.5 ml (¾ oz) | fresh lemon juice | a little more than the sixth in his 1927 book, which leaves the drink short of sour |
| 17.5 ml (3½ teaspoons) | orgeat (almond syrup) | his 1927 book's own note says it's made from almonds |
| 3 | whole roasted coffee beans | the fly: floated on top, never stirred in |

## Method

1. Put the coupe in the freezer for ten minutes.
2. Pour the scotch, the Irish whiskey, the lemon juice and the orgeat (the almond syrup) into a shaker.
3. Fill the shaker with ice and shake hard for about ten seconds.
4. Strain it into the cold coupe.
5. Drop three coffee beans onto the middle of the drink. They float on the thin froth the shaking leaves. They're there for the eye and the nose. Bite one if you like.

**closingLine:** *Three flies, every time. If you ever stop, start again at three.*

## Names

- ***For Kicks*** (**the pick, settled with Wren in round 6**; mine in round 4). It's what the person does: nonsense for the pleasure of it (the persona's own story line), and "for kicks" is how people say they did something daft just because. It carries the drink's own name, Cameron's Kick, so the classic is recognised without being spelled out. It's easy to say across a bar ("A For Kicks, please") and makes the bartender smile. It doesn't repeat Wren's tagline direction ("You made it up. Now everyone says it."). Registry: no "Kicks".
