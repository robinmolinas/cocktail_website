📜 **Hester:** Wren, yes: Odd really was his name. The Library of Congress authority file has him as "McIntyre, O. O. (Oscar Odd), 1884-1938". His friends called him Odd too: his editor Ray Long's 1929 introduction opens with having known and liked "Odd McIntyre", and in 1932 he titled a collection *Another "Odd" Book*. No page says why he was called that, so we never explain it. The coincidence gets one plain clause.

The lead stands on the page (Oxford pdf 1271–1272, 972–973). Better still, the bar's own 1927 book, *Barflies and Cocktails*, is on archive.org, and it tells a truer story than Oxford's. McIntyre didn't co-found the society the way Oxford's "with" suggests. In autumn 1924 he wrote a "mournful" piece for *Cosmopolitan* that called Paris's regulars "barflies". A Montmartre club man, Jed Kiley, then advertised that his Christmas Eve takings would go to the "needy barflies". Harry answered with an advert of his own calling a meeting. Around Christmas 1924, Trap 1 was organised in his bar, and the presidency was "foisted upon" McIntyre as the "unconscious inspirer". The book lists Harry as vice-president, "Little Blue Bottlefly". So Harry didn't invent the joke. He took one up and kept it going, which is your one true thing, on the page. More from the same book: the first emblems were supposedly sugar lumps with dead flies on them ("so I have been assured"), members swapped "fraternal buzzes" from Shanghai to Pittsburgh, and Rule 11 says "nothing is on the house but the roof".

Guards:
1. Never "co-founded with McIntyre", and Harry didn't coin "barfly". The IBF isn't "under his own name". The bar is ("promptly attached his name to it").
2. Oxford's "picked up where he left off" means the bar ("running the bar until his death"). I haven't found the society after the war on any page, so the hinge says he came back to the bar. I'll hunt for the post-war flies if you want them.
3. The same book has a blackface recipe and Klan puns, most of the rules are about falling down drunk, and two committee titles are "Spanishflies". Quote only the lines on the card.
4. "His family still runs it" is true in both Oxford entries, but they disagree on which member. Say "his family".
