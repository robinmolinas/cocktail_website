# Pour — For Kicks · The Wacky One (jester-creator)

## Cocktail

- **name:** For Kicks
- **tagline:** You made it up. Now everyone says it.
- **glassware:** coupe, chilled
- **contains:** `["nuts"]`

**recipe**

| amount | item | note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 30 ml (1 oz) | blended scotch whisky | one country |
| 30 ml (1 oz) | Irish whiskey | and another, half and half, the way Harry's book has it |
| 22.5 ml (¾ oz) | fresh lemon juice | a little more than his book's sixth, which leaves the drink short of sour |
| 17.5 ml (3½ teaspoons) | orgeat (almond syrup) | his book's own note says it's made from almonds |
| 3 | whole roasted coffee beans | the fly: floated on top, never stirred in |

**method**
1. Put the coupe in the freezer for ten minutes.
2. Pour the scotch, the Irish whiskey, the lemon juice and the orgeat (the almond syrup) into a shaker.
3. Fill the shaker with ice and shake hard for about ten seconds.
4. Strain it into the cold coupe.
5. Drop three coffee beans onto the middle of the drink. They float on the thin froth the shaking leaves. They're there for the eye and the nose. Bite one if you like.

**closingLine:** *Three flies, every time. If you ever stop, start again at three.*

## Reading

**epigraph**
*Three coffee beans float on top. They're meant to be flies. It's a long story.*

**whoYouAre**
Somebody once said something daft in passing, and you didn't let it go. You took it one step further, then another. Now there's a word people use without knowing it started with you, a thing you always wear, a little ritual nobody remembers agreeing to. It started as a joke, and it's still a joke. You just never saw a reason to stop.

At every age, someone has expected you to grow out of it. What you don't say is that you could. Normal isn't a mystery to you: you could do it tomorrow, and nobody would stop you. That's the part that frightens you, more than any look across a room: how quietly it could happen. Meanwhile, next to you, other people get to be a bit ridiculous too, and they are. They feel freer around you than they'd ever think to tell you. That's a bigger gift than being taken seriously.

**yours**

1. The way a Paris bar's own book tells it, it started in 1924 with a gloomy piece by a New York columnist, who called the regulars of the Paris bars barflies: the ones who never seem to leave. His name, and this is true, was Oscar Odd McIntyre. Then a nightclub owner joked about it in an advert: his Christmas Eve takings would go to the "needy barflies".

2. Harry McElhone, a Scot who ran that bar, Harry's New York Bar, answered with an advert of his own, calling a meeting, and around Christmas the International Bar Flies was founded there. The book lists McIntyre as president, "Big Blue Bottlefly", and Harry as "Little Blue Bottlefly". By 1927 it had over five thousand members, and branches, called traps, from Shanghai to Buenos Aires. My guess is they joined for the reason people join in with you: someone had gone first, and was clearly fine.

3. The joke wasn't his. He just wouldn't let it end where most people would have, which is exactly what you do with a joke. And he kept it going. In 1940, as the war closed in, he locked his papers in a safe, buried it and left. After the war he returned to the bar and picked up where he left off, and a later booklet lists him as the society's president: "Exalted Blue Bottle Fly". He'd been promoted. I like to think the silliness was one of the things he kept safe.

4. The cocktail I've made for you is my take on Cameron's Kick, a forgotten classic, a whisky sour that first appeared in Harry's 1922 book. It's half Scotch whisky and half Irish whiskey, shaken with lemon and orgeat, a sweet almond syrup. Two countries' whiskies in one glass sounds like it shouldn't work, and it plainly does. I've given it a little more lemon than the old printed recipe, and a touch more almond, so it tastes brighter. Then I added the flies: three coffee beans floated on top, the way sambuca, an anise liqueur, is often served "with the fly", because with a little imagination the beans look like houseflies. Here they're for the Bar Flies. They add a little roasted-coffee scent as the glass comes up, and if you bite one, a bitter crunch against the almond.

5. Some stretches of your life will need you to be serious, and when one comes, you'll manage it. It doesn't get to take the silliness with it. When it's over, pick up where you left off: the word, the ritual, the thing you always wear, all of it. You won't be the only one who missed it.
