## Recipe

- **serves:** 1
- **glassware:** a small stemmed pousse-café or cordial glass (about 75 ml), narrow and flared; no ice
- **contains:** `[]`

| amount | item | note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 10 ml | white crème de cacao | the bottom layer, and the heaviest. Clear as water, and it tastes of chocolate (2 teaspoons) |
| 15 ml | tawny port | the middle layer; a 10-year-old if you have one, any tawny if not (1 tablespoon) |
| 20 ml | beetroot bourbon (below) | the top layer, the darkest and the lightest (4 teaspoons) |
| | **For the beetroot bourbon** (enough for about 12 drinks) | |
| 250 ml | bourbon (40–45%) | any bourbon you like neat |
| 60 g | raw beetroot, peeled and coarsely grated | about one small beetroot |

## Method
1. Make the beetroot bourbon first, on the day or up to a few days ahead. Put the grated beetroot in a jar and pour the bourbon over it. Stir, and leave it at room temperature for about 30 minutes, tasting every few minutes. Stop when it's deep red and tastes of the earth as much as of the bourbon.
2. Strain it through a sieve lined with a coffee filter, into a clean bottle, and keep it in the fridge. Use it within a few days: the red fades in the light.
3. The first time you use new bottles, test them: pour a teaspoon of crème de cacao into a shot glass and run a few drops of port down the side onto it, then a few drops of the beetroot bourbon. Every brand weighs a little differently, so even makers who know the rule check it in the glass.
4. Pour the crème de cacao into the glass. Wipe off any drops on the inside of the glass above it.
5. Hold a teaspoon upside down inside the glass, the tip just touching the side, a finger's width above the clear layer. Pour the port onto the back of the spoon, slowly, so it runs down the glass and settles on the clear layer.
6. Raise the spoon to just above the port and pour the beetroot bourbon the same way, slower still. If you rush it, the layers run into each other, and they won't come apart again.
7. Serve it straight away. It's drunk from the top down: the red first, the clear last.

**closingLine:** *Pour the darkest layer last, and slowest. Then explain how it works: it'll still look upside down.*

- It's built on what no sibling has, **knowing doesn't undo it** (Hester D6), and it's true every time: explaining the sugar changes no density, so the glass looks just as wrong afterwards. It hands Wren's y5 ("Knowing it's sugar doesn't move a single band") its action, while y5 keeps the reason itself.
- v1's "Then tell them why you did it" and Wren's offer "Then give them the reason" are both out: *On Their Behalf* closes on "add the why", and its y5 is the give-the-reason position; *regular-guy-ruler*'s method says "tell them why" (Hester D6). "Explain" is in no closing line in the registry. It does appear in other readings, as advice *not* to explain (*Standing By*'s and *Unrehearsed*'s), which is the opposite move.
- The fear is in the first sentence: the slow pour is the only thing between the layers and a muddle that can't be undone.
- Avoided: "don't stir/shake" (*No Accident*); "over a spoon" and "brandy last" (*Till Spring*'s closing line); "float" (*A Place Kept*); "trick" (*Cold Water First*); "hold" (*Asked In*); "tell them" and "the reason" (above). - **Glass and drink:** a small stemmed pousse-café or cordial glass (about 75 ml), narrow and flared, about two-thirds full. Three sharp bands, bottom to top: **clear, tawny brown-amber, deep red**. The darkest is on top. No garnish, no ice, no straw.
- **Props (four, story):** the upturned teaspoon with its red film (how the layers were laid, slowly); the halved and grated beetroot (the spark you'd bet against, honestly shown); the brass balance with its two equal glasses (weight is the whole secret, and it's nothing to hide); the red drops (a little of the work left in view).
- **One impossible detail (Wren's, round 3):** the balance tipping toward the clear glass. It looks wrong and is physics: at the same volume the white crème de cacao (~1.12 g/ml) outweighs the beetroot bourbon (~0.945) by about a sixth (my estimates, unsourced; never in guest text). Shown honestly, it's the right "impossible" for someone whose illusions are never fake. The rising drop (v1) is gone: it was the one fake thing in the picture.
- **Must not appear:** flame, anise, a cloud or louche (*Rosetta*), cream, egg; a sugar cube or a flute (*No Accident*); playing cards, a top hat, a wand, a rabbit, a magician's cape; any Escher print or recognisable artwork; a staircase drawing; an orange or orange peel; a second drink; hands or people; readable labels or text.
