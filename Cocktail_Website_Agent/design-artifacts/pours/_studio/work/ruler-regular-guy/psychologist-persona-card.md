# Persona card: The People's Representative (ruler-regular-guy), Wren, round 1

Sources read: persona.py row (goal Control, fear Revolution; Regular Guy secondary: belonging, fairness, fear of exclusion and discourtesy), the Ruler plan and my sketch, sibling drafts *Go On* and *On Their Behalf*, Regular Guy readings *Four Shares* and *Everybody's*, the registry. Pages re-read: Oxford VIEUX CARRÉ (pdf 2102), *Imbibe!* pdf 182.

## The one true thing
When people split into camps, they walk over and take up the other side's thing (its drink, its song, its shirt) in public, and mean it. They mend with a gesture, never an argument. Mandela didn't make a speech in 1995; he put the shirt on. Their standing is spent on belonging, not on being right.

## Hides
- The hardest question comes from their own side, not the other: "whose side are you on?"
- They aren't neutral. They have a side and strong views. Crossing is a choice, and people misread it as fence-sitting.
- To keep the peace, they turn their own colours down. Their own side notices, and so do they.

## Fears
Being made to choose, and the split becoming permanent (the family that stops sitting at one table). Ruler chaos plus the Regular Guy wound: anyone made the outsider, the other side included.

## Gives (say it out loud once)
Proof that two camps can sit in one room without either giving itself up. Because of them, nobody has to choose.

## Position direction
Cross over with your own colours on. You don't have to water yourself down to be welcome on the other side: it's all of you they came to trust. (Not "take someone with you", which is *Left Standing*'s y5. Not reciprocity or "let them come to you", which is the *Four Shares* / *Next One's Mine* ground.)

## Differs from
- **Judge:** rules who's right. This one refuses the verdict and keeps both.
- **Caring Leader (*Go On*):** calms the heat in one room by listening. This one bridges two camps by crossing.
- **Noble Ruler:** keeps their own house in order, from the front. This one walks over, from the ground.
- **Reformer:** changes the rules. This one changes who sits together.
- **True Friend (*Four Shares*):** nobody left out, forgiveness. **Nurse (*Everybody's*):** the same for everyone. **Stand-up (*Is It Just Me*):** "nobody's the only one". **Entrepreneur (*Loose on Top*):** "the people you made room for". Keep all of those phrasings out.

## Traps
- Mandela, apartheid, race, sport, any politics. "Common touch", "down to earth", "man of the people", "diplomat", "peacemaker", "mediator", "bridge-builder" as labels.
- "Fence-sitter" is fine only as the misreading that gets turned around.
- "People take you for…" opens both Ruler drafts (*Go On*, *On Their Behalf*). A third would be a formula.
- Equal parts must read as **both at full strength**, never as halves or a compromise.
- Vieux Carré specifics: Creole and American as people's labels are out. Say the brandy the city drank and the whiskey that replaced it. Walter Bergeron (1889–1947) is never confused with Victor "Trader Vic" Bergeron (Oxford pdf 258). One clause for "a Manhattan variant" at most (Ciro's Manhattan is the Magnetic Leader's). Keep it off the Sazerac (no absinthe, no sugar cube). "Nearly forgotten, even in its own hotel" stays out, because the comeback isn't this person's story.

## Story test (revised from the plan)
1. **A real split on the page**: two camps, or one displaced by the other.
2. **A gesture on the page** that brings the other side's thing in, made by someone with standing.
3. **Neither side diluted:** both at full measure.
4. **One glass, or one room.**

The motive may be signposted ("I like to think…"). Distrust from both sides is an *event*, so it can't be signposted: if the page lacks it, it moves into the guest's half (whoYouAre's "whose side are you on?") and is never claimed of the story's person. Out: charity, a limp compromise, war, a verdict.

## Verdict on the Open row
- **Vieux Carré: promote to lead.** Split: *Imbibe!* pdf 182 (a brandy-julep town where whiskey had "displaced" brandy). Gesture with standing: the Monteleone's head bartender, 1930s (Oxford pdf 2102). Full measure: 30 ml rye with 30 ml cognac. And it's named for the old square, the older side's name. The side that won pours the side that lost back in, at the same weight, and gives the drink that side's name. That's the jersey, without the politics. The link between the two pages is ours: signpost it.
- **Piña Colada: drop.** Nobody makes a gesture. The split is two bars fighting over credit, which would make the guest the arbiter (the Judge's ground). The resolution is a government declaration (politics-adjacent). And Innocent holds the person.
- **Hunt, round 2 only (Hester):** a story with a *person* crossing that beats this one. Shapes: a bartender who put a rival bar's signature drink on their own menu under its own name; a drink a town named after the rival town; two camps of regulars brought to one bar (no sport). If nothing beats the Vieux Carré by the end of round 2, it runs.
- **Veto:** `nuts` (Bénédictine's nutmeg). The plan's floor allows it.

## Lines to hold onto (not drafts)
- "The hardest question you get isn't from the other side. It's from your own: whose side are you on?"
- Tagline seed: "Everyone wants you on their side. You keep ending up on both."
