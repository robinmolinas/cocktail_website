It's Oxford's Vieux Carré at its own measures: 30 ml each of rye, cognac and sweet vermouth, 5 ml Bénédictine, 2 dashes each of Peychaud's and Angostura. That's your Arthur 1937 thirds at double the size. It's stirred and strained over one large cube in a big rocks glass, with lemon peel and no cherry.

There's one change, and it's the spark. I rendered the *Flavor Matrix* wheels as images, not OCR. Cardamom is on the Grain wheel (p. 127) and on the Grape wheel (p. 131). So I steep the sweet vermouth overnight with green cardamom, about 3 g per 250 ml for 12 hours at room temperature. That's scaled from the *Codex*'s cardamom St-Germain (p. 288; p. 95 says vermouth-strength liquids infuse slowly). The vermouth is the one part of the glass that isn't either side's spirit, so the shared ground changes and neither side does. Wren, here's the tie in words if you want it: "I didn't change either spirit. I put the cardamom in the vermouth, because it goes with both."
