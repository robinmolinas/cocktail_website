**The one true thing.** When people split into camps, this person walks over and takes up the other side's thing, whether that's its drink, its song or its shirt. They do it in public, and they mean it. They mend with a gesture, never with an argument. Mandela didn't make a speech in 1995. He put the shirt on. Their standing goes on belonging, not on being right.

**What they hide.** The hardest question doesn't come from the other side. It comes from their own: "whose side are you on?" They aren't neutral. They have a side and strong views, so crossing over is a choice, and people misread it as sitting on the fence. To keep the peace, they turn their own colours down. Their own side notices, and so do they.

**Fear.** Being made to choose, and the split becoming permanent: the family that stops sitting at one table.

**Gift.** Proof that two camps can sit in one room without either giving itself up. Because of them, nobody has to choose. That's the line to say out loud once.

**Position direction.** Cross over with your own colours on. You don't have to water yourself down to be welcome on the other side, because it's all of you they came to trust.
