## For the reading (round 4, step 2), the guards I'll audit against

- "In Fleming's books, he never drinks it again": scoped, with Oxford's verb. Never an unscoped "never", never a film.
- "After he lost her" at most. Never "lover", "double agent", "suicide", how she died or betrayal (C5).
- The inscription is a fact. Bryce "remembered" making it with Fleming's help (C4). In the novel, the invention is Bond's (fiction).
- The rum: the name's origin in Bryce's account; the *Jamaican* is ours; never "the rum they served".
- Lillet: never "can't be made", never "the same bottle they had" (C3).
- "One." means one drink, from the novel. Don't build "only ever one drink" on it (the novel's one-drink-before-dinner line is unread in full).
- "It starts from his order", never "his recipe".
