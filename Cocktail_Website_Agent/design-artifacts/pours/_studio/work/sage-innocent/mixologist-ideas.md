## Lead: a Sidecar with the lemon swapped out for verjus, stirred and served clear
- **Shape:** Sidecar family · pisco (unaged grape brandy) · small stemmed cocktail glass, chilled, no ice. Pisco is a base nobody in the registry uses, and no Sage row holds a Sidecar (plan). The one Sidecar · cognac · coupe is the Auteur's, so cognac stays out of a coupe.
- **Working build (to balance in v1):** pisco, Cointreau, a little blanc vermouth, white verjus; stirred. Proportions open.
- **Why verjus:** it's "made by pressing wine grapes before they've ripened" (*Codex* p. 187, VERJUS box). Grapes' acids are tartaric and malic (*LI* p. 61), and tartaric "largely determines the acidity of the wine" (*Codex* p. 195). So the drink's sourness is the grape's own acid, which is the substance of Wren's lead. The *Codex* uses "a fairly large proportion of verjus to create a bright acidic backbone, especially in stirred sour drinks. This results in a clear cocktail, like a Martini" (p. 187).
- **The spark:** one change to a renowned classic. The Sidecar's lemon goes, and acid picked from grapes before they were ripe holds the drink up instead. Matrix p. 140 (GRAPES): grapes "have an affinity for sour flavors". I looked at its pairings (arugula/cress, walnuts, mustard, vinegar, honey, stone fruit, cilantro, Sichuan peppercorns) and set them aside: the swap is the spark. Walnuts would add a veto, and Sichuan pepper already has a row (`cognac_sichuan_pepper`).
- **Pisco and vermouth:** pisco is "intensely floral", "often bottled unaged", and its "intense bite… can be curbed by including blanc vermouth" (*Codex* p. 159). That makes three grape products in one glass.
- **Near miss, owned:** the *Codex*'s Four to the Floor (Tarby 2014, p. 187) is a Corpse Reviver #2 riff with pisco, grapefruit liqueur, blanc vermouth and verjus, stirred, in a coupe. Ours is a Sidecar (orange liqueur), so the structure is the Sidecar's and the method is the *Codex*'s. Say so in Checks.
- **Optional booster:** if verjus alone can't reach Sidecar acidity, a tartaric acid solution (*Codex* p. 195: a 10-to-1 solution; *LI* p. 58: it swaps gram for gram for citric). Guard: weighing acid crystals into a drink is *ruler-magician*'s gesture (citric + malic weighed in). If I use the powder, it's a quiet ingredient and never the gesture.
- **Allergens:** pisco, Cointreau and blanc vermouth are in the table and veto-free. Verjus is a new row (unfermented grape juice; I'll classify it on the safe side when I add it). The aim is veto-free.
- **Guards I'm setting myself:** "Stirred, so it stays perfectly clear" is *A Brother's Care*'s line (sage-lover). So the drink's clarity is a fact for the image only, never the meaning in the reading. *Making the Calls* (Sidecar · cognac · coupe) and *Still Yours* (Sidecar · vodka · saucer coupe) are the Sidecars nearest us.

## Notes for Hester (facts I'd like in the glass; I haven't sourced any of them)
- **The library has no Pasteur crystals.** Oxford's index ("Pasteur, Louis, 267") leads only to FERMENTATION (pdf 765), which is microbiology, so health guards it. The crystals need the web.
- **My own knowledge, unsourced:** grapes make only one of the two mirror forms of tartaric acid, the one that turns polarised light. Racemic acid is the two forms in equal parts, and it turns no light. If a source says so, the acid in this glass is the very form Biot's instrument read.
- **Wren's question:** "racemic" from the Latin for a bunch of grapes.
