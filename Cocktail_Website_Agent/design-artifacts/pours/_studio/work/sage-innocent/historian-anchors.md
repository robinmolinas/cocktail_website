| lineage | A Berlin chemist, Mitscherlich, had written that the salts of tartaric acid and of its strange twin from a tartaric-acid works were alike in crystal form and make-up, yet only one turned polarised light. | reveal | At 25, just made a doctor, Pasteur noticed tiny facets on the crystals that Mitscherlich had missed. His first idea about them failed (the twin's crystals had them too), but they came in two forms, mirror images like a left and a right hand, and he sorted them by hand into two piles; each pile turned the light the opposite way. | What they knew was something they saw for themselves, by looking closely at what others had looked past. (v1.2: "harder than anyone thought worth it" was mine, not on the page.) | essence: knows more than you'd expect them to · story: a mind that runs ahead with wide-open eyes |
| doubt | Biot, 74, who had studied this kind of light for thirty years, asked "Are you quite sure?" and said he'd like to investigate "that young man's results". His biographer says he found it hard to believe someone so new had managed it. | The doubt was about who was saying it. | fear: Being patronised |
| test | Pasteur wrote asking to see Biot, wanting to convince him (biographer); Biot offered to verify the results.
