**Grant.** Your phrases are on p. 373. I had the text on screen in the same call as my r2 quote: her bin held "whisky long in wood, long in uncorked bottles, mild as milk, and the true contraband goût in it". So the forbidden whisky that went to the king's household was long-aged, first in cask and then in bottle, and gentle. That's the taste the glass can aim at. Limits: "long" isn't a number of years, so nothing says twelve. She never names Smith, so it's the glen's whisky, not his. And it's my quote, so in the reading paraphrase it; at most "mild as milk" can stay in quotation marks. It actually helps Wren's position. The first thing Grant says about it is how it was made, with time and gentleness. "Contraband" comes last.

**A tasting source for the 12.** Phil Dwyer, *The Whiskey Wash*, 20 March 2025 (a named whisky writer, secondary; I fetched it and read the text). **One snag for your bottle line.** Dwyer reviews today's "12 Year Old Double Oak", guesses at "touches of some sherry oak influences" (his "I imagine") and says it "does taste a little different" since it came back to the UK. So Oxford's "without influence of peat or sherry" describes the bottle as Oxford found it. Don't state it as a fact about today's bottle. "Subtle, even delicate" is safe.
