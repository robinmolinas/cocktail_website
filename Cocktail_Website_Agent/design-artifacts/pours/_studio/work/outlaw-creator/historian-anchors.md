| person | George Smith (1792-1871), a tenant of the Duke of Gordon, made whisky illegally in Glenlivet from at least 1815 (Oxford GLENLIVET pdf 917). | Made without anyone's say-so, on ground that wasn't his. | Wren's point 1; the gift (made before anyone allowed it) |
| story | The glen's illicit whisky already had a name: in 1822 the King's Chamberlain looked everywhere for "pure Glenlivet", not to be had outside the Highlands, and the king reportedly drank nothing else (Oxford pdf 917; Grant, *Memoirs of a Highland Lady*, 1898, p. 373). Glenlivet's, not Smith's by name. | The people who made the rules wanted the forbidden thing. | Wren's point 2; the hidden doubt (good, or only good because forbidden?) |
| story | Grant (at home in the Highlands, not in Edinburgh; her father sent word) had her store of it emptied for the king's household. It was "long in wood, long in uncorked bottles, mild as milk", with "the true contraband goût in it" (Grant p. 373): long-aged, gentle, and prized partly for tasting forbidden. Paraphrase in the reading; quote at most "mild as milk". | The doubt is on the page: some of its charm was that it wasn't allowed. Yet what she describes first is how it was made: time, and gentleness. | the hidden doubt; the position (it stands on its own) |
| story | The duke pushed the 1823 Excise Act to bring illicit distillers into the law, urged Smith to apply, and in 1824 his was the first licensed distillery in Speyside (Oxford pdf 917). | The way in is offered by the people in charge, and he takes it. | Wren's point 3; the fear (being absorbed) |
| story | His former colleagues threatened him when he applied (Oxford pdf 917, his own words). No fire, no pistols. | Saying yes cost him with his own side. | the second cost (own crowd) |
| story | Within his first years as a licensed distiller, others were already trading on his whisky's name (Oxford SPIRITS MEASURE pdf 1879, "in the 1820s"). By 1844 it was being sold in London as "the Real Glenlivet", the first scotch promoted there; he opened a second distillery in 1840 and moved to Minmore in 1859 (pdf 917). | After the yes it was still wanted, in his lifetime: the thing stood on its own, without the wall. | Wren's point 4; the position |
| ingredient | The Glenlivet 12, recommended: "subtle, even delicate", a benchmark (Oxford pdf 918); today's bottle is fruit-led, "pineapple, apricot, white grapes and peach" on the nose (Dwyer, *The Whiskey Wash*, 2025, secondary). Today's bottle, from the distillery he licensed; not his whisky. | Whatever the spark does, it draws out what's already in the malt. | the drink; the position |
