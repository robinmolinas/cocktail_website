# Persona card: The Rule Breaker (outlaw-creator), Wren, round 1

Source: `persona.py outlaw-creator` (xlsx row), family plan `_studio/plans/outlaw.md`, Oxford GLENLIVET pdf 917–918 read whole this round.

## The one true thing
They've never been frightened by a no. **The yes is what they don't trust.** Being refused is fuel; being invited in (bought, licensed, framed, made official) is the day they fear they stop being able to make anything. Under that sits the doubt they hide: *is what I make good, or only good because it isn't allowed?*

## Hides / fears / gives
- **Hides:** that doubt; and, quieter, that part of them would like the thing to last, which usually means letting someone in charge have it. They're a little ashamed of wanting that.
- **Fears:** powerlessness in its specific form: being absorbed. Once they say yes, the people in charge own the thing, and them. Also the second, unspoken cost: their own crowd calling them a sell-out.
- **Gives:** they make the better version before anyone allows it, and so show everyone what was possible. Much of what people are later allowed, someone like this made first. (Say it out loud once, per Robin's 2026-09-30 rule.)
- **Position direction:** the thing was never good because it was forbidden. Let one thing be made that isn't against anything, and find out it stands on its own. In their interest because it frees them from needing a wall. Words not final; must not echo *Still Yours*.

## Traps
1. **Never make the guest a lawbreaker** or glamourise illegality (family trap). The mirror is "made without anyone's say-so", never "you break the law".
2. **No violence or fire:** the pistols out; the threat told as his old colleagues turning on him.
3. **Archetype words:** never "outlaw", never "creator". Count uses of "rule" and "break" (the name already spends them).
4. **The 1884 court order is the son's and lands on "keep it" (*Still Yours*):** out. The 1880s borrowed name may be one sentence of proof, no fight.
5. **Today's bottle:** Oxford says the Glenlivet is now owned by Chivas/Pernod Ricard and is the world's second-bestselling single malt. That is literally this guest's "defeat" and the Leading Edge's ending: stays out of the reading; and the reading never calls the bottle "his whisky" or "the same whisky" (nothing says Smith's illicit spirit was a twelve-year-old).
6. **"Same whisky after the licence":** my plan line needs a page. Oxford implies it (reputation held; Usher's "the Real Glenlivet" by 1844, in his lifetime) but doesn't say it. Hester.
7. **Opener:** "In 1824," is *Not Too Polite*'s y1 opener (same year!). Don't open with it.
8. **"Reportedly"** stays on the king. Duke's motive not stated beyond "urged by his landlord" and "largely responsible for promoting the Excise Act".
9. No Banksy, no shredding (a sale story, and a named living artist the guest hasn't asked about).
10. The drink: not a protest in a glass, not smuggled/hidden. Something that makes the whisky more itself.

## My story test (for any lead)
1. Made without anyone's say-so, on ground that wasn't theirs.
2. Good, and the proof is on the page: the people in charge wanted it before it was allowed.
3. The invitation in: the establishment offers a way in, and it costs them with their own side.
4. After the yes, the thing is still good, shown on a page, in their lifetime: it was never good because it was forbidden.

## Verdict on the plan's lead (George Smith of the Glenlivet)
**Accept.** Oxford pdf 917, read whole: (1) illegal since at least 1815, a tenant on the Duke's land; (2) a king in Edinburgh, 1822, "reportedly 'drank nothing else'", Usher's selling it by 1821: the people who make the rules drinking what the rules forbid; (3) the Duke "largely responsible" for the 1823 Act that "encouraged illicit distillers to 'go legal'", urged him; he took the first licence in the region (1824) and his former colleagues turned on him; (4) by 1844 Usher's were agents selling "the Real Glenlivet" in London, and by the 1880s thirty distilleries borrowed the name. Point 4 is the only soft one (see trap 6).
