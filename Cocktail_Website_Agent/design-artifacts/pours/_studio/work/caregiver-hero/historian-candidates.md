# Hester: mirror hunt, caregiver-hero (The Rescuer), round 1

Persona: goes into danger to help; goal service; fear selfishness (and, from the hero side, timidity). Example: the St Bernard.

## Candidate A (my pick): Félix Kir, the man a drink was named for
- Story: A priest who was a medical orderly in WWI; in 1940 the Germans arrested him for helping the people of Dijon and for resisting, two military tribunals sentenced him to death, and he was freed after two months. Arrested again in 1943 (accused of passing escapees to England), shot and wounded at home in 1944, then elected mayor at 69. The drink wasn't his: Dijon had been pouring white wine and cassis at receptions for decades; it took his name because of who he'd been.
- Mirror: the rescuer who never asked to be credited; the honour came afterwards, for a drink he didn't invent, made of what his own region grew. Fear of selfishness answered: nothing in it was taken for himself.
- Sources: fact card `felix-kir.md` (Oxford KIR pdf 1133; Assemblée nationale Sycomore bio).
- Risks: the 5,000 Longvic escapes and the naming-rights story are out (L2, L3). Kir's motives stay the bartender's reading.
- Drink note for Tomás: wine + cassis, veto-free; no warm drink (sibling rule); room for a spark.

## Candidate B: the St Bernard's keg (legend watch, persona's own example)
- The brandy keg is a legend (popularised by Landseer's painting; library has only Imbibe pdf 85's joke). Natural History Museum Bern: the dogs' main job was to find the way back to the hospice in blizzards; Barry "is said to have" helped rescue 40 people; he spent his last two years in retirement and died in Bern in 1814.
- Mirror: the real help was finding the way home, not the drink. But no drink's history carries it except a false one; keep it as a labelled legend or a whoYouAre image only. No anchor on the keg.
