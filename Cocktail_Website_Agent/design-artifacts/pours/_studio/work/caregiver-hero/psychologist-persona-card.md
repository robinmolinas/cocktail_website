# Persona card: The Rescuer (caregiver-hero)
Wren, round 1. Source: persona.py (essence "prepared to go to unknown and dangerous places to help people out"; goal Service; fears Selfishness / Weakness, Incompetence, Timidity; drivers Provide structure / Leave a mark; example the St Bernard dog).

## Core truth
They don't decide to go. By the time anyone else has decided, they've already moved. Their care is a reflex that runs *toward* the trouble, and it's the one moment in life when they feel completely sure of themselves.

## Shows / hides
- **Shows:** steadiness when things go wrong. The calm voice at the accident, the one handing out jobs ("you call, you hold this, you stay with her"). Physical, practical, fast. No speeches.
- **Hides:** that the emergencies are the easy part. Afterwards, when everyone's safe and the adrenaline drains, they're shaky, or flat, or wide awake at three in the morning, and nobody sees that. And the quiet, ordinary weeks are harder than the crises: without someone to go out for, they're not sure what they're for.

## Hidden fear -> hidden gift
- **Fear:** being the one who stood back. Hesitation feels to them like selfishness (Caregiver's fear) and like cowardice (Hero's). So they'd rather get hurt than be the one who waited. Under that: being the one who needs rescuing. They never call for help; they're the one who comes.
- **Gift:** nobody has to be alone in their worst moment when this person is around. They walk into the part everyone else backs away from (the hospital corridor, the row, the flooded kitchen, the phone call at 2 a.m.) and they bring order into it.

## The "real me" line to aim for (drafts)
- *"You don't decide to go. You notice you've already gone."*
- *"You're never calmer than when something's gone wrong. It's afterwards that your hands shake."*
- Position candidates (Ryu, earned last): let someone come out for you *before* it's an emergency; or: the part after the rescue is yours too, rest is not standing back. (Avoid "You're allowed to…": Four Shares / True Friend own it.)

## Truth vs. legend
They want the **true** story and they'll distrust anything heroic and inflated. They know real rescues are unglamorous. So the story's best mirror may be a legend *corrected*: the St Bernard's brandy barrel is (I believe) a painter's invention, and the famous dog Barry's legendary death at the hands of the man he was saving is (I believe) a legend too: he's said to have retired and been looked after. A rescuer who was, at the end, the one cared for. **Hester to verify; not a fact until she does.**

## Tone
Brisk, warm, direct. Hero's "assertive" becomes plain and short, not rousing. No praise: call them brave and they'll deflect. Serious with a dry edge is allowed; they're often the one making the dark joke in the waiting room afterwards.

## Distinct from siblings
- **The Understanding Spouse** stays for years, through thick and thin. The Rescuer comes for the worst hour, and is often gone before the thanks.
- **The Healer** repairs afterwards. The Rescuer gets them out first; the mending is someone else's.
- **The Nanny** imposes structure for your own good, over time. The Rescuer brings structure only into chaos, for minutes, and hands it back.
- **The Campaigner** fights a system, loudly, for many. The Rescuer goes for the one in front of them, now.
- **The Nurse** mucks in, everyday. The Rescuer is at their best when the day is anything but.
- **The Craftsman** (draft) = pride in the method. Not this.

## Traps
- **"Hero", "brave", "courage" as compliments.** Flattery; they'd deflect. Show the scene, not the medal.
- **Adrenaline junkie / needs a crisis.** Would read as selfish, their exact fear. The quiet-days line must be said with care: not "you need drama", but "you don't know what to do with yourself when nobody needs you".
- **Alcohol as rescue.** Never imply a drink warms a cold person or saves anyone (untrue, and the barrel legend is exactly this). No barrel garnish, no performed rescue in the glass.
- **Gender:** the St Bernard story is full of monks and "men"; the guest is never gendered.
- **Siblings' frames:** "You're allowed to…" (Four Shares), "What nobody sees…" (Overnight), "What you give people…" (The Other Berry).
