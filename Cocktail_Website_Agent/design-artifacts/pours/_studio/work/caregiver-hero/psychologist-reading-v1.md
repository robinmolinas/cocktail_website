## whoYouAre

Something goes wrong: a fall on the stairs, a phone call at two in the morning, a friend's voice that isn't quite right. By the time anyone else has decided what to do, you're already moving. You don't feel brave. You feel clear. You're the one saying "you call, you hold this, stay with them", and it's the surest you ever feel.

The part you keep to yourself comes later. When everyone's safe and home, your hands start to shake, or you lie awake until four. And the quiet weeks can be harder than the bad nights, because with nobody to go out for, you're not sure what you're for. Standing back has always felt to you like the worst kind of selfishness. So you've never let anyone come out for you. You'd rather be the one who comes.

## yours

1. High on the Great St Bernard pass there's a hospice, and for centuries its canons and their servants went out into the snow to find travellers who were lost. Over time they began taking dogs with them. The picture everyone knows is a dog with a little barrel of brandy on its collar. That's a legend. The dogs' real work was plainer: finding the way back to the hospice through a blizzard.

2. The most famous of them was Barry. He is said to have helped rescue forty people. The story usually told is that he died out there, killed by a soldier who took him for a wolf, and the museum in Bern that keeps him calls that a legend too. What's on record is quieter. At the prior's request he was brought to Bern, and he spent his last two years there, retired, until 1814.

3. Nobody wrote down who looked after him in those two years. I like to think someone did: that after all the nights of going out, someone else finally did the going, and he let them. The rescues get told. That part never is. You know the first part by heart. Finding the way back through the worst of it is the one thing you never doubt. It's the second part you've never allowed.

4. So this isn't a drink you carry out to anyone. It's a hot one, [aged spirit], honey and hot water with a strip of lemon peel, [spark], in a wide cup. There's no brandy in it, and that's on purpose. And it's hot for one reason: it doesn't keep. Put it aside for a better moment and the moment's gone. It has to be made for you, and you have to be there when it's ready.

5. You won't ask for help in a crisis. I know that, and I'm not going to argue with it. So turn it round. On some ordinary evening, when nothing's wrong and nobody needs you, let someone make you this, and sit down while they do. Let them do the going, just once.

## Swap test (self, before Resonance)
- Understanding Spouse: stays for years. This one comes for the worst hour: "already moving", "it's the surest you ever feel". Doesn't fit the Spouse.
- Healer: mends afterwards. Here "afterwards" is the guest's own shaking hands. Doesn't fit.
- Nanny: order over time. Here order only in chaos ("you call, you hold this"). Doesn't fit.
- Craftsman: no credit or pride theme anywhere. Ending is about receiving care, not naming the work.
- True Friend (Four Shares: "keep one cup back for yourself", "You're allowed to be looked after too"): **closest risk.** Both say "let yourself be cared for". The difference: the True Friend fears being left out and gives to belong; this one fears standing back, and the proposal is timing, *before* the emergency, not a share. I've kept "allowed to" and "looked after too" out of the guest's lines. Robin should see this adjacency flagged.
