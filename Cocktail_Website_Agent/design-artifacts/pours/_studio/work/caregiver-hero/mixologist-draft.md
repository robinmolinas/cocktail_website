## Recipe

- **serves:** 1
- **glassware:** a wide teacup (about 250 ml), warmed with hot water first; not a tall mug
- **contains:** `[]`

| amount | item | note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 45 ml | Irish whiskey (40–46%) | no brandy in this one, and that's on purpose |
| 2 level tsp (10 ml) | runny honey (clover or wildflower) | |
| 2 | fresh thyme sprigs | in for two minutes, then out: the part somebody else has to wait for |
| 110 ml | water, just off the boil | just under half a cup |

## Method
1. Fill the teacup with hot water to warm it, then pour that water away.
2. Put the honey and the thyme sprigs in the cup. Press the thyme into the honey with the back of a spoon a few times, just enough to bruise it.
3. Pour in 110 ml of water that has just come off the boil and stir until the honey has disappeared.
4. Leave it for two minutes. Then lift the thyme out.
5. Stir in the whiskey.
6. Hand it over while it's hot.

**closingLine:** *Two minutes for the thyme. Spend them sitting down.*

- It carries Wren's position ("let someone make you this, and sit down while they do") as something to do. The method's own two minutes are the time the Rescuer is asked not to fill.
- It avoids "someone else" (Making the Calls: "let someone else make the second"), "wait" (Wren's epigraph: "it won't wait"), "don't wait" (Four Shares), "look after", "allowed to", "your turn" (a name candidate), and any warming claim. A grep of every pour file finds no "sitting down", "sit down" or "two minutes".
- Alternative, for the record: *"Take the leaves out, then sit down. The rest isn't yours to do."* It's longer, and "isn't yours" leans on Still Yours.

Whiskey strength: 40% → 10.9%, 43% → 11.7%, 46% → 12.6%, all in range, hence "40–46%". **Heat and sweetness (scoped, round 7, Hester's T1):** Arnold's rule that hot drinks need less sugar is about **sucrose** ("your perception of sucrose increases as temperature increases", *LI* pdf 187). The `hot` sugar band is built on sucrose, so for honey it's a floor more than a target. That's why the recipe sits at **7.03 g, near the top of the band (5.5–7.5), on purpose**, and not in its middle. Acid goes in sparingly (pdf 187), here none at all. Too much alcohol in a hot drink gives "a very unpleasant, stinging nose" (pdf 184). Hot drinks are served in wide cups, "not in vessels with tall walls like a coffee mug", because tall walls concentrate the alcohol vapour (pdf 186–187). Hence the teacup. **Unsourced:** the honey row: 82% sugar by weight (*LI* p. 53, pdf 57) × a density of 1.42 g/ml (standard value, not from the library) = about 116 g per 100 ml. Heat loss and any evaporation over two minutes aren't modelled. The thyme adds no sugar, acid or strength (v2 re-run: 10.9% / 7.03 g / 0%, identical to v1). |
