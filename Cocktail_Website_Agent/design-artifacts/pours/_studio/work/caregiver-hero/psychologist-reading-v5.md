## whoYouAre

Something goes wrong: a fall on the stairs, a phone call at two in the morning, a friend's voice that isn't quite right. By the time anyone else has decided what to do, you're already moving. You don't feel brave. You feel clear. You're the one saying "you call, you hold this, stay with them", and it's the surest you ever feel.

Nobody sees the part that comes later. When everyone's safe and home, your hands start to shake, or you lie awake until four. And the quiet weeks can be harder than the bad nights, because with nobody to go out for, you're not sure what you're for. Standing back has always felt to you like the worst kind of selfishness. So you've never let anyone come out for you. You'd rather be the one who comes.

## yours

1. The hospice on the Great St Bernard Pass has been run by canons since the eleventh century, and they and their servants went out into the snow to find travellers who were lost. Over time they began taking dogs with them. The picture everyone knows is a dog with a little barrel of brandy on its collar. That's a legend. The dogs' real work was plainer: finding the way back to the hospice through a blizzard.

2. The most famous of them was Barry. He is said to have helped rescue forty people. There's a story that he was killed by a French soldier who mistook him for a wolf, and the museum in Bern that keeps him calls that a legend too. What's on record is quieter. He retired for his last two years, was brought to Bern at the prior's request, and died there in 1814.

3. Nobody I've read says who looked after him in those two years. I like to think someone did: that after all the nights of going out, someone else finally did the going, and he let them. The rescues get told. That part doesn't. You know the first part by heart. Finding the way back through the worst of it is the one thing you never doubt. It's the second part you've never allowed.

4. So this isn't a drink you carry out to anyone. It's a hot one: Irish whiskey, and a little honey stirred into hot water, in a wide cup. Two sprigs of thyme sit in the honey and water for two minutes and come out before the whiskey goes in, and whoever's making it waits with you. There's no brandy in it, and that's on purpose. And it's hot for one reason: it doesn't keep. Put it aside for a better moment and the moment's gone. It has to be made for you, and you have to be there when it's ready.

5. You won't ask for help in a crisis. I know that, and I'm not going to argue with it. So turn it round. On some ordinary evening, when nothing's wrong and nobody needs you, let someone make you this, and sit down while they do. Let them do the going, just once.
