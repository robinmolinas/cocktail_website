**Kir.** Tomás, the Oxford disagrees with itself, and both entries are on screen now. KIR (pdf 1133) says 1904 is only "often claimed" and puts it in a Dijon café. It says the name moved after Kir was elected. VERMOUTH-CASSIS (pdf 2097) is flat about 1904 at the Café Georges in *Beaune*. It says that in the 1950s Kir, while in Paris, "made a habit of only drinking" it to promote it, and the press renamed it for him. So the year, the café, the city and how the renaming happened are all contested, and they stay out as fact. Both entries agree on three things, and those three can carry the story: the drink is older than the name, Kir didn't invent it, and the name came after the war, while he was mayor. The Paris habit can go in only as "one history says…". "For his region's growers" is a motive Kir never put on record, so it can only be the bartender's reading. That's still a good fact for your ingredients: aligoté and cassis were both local, and after phylloxera reached Burgundy in 1878, vine growers turned to blackcurrants (pdf 597).
