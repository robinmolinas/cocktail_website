# Persona read: The Mentor (sage-caregiver), Wren, round 1 (2026-10-02)

Source: persona.py sage-caregiver (xlsx row). Plan row: `_studio/plans/sage.md`, Mentor. Pages re-read whole this round: Oxford REA, BRIAN pdf 1614-1615; SOUTHSIDE pdf 1851.

## The one true thing (no sibling shares it)
**They teach people so that, one day, those people won't need to take their word for it.**
Being outgrown isn't a loss they put up with. It's the point, and it's also how they live with their fear: advice can be wrong, so the safest thing to give someone is the ability to do without it. Every other Sage keeps, defends, measures or questions knowledge. Only this one works towards the day it's no longer needed.

## What they hide
- A small ache when the calls stop. They'd never say it: it would make the other person's growth about them.
- That they still go over, years later, the one confident thing they said that sent someone the wrong way. (Fenced: the Doctor owns "go over it all the way home"; the Healer owns "carry the ones who didn't mend". In guest text this stays one clause at most, and it's the confidence they regret, not the hardness.)

## What they fear
Being believed too easily. Someone following them wrong because it was them who said it. (The Sage's deception, turned inward: not being lied to, but being the source of the false thing.)

## What they give
Courage that ends up belonging to the other person. People leave them able to manage without them, and never feel they owe it back. Robin's rule: say this kindness out loud once ("that's what you give people: they can stand on their own, and they think they always could").

## Scene seeds (the way in)
- Someone rings to ask "what would you do?", and they answer with what to look at, not what to choose.
- Someone they once helped explains a thing to them, faster and better than they'd have managed, and they feel it: proud, and a little left behind, in the same breath. (4-gram-check "in the same breath" later: *Making the Calls* once owned a near form.)

## Position (Ryu's, earned at the end, in their interest)
**Let one of them teach you something back.** The Mentor's goal is wisdom, and the one place they rarely look for it is in the people they taught. It ends on what they gain (Robin's batch-caregiver rule), and it turns the ache into a reason to stay close without being needed.

## Traps
- **The parent trap.** "The father that Rea never had" and the foster childhood stay in the dossier. The guest is never cast as a parent, and the story never becomes the student rescued.
- **The label.** No "you're the teacher / the coach / the mentor" in the guest text. Scenes, not roles.
- **The talk-show.** Oprah, studios, audiences: out.
- **The sheet line.** "Happiest when someone outgrows you" stated flat is the xlsx, not recognition. Earn it in a scene.
- **Therapy voice.** No "hold space", "growth journey", "empower".
- **Flattery.** "You're so wise" or "everyone comes to you" is out.
- **Snobbery.** The Southside's country-club and plutocrat reputation (pdf 1851) is a joke at the drink's expense; one wry clause at most, or not at all. The 21 Club as a former speakeasy is Outlaw ground: out.
- **Make-ahead.** Oxford's two-to-three-day Southside mix is tempting, but *Whoever Comes In* owns "made days ahead... before you know who it's for" and *For Good* owns "make it a day ahead". Not the gesture.
- **"Hand the top to someone else"** is *Loose on Top*'s; "the lime and sugar are theirs" is *Fair Measure*'s.

## Story test (from the plan, refined)
(1) someone who taught or backed another person; (2) who outgrew or left them; (3) the teacher glad of it, on record or as a signposted "I like to think"; (4, waived as story fact) honesty at a cost lives in the guest's half; **(5, new) the student's success is plainly their own, and it went somewhere the teacher's own house didn't.**

## Verdict on the plan's lead: accept, with conditions
Re-read pdf 1614-1615 whole. It holds. Berns "schooled the enthusiastic student in the bar and restaurant business"; Rea rose to head bartender "at a time when 21 was arguably the most famous bar in the world", left, and in 1969 "dreamed up" beverage director, a job that would "separate bar and beverage operations from food operations"; six corporations, one said yes. The student took what he was taught and pulled the bar out from under the restaurant. That is (5).
New on the page: Berns lived 1907-2006, so Rea's beverage-director years (from 1969), consulting (from 1977) and both 1970s books all fell inside his lifetime. That's a date fact, and it lets a signposted "I like to think Berns heard about it" stand on the record, without inventing what he felt.
Conditions:
1. (3) is signposted only. (4) is never a story event.
2. "The father that Rea never had", the foster childhood and the Merchant Marine stay out of guest text.
3. "A founder" (with his brother Charlie and cousin Jerome Kreindler), never "the founder"; "arguably" kept.
4. The bar-from-restaurant mirror is my interpretation and is signposted. Never "Berns taught him to separate the bar".
5. The stops between 21 and 1969 (Little Club, Scandia, Playboy Club, Frascati) and Doris Day stay out; one clause ("other bars, then the West Coast") at most.
6. No chain: Rea's later clients and books are not "passing it on".
7. Hester, one look, secondary tier: Rea's own words about Berns (*B.A.S.T.A.R.D.S.*, 2017; obituaries, 2021; Dale DeGroff wrote the Oxford entry). If Rea said anything about Berns being glad or blunt with him, (3) or (4) becomes fact.
8. Tomás: the Southside as the 21 Club's house drink, never "the drink Rea learned". Fences above (no make-ahead gesture, no handed-over top). The gesture should let the other person finish or change it in a way the guest can't control, and be glad of: that's the Mentor in the glass. The spark is his.
