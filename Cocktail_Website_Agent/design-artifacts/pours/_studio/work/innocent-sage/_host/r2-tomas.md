It's the Whiskey Sour that was ordered, made right and made better, by Regan's own book. His printed spec (*Joy* pdf 406: 2 oz bourbon or rye, 1 oz lemon, ½ oz simple) reads OUT on acid (1.11% against a 0.94% top), so I cut the lemon to 20 ml. The touch comes from *Joy* pdf 151, where he says to muddle lemon with granulated sugar for a Whiskey Sour, because "the sugar abrades the zest of the lemons and produces a far fresher-tasting drink". So: 60 ml bourbon, 20 ml measured lemon, 2½ tsp (11 g) sugar pressed into the squeezed lemon half, shaken and strained twice into a chilled stemmed sour glass. No egg white, no honey, no ice, no garnish. One departure is mine: he muddles wedges, I measure the juice and muddle the squeezed shell, so the acid stays put. It isn't oleo-saccharum, because nothing rests. `balance.py`: 19.5% · 8.18 g · 0.867% · dilution 59.3%, all in range. Bourbon at 40% is all in, 47% edges on strength, and bonded (50%) goes OUT, so the recipe says 40 to 46%. If the shell gives up another 2.5 ml of juice, acid only edges (0.95%). `allergens.py`: veto-free. The Scotch trace is out.
