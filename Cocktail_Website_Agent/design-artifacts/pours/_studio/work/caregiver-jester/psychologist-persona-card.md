# Persona card: The Reassurer (caregiver-jester)

Wren, round 1. Source: persona.py (essence "Cheering people up with a smile and a little joke"; goal Service; fear Selfishness; Jester fears Boredom, Monotony; example "Hang in there" meme).

## Core truth
They joke *because* it's serious, not because it isn't. The joke is how they hold someone in a bad moment: a laugh says "this is still survivable, and you're not alone in it."

## Shows / hides
- **Shows:** the light line in the waiting room, the text that makes someone snort on the worst day, the calm that arrives with a grin.
- **Hides:** how carefully the joke is timed. They read the room first, wait, and sometimes swallow the line because it's too early. And they hide the worry underneath: that the joke was for *them*, to make the moment bearable for themselves, or that someone heard it as not caring.

## Hidden fear → hidden gift
- **Fear (Selfishness):** "Was that joke for them or for me?" and its twin, being taken as flippant ("this isn't funny"). Once or twice it landed wrong, and they still remember the face.
- **Gift:** they give people permission to laugh while it's still hard. They let someone be okay for a minute before things are okay.

## The "real me" line to aim for
> **"You don't joke because it isn't serious. You joke because it is."**

Runner-up (for the fear): "You've always known when not to say it. That's the part nobody gives you credit for."

## Position (Ryu's order: earned, at the end)
A gentle hard truth, truly in their interest: the laugh is how they get in; it can also become how they get out. **After the laugh, don't fill the next silence.** Let the quiet minute happen; they're allowed to be in it too without a line ready. (Distinct from the True Friend's "keep one cup back" and the Craftsman's "stop saying it was nothing".)

## Truth vs. legend
They want the kind story, told with a wink: something went wrong, someone was worn out, and a person looked after them *and* made them laugh. True, warm, a little funny. Not a con, not a lecture.

## Tone
Light on the surface, tender underneath. The reading is allowed one real smile (a line that's genuinely funny, earned by the scene), then goes quiet and serious for the "real me" line. Never jokey throughout: that would be performing at them, which is exactly what they'd see through.

## Distinct from siblings
- **The True Friend (innocent-regular-guy):** stays and forgives, makes people feel safe "without a speech". The Reassurer *does* say something, and it's funny, on purpose, with timing. Avoid "safe", "you just stay", "looked after too" (all True Friend's).
- **The Morale Booster (jester-caregiver, the mirror pairing):** lifts a group's energy; fun comes first, the care follows. The Reassurer is one-to-one, in hard moments; care comes first, the joke is the tool. No parties, no rooms full of people.
- **The Craftsman (caregiver-creator):** care hidden in method nobody sees. The Reassurer's care is audible and quick. **The Nurse / Healer / Rescuer** act on the problem; the Reassurer changes how the moment *feels*, not the problem.

## Traps
- The sad clown ("but who cheers you up?"): cliché, and Barnum.
- "Life of the party" / "the funny one" as a label (Robin: no labels, scenes only).
- Kitsch: "Hang in there" poster, Comic Sans cheerfulness in the words.
- A drink that's a gag or a performance (room lesson: no drink you perform). The drink can be *witty*, never a prank.
- Making light of pain in the reading itself. Never mock the hard moment.
- Relentless positivity: they aren't naive. They see exactly how bad it is. That's why they joke.

## Story hints for Hester (person first)
A drink made to comfort someone worn out, and named with a laugh. My first thought: **Ada Coleman's Hanky Panky** at the Savoy, made for the tired actor Charles Hawtrey, who named it on the first sip (as usually told; Hester to source, and to separate fact from legend). Also worth a look: remedy drinks with joke names (the Penicillin; Craddock's joke on the Corpse Reviver No. 2). The mirror I want: care and a laugh in the same gesture, for someone having a hard day.
