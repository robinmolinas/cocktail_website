## whoYouAre

When something goes badly for someone, you're the one who says the thing that makes them laugh. Not straight away. You wait. You watch their face, you work out how bad it is, and sometimes you keep the line to yourself because it's too early. Then, at the right moment, you say it, and for a second everything weighs a little less. They laugh, they're half embarrassed to be laughing, and then they breathe out.

People sometimes take it to mean you don't take things seriously. It's the other way round. You don't joke because it isn't serious. You joke because it is. The worry you keep to yourself is that the joke was really for you, to make it easier to stand there. It wasn't. It was how you told them you'd seen exactly how bad it was, and that they weren't in it alone.

## yours

1. Joe Scialom trained as a chemist. In 1938 he left a career in pharmaceuticals to tend bar at Shepheard's Hotel in Cairo, and his signature drink there was a cure for the morning after. It was called the Suffering Bastard. I've never found out who named it. I like to think it was someone fond of the people drinking it, because that's the only way you can call someone that.

2. As Jeff Berry tells it, one morning in October 1942 his regulars from the Eighth Army were sent out to El Alamein after a long night. From the front, they cabled Scialom for the cure. He sent four gallons of it in Thermoses, by taxi.

3. The journalists at the bar seized on it, and the version they printed was that his drink helped win the battle. It almost certainly didn't. The battle is the part that gets repeated. I'd rather tell you about the taxi.

4. The taxi is the part I think is yours. People remember that you made them laugh. They don't always notice that you came to where it was worst, with exactly what they needed, and that the laugh was how you delivered it. So this one is long and bright, and the first thing anyone notices is the lift. Under it, the ginger is cooked rather than raw, so it warms instead of stinging, and a little toasted cumin sits under that, where you'd only find it if you went looking. And it's made to travel: everything but the soda goes in a bottle, and the soda goes in when you arrive.

5. The laugh is how you get in. Don't let it become how you get out. When the laugh comes, don't reach for the second line. Let the quiet after it happen. You're good company there too, without saying a thing.
