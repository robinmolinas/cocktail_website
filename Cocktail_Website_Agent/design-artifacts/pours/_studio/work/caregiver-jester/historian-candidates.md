# Hester: mirror candidates, caregiver-jester (The Reassurer), round 1

Persona: cheering people up with a smile and a little joke; comfort in hard times; "Hang in there"; goal service, fear selfishness.

1. **Joe Scialom, Suffering Bastard (pick).** A trained chemist who left pharmacy for a bar (F1–F2), whose signature was a hangover cure (F4), and who, as Berry tells it, sent four gallons of it in Thermoses by taxi to his regulars at the front (F5). The care is real; the joke is the name, and the joke the journalists made of it ("it won the battle", which Oxford calls highly unlikely: F6). Card: fact-cards/joe-scialom-suffering-bastard.md. Risks: date conflict (C1), the swear word, the lime-cordial lineage belongs to caregiver-creator.
2. **Penicillin (Sam Ross, 2005; Proper pp. 170–171).** Honey, ginger, lemon, smoke: a drink with a medicine's name. On record: Ross "went to bed ... not thinking much of it"; nine months later waitress Lucinda Sterling said every table needed one. Mirror is weaker (the name's origin isn't on the page), but Lucinda (the one who pushed it out to the tables) is a niche person who fits service. Not carded yet.
3. **Corpse Reviver (Oxford pdf 584).** A name that became a joke: British journalists listed it as a typical American drink; American bartenders answered "What's that?" (1895). Became generic for a morning drink; Craddock tethered it to recipes in 1930. Funny, but no person caring for anyone. Runner-up only.
- Irish Coffee (card joe-sheridan-irish-coffee.md): withdrawn in caregiver-creator because a warm welcome drink reads as the whole family's. Not re-proposed.

## Round 2: Wren's Hanky Panky question
- Fact (card hanky-panky.md): Hawtrey came in worn out and asked to be made "quite alive" (Sphere 1926, F4); Coleman said she spent hours experimenting (People 1925, F5); "hanky panky" meant trickery/conjuring (F7).
- Legend: "By Jove! That is the real hanky-panky!" on the first sip (C3: named on the spot or next day).
- Status: it is the whole story of creator-caregiver *Quite Alive* (draft, reading paras 1-2, name from Hawtrey's words). Using it again would repeat a sibling's story. Out.
- Scialom vs Wren's fences: the regulars were a group of officers (plural, "his Eighth Army regulars"): honest tension with "one person, no groups". Our reading can keep the frame on him, one man doing it for people who asked. The name's origin is NOT on record (L2), so "named with a laugh" can't be claimed for him either; the laugh on record is the journalists' battle story (F6).
