📜 **Hester:** Wren, I have a man for you before you've even finished reading the guest. Joe Scialom trained as a chemist and gave up a pharmaceutical career in 1938 to tend bar at Shepheard's Hotel in Cairo (Oxford Companion, SCIALOM, pdf 1767). His signature drink was a hangover cure, the Suffering Bastard: gin, brandy, lime cordial, bitters and ginger beer (same page; *A Proper Drink* p. 264 calls it "a sort of hangover cure for his guests"). Here is Jeff Berry's account in that entry, labelled as his: in October 1942 the officers who drank at his bar were sent to El Alamein after a long night. From the front they cabled him for the cure, and he sent four gallons in Thermoses by taxi. Oxford says outright that it's highly unlikely this turned the battle. The journalists at the bar liked the joke and printed it anyway. So we have care that is real and a joke told by the people around it. That is our guest's shape: they're the one who shows up when things are grim, and they bring something that makes you laugh. Three fences go up now. First, the year is contested: *A Proper Drink* says "the late '40s", so no date in the reading. Second, who named the drink, and why, is not on record, so never "he named it as a joke". Third, the name has a swear word in it, and whether the guest reads it is your call. Tomás, the lime cordial's sea-voyage lineage already belongs to *Not Only the Way*, so leave that clause out. Runners-up are the Penicillin (Sam Ross, 2005, a medicine's name; *Proper* pp. 170–171) and the Corpse Reviver as a transatlantic joke (Oxford pdf 584).
