# Anchors: regular-guy-innocent, The Bumpkin (Hester, round 3; revised round 4 with audit v2)

Story: the Caipirinha. A plain cane-and-lime drink from the countryside. City versions added syrup, egg white and gin. Wondrich describes a third way that took only the ice and stayed built in the glass. It carries the countryside's name, fashionable city drinkers later swapped its cane for vodka, and Brazil's law now defines it as cachaça, lime and sugar. Card: `_studio/fact-cards/caipirinha.md` (F, C, L, G numbers below). Library: Oxford Companion (`oxford`, primary), *Cocktail Codex* (`codex`), *Joy of Mixology* (`joy`). Law: planalto.gov.br decrees (primary, fetched 2026-10-01; translations ours). **No motive is on record for anyone in this story: not for the drinkers who refused the additions, not for whoever named it.**

## Guards (keep out of guest text)
- "Little Hillbilly", the cures (evil eye, the 1856 cholera ration), "peasants and the enslaved" (pdf 378), Burton's "lowest grade", backwoods stills that refill bottles (Wren, r1).
- The US's "more mentioned than served" (Wren).
- The 1918 Spanish-flu-remedy origin: a legend, not in Oxford (G1).
- Any decade for the drink's success abroad (C1). Kenya and the Dawa: dossier only.
- "The whole lime": our spec muddles 6 of 8 pieces (*Codex*: "almost an entire lime"). Say "the skin as well as the juice".

## Legends and inferences
- **Inference (signpost):** that the drinkers who refused the additions were the ones who made the caipirinha.
- **Contested (Wondrich vs Smith, both Oxford):** whether the caipirinha was a distinct third drink or the Batida Paulista under another name. Told as Wondrich's account.
- **Unknown:** who named the drink, and whether *caipira* was meant fondly or as a jibe.
