Round 4: Hester's A1–A5 applied (audit v1); closing line moved off "tell" and off "to the end" (Wren r3; *No Accident*'s changing-as-it-goes motif); names section answers the three-way split.

The story rests on Hester's card (`_studio/fact-cards/caipirinha.md`). It is Wondrich's third way, signposted: it "incorporated the ice from the new drink into a nostalgic, rustic preparation built simply in the glass" (Oxford CAIPIRINHA pdf 384, F6). The plain floor is Brazil's legal definition: cachaça, lime and sugar (L1–L3). The drink is exactly those three, plus the ice. Spec: `_studio/specs/regular-guy-innocent.json`. Sweeps: `mixologist-sweep.py`.

**Glass:** rocks glass (double old-fashioned, about 300 ml), filled with cracked ice
**Contains:** veto-free

## Recipe
| amount | item | note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 60 ml | unaged cachaça | white (prata or branca), any bottle of 38–45%: the cane spirit itself |
| 1 | medium lime | cut across the middle, then each half into quarters; 6 of the 8 pieces go in |
| 2 heaped tsp (about 10 g) | granulated white sugar | grains, not syrup: they grind the oil out of the lime skin, so you taste the skin as well as the juice |
| | cracked ice | to fill the glass |

## Method
1. Cut the lime in half across the middle. Lay each half cut side down and cut it into quarters. Pick out any seeds you can see, because they make it bitter.
2. Put 6 of the pieces in a sturdy rocks glass and add the sugar. Keep the last 2 pieces for the next one.
3. Press each piece firmly with a muddler, or the end of a rolling pin, giving it a slight twist, until every piece is crushed and the sugar has mostly gone. Then stop.
4. Fill the glass with cracked ice. To crack it, wrap ice cubes in a clean tea towel and give them a few knocks with the rolling pin.
5. Pour in the cachaça and stir from the bottom for about ten seconds, so the sugar comes up into the drink.
6. Leave the lime pieces in. They keep flavouring the drink, and the peel can add a little bitterness, which is part of the pleasure.
