**The one true thing.** You can see this person enjoying things. Bread rolls at a wedding, the first evening warm enough to eat outside, a tomato that actually tastes of tomato: "oh, that's good," mouth still full, and they mean it. And they know exactly what the fancier option is. They aren't pleased because they don't know better. They know, and they still like this one more. No sibling has that: plain taste, held openly *by choice*, in rooms where taste decides who belongs.

**Fear.** Exclusion (goal: belonging), but a particular kind. Not being shut out: being let in as the sweet one. The half-smile, the "aw", the moment they see their delight has been filed as not knowing better. Let in as the mascot, not as one of them.

**What they hide.** They've learnt the words. They can nod along to the wine talk and call the foam "interesting". In some rooms they turn the delight down a notch, and once or twice they've ordered the clever thing to keep their seat. That costs them more than anyone guesses.

**What they give** (said plainly, Robin's rule): when they don't turn it down, everyone else can stop performing. Someone admits the bread was the best bit too. They make enjoying things easy to join, with no knowledge needed to get in.

**Where I'd end:** keep saying it first. What they gain: they get to enjoy it, and they find out how many people were waiting for someone to go first.
