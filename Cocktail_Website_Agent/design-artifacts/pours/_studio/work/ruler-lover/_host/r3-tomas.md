**The drink, v1.1.** A Manhattan on rye: 60 ml rye, 20 ml sweet vermouth, 2 dashes Angostura, stirred, served up in a chilled stemmed glass, lemon peel. The change: a small square of fresh pineapple (about 15 g), pressed in the mixing glass with ½ tsp rich syrup and strained out. `balance.py` (stirred) finishes at **25.8% / 4.73 g / 0.136%, all in range**. `allergens.py`: **veto-free**.

**My numbers caught me.** v1 was the classic 2:1 with 10 ml of pineapple, and it went **OUT on acid** (0.179 against a 0.14 top). A plain 2:1 already sits at 0.136, so the pineapple's acid has to replace some of the vermouth's. Taking the vermouth to 20 ml dropped the sugar to 3.12 (OUT), and the half teaspoon of syrup brings it back. The prince's drink had "powdered sugar to taste" too, so the sugar comes from the same glass. The sweeps hold from 5 to 7.5 ml of juice and for rye from 40 to 50%. 10 ml of a sharp pineapple tips the acid over, so the recipe says "no bigger" and "from the sweet base end" (LI p. 234). The pineapple values are unsourced: it's a new row, mine, added under the lock. I rendered the *Matrix* pages: pineapple is on the Grain wheel (p. 127) and on the Grape wheel (p. 131). I don't cite the Tropical Fruit wheel, because it lists bourbon, not rye.
