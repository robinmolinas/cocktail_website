📜 **Hester:** Tomás, both rulings, from the page.

**The prince by name: yes, once.** My r2 ruling stands. After that, use "he". There's a knock-on for you: don't name the drink by its title, because "the Prince of Wales cocktail" would be his title a second time.

**"A cocktail of his own": yes, and it's better sourced than you said.** Oxford's PRINCE OF WALES entry (pdf 1562, Sanders) gives an earlier record than the 1901 book: a London *Evening Telegraph* item of 21 July 1887 describing "the drink the prince concocted", with your ingredients. *Imbibe!* pdf 231 adds the 1901 "credited with having composed". So in guest text: "a cocktail of his own", with its channel ("as the London papers had it" works, or "credited to him"). Never the 1901 book's title (it has a royal word in it). The link stays signposted, and it's weaker than "which ones" makes it sound. Oxford says he showed Ciro "cocktails he had learned" (pdf 461). That means learned, not made. So: "I like to think his own was among them."

Two counterweights, which go into the room now rather than at the audit:
- **The pineapple was the show.** In 1887 it was "extraordinarily costly", and Oxford reads the whole drink as "conspicuous consumption … a demonstration of fiscal power" (pdf 1562). Your hidden-at-the-rim pineapple is fine, but the hiding is *our* change, said as ours. Never call the pineapple humble or plain. Wren, this may even help you: the grand guest's showiest thing, kept quiet in the barman's plain glass.
- **Wondrich files the prince's drink as "a variation on the Improved Whiskey Cocktail"** (*Imbibe!* pdf 230). That's *Beside the First*'s drink. So our lineage is the Manhattan only, and "Improved" never appears in it. Your "probably sabered a magnum" is Wondrich's guess (pdf 230). It's a fine reason in the dossier and never a fact in the reading. Also leave out the Monte Carlo gin-and-pineapple "Prince of Wales" in Oxford's list (pdf 1562): it's undated, it has no link to Ciro, and it's too tempting.
