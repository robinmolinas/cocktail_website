## Passes (checked)

- **Epigraph:** "The vermouth in this glass is there because hardly anyone stopped at one." This holds as a compression of pdf 490. In Oxford's chain, a strong drink plus drinking in rounds meant "drinkers rarely stopped at one", and vermouth was "one solution". "Stopped at one" carries the strength. It makes sense cold, and it names no date or bar. (Optional: "first went in" would put it on the ancestor. Wren's call, not required.)
- **y1:** wartime law in force before national Prohibition; "the Greenwich Village Inn …, which a man named Barney Gallant ran" (C1: "ran", as ruled); raided that year; arrested with half a dozen of his waiters; plea "on one condition: that his waiters were let go" (F1–F3, *Joy* pdf 59).
- **y2:** "sentenced to thirty days in jail, and he served far less" (F4; stated once, as ruled). "His name really was Gallant" (F2; nothing claims it was his birth name). "His place to answer for", "maybe it was simply good business" and "I like to think… decided the cost was his" are signposted; no motive is claimed.
- **y3:** the wonder is signposted and one sentence long. "You've stood where Barney stood" is about the guest.
- **y4 (as far as written):** rounds, turns to pay, hardly anyone stopped at one, the drink too strong, the equal split, "the Manhattan and its family" (pdf 490: "in the form of the Manhattan and the Martini and the Metropole", divided equally; pdf 1221: "progenitor"). "I kept that split" is true of the spec (45 / 45 ml, pdf 1224's 1:1). "Half the weight" is true of the core (rye and vermouth), not the whole glass, and it's our meaning, signposted by "I kept it for what you won't allow yourself".
- **y5, whoYouAre, tagline:** no historical claims.
- **Closing line:** "Let someone get this one. Don't get the next." No fact, and no sibling closing line uses "get" (grep of the pours). "The Next One" was only an alternate in *Making the Calls*' dossier, never shipped.
