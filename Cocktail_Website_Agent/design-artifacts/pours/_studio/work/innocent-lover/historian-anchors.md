| story | Many drinks have been called the Brooklyn Cocktail; most of their inventors wanted one to rival the Manhattan and the Bronx (Oxford BROOKLYN COCKTAIL pdf 360; *Imbibe!* pdf 211). | person | July 1910: Maurice Hegeman, a Cincinnati man living in Brooklyn, told a New York paper he couldn't sleep one night, thought up a cocktail for Brooklyn that would compare with the Manhattan and the Bronx, and got up to make it at a café by the Brooklyn end of the bridge (hard cider, absinthe, ginger ale, in a long glass). Reprinted in Kansas City and Cincinnati (*Cincinnati Enquirer* 7 Jul 1910 p. 4, crediting the *New York Telegraph*; *Kansas City Star* 4 Jul 1910 p. 7). | origin | The Brooklyn Club's drink: Oxford dates the earliest Brooklyn to 1883 and says it apparently never left the club (pdf 360). In 1945 the *Eagle*'s columnist, after asking the club, printed it as half Jamaica rum (100 proof), half Italian vermouth and a dash of Angostura, and its bartender said he didn't get many calls for it (*Eagle* 13 Dec 1945 p. 3; the column Oxford cites, pdf 361). F2, F17 | **For Tomás:** the rum Manhattan now has a recipe on record, as served in 1945 (never "the 1883 recipe"; the method is garbled, so not sourced).
