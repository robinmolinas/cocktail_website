[28] Investigation of Milk. The milk question is engaging the attention of New York, Philadelphia, and other large cittes, and the discussion hag waxed so warm that health departments, to facilitate the solution, are publishing weekly bulletins. So far the net results seem to de a clearing of the question of much erroneous belief and opinion. The common ground has been reached in New York, at least, that sclentific research must be continued until the truth can be ascertained. In other words, nobody knows the milk question as it should be known. The fight hinges upon the relative nutritional value of pasteurized and unpasteurized milk. The pasteurized milk champions are divided into two camps, advocating "thoroughly pasteurized" and "commercially pasteurized" milk. The general consensus of opinion occupies the common ground that pure raw. milk 13 the best, if it can be had. If it cannot be had, or it there is any doubt about getting it, then It is agreed that some form of protection from Injurious bacterla should be adopted. The health department of Philadelphia wisely points out that unless the milk is properly handled and cared for at the farm, its Initial' starting point, no processes which are applied later can be of any use in preventing deterioration. The health department might go further than this and relieve the farms of some blame by saying that even if milk ig started from the farm 1 in good condition, with due regard to all sanitary precautions, it is very possible that in its subsequent handlings before it reaches consumers rapid deterioration may be caused by careless and uncleanly methods. The question is now in a fair way of being settled, because In some of the cities there has entered into the discussion the personal element of controversy, which always stirs up activity. Purveyors of milk whose methods are criticised and medical men whose opinions are doubted are arrayed to vindicate themselves in the ultimate settlement of the question. Meanwhile the shifting of the blame for poor or unwholesome milk goes merrily on between the farmer and the final dispenser.

===== Morning Call 1914-12-17 p11 (page id 552538207) items=23 match=[1]
[1] Hackensack, Dec. 16.-Henry Wellington Wack, a lawyer of 42 Broadway, Manhattan, who has for years been in the public eye in roles varying from inventor of a Brooklyn cocktail to that of defender of Dr. Cook, self-confessed discoverer of the North Pole, had an unpleasant time in Judge Seufert's court today as the defendant in an action to break the will of the late William G. Koeppen, of Hillsdale, who left most of his estate of $30,000 to Wack. Dorothy Harris, of 271 West Fiftysecond street, New York, was the displeasing witness. She said that when Koeppen was in the upholstery business in New York he very chummy with Wack, who was his counsel. She said that she introduced them in her apartments and that they often came there together. Her recital of the nature of these calls made Wack's counsel particularly anxious to crossexamine her, but inasmuch as the court stenographer was a young lady Judge Seufert decided to adjourn the case and to get a male stenographer for the next hearing. Mr. Wack's counsel declared that no matter what Dorothy Harris might testify to, Mr. Wack would never consent to have the will of Koeppen set aside. The will was drawn by Wack :. few days before the death of Koeppen and Mr. Wack is satisfied with it, notwithstanding the contention of the complainants that he brought undue influence to bear upon the testator.
