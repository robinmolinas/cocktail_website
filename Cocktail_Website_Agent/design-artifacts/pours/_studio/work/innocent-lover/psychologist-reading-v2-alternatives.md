## y4 (the drink paragraph) if the flamed peel stays

The cocktail I've made for you is a close relation of the Manhattan, one of the world's best-known cocktails. It starts from the drink the Brooklyn Club gave as its own in 1945: strong Jamaican rum with Italian vermouth, plus Angostura bitters, just one dash. The club poured equal measures of the two; mine is an aged Jamaican rum, two parts to one of vermouth, so it stays bright. Where Grohusko's had a little French bitter made from orange peel, mine has a spoonful of a dark, bittersweet Italian liqueur that tastes of burnt orange. It's stirred cold, rich and warm from the rum, with a bitter-orange edge, poured into an ice-cold cocktail glass. The last touch is mine too, in front of whoever's there: a wide orange peel, squeezed through a lit match, so its oils flash for an instant and land on top. If the match doesn't take, you strike another. As a nod to you, the Brooklyn that stayed indoors is the one that now announces itself.
