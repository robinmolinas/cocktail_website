## Wren's four questions

1. **"of Friuli in Italy's north-east":** no library page puts Friuli in the north-east (APEROL pdf 126 and *Codex* p. 28 only name the region). It's plain geography any atlas gives, with no claim about anyone in it. **Passes**, labelled "common geography, no library page" in Notes.
2. **"since the 1700s or earlier" for grappa:** **passes, on a better page.** BORTOLO NARDINI (pdf 319): Italy's first grappa house was founded in 1779, and "grappa had been made in Italy previously". The same entry gives the thrift: a farmer squeezing every drop of alcohol from his harvest. Put Nardini pdf 319 in the Notes beside POMACE BRANDY.
3. **"every kind together … buried":** **fails as joined** (fix W3). The burying is POMACE BRANDY's, said of pomace brandies in general (pdf 1548). The mixing is the house's "custom" (H3). Together they become an unsourced grappa tradition. The mixing is already implied by y2's "on their own", so y1 can lose it.
4. **"Before the 1970s, no grappa did":** **fails** (fix W1). The family itself says its 1967 "Aquavitae Optima" was distilled from single-variety pomace (H2), and y2 tells that bottling. The epigraph's absence dies to our own paragraph. "This grappa" also makes the cocktail a grappa. New conflict **C6** on the card: Oxford's "first" (early 1970s) vs the house's 1967 single-variety test. The house itself calls 1967 "the first of a series of tests" and 1973 the first single-varietal grappa, so y2's "the first grappa from a single grape" can stand **if** the 1967 sentence stops calling itself single-grape (W2).
