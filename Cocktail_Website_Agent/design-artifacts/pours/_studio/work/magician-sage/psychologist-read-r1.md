So the Clairvoyant can't be "the one who runs it forward and fears the blind spot" (Omnipotent, Grandmaster), nor "the early warning nobody heeds" (Legend), nor "the one who turns the written-off into something better" (Alchemist). What's left is narrower, and truer to the sheet.

## The one true thing

**They see the part someone or something is going to play, from small things nobody else noticed, long before anyone can see it, and they mostly can't show why.** The sheet's own image is the Gandalf one: sensing that even the creature everyone despises still has a part to play. The Clairvoyant doesn't fix that person (the Alchemist), doesn't heal them (the Healer), doesn't arrange their evening (the Godparent, the Omnipotent One). They *see* the part, early, and keep a place for it. Their advice "sounds mysterious because it rests on things others haven't noticed yet": a pause, a habit, the way someone handled one small thing. Said out loud, the reason sounds like nothing, so they give the conclusion and keep the working.

A scene that's theirs: someone at work everyone has quietly given up on. The Clairvoyant noticed how that person stayed behind once to put something right that nobody would have checked, and from then on talks about them as if their moment is coming. Two years later it comes, and people ask "how did you know?" and the honest answer ("the way they tidied a drawer") sounds too small to say.

## Hide, fear, give

- **What they hide:** that they can rarely say how they know. The calm certainty is real, but it rests on things so small they'd sound silly as evidence, so they give the verdict and keep the reasons. That's why they come across as mysterious, and it's a little lonely.
- **What they fear:** not a blind spot in a plan (that's the Omnipotent One's and the Grandmaster's), but that the thing they saw in someone was only what they hoped to see, and that the person acted on it. "The thing they failed to foresee" lands hardest when it's a person they believed in: raising a hope that didn't come true is their version of the Magician's unintended result, and the Sage half's fear of being fooled, turned on themselves.
- **What they give:** nobody is ever finished in their eyes. People they've quietly kept a place for turn out to matter, and many of them never know who kept it. Robin's rule: say it plainly once in the reading, and say it's worth something.

- **Fairy Godparent:** acts unasked and leaves. The Clairvoyant doesn't act; it sees, and waits.
- **Genie:** hears the wish under the words today. The Clairvoyant sees where someone is heading, not what they meant.
- **Entertainer:** practice for a stranger's wonder. Nothing to perform here.
- **Alchemist:** tries forty ways to make the written-off better. The Clairvoyant changes nothing; it sees the part that was already there.
- **Good Wizard:** saves it and remembers the cost. The Clairvoyant isn't the rescuer.
- **Apprentice:** fears success running away. The Clairvoyant fears having seen wrong.
- **Illusionist:** changes what you see. The Clairvoyant sees first and lets you catch up.
- **Siren:** people change around them. The Clairvoyant notices who will change before they do anything.
- **Trickster:** the plan, the trick, "moves ahead". None of it.
- **Omnipotent One:** holds all of it and watches for the unplanned piece. The Clairvoyant holds nothing; it reads one person.
- **Cousins:** Grandmaster (own move, looks wrong for a while), Legend (warning unheeded), Game-Changer (knowledge at scale), Trend Setter (liked it first), Healer (recovery). None is about *a part still to play*.

## Traps

- Psychic, fortune-teller, mystic, "reads minds"; crystal balls, tarot, runes, stars; "knows what you're thinking" (the Genie's, and invasive).
- Gandalf, Gollum, wizards, Tolkien quotes. The sheet's example is never the mirror and is better left out entirely here, since the Gollum image is the persona's own and quoting it would make the reading a summary of the sheet.
- "Foresaw", "predicted", "knew it would" about anyone in the story: the page never says it. Signpost what they saw.
- Every phrase in the owned-ground table above.
- "I told you so" smugness; being always right. This person is fallible, and the reading must let them be.
- Quality alone: price, "super-premium", hand-blown bottles. The story is about seeing a part early, not about making something posh.
- "Moonshine", "workingman's drink" and "dregs" are the ridicule, quoted as such, never our judgment of anyone.
- A position that says "say it early" (the Legend's) or "tell someone what they haven't heard of yet" (the Doctor's).

## Story test (for this pour)

1. Someone saw early what a person, drink or ingredient would become, from small signs, while others dismissed it.
2. What they chose or noticed is on the record (the signs as choices, if the sight itself isn't stated; then signposted).
3. A wait before the world caught up.
4. Right, but not completely: one thing that didn't go as they'd have seen it, owned gently.
5. Best: something or someone written off. And now, to keep apart from the Alchemist: the part was *already there*, chosen or noticed, not manufactured.

## Verdict on the plan's lead (the Noninos and single-varietal grappa)

**Accept, with conditions.** Tomás builds drink v1 in round 2.

Why it holds, read against the pages and not the plan's shorthand:
- (1, 5) "Until the early 1990s, grappa was much ridiculed outside of Italy" as rough stuff made from the leftovers of winemaking (GRAPPA pdf 936). The change "began in the early 1970s with producers like Giannola Nonino", with "the first super-premium grappa, a single varietal made from the native Friulian grape picolit using only freshly and lightly pressed pomace". POLI (pdf 1544): "In the 1970s, Nonino created the first monovitigno."
- (2, and the line against the Alchemist) What she changed was a choice of what was already there, not a process: one grape, and the leftovers taken fresh. GRAPPA's own sentence, "the best grappa comes from fresh vinaccia, which usually has a higher residual sugar content thanks to light crushing", lets me say she treated what everyone threw away as if it mattered, *at once*. That's seeing the part, not making it better.
- (3) Early 1970s to early 1990s: about twenty years of ridicule abroad after she'd chosen.
- (4) GRAPPA pdf 937: production "has stayed relatively flat", "most consumed in Italy". She was right about what it could be, and the world never drank it the way that implies. Gentle, true, and it carries the fear without drama.
- The mirror for the guest: the leftovers of the press had a part still to play, and someone took them seriously while everyone else laughed. That's the sheet's Gollum, said without Gollum.

Conditions:
- **C1, foresight signposted.** Never "foresaw", "predicted", "knew". Only what she did is fact; what she saw is "I like to think…".
- **C2, credit.** GRAPPA names Giannola Nonino and says she "had inherited the Nonino distillery"; POLI says "Nonino" (the house). "Producers like" means others were there: never alone, never "single-handedly". No family history beyond the pages unless Hester sources it. Hester: if one web visit is spent, spend it on the Noninos' own account of the first single-varietal (year, grape, what they had to ask of the winemakers to get the pomace fresh, and whether anyone mocked it). That is the one thing that would turn "thin" into a story, and it's where "the small signs" may actually be on record.
- **C3, the Alchemist line.** The reading never says grappa was "made better" or "turned into" anything. She chose; the part was already in the press.
- **C4, the Disrupter line.** Different words for what grappa is (not "skins, seeds and stems left once grapes are pressed for wine"); no "single-vineyard", no "out of obscurity", no Piedmont or Nebbiolo, no syrup of the grape's own wine. One grappa beside another pour's is fine (Robin: a category is never taken); a twin motif isn't.
- **C5, quality isn't the point.** No price, no Murano bottles, no "super-premium" in guest text.
- **C6, Amaro Nonino** stays out of guest text (it's in *innocent-jester*'s Paper Plane shape). Dossier note only.
- **C7, a possible tie, for Hester to check:** POLI says Poli and others began selling grappa "with varietal designations": the grape's name on the bottle. If the page or a source confirms the grape was named on Nonino's label, that is the reading's best bridge to the position (below): she didn't ask anyone to believe her, she put the grape's name on it and let them taste. If it isn't sourced, I won't use it.

**The backup (Arroyo)** stays the backup: he "noticed" rum oil while others looked at esters, which is the small sign, but nobody wrote anything off and there's no wait. **Tested and set aside:** Sue Fedroff hiring Phil Ward (*A Proper Drink* pp. 151-152). The page shows kindness and a hire, not a part seen early (Ward "got himself noticed", and Reiner made him bartender), Ward is Hero's lead person, and the "homeless man" line is Ward's joke about Fedroff's patience, which a guest's reading can't carry with dignity.

## Position direction (mine to finish in the reading)

**Show them what you noticed, not just what you see coming.** The small thing, the habit, the stayed-behind evening: hand over the reason, not only the verdict. Then they're not carrying your forecast. They're holding the evidence, and they can see for themselves. What the guest gains: they stop being the only one who can see it, and if they ever saw wrong, the other person can tell why, and nobody was steered by a hope. Kept clear of the Legend ("say it early") and the Doctor ("tell someone what they haven't heard of yet"): this one is about the *reason*, not the timing or the news.
