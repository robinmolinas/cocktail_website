**Two strikes first, before anyone builds on them.** (1) Your "she treated what everyone threw away" is false. Pomace has been distilled since at least the 1700s, "a product of thrift" (pdf 1548), so nobody threw it away. The truer contrast helps you: by tradition the pomace was buried until the wine was made and only then distilled, all of it together (pdf 1548; the house says white and red pomace were mixed). She took one grape, and took it fresh. Nothing was rescued; she just looked at it differently. That suits your "changes nothing". (2) Oxford says Giannola "had inherited" the distillery, but the house says it came down Benito's line and she married into it in 1962. That's conflict C1, so never "inherited". Credit "Giannola and Benito Nonino", or "Giannola Nonino and the house". Also, "first" means the first single-varietal *grappa* only, because France has a single-grape marc (pdf 1231).

**C2, their own account.** She chose where to look, in her own words: she and Benito "selected the best Friulian winemakers" for their picolit pomace. There was one doubter on record, Isi Benini, "initially 'skeptical'" and later a supporter (her speech, quoted by the house). Nobody else is recorded as mocking it, so never "everyone laughed". The "twenty years" was grappa's ridicule *outside Italy* (pdf 936), not a wait for her own success: "nearly twenty years" at most, and never "nobody believed her".

**C7, the label: no.** In POLI, the clause about selling grappa labelled with its grape belongs to "Poli and others" who followed. No page I read shows the 1973 label naming picolit. There's a better bridge for your position, though. In 1967 their single-variety test bottling, "Aquavitae Optima", printed the year, the amount distilled, the litres and the bottle's number on the label, the first of the trials that led to 1973 (the family's account). They showed their working on the bottle, not just the verdict.

**One for you to rule on.** The family says that in 1975 they set up a prize to save old Friulian vines (schioppettino, pignolo, tazzelenghe) that were dying out because growing them was forbidden. Their own stated reason was that they wanted to distil those grapes. That's your "nobody is ever finished in their eyes", with the self-interest told openly. It rests only on the house's account, so it's "the family says" or the dossier. Your call.
