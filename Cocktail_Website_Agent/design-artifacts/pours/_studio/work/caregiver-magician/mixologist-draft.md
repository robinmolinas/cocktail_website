## Recipe

- **serves:** 1 (the syrup makes about 400 ml, enough for many drinks)
- **glassware:** a tumbler (about 300 ml), filled with ice
- **contains:** `[]`

| amount | item | note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 45 ml | Bigallet China-China Amer (*recommended*), or any bittersweet French orange amer of 30% or stronger | a modern amer made with the old Amer Picon recipe in mind: bitter orange and bitter roots |
| 1 tsp (5 ml) | pomegranate and orange syrup, homemade (below) | the sweet orange peel sits here, beside the bitter one |
| 120 ml | soda water, very cold | |
| 15 ml | cognac | poured last, so it rests on top |
| 1 | strip of orange peel | |
| | *for the syrup:* 250 g pomegranate juice (unsweetened, from a bottle is fine), 250 g sugar, the peel of 1 sweet orange | adapted from the *Cocktail Codex*'s house grenadine |

## Method
1. Make the syrup ahead: an hour before, or the day before. Peel one orange thinly, just the coloured part, and put the peel in a jar with the sugar. Rub the peel into the sugar with a spoon for a minute, until the sugar smells of orange.
2. Pour in the pomegranate juice and stir, or shake the closed jar, until the sugar has dissolved. No heat. Leave it for an hour, then take out the peel. It keeps in the fridge for about two weeks.
3. When it's time, fill the tumbler with ice. Pour in the amer and the teaspoon of syrup and stir a few times so they're cold.
4. Add the cold soda and give it one gentle lift from the bottom with a spoon, so the syrup doesn't stay at the bottom.
5. Hold a spoon upside down just above the drink and pour the cognac slowly over the back of it, so it spreads across the top instead of sinking.
6. Twist the orange peel over the glass so its oils fall on the drink, then drop it in.

(Method note: the first sip is the cognac, soft and warm. The bitter orange arrives underneath it, and it stays. Nothing covers it.)

## Names (≥3; bartender-sayable)
- **Till Spring** (**the pick: Wren's, and I back it**). The name resonates with the person, not the drink (rulebook). It's whoYouAre's scene ("give it till spring"), the belief held for someone until it comes true. It's easy to say across a bar ("a Till Spring, please"), and it makes no claim about the drink healing anything. My one hesitation: it could read as seasonal, and the drink isn't. It's still the better name.
- **Easy to Take** (my round-4 pick). It's the person's gift in Wren's own words ("you make yours easy to take"), and it's the drink, since the gentle float comes first. Said across a bar, "an Easy to Take, please" is warm and a little odd, and it invites the question. **Risk:** if the tagline or whoYouAre already uses "easy to take", the title block would spend it twice, and then I'd move to *Sweet Peel*.
- **Sweet Peel.** Picon's formula ("bitter and sweet orange peels") and the drink's spark in two words. It stays tender without claiming anything.
- **Side by Side.** The bitter and the sweet sitting together, neither one hiding the other, which is Wren's brief. It's also how the Healer stays with someone. A little generic.
- **Philippeville.** Where Picon's first small distillery stood (Oxford pdf 1509). It's a real place a guest can discover, but it's now Skikda, and the colonial name needs care. For the record only.
- **Rejected:** *A Spoonful* (it calls up "a spoonful of sugar helps the medicine go down", which is a health claim by association); anything with "cure", "tonic", "remedy" or "better" (health rule); anything with "kind" or "kindness" (*The Other Kindness* is a sibling).
- **Epigraph candidates** (about the drink, must make sense cold):
  - *"Its maker set out to make a bitter drink taste better. He kept the bitter."* ("set out" is Hester's "tried"; "kept the bitter" is the formula's bitter peel and gentian, pdf 1509. **Hester to audit.**)
  - *"The first thing you taste is the gentle part. The bitter comes after, and it stays."*
