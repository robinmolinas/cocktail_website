## Legends, inferences and fences
- **"Frenchman":** he was born in Genoa while it was under French control, and raised in Marseille (pdf 1509). Say "from Marseille", never "a Frenchman" or "an Italian".
- **Motive:** nothing on record in his own words. No "to spare others", and no "he made it kinder" as fact. His *aim* was a better taste (Oxford); the reading may say he tried, and that people drank it in quantity.
- **Picon Punch origin date and place:** a search summary claimed a San Francisco boardinghouse, c. 1899. I couldn't open the page (Saveur 403), so it stays out. Only the Smithsonian page's claim (Basque American West, travelled east) may be used, labelled secondary.
- **Quinine's discovery legend** (the Countess of Chinchón): not in the library. Never told.
- **The tonic's inventor:** Oxford names two different 1858 patentees (pdf 2043 vs pdf 898). Stays out.
- **Health:** no "tonic", "cure" or "remedy" as the drink's effect. They appear only as the history.

## Round 4 notes
- **The spark has a lineage too:** grenadine (a pomegranate syrup) is in the Picon Punch (Rosemann 2016, secondary), so the pomegranate is part of how the amer was drunk as well as the Matrix pairing. ## Round 5: anchors corrected with audit v1 (these supersede the rows above where they differ)
- **lineage (Picon Punch), corrected:** this is **one of the ways** the amer was drunk (the others: with beer in France, by the spoonful in the Brooklyn). Method as on the Smithsonian page: over ice in an old-fashioned glass, amer, grenadine, soda, then "float brandy and lemon peel on top" (Rosemann 2016, secondary). The brandy goes on **top**, never "underneath".
- **ingredient (the formula), for the reading:** "bitter and sweet orange peel, with bitter roots and a bark" (gentian and rhubarb are roots, quinquina is a bark; pdf 1509). Don't name gentian, cinchona or quinine again: the sibling *The Other Kindness* owns them. Quinine gets one clause only (the fever treatment).
- **story (motive):** "so people would come back for it on their own" is ONLY interpretation, inside "I like to think". The sourced fact is the pairing of bitter and sweet peels, and his aim of a better taste (Oxford).
- **ingredient (today's bottle):** Oxford says 18% since 1989; *LI* p. 136 lists 15.0%. The sources conflict, so no figure goes in the reading. China-China is "crafted with the classic Amer Picon recipe in mind" (*Codex* p. 87): "the old Amer Picon", never "his recipe".
- **treatment:** "the usual way then" / "as was traditional" (Oxford), never "everyone".

## Round 6: anchor additions
- **epigraph label:** "bitter orange" = the taste of the amer (*Codex* p. 260: bittersweet, "bright orange quality"). That China-China's recipe uses Seville orange is not sourced (brand copy only).
- **proportion:** the amer is 45 of 185 ml (about 24%): "the heart of the glass", never "most of the glass".
