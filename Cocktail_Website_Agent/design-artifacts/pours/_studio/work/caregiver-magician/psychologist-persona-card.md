# Persona card: The Healer (caregiver-magician)

Wren, round 1. Sources: persona.py (both sheets), the sibling list, BOND voice ledger, MEMORY (Caregiver family notes).

## Core truth
The Healer believes someone will be all right *before that person does*, and holds that belief for them until they can hold it themselves. People leave them lighter and can't say what was done. The Healer knows: nothing flashy, just attention, and refusing to give up on them.

## Shows / hides
- **Shows:** calm. A hand on the shoulder, the right word, the tea made before anyone asked. People feel better near them, and call it a gift or a knack.
- **Hides:** that it isn't magic, it's work: watching closely, remembering, guessing what hurts. And that the ones who *didn't* get better stay with them. They go over what they missed.

## Hidden fear → hidden gift
- **Fear (Caregiver: selfishness; Magician: unintended results, insignificance):** being useless in front of someone's pain. "If I can't make this better, what am I for?" Under that, a quieter one: that wanting to be the one who helps is a little bit for themselves.
- **Gift:** hope held on someone's behalf. They don't just fix; they keep believing for the person while the person can't. That belief *is* most of the medicine, and they rarely count it.

## The "real me" line to aim for
Draft: *"You believe someone will be all right before they do. And you hold on to that for them, for as long as it takes."*
Second, the hidden part: *"The ones who didn't get better, you still carry. You go back over what you might have missed."*

## Truth vs. legend
The true story, told gently. They want to know how a thing really works (the Magician), but kindly, not coldly. A legend is fine only if owned as a legend; they'd distrust a miracle sold as fact.

## Tone
Tender and quiet, with a little wonder in it (the Magician's "how did you do that?"). Not clinical, not mystical. No jokes at the pain; a soft smile is allowed.

## Distinct from siblings
- **Unlike the Rescuer (hero):** no dramatic save at the edge. The Healer's work is slow, after the crisis, while things mend.
- **Unlike the Doctor (sage):** not the diagnosis or the answer. The Doctor knows what's wrong; the Healer knows what will help *this* person feel better, and believes they will.
- **Unlike the Nurse (regular guy):** not the steady everyday shift of care. The Healer's mark is transformation: people come out different.
- **Unlike the Craftsman (draft):** no pride in method. The Healer hides the method on purpose, so it feels like it simply happened.
- Keep an eye on the Reassurer (humour eases pain) and the Understanding Spouse (one person, closeness): the Healer's care is for whoever is hurting in front of them, and it's about recovery, not company.

## Traps
- **Health claims.** Never let the drink, or any line, suggest alcohol heals, soothes or is good for you. A remedy story stays history, clearly past tense.
- **"Remedy" alone isn't distinctive.** Warm remedies belong to the whole Caregiver family (MEMORY). The Healer's story needs *transformation* and *hope held for someone*, not just "it was once medicine". The Doctor and the Nurse will want medicine too.
- **Martyrdom and horoscope:** "you give too much of yourself" fits every Caregiver. Cut it.
- **Diagnosing the guest** (ironic for this one): no "wounded healer" claim that they were hurt once; many weren't, and it would misread them.
- **The imagery's crystals and mystical landscapes, E.T.:** not in the reading. Keep it human.
- **Sibling motifs:** Angostura as a base is Off-Label's; green Chartreuse in the absinthe slot is Beside the First's; "What nobody sees…", "You're allowed to…", "What you can't stand…", "What you give people…", "Go back to it" frames are taken.

## Position (Ryu), to earn
Not every mending needs your hands on it. Something like: let one thing get better without you, or let someone sit with you when you're the one who's tired. Candidate, not final.

## What the story should mirror (for Hester)
A remedy, ingredient or person where **something bitter or hard was turned into something people wanted**, or where the cure worked slowly and someone kept faith in it. Ingredient histories count (Robin 2026-09-26). The mirror must carry change and patience, not only "it was medicine".

## Round 2: the story sharpens the card (Picon)
- **Accepted: Picon, as the story's mirror**, but not as "the healer was the patient first". Said of the guest, that is a diagnosis (the wounded-healer trap above). The mirror is **knowing that help can taste bitter, and making it kinder**. Being looked after can feel like being told you're weak, so the Healer's help arrives as tea, a walk, a joke, and nobody feels treated.
- **Real-me line, v2:** *"You know help can taste bitter. So you make yours easy to take, and most people never notice they were being looked after."*, kept alongside the hope line (believing they'll be all right before they do).
- **Distinct:** the Craftsman hides pride in the method; the Healer hides the *medicine* inside the comfort. The Reassurer uses humour; the Healer uses whatever will go down.
- **Army:** leave it out ("a young Frenchman in Algeria in the 1830s"). If it's named, colonisation needs a plain clause, which would take over a tender reading. Half-naming it is worse than either.
- **Runner-up (Penicillin):** that one is about care someone else notices (Lucinda). It suits the Nurse or the Craftsman better. It isn't this one's truth.

## Round 3: Lucinda vs Picon (my ruling: Picon holds)
- Lucinda believed in a **drink**. The Healer believes in a **person who is hurting**. Swap test: "championed it before its maker did" fits the Campaigner (caregiver-outlaw) better than this one.
- The drink's name, Penicillin, next to "the Healer", makes the cure joke for us even if no line does. That's a health claim by association, and every guest will hear it.
- Picon is the Healer's own method: the cure already existed, and he made it kinder to take. The part that fits this person is the kindness in how the help reaches someone, not the fact that it was once medicine.
- What would change my mind: a Picon fact that only works as "it was medicine". It hasn't come up.

## whoYouAre, direction draft (v0, not for the assembler)
Someone you love has a bad week, and you don't ask what's wrong. You turn up with a reason to go for a walk, or you get them laughing about something else entirely. By the end of the evening they feel lighter, and they couldn't tell you why. You could. You were paying attention the whole time.

You know help can taste bitter. Being looked after can feel like being told you're not coping, so you make your help easy to take, and most people never notice they were being looked after. What you keep to yourself is how much you believe in them while they can't. You decide they'll be all right before they do, and you hold on to that until they catch up.
