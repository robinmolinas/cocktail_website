# Wren's candidates and offers (round 5): not for the assembler

**name** — pick: Till Spring (the belief they hold for someone until it comes true). Acceptable common ground: Sweet Peel (Tomás's second: the sweet beside the bitter, which is the person's gift and the formula). Also: Side by Side, Three Million Oranges. **Against Easy to Take:** it's pill language ("easy to take" is what you say about a tablet). On a drink sitting under "The Healer", it makes the medicine wink I argued against with the Penicillin. In whoYouAre it's about help. On the label it's about the glass.

**tagline** — pick: You believe they'll be all right before they do. Others: People get better around you, and never quite know when it started. · Your help goes down easy. That's the whole trick.

**epigraph** — pick: *Bitter orange and sweet orange, side by side. Neither one is hiding the other.* True of v2 as specced (amer + sweet-orange syrup). Set aside: Tomás's "Its maker set out… He kept the bitter." ("its maker" reads as this glass's maker, but the amer is China-China, not Picon's; the bare "He" is read cold; and "kept" is an intent claim). Set aside my own alt "drunk long… a long way from home" ("its bitter orange liqueur" isn't China-China either).

**closing line** (Tomás's to rule on) — his *"Pour the brandy last, over a spoon. Let the first taste be the gentle one."* is true every time and carries the whoYouAre gift. The rule is that the closing line agrees with y5's position (put down what isn't yours to finish). A merge that carries both: *"Float the brandy last, over a spoon. Give the gentle part. The rest was never yours to finish."*
