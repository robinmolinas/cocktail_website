# Persona card: The Pioneer (explorer-hero)

Wren, round 1, 2026-09-30. Read from `persona.py explorer-hero`, my family sketch (`plans/work/explorer/psychologist-sketches.md`), the Explorer plan row, the three Explorer pours so far (*Left Standing*, *For Good*, *Brought Home*), the Hero-secondary pours (*Off Duty*, *Down the Line*, *Next One's Mine*), and the pages behind the lead (Oxford BLANTON'S pdf 283, SINGLE BARREL pdf 1806, WHISKY, BOURBON pdf 2143, BONIFICATEUR/Noe pdf 313), all opened this session.

## Core truth
They're frightened every time, and nobody has ever been able to tell. They go first anyway, because the regret of not going is the only fear bigger than the fear itself. And they spend years getting ready, where nobody can see, so that when it's finally in front of everyone it can be done steadily.

## Shows / hides
- **Shows:** steadiness; the prepared one; understatement afterwards ("it went fine", "nothing to it").
- **Hides:** the fear, before (not after: after is the Rescuer's). The years of practice nobody saw. And a quieter thing they'd never say: they *want* it seen. It only counts as proof if someone watched. Then they shrug it off, and the shrug isn't modesty. It's for the next person: if it looks possible, someone else will try.

## Hidden fear -> hidden gift
- **Fear:** the day they could have gone and didn't: their own "no", not other people's. Behind it, the secondary's fear: being seen to flinch.
- **Gift:** proof. Once they've done it in front of people, other people know it can be done. The kindness to say out loud once (Robin's rule): the ones who go after you go partly because of you.

## The "real me" line to aim for
*"You're scared every time. Nobody's ever been able to tell."*
Its gift half: *"And because nobody could tell, someone behind you decided they could do it too."*

## Truth vs legend
The true story, plainly. "The first" is the thing they value most, so a false first would be a lie about the very thing they are. Grant legends, never lean on them.

## Tone
Direct, spirited, a little dry. The Hero's tone line ("motivational, empowering") is the trap: no pep talk, no poster. An understated reading for an understated person: we honour them by not making a fuss.

## Distinct from siblings (and near neighbours)
- **The Rescuer (*Off Duty*):** moves in a second, for someone in danger; the shaking comes *after*; owns "You don't feel brave. You feel clear", "already moving", "Nobody sees the part that comes later". The Pioneer's moment was prepared for years, saves no one, and the fear comes *before*.
- **The Visionary (*Down the Line*):** hides doubt because others are building on their belief; owns "What frightens you isn't… It's…", "see the finished thing first", handing it on. The Pioneer hides fear for themself, and stands on the new ground in person.
- **The Angel (*Before the Room*):** owns calm as something carried in for others ("People think calm just comes to you"). So the Pioneer's reading never leads on the word *calm*.
- **The Geisha (*Hoping You'd Come*):** owns *ready* as preparation that might go unused ("the ready room with nobody in it"). The Pioneer's cage is their own "no", never wasted preparation, so the key frame is the day you didn't go, not "all that getting ready".
- **The Tourist (*Brought Home*):** can't wait to tell everyone. The Pioneer says as little as possible afterwards.
- **The Trailblazer (unauthored):** no permission, irreverent. The Pioneer is sanctioned, disciplined, and accepts being watched.
- **The Expedition Leader (unauthored):** brings everyone home. The Pioneer carries only the first step.
- **The Saviour (*Next One's Mine*):** owes no one; owns "feels like weakness".

## Traps
- "Fearless", "brave" (the Rescuer's), "calm" as the frame (the Angel's), "ready" as the frame (the Geisha's), "already" moving (the Rescuer's).
- Moon, astronaut, "small step", "giant leap", Armstrong (the sheet's own; the guest may just have read it). Summits, flags.
- Bravado, pep talk, motivational poster. War (Lee's wartime service stays out).
- Oxford's word for Lee is "pioneered" (SINGLE BARREL pdf 1806): never quote it, it winks at the guest's own name. Oxford calls his generation "visionary distillers" (pdf 313): a sibling's name, never.
- Registry phrases: "nothing to hide behind" (*Half a Rim* y-text), "don't go alone. Take someone" (*Left Standing* y5), "You're allowed to…" (*Four Shares*), "What frightens you isn't…" (*Down the Line*), "What you keep quiet is how it starts" (*For Good*), "Truth is" / "Softer still" (*Brought Home*).
- Quality-only ("the best bourbon"; "premium was the way back"). Secrets (Booker Noe's "clandestine" project stays out).
- Gender: Armstrong and Lee are men; second person only. Profession labels: pilot, climber, distiller, master anything.

## Story test
A **first, solid on the page**, done **where failure would be seen** (in public, named, judged). It must show **the preparation beforehand**, **the doing, steady**, **the fear** (on record, or one signposted sentence), and ideally **understatement on record** afterwards. What must hold: **it worked, and others followed because it had been shown possible**. The story is about someone *going*, not about a product being good. Out: luck, defiance, rescue, a group brought home, a secret.

## Verdict on the plan's lead (Elmer T. Lee, Blanton's)
**Accept, with the mirror moved.** As the plan tells it, the lead passes on the skeleton (the first is Oxford's sentence twice; decades under Albert Blanton; a category followed) and fails on the heart: nobody is watching, and the fear, the steadiness and the understatement are all ours. The pages give the heart back, through the barrel itself:
- Distillers "dump" many barrels together to ensure consistency; a single barrel carries all the variations of one (SINGLE BARREL pdf 1806). That's going out alone, where everyone can taste exactly how it turned out, with its number on the label.
- Blanton's habit of bottling one fine barrel for himself or as gifts to insiders was "not uncommon among distillery executives of his era" (BLANTON'S pdf 283). So it had been done for decades, in private, among friends. Lee's first was doing it on a shelf, in front of everyone.
- That is the Pioneer exactly: the years where nobody sees, then the one time everyone does.

**Conditions:**
1. The recognition rests on *private for years, then in front of everyone*: never on quality, never on "ready" as a word (the Geisha's), never on fear as the anchor (one signposted sentence at most).
2. No "alone against the slump": Oxford says it arrived as "market watchers began noticing an uptick" in niche bottles (pdf 283), and Booker Noe was working on his own project at the same time (pdf 2143). Say "the first single-barrel brand", and "one of the first to see it" if the market comes in at all.
3. Hester: find Lee's own words. The obituaries Oxford cites (Vitello, *NYT*, 21 July 2013; *Business Lexington*, 16 July 2013) may hold an understated line, and the test wants one. Also check whether the risk (every barrel different, so every bottle judged alone) is stated anywhere, and how Blanton's labels are marked (the barrel number by hand is generic in Oxford).
4. Hester: my memory (unsourced) says a single-barrel bourbon was later sold under Lee's own name. If so, the plan's "the brand carried his old boss's name, not his" is false as a whole, and it goes.
5. One maker isn't the maker: Falk and Baranaskas bought the distillery and started the business; Lee "guided the creation". One clause.
6. Registry neighbours: the Magician plan's bottled-in-bond bourbon Old-Fashioned (Taylor) and the Trailblazer's bacon-washed one; own it in a clause if both ship.

**For Tomás (person only, no spec):** the barrel must go out alone. No split base, no second whiskey, nothing that blends it away, since blending is exactly what the story refuses. Any gesture must be true of whichever single barrel the guest buys (they all differ). A spark welcome if it makes the barrel louder, not covered.

## Position direction (revised from my sketch)
My sketch said "tell one person you were scared, you won't be so alone behind it". That's too close to *Left Standing*'s y5 ("don't go alone. Take someone"). New direction: **the steadiness got you there; the fear is the part the next person needs.** When someone asks how it was, tell them it was frightening, and that you went. Your calm told them it could be done. Your fear tells them they don't have to be fearless to do it. Gain for the guest: you stop having to pretend it was nothing.
