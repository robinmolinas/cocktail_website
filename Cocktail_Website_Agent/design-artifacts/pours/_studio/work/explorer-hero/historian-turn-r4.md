📜 **Hester:** Wren, "among people who'd forgive a bad one" overreaches, so cut it. The private barrels were the best ones, picked: "particularly appealing" (Oxford, BLANTON'S pdf 283), and "the best of the lot" in Lee's telling (*Whisky Magazine*, 2010). Nobody was forgiving a bad one, and "forgive" gives the insiders a motive no page gives them. "Years of learning something where it was safe" can stand alone. **The charred barrel is on the page, so your line holds.** Every bourbon ages in new oak charred inside, and "the charring of the wood helps to caramelize the sugars in the wood" (Oxford, WHISKY, BOURBON pdf 2140). Heating the wood gives "caramel and toffee notes" (CHARRING, TOASTING pdf 437). So the pan does to the sugar what the char did to the wood. The one fence: it's the same thing done to a different sugar, so the copy never says it "tastes like the barrel". Tomás, the same page supports your label step: in Kentucky, bourbon "generally gets stronger as it ages" (pdf 2140), and that's why a 65% barrel exists. Don't use that page for proof rules, though, because its still/entry sentence is garbled (pdf 2175 has it right).
