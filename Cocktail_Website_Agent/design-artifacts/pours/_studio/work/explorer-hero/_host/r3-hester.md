📜 **Hester:** Tomás, both your asks, and one finding that answers Wren's label question from last round. **Corn:** bourbon must by US regulation be "at least 51 percent corn (most have substantially more)" (Oxford, CORN pdf 583), under thresholds set in 1938 (MASH BILL pdf 1255). So "mostly corn" is true of every bourbon, and your Matrix pairing stands on the grain. Blanton's own mash bill isn't published, so it's never "most of the flavour is corn". I opened your pages too: caramel lists bourbon as a substitute and warns that burnt sugar goes "bitter, acrid" (Matrix pdf 72); corn lists caramel as a best pairing (pdf 88); and demerara "deepens the richness already found in the spirit" (Codex p. 12). They're all good. One fence: the Matrix says caramel's flavour comes "largely from Maillard reactions", and that's the book's chemistry, not ours to repeat, so no cause goes in the copy. **Strength:** the standard Blanton's is 93 proof, 46.5% (Breaking Bourbon, 2021; a named enthusiast review site, secondary, the weakest tier I'll use). Its cask-strength Straight From the Barrel "varies by bottle depending on the barrel". It goes into the cask at 62.5%, and the bottle reviewed was 65% (Breaking Bourbon, Jordan Moskal, 2016). So your "past 60%" holds, 65% is a sample and not a ceiling, and I'd sweep at least to 65%. **Labels, Wren:** the reviewers copied two real Blanton's labels. Each gives the dump date, barrel number, "Warehouse H", rick number and bottle number. "Handwritten" still isn't on a page I trust, so the reading can say the label names the barrel and the warehouse it aged in, and nothing about whose hand wrote it. **The fence on "every barrel is different" gets firmer:** the same 2021 reviewer says Blanton's manages to "replicate its pour so closely from one barrel to another", which fits Oxford's "specific flavor profile" (pdf 283). So the guest's barrel may not taste very different, and the gesture can't promise it will. "Read the label before you stir" is sound for any single barrel at cask strength or under another brand, but for Blanton's standard bottle the number never changes. And Codex p. 12 is on your side for the sweep: a heavily aged whiskey tastes sweeter, so start at about half a teaspoon of syrup. Card updated (F19–F24), with an ingredient row added to the anchors.
