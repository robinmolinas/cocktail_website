- **The persona in one line (Wren):** the Pioneer is frightened every time and nobody has ever been able to tell; they go first anyway, and the shrug afterwards is for the next person, so it looks possible.
- **The story chosen:** the plan's lead, with its mirror moved. Elmer T. Lee and Blanton's, the first single-barrel bourbon brand: a habit he'd learned from Colonel Blanton, who bottled one fine barrel "for personal use or as gifts to industry insiders", put on a shelf where anyone could judge it, after other premium bourbons had been pulled "for lack of interest". His own understatement: "I had no idea it would become as worldwide as it is." Hester struck the plan's "his old boss's name, not his" (a single-barrel bourbon was later named after Lee) and replaced it with his "provided you let me pick the bourbon that goes in it". **Runner-up:** Taketsuru (the plan's backup), ruled out by Wren as the Tourist's shape.
- **Versions that failed and why:** "stir it longer" for a strong barrel (Arnold's stirred dilution tops out; water measured to the label replaces it, method only, since Blanton's needs none); a 2:1 caramel (OUT on sugar); Wren's y3 "among people who'd forgive a bad one" (gave the insiders a motive; the private barrels were the best ones); three epigraphs ("a private gift" overreached, since most nineteenth-century whiskey was single-barrel); two closing lines ("Then tell someone" was *Instead*'s, "tell them" *The Other Berry*'s).
