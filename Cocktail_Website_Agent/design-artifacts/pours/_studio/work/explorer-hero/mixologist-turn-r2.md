🍸 **Tomás:** Wren, I'll take "the barrel goes out alone" as my brief. It's physics I like: one bourbon and nothing to blend it with, so the frame is the Old-Fashioned, the family the *Codex* builds with the focus "squarely on the spirit" (p. 6). No split base, no vermouth, no second whiskey. Three leads from the drink side, and no spec until you and Hester have ruled. **(1) The serve carries your mirror.** Stirred cold, strained into a small chilled tumbler, no ice. Nothing melts after the pour, so the last sip is the same barrel as the first. That's the plan's shape and I'd keep it. **(2) The spark: cook the sugar instead of just stirring it in.** An amber caramel syrup (sugar heated in a pan until it turns colour, then loosened with water) replaces the plain syrup. The *Flavor Matrix* backs it twice: bourbon is the last of caramel's listed substitutes (pdf 72), and corn lists caramel among its best pairings (pdf 88). Hester, "bourbon is mostly corn" needs Oxford's line. It brings in no new flavour to cover the barrel. It pulls the toasted side forward, the same job the *Codex* gives demerara, to deepen "the richness already found in the spirit" (p. 12). So whichever barrel the guest buys, that barrel gets louder. Two edges I'll own. Cooked too dark, caramel goes bitter (pdf 72 says so), and `balance.py` can't hear bitterness, so the method stops at amber. It also sits near the Trailblazer's maple, which the Matrix lists as a caramel substitute too, so if both ship, the rooms say in a clause how the syrups differ. From the same pages I set aside vanilla (corn's surprise pairing, pdf 88; the barrel already gives it, so adding more would cover the barrel, not show it), apple (*Hoping You'd Come*'s roasted apples), coconut (nuts veto) and red wine (it colours the barrel). **(3) A gesture that holds for any single barrel: read the label before you stir.** Single barrels come at different strengths. Blanton's standard bottling is around 46.5% and cask-strength single barrels run past 60% (my knowledge, unsourced; Hester, please check). With no ice after the pour, a stronger barrel needs more stirring or a spoon of water. So the guest's first move is to see what this one barrel is, and prepare for it. Once the story is ruled I'll sweep sugar and stirring across 45-65% and write the rule from the sweep. What I won't do: a make-ahead or freezer batch, however good the physics, because "Make it a day ahead" is *For Good*'s closing line and *Overnight* owns leaving it; and nothing that changes as you drink it, which is *No Accident*'s. On the neighbours: *No Accident* (flute, unstirred cube), the Magician plan's bottled-in-bond one (cubed ice, no brand) and the Trailblazer's (bacon, maple, one large cube) are all bourbon Old-Fashioneds. This one alone is served up, with no ice and a cooked sugar. Veto-free on the plan; I'll confirm the allergens when the caramel syrup goes into the table.
