# Wren: persona card, The Conservationist (explorer-caregiver), round 1

Read fresh from `persona.py explorer-caregiver`, my Explorer family sketch (`_studio/plans/work/explorer/psychologist-sketches.md`) and the family plan's row. Archetype names never reach the guest.

## The sheet, in one breath
Goes out to see the world as it really is, close to wild things, never wanting to own them. Freedom with a duty attached: whatever they find, they feel responsible for. Travels light, leaves no trace, notices what's disappearing before anyone else. Haunted by coming back one day to find the place they loved is gone. Goal freedom, fear entrapment (family-wide, so unusable); secondary fears selfishness, indifference.

## The one true thing (no sibling shares it)
**They love things they refuse to own, and they start missing them while they're still here.** Their care takes the form of *not taking*: no souvenir, no claim, sometimes not even a photo. What only they carry is grief in advance: they see what's thinning out while everyone else is still enjoying it.

Their cage word (family table): not a cage but a loss, **gone**. Note: "You've already gone" is *Off Duty*'s tagline, so "gone" is the reading's key idea but never the tagline's word.

## Hide / fear / give
- **Hide:** that it hurts. It looks like principle ("don't pick that", "leave it where it is"), but it's closer to mourning, and they'd rather be thought strict than sad.
- **Fear:** coming back and it's gone. Underneath: being part of the reason it went (their own footprints on it). That's where the secondary fear, selfishness, lives.
- **Give:** they make other people *see* a thing while it's still there, and they hand places back whole to the people who come after. Robin's rule, "say the kindness out loud": the reading must state plainly that what they leave untouched is why there's still something for others to love.

## The scene I'd open with (a direction, not copy)
Going back to a place you loved, and bracing before you've even arrived: what's changed, what's been built, what's missing. Everyone else sees the view. You're counting what's left.

## Traps
- **The lecture.** Climate guilt, politics, "the future depends on all of us" (Oxford's own tone on agave). The guest is recognised, never told what to buy. The recommended bottle can sit in the recipe; the reading never argues for it.
- **"Saved the bat."** The delisting had many causes.
- **Doom.** Extinction as the point, grief with nothing holding. The story has to end with something still alive.
- **Morbidity.** "Flowers once and then dies" is the plant's one great night, not a death scene.
- **Attenborough** (the sheet's own example): no name, no voice pastiche. "Leave only footprints" (cliché). "Someone had to say it" (the Campaigner's).
- **Registry echoes:** "leave it" (*Overnight*), "stop cutting" (*Half a Rim*, also tequila), "keep one back" (*Four Shares*), "people you'll never meet" (*Serviceable*), "go back to it" (*Off-Label*), "gone" in a tagline (*Off Duty*).
- **Let the drink sound worth having.** The flowers "smelling like rotting fruit" (Oxford) is a lovely fact for the story, and must stay well away from the drink's description.
- One travel word at most, never in the epigraph.

## Story test
The story's person (or an ingredient's keepers) found something precious and **chose not to own, enclose or exploit it**, or kept it going for others. They **faced the pull to take it** (profit, credit, the easy harvest) and **it cost them**. **The thing survives**, ideally still there today. Out: rescue-from-the-brink heroics, a secret, the sea, medicine.

## Verdict on the family plan's lead (the agave left to flower for the bats)
**Accept, conditionally.** It's the right shape: restraint, not rescue; the pull is the harvest itself; the thing is still alive.

What I checked in the library myself (Oxford, AGAVE):
- On the page (pdf 74): each agave flowers once and then dies; a flowering stalk grows up to 20 cm a day, 2–10 m tall; nectar comes at night; mostly pollinated by bats.
- On the page (pdf 77): as tequila grew, "producers ceased using reproduction through the inflorescence", which removed a primary food of *Leptonycteris* bats and shifted their migratory paths, and narrowed the crop's genetic diversity.
- **Not on any library page:** a named grower who lets agaves flower, and the cost (why a flowered plant is lost to the still). The library never says why producers stopped.

Conditions for round 2:
1. **One person who actually chose not to take.** My preferred person is David Suro-Piñera, if a page shows his own fields let agaves flower: then one person carries the whole test (co-wrote the proposal, and gave up part of their own crop). *My knowledge, unsourced: he also runs a tequila company.* Hester to confirm or kill. Medellín, who noticed the bats going, gets one clause at most, or the reading splits in two.
2. **The cost found stated on a page** (the flowering stalk uses up what the distiller wants; a flowered plant can't be harvested). No inference.
3. **The recognition mirror, signposted:** Oxford's own sentence that the bats' migration paths shifted gives the persona's fear to the bats: they came back and the flowers were gone. Leaving some to flower makes it a place worth coming back to. That's an "I like to think" line, never a fact about any one field.
4. No "5%" unless it's the paper's figure said as a proposal; no "saved"; no villages or mezcal.

**If 1 or 2 fails:** the room writes Open. The runner-up with the most literal mirror is the Cape Royds whisky (conservators found it, studied three bottles, then put them back in the hut), but it's the Leader row's drink tie, its cost is thin, and nobody can go and see it. ## Position direction (for the reading's ending)
You don't have to carry every loss by yourself. Take someone to see it while it's still there, so there's one more person who'd notice. Ends on what they gain: company in the watching.
