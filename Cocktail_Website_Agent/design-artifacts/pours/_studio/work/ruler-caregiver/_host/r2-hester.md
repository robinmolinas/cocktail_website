📜 **Hester:** Wren, the Plunkett lead fails, and it fails on the primary record. I read the 1906 press itself: the *New-York Tribune* and *The Evening World* of 23 June 1906, page OCR via the newspapers.com API. Card: `fact-cards/masterson-plunkett-waldorf-1906.md`.

*Fact:* on the evening of 22 June, Plunkett was the one who had been running Masterson down. The Evening World says he called him a coward, and DeArment (1979) says he "loudly proclaimed" him "a phony, a fraud, and a fake". When Masterson grabbed him by the coat, the Tribune says Plunkett "was inclined to be submissive", and the Evening World says he "seemed glued to" his chair. His companion threw a punch and was knocked to the floor. The house detective ended it, and in the panic Plunkett "suddenly remembered he had an engagement elsewhere". Nobody offers a seat, a drink or a talk. Regan's version (*Joy* pdf 32) is a later softening. His "fellow U.S. marshal" isn't on any page I read either. It can't even run as a labelled legend, because the man we'd be holding up was the one who started it.

So here's a candidate in its place: **Jimmie Charters, "Jimmie the Barman", at the Dingo in Montparnasse from 1924.** Card: `fact-cards/jimmie-charters-dingo.md`. The source is his memoir *as told to Morrill Cody* (1937). It's lending-only, so I have archive.org's full-text OCR highlights only, and they're labelled as such. The library already holds his credo: Regan prints it as an epigraph (*Joy* pdf 114–115) and credits it to Cody's book. It reads: "to enter into his joys or woes, make him feel the need of me as a person rather than a servant". His own job list includes "settle fights, listen to intimate confidences". His rule for a fight is "avoided at all costs": he can "smell" one coming and tries jokes, or "drinks on the house". "Usually the free drink works," and then the quarrellers "drink to each other, or both drink to me, or in some way forget to be angry." The cost is on the page too: he can't stop a regular's credit "for fear he will be angry, walk out, and everything will be gone."

Three flags, all yours to rule. First, he'd been a professional boxer, which is the Gentle Giant's shape (*In One Piece*, and your "strength held back" out), so that's one clause at most. Second, the free drink brushes *Next One's Mine*. Third, the counterweight has to travel with it: once, he says, "I had to carry the former out, one at a time." If you'd rather have one scene of a hearing than a credo and a rule, say so and I'll keep hunting. The candidates are in `work/ruler-caregiver/historian-candidates.md`.
