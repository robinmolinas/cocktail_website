# Persona card: The Caring Leader (ruler-caregiver), Wren, round 1

Source: persona.py ruler-caregiver (essence, story, goal Control, fear Revolution/chaos, driver Provide structure; Caregiver fears selfishness, indifference, cruelty). The source's "story" is the brief: holds authority lightly, uses it to protect, **listens first**, sets clear boundaries, calm when everyone else is shouting, so people feel safe enough to do their best; power comes from trust earned; deepest fear is chaos breaking out on their watch and the people in their care hurt by it.

## The one true thing (no sibling shares it)
**When it gets loud, they don't raise the stakes. They give the hearing.** Everyone is talking at once, someone is angry, it could tip. They're the one person who could just settle it (they have the standing, and everyone knows it), and instead they ask a question, pull up a chair, let the angry one finish. The decision comes after, and it holds *because* everyone was heard. Their authority isn't volume or force: it's on loan from everyone who trusts them, and they spend it by listening.

## What they hide
- That they could end it with one word, and often already know what they'd decide. Waiting is the hardest part, and the patience is effort, not temperament.
- That under the steady face they're afraid. Not of the angry person: of getting it wrong for the people who are depending on them.
- That people check their face first, and they know it, so the face is held for everyone else.

## What they fear
Chaos on their watch, and someone in their care paying for it. The private version: that they listened too long, or chose the wrong moment to step in, and it came apart anyway.

## What they give (say it out loud, Robin's caregiver rule)
People are safe enough to say the real thing and to do their best. Even the angry one leaves heard, and still welcome. Name this plainly once: being heard by you is why people trust you with the hard calls.

## Traps
- Gandhi (persona example): never named; politics and religion.
- Job labels ("the manager", "the boss", "the one in charge"). Scenes yes: a family row at dinner, a trip going wrong, a friend's panic, a meeting about to turn.
- Saintliness: the calm must cost something (patience, pride, the urge to decide).
- Owned words: "calm" as the key word (*Before the Room*), "raise your voice" (*In One Piece* tagline), "room settles", "Everyone else…" tagline opener (*Off Duty*), "against the fun / why it lasts" (*Fair Measure*).
- Nothing may claim anyone was kept safe or did their best because of the story's person unless the page says so (signpost it).
- No gendered words ("man-to-man", "fellow marshal" only as the source's description of someone, never the guest).

## Story test
1. A person with real power (standing, rank, or the right to use force) in a moment of heat aimed at them or about to spread (anger, accusation, a crowd tipping).
2. Their answer is a hearing, not force: a seat, a question, time to talk.
3. It costs them something visible (pride, being shown up, patience).
4. Nobody is hurt; ideally the angry one stays welcome.
5. Out: rescues, rationing/limits for fun's sake (Nanny), size held back (Gentle Giant), mood lifted (Angel), verdicts (Judge), politics/war, Petraske (Oxford pdf 1499 "Buddha-calm", owned by Lover/Trend Setter).
