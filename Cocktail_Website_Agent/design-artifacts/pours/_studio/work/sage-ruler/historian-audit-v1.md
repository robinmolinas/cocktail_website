## Passes (sentence by sentence; only the ones that needed a look)

- **Epigraph:** "A careful 1887 book on whisky hardly mentions casks. The vanilla here is for them." Pass. "Careful" is our word for F5, 1887 is F3, and "hardly mentions" is F6 at Oxford's level. "Is for them" is a nod, not "is the cask".
- **y1:** pass. "Sent" for "commissioned" is fine. "Visit every" is the commission's aim, and "Britain and Ireland" is C3. "Regarded as the first whisky writer" is F1. "Small details of much of the equipment" keeps the "much of". "Earliest and most accurate account of the industry in its day" is attributed, and "in its day" fairly scopes Oxford's "at what was a major turning point".
- **y2, "Writers still count from his numbers":** pass. Oxford's Irish counts (pdf 1079, 2153; 2153 is signed by Wondrich), its "landmark survey" (pdf 2177), and outside the library an archive.org OCR highlight of Milroy's 1989 *Malt Whisky Almanac* ("he found no less…", cut off), so the plural holds. "Old pictures of still houses are captioned as he found them": pass (pdf 2150, 2168). "Sociable and funny … dotted … a travel story too": pass, paraphrased from F8. "A hundred years on, it was reprinted": pass (1887 → 1987, pdf 212). It doesn't say "first reprint", and it mustn't.
- **y3:** "I like to think … can be checked … enjoying himself": pass, signposted (my condition three).
- **y4:** "a true classic, the whisky highball": pass (*Codex* p. 199, root Highball). "Two parts very cold soda": pass (p. 214, 2:1). "A frozen glass and cold soda keep the bubbles": pass (p. 214, chilled glasses and "extremely cold seltzer" help "maintain the seltzer's bubbles"). "The bottom cube is lifted once, to mix it without knocking out the fizz": pass (p. 214 "gently mix"). "Every step has a reason": pass on p. 214 as Tomás uses it. "Oak casks give whisky some of that same flavour": pass (MATURATION pdf 1265, vanillin). "It's there in the recipe": pass.
