📜 **Hester:** Tomás, both answers, read on the page this call.

**(1) "Mostly ex-bourbon": no page says it, so neither "mostly" nor "usually" can stand.** The nearest lines are these. Oxford pdf 1769: Scotch and Irish are both aged in used barrels, "mostly a combination of bourbon and sherry casks". So it's the two together, with no share given to either. BARREL pdf 214: it's "safe to say" that most barrels maturing spirits today "began their working lives as bourbon barrels". That's Oxford's own hedge, and it's about all spirits, not Scotch, which makes it the family and not the member (my standing pitfall). HOGSHEAD pdf 1020: Scottish distillers "often" build hogsheads from the staves of bourbon barrels. That's enough for **"from bourbon casks" as your chosen style**: such casks are plainly used in Scotland. It isn't enough for any claim about how common they are. And no page says labels name the casks, so please make it "if the label says which casks". Your p. 208 line holds as the *Codex* words it: "many of the barrels used for aging scotch" once held sherry, madeira or port. Keep the "many".

**(2) Colour: no page gives one for this style, only a range.** Oxford pdf 2206: spirit comes off the still "as clear as water", and barrels give it colour "ranging from pale yellow to straw to gold to amber or even mahogany". The shade depends on how often the barrel was used before, what it held, its size, the warehouse heat and the years. Two barrels from the same source can even "come out two different shades". So the image may say **"pale straw"** as a choice from that range, written as the image's choice and never as what every bottle of the style looks like. Lengthened two to one with soda, the glass is lighter again. No gold word unless you want it from the same range. Nothing in the drink changes.
