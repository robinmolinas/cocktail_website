Spec: `_studio/specs/regular-guy-magician.json`.

The drink is a mezcal Old-Fashioned that makes do. There's no syrup and no bitters bottle: one spoon of orange marmalade does both jobs, sugar and bitter peel. It's shaken in a jar with a screw lid and poured out through the gap under the lid, so a kitchen jar does the shaker's job and the strainer's. A nearly finished marmalade jar is best: the last spoonfuls are the sweetener, and the jar you'd have thrown out is the shaker. It goes into a small tumbler over one large cube. There's no okolehao in it (see **The bottle**). The base is its nearest relative, an unaged mezcal, whose agave is traditionally roasted underground (Oxford MEZCALEROS pdf 1287) the way the Hawaiians roasted ti.

**Glassware:** a small tumbler (about 200 ml), one large ice cube.
**Contains:** veto-free.

## Recipe

| amount | item | note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 60 ml | unaged (joven) mezcal | any joven mezcal from 40% to 50%; an espadín is the easiest to find |
| 1½ teaspoons (7.5 ml, about 10 g) | orange marmalade | an ordinary one, not reduced-sugar: the sugar and a bitter edge in one spoon |
| to fill a jar two-thirds | ice cubes | for shaking |
| 1 | large ice cube | in the glass |

## Method

1. Take a clean jar with a screw lid (about 350 to 500 ml). A marmalade jar that's nearly finished is best: leave about 1½ teaspoons in it and scrape the sides down. Otherwise, spoon the marmalade into any clean jar.
2. Pour in the mezcal. Stir with the spoon for about 20 seconds, scraping the bottom, until the marmalade has loosened into the spirit.
3. Fill the jar two-thirds with ice. Screw the lid on tight and shake hard for about 10 seconds, until the jar is too cold to hold comfortably.
4. Put the large cube in a small tumbler. Loosen the lid a quarter turn, hold it in place with your thumb and strain it through the gap: the lid holds the ice back. A few shreds of peel will come through. Leave them in.
