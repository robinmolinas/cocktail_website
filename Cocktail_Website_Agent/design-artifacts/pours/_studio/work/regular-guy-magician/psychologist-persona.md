# The Mechanic (regular-guy-magician): persona card, Wren, round 1

Source: `persona.py regular-guy-magician`. Essence: "They can fix anything - even when you wouldn't have thought it possible." Goal Belonging, fear Exclusion; drivers Build connection / LEAVE A MARK. Secondary fears: unintended results, insignificance, dullness, inertia.

## The one true thing
Their hands are how they belong. A thing that has stopped working isn't a problem to them, it's a way in: they're on the kitchen floor with the back off the washing machine before anyone has finished saying "it's dead". And they do it **with what's to hand**: a hair grip, a folded beer mat, a bit of tape, the wrong part that works. It often looks like a bodge. It holds for years. What they keep alive is **someone's own thing**, not a replacement: the bike someone learned on, the lamp from a grandparent, the car that gets someone to work.

## What they hide
- They're easier in a room when there's something to do. At a party where nothing's broken, they're less sure of their place. Fixing is their ticket in, and they'd never say so.
- A small, private pride in the bodge: they know it looks odd, and they know it'll outlast the new one.
- They can't throw a thing out that could still work. Not thrift: someone loved it.

## What they fear
- Lead: **"Don't worry, we'll just get a new one."** A world that replaces instead of mends doesn't need them (exclusion and insignificance in one sentence).
- Smaller: the fix giving way when they're not there (unintended results). Keep it minor.
- Not their fear: being out of their depth (Have A Go Hero), "it's always been like that" (Innovator), being the fallback (One Next Door).

## What they give (said out loud, once)
People keep their own things, and the life attached to them. And they make the impossible ordinary: they show you what was wrong ("it was only this"), so nobody's afraid of their own stuff any more. Driver "leave a mark": all over town there are things still going because of them, held together with something they were never meant to have.

## Position (direction only; the room decides in r3)
You don't need something broken to have a place in the room. Turn up with nothing to fix. (Avoid: "You're allowed to…" (Four Shares), "stop saying it was nothing" (the Craftsman's), "let it show" (First Choice's y5).)

## Tone
Can be funny. Practical, warm, a bit wry. The story's name is a joke, so the reading may smile.

## Traps
- The trade: never cars, garages, "handyman", toolboxes as identity. No job label.
- Gender: no garage-dad or shed imagery; scenes mixed and domestic (a zip, a phone screen, a bike chain, a café table, a lamp, a lock).
- No "magic hands", "wizard", "fix" pun (drink/drugs sense too).
- Don't make them the free help everyone uses (Healer and One Next Door ground, and it insults).
- Story: no colonial "gift from outside" frame; no fugitive, missionaries, bans, bootleggers, "moonshine".
- Drink: a make-do story can't ask for a bottle nobody can get.

## Story test (for this guest)
1. Something made to work **with what was at hand**: wrong or discarded parts, things others had given up.
2. **The how is on the page**, so the magic is explained.
3. It **held**, and ordinary people used it long after.
4. (new) **It was taken in**: the fix became part of the place or the people (kept, named, made theirs). This is the belonging limb the plan test lacked.
5. Delight may live in whoYouAre; never claimed of the source's person.
Out: cures, glamour inventions and patents, lab kit, the system fix (Innovator's), any injustice as a mirror.

## Verdict on the plan's lead (okolehao, Oxford OKOLEHAO pdf 1419–1421, Berry)
Re-read the whole entry (1419, 1420 caption, 1421). **Accept.** Limbs 1–3 pass on pdf 1419. Limb 4 passes too, and better than I'd seen: the name is Hawaiian and a joke about the contraption (okole "bottom", hao "iron"), so the makeshift itself got the nickname and the spirit became the islands' own. That's "leave a mark": not Stevenson's name, the bodge's. Made again from ti root since 2012 (pdf 1421).
Conditions:
- Credit first: Hawaiians had long roasted ti underground for days and brewed from it; Stevenson taught distilling and rigged the still. Never "brought" anything as a gift.
- Out of guest text: "fugitive" and "Australian" (in the 1790s it invites a convict story nobody sourced), "gift from heaven" (frames the still as a gift from outside), missionaries, "tree of sin", bans, Kamehameha's decanter, the bootleggers' cheap starch "at hand" (make-do, but Outlaw ground and jail), "Hawaiian moonshine", the ersatz 1960s bourbon.
- Delight never claimed of Stevenson.
- "Jury-rigging" said plainly ("rigged up from").
- "Bottom" once, warmly; never cruder.
- Drink: if okolehao isn't reasonably findable, the glass carries the story through the page's own comparison (ti roasted underground the way agave is; a relative of agave) and a make-do method, and never calls itself okolehao. It must be makeable from an ordinary kitchen.
- Single source is fine (Oxford, signed); the entry cites Rowley 2014 and Hoover 2003 if Hester wants a second.
