The one ti-root okolehao I could price today is Ola Distillery's First Release: $500.99, "only 1 bottle left", at one US retailer that only ships within the US. And the label doesn't promise ti. *Aloha State Daily* (24 Feb 2026) says Hawai'i is legislating because, of about nine products filed as okolehao since 2015, "several are described as rums", and one is a blend of bourbon, whiskey, rum and ti. That's the Kamehameha decanter again (Oxford pdf 1421). So no okolehao in the recipe, not even as "recommended". Hester, that's web, secondary; it's yours to tier.

What I'd pour instead makes do, the way the story does. **Provisional draft, pending Hester's check** (`work/regular-guy-magician/mixologist-draft.md`, spec in `specs/`): a mezcal Old-Fashioned with no syrup and no bitters bottle. 60 ml joven mezcal and 1½ teaspoons of orange marmalade, which does both jobs: the sugar and the bitter peel (*Codex* p. 77, "a touch of sweetness and a pleasant bitterness"; Oxford MARMALADE AND JAM pdf 1240). It's shaken in a screw-top jar, ideally a marmalade jar on its last spoonfuls, and poured through the gap under the loosened lid onto one big cube. So a jar you'd have thrown out is the sugar, the shaker and the strainer. Mezcal is the page's own bridge: ti "is a relative of the agave", roasted underground "as is done with agave piñas" (OKOLEHAO pdf 1419). Nobody ever calls it okolehao.

The numbers: `shaken-spirit`, **24.3% / 5.26 g / 0.040%, all in range**, kept at the sweet end because the peel's bitterness doesn't register in `balance.py`. Sweeps: 10 ml of marmalade goes OUT (6.79 g), and so would a reduced-sugar one (about 3.0 g). Mezcal from 40% to 50% stays in, and 55% is an edge. Veto-free; `orange_marmalade` is new in the table, with no allergen class. Owned near misses: the Oaxaca Old-Fashioned (*Proper* p. 274, which I didn't use), the Tourist's mezcal and the Hero plan's Breakfast Martini backup. Provisional pick: *Not a New One*. Closing line: *"Pour it through the gap in the lid. Then put the jar down, and enjoy a room where nothing's broken."* Wren, the words are yours to cut.
