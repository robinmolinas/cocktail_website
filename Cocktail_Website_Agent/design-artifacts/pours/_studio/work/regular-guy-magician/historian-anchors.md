# Anchors: regular-guy-magician, The Mechanic (Hester, round 2 draft; revised round 3 with audit v1)

Story: okolehao. Hawaiians already roasted ti underground; a still was rigged from iron pots off the ships and a gun barrel; the contraption gave the spirit its name; chiefs took the still up; the spirit is made from ti again today. Card: `_studio/fact-cards/okolehao.md` (F/C/L numbers). **No motive or delight is on record for Stevenson or anyone else** (Wren's limb 5: the delight is the guest's).

| what was already there | Hawaiians traditionally roasted the big ti stems underground for several days, as agave is roasted in Mexico; roasting turns the starch to sugar (Oxford OKOLEHAO, pdf 1419; Alexander 1891 p. 157 describes the baking). F4 | The skill and the plant were the islands' own before any still: comes first in the telling (Wren). **Never** the traditional ti "beer" (C1: Alexander p. 87 says Hawaiians used neither fermented nor distilled drink). | with what's to hand |
| the bodge | Oxford: a still "jury-rigged" from "two discarded whaling ship blubber-boiling pots, with a gun barrel to draw off the spirit" (pdf 1419). Alexander 1891 p. 157, the earliest account: iron pots "procured from ships", a gun barrel as the tube. F5, F6 | Cooking pots and a gun barrel doing a still's job: the wrong parts that worked. Pots and gun barrel on two sources; "discarded" and "whaling" are Oxford's alone (C3), so say them as Oxford tells it or leave them. | can fix anything, with whatever's to hand |
| who | Oxford: William Stevenson, 1790s (pdf 1419). Alexander: distilling "is said to have been first practiced here by" him, "before the year 1800" (p. 157). F2, F3 | Name him plainly, once, as the man the story credits ("the man usually credited"). Never "fugitive", "convict", "Australian". The guest isn't him: the fix, not the maker, is the point. | (the fix remembered, not the maker) |
| the name | *okole* = "bottom", *hao* = "iron" (Oxford pdf 1419). Why: Oxford, the twin pots looked like buttocks; Rowley 2014, they "were said to" and the nickname "stuck". F7 | The bodge's own joke became the spirit's name: the mark it left. Meaning of the word is fact; the reason is "the story goes". "The name is Hawaiian", never "the Hawaiians named it". "Bottom" once, warmly. | leave a mark; taken in |
| taken in | "At one time almost every chief had his still" (Alexander 1891 p. 157). F8 | The makeshift wasn't a one-off: it was copied and kept. **Chiefs, not ordinary people**, and no date. Wren's limb 3 ("ordinary people used it for a long time") has no page for the still itself: what lasted on the record is the spirit and the name. | belonging: the fix taken in |
| made again | Local Hawaiian distilleries make okolehao again from ti root with sugar cane (Oxford pdf 1421); Island Distillers' 100-proof cane-and-ti (Rowley 2014). F10 | Made **again**, never "still": production broke off (pdf 1421, "only since 2012 … again"). The thing is kept, not replaced. Present tense on 2014/2021 sources; **no year** (C2); bottle availability is Tomás's to check. | "don't worry, we'll just get a new one" answered |
| the bridge in the glass | Oxford: the ti "is a relative of the agave", its stems roasted underground "as is done with agave piñas in Mexico" (OKOLEHAO pdf 1419); traditionally, mezcal piñas are roasted "in a conical earthen or stone-lined pit underground for several days" (MEZCALEROS pdf 1287). Joven mezcal is unaged (MEZCALEROS pdf 1290; *Codex* p. 165, "young"). F4 | Why mezcal: the nearest relative, traditionally roasted the same way. **"Traditionally" stays** (R10): not every mezcal is pit-roasted today. **Never called okolehao** (Wren). Say "joven mezcal", never "joven" alone: a "joven" tequila can be a caramel-coloured gold (Oxford SAUZA pdf 1743). The bottle is a stand-in, so nothing about it is "his" or "theirs". | makeable from an ordinary kitchen; with what's to hand |
| why no okolehao | A label reading okolehao doesn't promise ti: the 1960s Hawaiian Distillers' was bourbon with ti extract (Oxford pdf 1421); in 2026 an attorney estimated nine label applications since 2015, "several" described as rums (Aloha State Daily, 24 Feb 2026, secondary). F11, F12 | The dossier's reason the glass makes do. Guest text needn't say it; if it does, "a bottle by that name may not be what the story made". | (dossier) |
