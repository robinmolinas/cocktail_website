# Persona card: The Genius (explorer-sage), Wren, round 1

Sheet: essence "an expansive, inquisitive mind, that leaves no stone unturned"; goal Freedom; fear Entrapment; secondary fears Deception, Ambiguity; example Einstein (one question held loosely for years; trusts evidence, distrusts authority that can't explain itself).

## The one true thing
They hold one question for years, through an ordinary life, because they need to *understand* it. Not for the fun of not knowing (the Free Spirit), not to fix a nuisance (the Innovator), not for someone who's hurting (the Researcher). An answer that's true but explains nothing ("rum and fruit juice") doesn't close it; it's the fence they spend years getting behind. They don't love the chase. They love the day they finally know, and then they hand the whole answer over.

## Hidden
- How long it's been. There's a question they first asked years ago and still haven't put down. They've stopped mentioning it, because people think it should have been settled or dropped long ago.
- That it's not about being right. They're taken for a know-it-all; really they can't rest inside something they're not allowed to understand.

## Fear
Being stuck with an answer they can't get behind, for good. Entrapment of the mind: living inside "it just is", or a confident answer nobody will show the workings for. Quieter fear: settling for it themselves one day.

## Gift (say it out loud, once)
When they finally know, they give all of it away, plainly, so nobody else has to take it on trust. What others had shrugged at or written off gets seen properly, and people can make it, check it, use it themselves. The freedom they wanted, handed on.

## Traps
1. The Einstein template: the day job as the punchline (patent clerk → trailer writer). One clause, never the reveal. The Home Alone tagline stays out (trivia that flatters).
2. Praising the mind: "brilliant", "genius", "clever". Recognise the stubbornness, never the IQ.
3. Secret-recipe romance and code-breaking (family rule): the decoding gets one clause ("Don's Mix" spelled out may be the drink's proof, not the story).
4. Grievance and villains: Berry's "80 percent" and "shamed" lines out; Ray Buhen and Donn Beach are never villains (Ray was "acting on ingrained instincts"; the secrecy was against rival bars).
5. The lonely genius: "working completely in isolation" can be the scene, but the reading never says the guest is lonely.
6. Cages, desks, "fenced in": the sheet's imagery said literally.
7. Real vs fake (*The Long Answer*'s turn).
8. Medicine: the Zombie's "mender of broken dreams", Beach's two-per-customer limit: out.

## Story test
1. One question held for years, on record, with its start and its answer both on a page.
2. The answer given explained nothing, from people with the standing to know.
3. Held alongside an ordinary life (one clause).
4. Evidence wins: paper, people who were there, not argument or charm.
5. The answer is given away whole, and changes how others see the thing.
Out: an accident, an irritation fixed, care for someone else as the motive, grievance, secret-recipe romance, flattery, medicine, deception.
