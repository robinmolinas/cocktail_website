## Title block
- **Name (my pick, held to the vote):** By Design
- **Tagline:** Everyone knows the effect you have. Almost nobody knows you designed it.
- **Epigraph:** Named in Havana for America's Sweetheart. This one's for the woman behind the curls.
- **"This is me" line:** People fall for what you made. Very few ask to meet the one who made it.

## whoYouAre
You know what you do to people, because you worked it out. The voice, the pause before you answer, the one thing everyone remembers you wore: none of it is an accident, and you'd be quietly proud if anyone asked. They don't. They think you were simply born like that.

People fall for what you made. Very few ask to meet the one who made it. That part is lonely, and you almost never mention it, because to everyone else it seems like having it all. And what you give, with no credit for it, is this: when you're steering, an evening can turn warm, even daring, and never tip into trouble. People feel more alive around you, and safe. That's care too, though it looks like a show.

## yours
1. Before she was America's Sweetheart, she was Gladys Smith. A producer wanted a more star-like name, so one was made, partly from a fib about her middle name. When D. W. Griffith offered her five dollars a day, she asked for ten, and got it.

2. In 1919, with Chaplin, Griffith and others, Mary Pickford founded United Artists, so filmmakers could distribute their own films. The satisfaction, she said, was in one word: freedom. Then the twist: the company needed money, and the money was in the girl with the golden curls. The thing she built to be free kept her in them.

3. In 1928, the year her mother died, Pickford cut them off. Her fans were furious. Looking back, she said people reacted as if she'd killed someone, and maybe she had, to give the one who came next a chance. I like to think she knew who that was: the one who'd been running things all along.

4. That trap may sound familiar. You built the effect, and it works, and that's exactly what makes it hard to change: people come for it, and you're good at it. A bartender in Havana named a cocktail for the star, light and sweet, a drink for the curls. The cocktail I've made for you is for the one behind them. The rum leads now, the lime keeps it dry, and the grenadine is homemade, from real pomegranate with thyme steeped in it. It's made at home, by you, as a nod to the one who made the image.

5. A hard year shouldn't be what makes the change for you. Choose the next version of yourself while the choice is still yours, and let a few people watch you choose. Some will miss the old one for a while. The ones who stay will be the ones who wanted you, and that's the company you've been missing. I hope you find plenty of it.

## Resonance
**As the guest (a Sex Symbol).** The tagline gives me my power first and then a quiet hook: yes, I designed it, and no, nobody credits that. The epigraph, read cold, is a cocktail named for a film star and a promise of the woman behind the image; I want the story. whoYouAre ¶1 names the work without flattering the looks. ¶2's first two sentences are the "how did they know": wanted for what I made, unmet as the one who made it. The gift is said out loud: I keep the heat safe. The story mirrors me without asking me to be her: she built the name, the pay, the company, and the company held the image in place. Her words about the one who came next give me permission. The drink is proof (the grown-up leads; the grenadine is the part I make). The position is in my interest and gives something back (company, not exposure).
**As a sceptic.** Swap test: the Charmer (*Worth Finding*) holds back; this person holds nothing back on show, and the problem is that nobody looks past it. The Muse (*In Your Own Hand*) is made into other people's work; here the work is their own. *Up Close* is attention given; this is being looked at. *Accomplices*' "drop the act" is not the position: the position is to choose the next version, not to stop performing. Barnum risk: "lonely though it looks like having everything" alone could fit many; it's anchored by "what you made / the one who made it", which only this persona carries. Sexualising: none; nothing about bodies. Gender: none about the guest (she/her only for Pickford). History share: y1–y3 plus one sentence of y4, under half (counted with the lint's words()).
**Verdict:** ready for audit. **The line that lands hardest:** *People fall for what you made. Very few ask to meet the one who made it.*
