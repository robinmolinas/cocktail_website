# Wren · persona card · lover-ruler · The Sex Symbol (round 1)

Source: `persona.py lover-ruler`. Essence: "The lover who knows their own power, and uses desire to control." Story: built the voice and the walk, took the career back from a studio that wanted to own it; "wanted by everyone, known by very few." Goal intimacy; fear isolation; secondary drive: provide structure (control, status; fears chaos, vulnerability).

## The one true thing (no sibling shares it)
**They made the thing people want, on purpose, and they run it. What they're missing is someone who wants the one who made it.**
The desire is their own work, not luck and not other people's art. That authorship is the pride. The loneliness is that the work is so good nobody looks past it to the author.

Recognition-line candidates (to be 4-gram checked before use):
- "People fall for what you made. Hardly anyone asks to meet who made it."
- "You decided how they'd see you. Nobody's asked what you'd decide next."

## What they hide
- That it's built, and that the building is the part they're proudest of. They don't say it, because "I designed this" sounds cold next to "it's just me".
- That being wanted and being known are different, and they've had a lot of the first.
- That they'd trade some of the room's attention for one person who knows the decisions behind it.

## What they fear
Isolation, in its quietest form: that the image is all anyone wants, and if they changed it, the wanting would go and nobody would stay. (Ruler under it: losing control of the image to someone else, being made into something they didn't decide.)

## What they give (say it out loud once, per Robin's caregiver edits)
They hold the heat in a room with a steady hand. Around them, desire feels safe: glamorous, a little dangerous-looking, and never out of control, because someone is steering. People feel more alive near them and never unsafe. That's care, even when it looks like performance.

## Siblings and owners to keep clear of
- **Charmer (*Worth Finding*)** owns withholding, "always more to know", the door left open. The Sex Symbol holds nothing back on show: the hiding is in plain sight, the show is so complete nobody asks for the person.
- **Muse (*In Your Own Hand*)** is made into *other people's* work. The Sex Symbol made *themselves* into their own work. Keep "put your name on it" out (Muse's closing line).
- **Flirt (*Only Half Joking*)** owns play, the tease, and pink. Not a game here; and no colour words.
- **Bad Influence (*Accomplices*)** owns "drop the act". The position here is never "stop performing".
- **Pleasure Seeker, Geisha, Knight, Rough Diamond, Pure Heart**: no overlap of substance; watch "ready" (*Hoping You'd Come*) and "the room" openers.
- **Mirror pairing, ruler-lover (*Up Close*, the Magnetic Leader)** owns attention given ("You know exactly what your attention does. And you mean all of it."), and pineapple "still there at the rim. So are you". Our pineapple never gets that line. Theirs is attention paid; ours is being looked at.
- **Diva (*Say So*)** owns "you walk in and you know at once" and standards for the room.
- Owned phrase families: "looks effortless / it isn't" (*Not Too Polite*, *Nothing to It*, *Half a Rim*, *Cold Water First*): the craft can be named, never as the effortless reveal. "On your own terms" is magician-creator's. "Known by" appears in three pours: grep before use.

## Traps
- Any sexualising of the guest. The name is provocative; the reading is about power, authorship and loneliness, never bodies.
- Gender: no "girl", "woman", "she" about the guest; Pickford's "girl with the curls" is about her only.
- Monroe stays out entirely (the persona example; tragic death).
- Loneliness as tragedy. It's a cost, said gently, then turned.
- Flattery ("you're irresistible"). Recognise the work, not the looks.
- Pickford's later seclusion and drinking, Fairbanks, the cocktail as something she drank, Prohibition Cuba beyond one context clause, the El Presidente and the first Daiquiri (other families).
- "As if she'd murdered someone" is strong on the page; quote at most once, framed as her words about the reaction, or paraphrase.

## Story test
A real person who deliberately designed an image or effect and took control of it (their own name, company or brand, against someone who wanted to own it). It shows authorship (skill and choice, not luck) and a private self the image kept out of view. It costs being known, and holds that they kept the control. Out: tragic death, scandal as the story, beauty alone, studio victimhood.

## Verdict on the plan's lead: ACCEPT (Mary Pickford and the Mary Pickford cocktail)
All four limbs: authorship (negotiated her own pay; co-founded United Artists, 1919, so the makers distributed their own films: "freedom"); control kept against owners; the cost (the business wanted the girl with the curls into her thirties; the world fell for the image, not the woman running the company); and the ending in her hands (1928, she cut the curls herself).
**The mirror I'll build the reading on:** the cocktail was made in 1922 to mark the star's visit, so even the drink honours the image. The reading can say: a Havana bartender made this for the famous face; the cocktail I've made for you is for the one running the company. That's the tie between story and glass.

Conditions:
1. Tagline puts the power first (intrigue, not pity).
2. Never that she drank it; "named for her" only.
3. No colour words (pink is the Flirt's).
4. Fairbanks, her later years, Monroe out. Prohibition Cuba: one context clause at most.
5. Hester to check on the fetched PBS page (no new visit needed): whether it says who gave her the stage name (born Gladys Smith) and the "America's Sweetheart" label. If on the page, the name itself was a made thing: a strong second beat. If not, leave it out.

**Backup (Dean Martin, Flame of Love)** stands, weaker: the control-against-an-owner limb is unverified, and a drunk act on a drinks site needs heavy guarding.
