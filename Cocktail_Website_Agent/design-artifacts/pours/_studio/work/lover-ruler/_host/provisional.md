# Pour — By Design · The Sex Symbol (lover-ruler)

## Cocktail

- **name:** By Design
- **tagline:** Everyone knows the effect you have. Almost nobody knows you designed it.
- **glassware:** cocktail glass, chilled
- **contains:** `[]`

**recipe**

| amount | item | note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 60 ml | white rum, 50% or stronger | unaged or lightly aged, not spiced or sweetened; labelled 50% ("100 proof" in the US) or a little stronger; anything from 50% to 63% works |
| 30 ml | fresh pineapple juice | or an unsweetened, not-from-concentrate juice |
| 20 ml | fresh lime juice | squeezed today |
| 12.5 ml | thyme grenadine | homemade, the day before: pomegranate juice, sugar and thyme (see Method) |

**method**
1. The day before, make the grenadine. In a clean jar put 250 g of pomegranate juice (100% juice), 250 g of white sugar and 6 sprigs of thyme, crushed lightly in your hand. Close the lid and shake until the sugar has dissolved, which takes a few minutes. Leave it in the fridge overnight, then strain out the thyme. It keeps about a week in the fridge.
2. Put a cocktail glass in the freezer.
3. Measure the rum, pineapple, lime and 2½ teaspoons (12.5 ml) of the grenadine into a shaker.
4. Fill the shaker three-quarters with ice and shake hard until the outside is too cold to hold. The pineapple will foam a little, and it should.
5. Strain into the cold glass. No garnish.

**closingLine:** *Make the grenadine yourself, the day before. Then choose your next version before anyone asks for the old one.*

## Reading

**epigraph**
*Named in Havana for America's Sweetheart. This one's for the one behind the curls.*

**whoYouAre**
You know what you do to people, because you worked it out. The voice, the pause before you answer, the one thing everyone remembers you wore: none of it is an accident, and you'd be quietly proud if anyone asked. They don't. They think you were simply born like that.

People fall for what you made. Very few ask to meet the one who made it. That part is lonely, and you almost never mention it, because to everyone else it seems like having it all. And what you give, with no credit for it, is this: when you're steering, an evening can turn warm, even daring, and never tip into trouble. People feel more alive around you, and safe. That's care too, though it looks like a show.

**yours**

1. Before she was America's Sweetheart, she was Gladys Smith. A producer suggested a more star-like name, so one was made, partly from a fib about her middle name. When D. W. Griffith offered her five dollars a day, she asked for ten, and got it.

2. In 1919, with Chaplin, Griffith and others, Mary Pickford founded United Artists, so its founders could distribute their own films. The satisfaction, she said, was in one word: freedom. Then the twist: the company needed money, and the money was in the girl with the golden curls. The thing she built to be free kept her in them.

3. In 1928, the year her mother died, Pickford cut them off. Her fans were scathing. Looking back, she said people reacted as if she'd killed someone, and maybe she had, to give the one who came next a chance. I like to think she knew who that was: the one who'd been running things all along.

4. That trap may sound familiar. You built the effect, and it works, and that's exactly what makes it hard to change: people come for it, and you're good at it. A bartender in Havana named a cocktail for the star, light and sweet, a drink for the curls. The cocktail I've made for you is for the one behind them. The rum leads now, the lime keeps it dry, and the grenadine is homemade, from real pomegranate with thyme steeped in it. It's made at home, by you, as a nod to the one who made the image.

5. A hard year shouldn't be what makes the change for you. Choose the next version of yourself while the choice is still yours, and let a few people watch you choose. Some will miss the old one for a while. The ones who stay will be the ones who wanted you, and that's the company you've been missing. I hope you find plenty of it.
