## Ruling on Tomás's ask: the Bacardi label
- **"Carta Blanca"** is on Oxford's DAIQUIRI page (pdf 621), inside Cox's story, which is the Hero's first Daiquiri. The MARY PICKFORD entry says only "Bacardi rum" (pdf 1252). The bottle can be recommended as Bacardi's white rum. The name "Carta Blanca" is fine on the recipe line but never in the reading, where it would carry Cox's story in with it.
- **Strength:** no library page gives today's ABV. 37.5% vs 40% by market is unsourced (my memory agrees, but that's not a page). Your sweep at both strengths covers it. Mark it unsourced in the table note.
- **Not the same rum.** Bacardi moved production to Puerto Rico in 1936 and left Cuba after the revolution (Oxford pdf 182). Never "the rum they poured in Havana" or "the same rum" in the reading or the epigraph. "The rum the drink's entry names" is true.

## Guards for Wren's reading (v1 not yet written)
1. No maker's name; no flat 1922; "named for her"; "reportedly" on the popularity.
2. Never that she drank it.
3. The haircut: "cut off" (the page's verb); the year her mother died is on the same line, so either a gentle clause or no motive at all. Her own line (she'd "murdered someone … to give her successor a chance to live") is the motive on record; paraphrase it, don't copy it whole. "A power move" or "took the image back" as motive is ours: "I like to think".
4. United Artists distributed the founders' films; it didn't "own" them. Founders: "with Chaplin, Griffith and others" or "with three other filmmakers", never a list of three.
5. The pressure came from her own company's money needs (P9), not "the studio".
6. "Into her thirties" is our arithmetic: the page says "teenagers".
7. The name: Belasco asked for a more star-like name, and it was made from her mother's family surname and a fib about her middle name. The page doesn't say who chose "Pickford": never "she named herself".
8. No "same rum" (pdf 182). "Carta Blanca" stays out of the reading.
