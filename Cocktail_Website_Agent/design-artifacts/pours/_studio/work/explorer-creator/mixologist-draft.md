## Recipe

- **serves:** 4 (made as one batch, frozen in two bags of two drinks; a blender does single drinks badly, *LI* p. 141)
- **glassware:** a large stemmed goblet (about 350 ml), chilled, no salt rim
- **contains:** `[]`

| amount | item | note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 240 ml | tequila blanco | unaged, bright and clear |
| 120 ml | Cointreau (recommended), or any clear orange triple sec at 40% | his father always insisted on Cointreau |
| 90 ml | simple syrup (equal parts sugar and water, stirred until clear) | |
| 450 ml | cold water | the melt, measured once, so the ice never decides |
| ½ teaspoon (2.5 ml) | pure vanilla extract | orange and vanilla, borrowed from the ice-cream counter |
| 150 ml | fresh lime juice (about 5 limes), added only when you blend | the one fresh thing, every time |

## Method
1. A day ahead, stir the tequila, Cointreau, syrup, water and vanilla together in a jug.
2. Pour half into each of two freezer bags or wide lidded containers, about 450 ml each. Each one makes two drinks. Seal them and lay them flat in the freezer for a day. The alcohol and sugar keep the mix from freezing hard: it sets thick and icy.
3. If it's still liquid after a day, your freezer isn't quite cold enough. Add the lime (75 ml to each bag) three to six hours before you serve, and it will set.
4. To serve two, put two goblets in the freezer for ten minutes. Squeeze 75 ml of lime juice. Break up one bag with your hands through the plastic, tip it into a blender with the lime, and blend just until smooth and thick, a few seconds. Blending longer only warms it.
5. Pour into the chilled goblets. No salt and no garnish. The second bag makes the next two, and they'll come out the same.
