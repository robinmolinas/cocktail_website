**Your question 3: yes, it's a conflict, so "Rickey disliked the lime" goes.** There are three tellings, and they're all on Oxford pdf 1663 or *Imbibe!* 118. (1) Rickey's own, as Oxford relays it: he had used lemon, the drink caught on at Shoo's with lime, "which Rickey disliked", and he put the change down to "one of the bartenders". The dislike is in Oxford's narration, not quoted from him. (2) Brown, 1930: a stranger gave Williamson limes, and next morning Williamson made one for Rickey, "who approved" (and "was said to" covers it). (3) Wondrich: lime from the start, no lemon at all. My own card for *Word Gets Round* already fenced lemon-before-lime as a conflict on 2026-10-01, so I'm ruling the same way here. What survives is better anyway, and both books have it (Oxford pdf 1662, *Imbibe!* 118): in 1895 Rickey wrote his recipe out by hand for a Syracuse paper, and it says "lime juice". Whoever started it, by then the lime was in the famous man's own recipe. If you want the bar's part, tell it as "the story goes": in two of the three tellings the lime came from behind the bar.

On your other conditions, checked on the page. (2) Holds. "More than likely Williamson" is Oxford's inference, and his "not the author" pointed at his friend Enno Sander. (4) Holds, and it's a three-way conflict: Oxford RICKEY says he "had even owned" it for a couple of years after 1883, SHOOMAKER'S says he bought it in 1883 and sold it three years later, and Wondrich says he owned it "at the time". So never "his own bar". (5) Williamson's title won't hold still: "head bartender" (pdf 1789), "principal barkeep" and an unexplained "president" (pdf 1663), and "secretary" of the company in a 1915 fragment. So the only safe title is "head bartender". Two small strikes from your read: "there for decades" isn't on any page (Oxford has him there for "much of" 1880s–1917; say "for years"), and "made the bartender's way" rests on the contested lime. "Whether Rickey drank it or not, his friends did" is exactly on pdf 1663. "Instructed bartenders everywhere" is Wondrich's own run, so paraphrase it.
