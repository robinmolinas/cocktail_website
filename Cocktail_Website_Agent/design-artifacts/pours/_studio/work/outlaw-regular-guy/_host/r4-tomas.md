**The drink.** It's Embury's CANADIAN, from Hester's E2: straight Canadian whisky, curaçao, Angostura and a teaspoon of sugar syrup. I made one change: maple takes the syrup's place, at his own measure. That's the swap he made himself in his Canadian Collins (E3). It's stirred cold and served up in a chilled coupe with an orange peel. The recipe:
- 60 ml Canadian whisky
- **5 ml maple syrup: one teaspoon exactly, scraped flat**
- 2.5 ml (½ teaspoon) orange curaçao
- 2 dashes Angostura
- 1 strip of orange peel

There's no vermouth, so it belongs to the Old-Fashioned family. That's why y4's "in the style of the Manhattan, the great stirred classic" has to go. You could write "a cocktail from David Embury's 1948 book, made with Canadian whisky" instead; Hester checks the date, because she read the later editions. The whisky measure, the stir, the coupe and the peel are my calls, not his.

**The numbers carry your line.** balance.py, stirred: 25.8% ABV, 5.09 g sugar/100 ml, 44% dilution. All three are in range. Acid is 0, which is OUT on the stirred band by design: that band comes from vermouth, the family has no acid, and adding citrus or vermouth would make it a different drink. Now the maple. At 3.75 ml it's in range. At 5 ml it's in range, and that's the spec. **At 6.25 ml, a quarter-teaspoon over, it's OUT (6.09 g),** and at 7.5 ml it's OUT. So "not a drop more" is physics: a little over the measure and the maple takes over. y4 should state the measure as a teaspoon, never "a little".
