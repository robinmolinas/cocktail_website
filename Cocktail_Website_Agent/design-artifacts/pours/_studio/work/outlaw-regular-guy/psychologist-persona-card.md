# Persona card: The Gangster (outlaw-regular-guy), Wren, round 1

## The one true thing (no sibling shares it)
They don't escape the rules, mock them, argue with them or break them to make something. They replace them: with a code of their own, for their own people, that they keep first and keep when it costs. What they can't bear isn't rules. It's rules written by people who will never have to live under them, and who let themselves off.
- Draft "this is me": "It was never the rules you minded. It was the people who wrote them and never had to keep them." / "Your word costs you before it costs anyone else. That's why it holds."

## Show / hide / fear / give
- **Show:** knows how things actually get done; looks after their own personally (a call, not a form); a word that holds.
- **Hide:** how much the code costs to keep, especially when the other side doesn't keep theirs. And that being the one who decides is how they stay unafraid.
- **Fear:** powerlessness: being at the mercy of a desk, a process, someone who'll never know their name. (Never "people like you": no class or background assumed.)
- **Give:** around them, the rule is the same for everyone, the one who made it included. People know exactly where they stand. Nobody they vouch for faces the machine alone. (Say this out loud once: Robin's "say the kindness" rule.)

## Story test
1. The person keeps a code of their own that is stricter where it matters than their trade or the rules around them, in their own words or deeds.
2. It costs them, and they keep it.
3. It binds them first, not only the people under them.
4. The system that pushes them around is shown not keeping its own rules (or a stricter standard for others than itself).
5. Their own people (crew, neighbourhood) are the reason. No violence, no glamour.
