# Tomás: drink draft, outlaw-regular-guy (The Gangster), v2, round 6 (Hester audit v2: D1, D2, D4, D6 applied; the drink is unchanged)

Spec: `_studio/specs/outlaw-regular-guy.json` (v1.1). It's David Embury's CANADIAN from *The Fine Art of Mixing Drinks*, made as his own maple variant: the entry ends "Maple syrup is sometimes used in place of the sugar" (Hester, corrected E2), so the maple goes in at his measure, one teaspoon. **Mine, not his** (his entry gives no measure, stir or glass): the 60 ml of whisky, the stir, the chilled coupe and the orange peel. The peel is the one thing added to his recipe.

**Glassware:** coupe, chilled
**Contains:** veto-free

## Recipe

| amount | item | note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 60 ml | Canadian whisky | any blended Canadian whisky at 40% or a little above; the kind that filled his cases |
| 5 ml (1 teaspoon, exactly) | pure maple syrup | a flat teaspoon, never heaped: a quarter-teaspoon more tips the drink too sweet. I like a dark one; any pure maple syrup, never pancake syrup |
| 2.5 ml (½ teaspoon) | orange curaçao | about three dashes, the top of Embury's "2 or 3" |
| 2 dashes | Angostura bitters | |
| 1 strip | orange peel | |

## Method

1. Put the coupe in the freezer for 10 minutes so it's properly cold.
2. Measure the maple syrup into a mixing glass or jar: fill the teaspoon, then scrape the top flat with a knife. Add the curaçao and the bitters, pour in the whisky, and stir for a few seconds without ice, until the maple has dissolved. It's thick, and it won't dissolve once the drink is cold.
3. Fill the mixing glass with ice and stir for 20 to 30 seconds, until the outside of the glass feels very cold.
4. Strain into the cold coupe.
5. Peel a strip of orange peel about the length of your thumb, with as little of the white as you can. Hold it skin side down over the drink and squeeze it so its oils fall on the top, then drop it in.

**closingLine:** *Scrape the teaspoon of maple flat. Then show someone close where you draw the line.*
