# explorer-lover: drink draft v4 (Tomás, round 6; audit D1-D2, I1-I2 applied; closing line agreed with Wren). Story and balsamic ruled (Wren r3).

**Glassware:** old-fashioned glass (rocks glass), filled with crushed ice
**Contains:** veto-free

## Recipe
| amount | item | note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 60 ml | London dry gin | any London dry, 40% or stronger |
| 22.5 ml | fresh lemon juice | |
| 15 ml | simple syrup (equal parts sugar and water) | |
| 15 ml | crème de mûre (blackberry liqueur) | check that the label says blackberry; the balsamic is stirred into it |
| ½ tsp (2.5 ml) | balsamic vinegar | the dark, sweet Italian kind; the vinegar, not a thick glaze |
| 1 | fresh blackberry | on top |
| 1 | lemon wedge | on the rim |

## Method
1. Fill the glass with crushed ice. A tea towel and a rolling pin will crush it fine.
2. Shake the gin, lemon juice and syrup hard with ice for about 12 seconds, until the shaker is too cold to hold comfortably. Strain into the glass.
3. Pile on more crushed ice, so it stands in a little mound above the rim.
4. In a small glass, stir the balsamic vinegar into the crème de mûre. Pour it slowly over the top of the ice, so it runs down through the drink in dark threads.
5. Put the blackberry on top and the lemon wedge on the rim.

## Names

**Pick: *Straight Back*** (Wren's, and mine). It's the person, not the drink: the speed of one taste taking you somewhere ("back in that blackberry patch", *Proper* p. 31), in two words someone could say across a bar. It sits beside the tagline without repeating it: the name is the moment, the tagline the habit. One caution, from the bar: "straight" means neat and a "back" is a chaser, so a bartender might hear an order for a neat spirit. The menu context settles it, and I'd still put my name to it.
