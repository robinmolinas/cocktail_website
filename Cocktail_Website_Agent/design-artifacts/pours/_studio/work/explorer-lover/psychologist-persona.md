# Persona card: The Sense Seeker (explorer-lover), Wren, round 1

## The one true thing (no sibling shares it)
They live, and remember, through the body. One taste or smell takes them somewhere whole, years back or miles away, in a mouthful. The senses are their freedom: a door no one can lock, needing no ticket and no permission. They can't tell you what year it was; they can tell you exactly what the bread tasted like.

## What they hide
How hard a sensation hits them. A smell can undo them in the middle of an ordinary afternoon. They keep it light ("mm, nice") because they've had the look across the table: "it's only bread", "do you have to smell everything?". Not "too much" (owned: Campaigner, Obsessed); the hidden part is the depth of the feeling, played down so nobody calls it fuss.

## What they fear
Two halves (Explorer x Lover):
- The trap: a life with the senses turned down. Someone else choosing the menu; the same lunch at the same desk; having to justify wanting something. (Not the Photographer's blur of time, not the Discoverer's lost tug.)
- The cold: eating with someone who isn't tasting. That's lonelier than eating alone.

## What they give
They hand people a sensation, wordlessly: the spoon held out, the leaf crushed under someone's nose, "here, smell this". The other person is suddenly present in their own body. Years later, people's own taste-memories have the guest in them. The kindness to say out loud: you give people places they can go back to by taste.

## Siblings and neighbours (keep apart)
- Pleasure Seeker (Curtain Call): says yes first, enjoys out loud, fears the end of the night. Owns "yes", forkfuls, the dish nobody orders, lights coming up. The Sense Seeker is close-up and quiet, eyes shut on one mouthful; about depth, not the evening.
- Tourist (Brought Home): trips, the street, the market, home, telling it, directions, "no ticket". The Sense Seeker never needs to go anywhere, and doesn't tell: hands it over.
- Discoverer (In a Minute): stopping on a walk, small things, time, "the tug". Mind and attention; the Sense Seeker is appetite and body.
- Photographer (As It Was): keeps the ordinary, fears the blur. Not keeping, not memory as archive.
- Obsessed (Rosetta): one thing over and over; "I love it" sounds like too much.
- Conservationist (Left Standing): doesn't pick the flower, doesn't touch. The Sense Seeker picks it and smells it. Nice contrast; never echo.
- Connoisseur (A Brother's Care): knowing, ranking. Out.
- Pioneer, Innovator, Mad Inventor: no first, no fix, no long way round.

## Traps
1. Drink in hand: sensory writing slides into "as you sip". Proposal points to life.
2. Nostalgia: the blackberry patch is a door, not a keepsake (As It Was's ground). The reading is about living by sensation now.
3. Home/travel: never "a taste of home" (Tourist: "Home is half the trip"). "The island where he grew up", once.
4. Sexual drift: Lover + senses. Food, smell, touch of fabric, warmth. No bedroom, no "sensual".
5. Dignity of appetite: no "greedy", "gluttony", "indulgent", no quantity or diet. Appetite for sensation, not for more.
6. Connoisseurship: Bradsell's gin knowledge and "some brands were crap" stay out.
7. Trend Setter: the vodka clause stays one light sentence (following the taste, not liking it first).
8. Mûre: Oxford MULBERRY (pdf 1368) calls crème de mûre a mulberry liqueur and names the Bramble; BRAMBLE (pdf 336) says "blackberry liqueur", Bradsell says blackberry patch. "Mûre" is both words in French. The bottle Tomás names must be a blackberry one, or the story isn't in the glass. Hester to rule.
9. Year contested (1989 Oxford vs 1991 or '92 Proper): no year.
10. Phrase fences found: "too much" (14 pours), "grey" (9), "fuss" (3), "patch" (2: check context). Avoid "too much"; "grey" only if fresh.

## Story test
1. One sensation on record, in the person's own words or the page's, that took them somewhere whole or set their course.
2. They followed it without asking anyone (against fashion, advice, the approved menu).
3. They handed it to other people, who got the sensation too.
4. That sensation is really in the glass, tasted first and clearly.
Out: ranking or expertise, making for its own sake, restraint, travel and bringing home, memory kept as archive, anything sexual, health, quantity.

## Verdict on the plan's lead (Bradsell, the Bramble)
Pass on all four, from his own words (Proper p. 31, read whole): three liqueurs from the cider man (cassis, framboise, mûre); "As soon as I tried the mûre, I was back in that blackberry patch on the Isle of Wight. It was spot-on, that flavor." (1, and instant: of three, only one took him anywhere.) "The scene was totally vodka then" (2). "Became popular straight off", "You can do it at parties right away" (3). Blackberry liqueur poured over last (4), if the bottle is blackberry.
Mirror I'd like in the reading (signposted): I like to think a room full of strangers in Soho stood in his blackberry patch without knowing it.
Backup (Baker): its best line ("not solely through gluttony... high adventure") is the hidden need exactly, but it's broad travel and a sea-and-bedroom minefield. Dossier only.
