📜 **Hester:** Wren, the lead holds on the page. I read *Proper* pp. 31–32 and Oxford BRAMBLE and BRADSELL (pdf 335–336) whole, and your quotes are exact: the cider supplier, the three liqueurs, "back in that blackberry patch on the Isle of Wight", "the scene was totally vodka then", "popular straight off". **Ruling on the mûre: blackberry.** You're right that the word covers both (Larousse: *mûre* is the mulberry's fruit, and *mûre sauvage* the bramble's). But in this drink every source near Bradsell says blackberry. His own taste took him to a *blackberry* patch (*Proper* p. 31). Oxford's BRAMBLE, built on a 2011 interview with him, writes "crème de mûre (blackberry liqueur)" (pdf 336). Simonson calls it "a blackberry liqueur nobody thought of at all" (*Proper* p. 25). And a bramble is the blackberry bush. MULBERRY (pdf 1368) is the outlier: one line, no source given. It goes in the dossier as a recorded conflict and never in the reading. Tomás, the bottles bear this out: Giffard's Crème de Mûre is "made from concentrated blackberry juice", and Briottet sells its as "Blackberry Liqueur" (producer pages, secondary; Briottet's English text slips to "mulberry" itself, so the mix-up is a translation one). Spec it as "crème de mûre (blackberry liqueur)" and check that the label says blackberry. Three guards before anyone writes. (1) **One counterweight, said early:** Oxford says he built the Bramble from a Singapore Sling he'd already made at the Zanzibar, cut short and renamed (pdf 336). So the taste gave the drink its fruit and its name, not its whole shape. Never "one taste and the drink was born". (2) **No year:** 1989 in Oxford, "1991 or '92 (Bradsell was not sure)" in *Proper*. (3) Fred's was a private members' club "favored by media and celebrities", so your "club full of strangers" works as signposted "I like to think", but not as fact. And "you can do it at parties right away" is about how easy it was to make, not about handing someone a taste, so it can't carry your clause 3 alone. "Popular straight off" can.
