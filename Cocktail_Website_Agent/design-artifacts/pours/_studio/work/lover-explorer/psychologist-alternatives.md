4. The cocktail I've made for you starts from a recipe in his cocktail guide of 1941, a witty book full of recipes sent in by famous names, and one of the ways the Bloody Mary spread. Gaston Lauryssen of New York's St. Regis hotel sent him his hotel's version, under the politer name it went by there: the Red Snapper. It was equal parts vodka and tomato juice, with a little Worcestershire sauce and a pinch of salt, and it was served straight up, the way cocktails were then, with no ice in the glass. Both of those stay. I took out the Worcestershire and put in a splash of manzanilla, a bone-dry Spanish sherry, so his cellar and his kitchen share the glass. With fresh lemon and a pinch of celery salt, it's savoury, bright and properly cold. And because it's served up, it's short, and it has a real ending. I wanted that for you.
