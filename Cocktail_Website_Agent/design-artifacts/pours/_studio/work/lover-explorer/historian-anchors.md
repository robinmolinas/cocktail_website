# Anchors: lover-explorer (The Pleasure Seeker)

Hester, v3 (final), round 7 (2026-10-01), confirmed by audit v3 (`historian-audit-v3.md`; earlier `historian-audit-v1.md`, `-v2.md`). Closing line (v1.4): *There's no ice in it, so it ends. Choose the night's ending yourself, before the coats come out.* Name: *Curtain Call* (all three). Wren took *Dining with My Friends* for the after (r3). Story: Crosby Gaige, with Wren's reframe (his trade was nights that end). Card: `_studio/fact-cards/crosby-gaige.md` (F1–F22, C1–C3, L1–L3, U1–U5). Drink: Tomás's spec v1 (`_studio/specs/lover-explorer.json`), a Red Snapper served up, with manzanilla and lemon, and no Worcestershire or cayenne.

**Ceilings (every row, and the reading):**
- The year: **1941**, or no year. Never 1945 (C1). Tomás's spec line and his r2 "the 1945 drink" both need it.
- The base: **vodka**, as printed in the guide (F17). Never gin (L3).
- The crash: Oxford says only that he "looked to these interests for livelihood" (F2). Never "lost everything" or "ruined" (L1). And never "the curtain fell in 1929": he was still producing in 1935 (F9).
- "150 plays" is Oxford's count for his twenty-five-year career (F1), not 150 he produced alone. "Seen 150 plays close" works inside "I like to think". "Brought down 150 curtains" claims too much.
- "Celebrity contributions" is Oxford's phrase, never "friends'" (Wren). "His cocktail guide", never the full title (gender, Wren).
- Watch Hill: flower gardens (F12), never herbs (U1).
- Never name Beebe (family guard). Keep out "Madame, Can You Walk?", "Let's Slide Downstairs", the champagne names and "playboy" (Wren).
- Our glass is a riff: manzanilla in, the lemon raised, the Worcestershire and cayenne left out. So it "starts from" the Red Snapper Gaige printed. Never "his recipe" or "Gaige's drink".
- "Served straight up as a cocktail" is Joy's (pdf 249), not read on the guide's page (F17 note).
- Lauryssen was the St. Regis's manager/host who gave the recipe (F7, F17). He didn't invent it. Petiot is in Joy's story, not in ours.
- His death: no cause, no hospital, no date (F8). Never that he saw *Dining with My Friends* published, and never "his last words".
- Hangover roots (F22): no cure, hangover, morning, brunch or pick-me-up. Equal parts isn't a first (the 1929 gin mix, pdf 292). No year for the St. Regis drink (C3), and no Petiot or Jessel.
- The Codex's manzanilla replaces the vodka (p. 221). Ours sits beside it, so it's "my splash", never "the Codex's recipe".
- The guide's seasonings: "Worcestershire sauce and salt among its seasonings", never "a little Worcestershire and a pinch of salt" as if that were the whole list (audit v2 R1).
- The epigraph's subject is the ancestor (the Bloody Mary), never "this drink" (audit X1).
- His passions: "true passions" (Oxford), never "loved most" (X2). The column is "called Cellar and Pantry", never "about his cellar" (X4). His living came "from those two loves too", never "turned into his living" (X3).
