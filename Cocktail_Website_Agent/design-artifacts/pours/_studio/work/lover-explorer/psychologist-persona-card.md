# Persona card: The Pleasure Seeker (lover-explorer), Wren, round 1

Sources: persona.py lover-explorer (xlsx row, both sheets); my family sketch (`_studio/plans/work/lover/psychologist-sketches.md`), revised here; the Lover plan row; the registry; sibling readings (*Brought Home*, *Anyway*, *Worth the Trip*, *Hoping You'd Come*, *Their Night*, *Any Day*, *In Your Own Hand*, *The Long Answer* plan row).

## The one true thing
They're brave about enjoying things. They're first into the cold sea, the one who orders the dish nobody else will try and passes it round, and the one who says "one more place" at midnight. What only they carry: **it's never the cost of the night they mind. It's the lights coming up.** They enjoy things so fully partly because they can feel the ending coming, even at the best moment. They keep a night going because they're bad at endings, not because they're greedy.

Candidate "this is me" line: *It's never the bill. It's the lights coming up.* (registry-clean: no pour uses "lights coming up".)

## Hides / fears / gives
- **Hides:** the flat feeling in the taxi home, when the noise stops. They also hide how much of the "one more" is about putting off the goodbye.
- **Fears:** that when the music stops, everyone goes home to their own lives and they're left in the quiet. (Lover: isolation. Explorer: being stuck, stilled. The sheet's Casanova ends shut away in a castle.)
- **Gives:** permission. With them, people enjoy things without apologising: the good bottle on a Wednesday, the second pudding, the swim. Once in the reading, say it plainly: they make life feel worth tasting for everyone near them.

## Siblings and cousins (how this one differs)
- **The Geisha** (*Hoping You'd Come*): the care comes *before* people arrive (the ready room). The Pleasure Seeker's weak point is the *end* of the night. Before vs after.
- **The Bad Influence** (unauthored; plan: Employees Only, soup for whoever stayed at closing): breaks rules *with* you, for realness, and fears being tamed and then dropped. Its ground is who stays for the quiet part. The Pleasure Seeker's fear is about time (the ending), not identity (being boring).
- **The Flirt:** plays with one room's spark. The Pleasure Seeker wants the whole feast.
- **The Romantic:** letters, depth. Paper belongs to the Romantic.
- **The Tourist** (*Brought Home*, secondary cousin): owns "the best night of a trip is sometimes the one after it, at your own table, telling it", the find brought home, and "stuck somewhere with no one to tell". The Pleasure Seeker never needs to tell it afterwards. They want to be *in* it with people.
- **The Morale Booster** (*Anyway*): makes a good night on purpose, for a group, and fears a gloom nobody can lift. The Pleasure Seeker isn't putting on the night for others. They're enjoying it, and the others catch it.
- **The Reassurer** (*Worth the Trip*): owns "a cure for the morning after" and "Let the quiet after it happen" (y5).
- **The Fairy Godparent** (*Their Night*): owns "don't slip out early" and the 1911 Broadway musical.
- **The Idealist** (*The Long Answer*, Fougner): owns the daily drinks column (1933).

## Traps
- Moralising: the punished pleasure-lover, the lonely old libertine as a warning, the after as pity.
- Conquest: Casanova as a seducer; Joy's "a playboy himself" (pdf 356). Out.
- Praising "one more", drinking more or harder, the morning after, hangovers, cures (the Reassurer's anyway).
- Pleasure as luxury or status (the sheet's brands, "made millions"). The pleasure is sensory and shared, never price.
- Gender: the book's own title (*…and Ladies' Companion*): say "his cocktail guide". Leave out the drink names "Madame, Can You Walk?", "Let's Slide Downstairs", "She Couldn't Say No", "Her Sarong Slipped" and "husband beaters" (Joy pdf 266): they are drunk jokes and gendered.
- My old fence ("stay for the end of one night / when it's quiet") is **withdrawn**. Staying for the quiet is now the Reassurer's y5 and the Bad Influence's planned ground.

## Story test (revised from the sketch)
A real person who took pleasure seriously and shared it (a table, a cellar, a feast others joined), whose story has a clear end of the party (a closing, a crash, a last night).
- Must show: the pleasures were shared, not hoarded.
- Must show: the ending was met head on, and ideally *chosen* or made into the next thing, not run from.
- Must hold: what lasted was the company or the pleasure itself, not regret. The after never reads as punishment.
- Out: ruin, debt or disease as the ending; conquest; "died alone"; one more; the morning after; the memoir as the whole story.

## Position fence (my preference; the room tests it)
**Choose one ending.** Once, be the one who calls it, on the best note, while it's still wonderful, and say so. What they gain: the ending becomes theirs (freedom, not being left), and they find the people don't vanish when the music stops. They ask when the next one is. Tone: encouragement, never "slow down". Not "stay for the quiet" (Reassurer, Bad Influence). Never "slip out" (Fairy Godparent). And not "enjoy it alone" (on a drinks site that reads as drinking alone).

## Verdict on the plan's lead: Crosby Gaige, accept with a reframe
On the page (Oxford GAIGE, CROSBY, pdf 861): a postmaster's son from Skunk Hollow who made millions producing about 150 Broadway plays over 25 years. His "true passions" (fine wine and cooking) "lay outside the theater". After the 1929 crash he looked to them for a living: a monthly column, NBC's daily *Kitchen Cavalcade* (1937), cookbooks, and two cocktail guides. The first is full of wit and celebrity contributions, and it's one of the ways the Bloody Mary spread.

**Why it passes, reframed:** the plan reads the crash as the music stopping, and it is: the century's loudest party ended in 1929. That's a familiar touchstone, and we need no more than one. But the mirror I want is sharper. His trade was nights that end: every one of his 150 plays had a closing night, and every show ends with the house lights coming up. What he loved most sat outside the show, in the cellar and the kitchen. When the big party stopped, the pleasures he'd loved all along carried him, and he went on sharing them every day, on the radio, to people he'd never meet. Signposted: I like to think a man who'd brought down 150 curtains knew the ending is part of the show. That gives us the position: choose your ending.

**Conditions**
1. Don't inflate the crash. Oxford says only that he "looked to these interests for livelihood": no lost fortune unless it's sourced.
2. Radio over paper. The column gets a clause at most (Fougner's daily column is *The Long Answer*'s). His 1948 memoir *Footlights and Highlights* stays out: telling it afterwards is the Tourist's ground and Casanova's frame on the sheet.
3. "Celebrity contributions" is Oxford's word. The plan's "friends' contributions" overstates it unless a source says friends.
4. Beebe is never named (plan guard). The Drake Hotel "young impudent sir" story (Joy pdf 67) is muddled on the page about whose encounter it was, so it stays out.
5. **Date conflict:** Oxford dates the guide 1941 (M. Barrows); Joy says 1945 (pdf 249, 266, 356, 373, 429). Hester settles it, or the reading gives no year.
6. Broadway is his living, told in a clause. The 1911 musical and its party are *Their Night*'s, so no opening-night glamour.
7. For Tomás: the Red Snapper (Joy pdf 249, 373) is his to decide. My person-side guards: never the morning after, a cure or brunch-as-recovery (the Reassurer owns "a cure for the morning after"), and tomato's surprise is *The Other Berry*'s.

**Leads for Hester to verify on the web** (my knowledge, unsourced; don't anchor until fetched): a last book, *Dining with My Friends* (1949), which would be the "company lasted" limb if true; his country place, Watch Hill Farm near Peekskill, and its herb garden (Oxford's 1936 *Peekskill Evening Star* piece, "Tells How to Live a Life", points there); whether he published fine editions in the late 1920s. Any one sourced detail of his last years showing company or delight would firm up the "after".

**Backup (Beebe and Clegg):** stands, but it's weaker for this person. Its after needs a partner (family guard: no reading requires one), and the Clegg trap is live.
