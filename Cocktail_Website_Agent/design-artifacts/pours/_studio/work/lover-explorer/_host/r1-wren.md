**Verdict on Crosby Gaige: accept, with a reframe.** I read GAIGE, CROSBY (Oxford pdf 861). His true passions, wine and cooking, "lay outside the theater". After 1929 he turned to them for a living, including NBC's daily *Kitchen Cavalcade*. The crash as the music stopping works, and it's the one familiar touchstone we need. The sharper mirror: his trade was nights that end. Every one of his 150 plays had a closing night. When the big party stopped, what carried him was the pleasure he'd loved all along, shared every day with strangers. Signposted: I like to think a man who'd brought down 150 curtains knew the ending is part of the show. That's the position, and it's in his life.
