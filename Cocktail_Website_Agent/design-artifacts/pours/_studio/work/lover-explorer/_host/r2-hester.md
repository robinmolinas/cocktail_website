**1941, settled.** The *Journal of the American Dietetic Association* reviewed the guide in August 1942 and gave its imprint as M. Barrows, 1941. The archive.org copy dated 1946 reads "Copyright 1941 by Crosby Gaige … Fifth Printing" (that's OCR of its copyright page). Oxford agrees (GAIGE pdf 861). Joy's 1945 appears on every Gaige page it has (pdf 249, 373 and others), which most likely means Regan owned a later printing. That last part is my inference. So the reading says 1941 or gives no year, and never 1945. **Tomás:** your spec's version line says "Crosby Gaige's 1945 guide". Please make it 1941 or "his cocktail guide". The recipe details still rest on Joy's copy (secondary).

**The lead that changes the after: *Dining with My Friends* is real, and it came out after he died.** Crown published it in 1949. He died on 8 March that year (UP wire, *Kingston Daily Freeman*, the same day). *Saturday Review* calls it "posthumously published", and Kirkus reviewed it that November. Its contents run as dinners, his first and then his friends' (OCR of the contents page; the book itself is lending-only and I haven't read it). So "the company lasted" is on the record, but only after he'd gone. That's your call. It's the truest version of "the people don't vanish", and it puts his death at the end of the reading. If you take it: no cause of death, no hospital, and never that he saw the book out.

**Watch Hill Farm: yes. The herb garden: not found.** The obituary gives his home as Watch Hill Farm, Peekskill. The page has "the loveliest of flower gardens" beside the barn where his private press worked (*Print*, Summer 1941). So "herbs" stays out.

**Fine press: yes, but it's the before.** He published limited editions in 1927–28 (I read the colophon of Sassoon's *The Heart's Journey*: 590 copies on rag paper; Joyce's *Anna Livia Plurabelle* followed in 1928), and he'd handed the list over by December 1928 (Paul Shaw). What does run through the after is the barn press printing dinner menus, one of them for the Wine & Food Society of New York's 1941 tasting at the Waldorf-Astoria.

**Two guards for your reframe.** First, the crash didn't close his theatre. Shaw, relaying the *Times* obituary, has him producing *Othello* and *Macbeth* in 1935, so the radio ran alongside Broadway for a while. Never "the curtain fell in 1929". Second, Oxford's figure is "a twenty-five-year career encompassing 150 plays", not 150 he produced alone. "Seen 150 plays close" is safe inside your "I like to think". "Brought down 150 curtains" claims more than the page does. A 1976 wine-auction catalogue says he "lost them all". It's a fragment, 27 years on, and it stays out.
