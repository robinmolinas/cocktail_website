## whoYouAre

You walk into a room and it shifts a little. People notice you, and some of them are wary before you've said a word. So you've learned to go slowly and speak softly, and let them find out for themselves.

People assume you were born gentle. You weren't. You decide it, again and again, and you've never told anyone how often. There's a temper in you, and you know exactly what it could do, most of all when someone is picking on a person who can't answer back. So you don't shout. You go and stand beside them, and the room settles. That's what you give people: when you're there, nobody has to fight. Nobody even has to be brave.

What you'd rarely say is what frightens you. Not getting hurt. The day being strong isn't enough: when it's happening to someone you care about, and nothing you've got can stop it.

## yours

1. In 1949, California's liquor board ruled that the Black Cat Café in San Francisco was a "disorderly house". The ruling's complaint was who drank there: gay men and women met at the Black Cat. Its owner, Sol Stoumen, stood to lose his licence. He kept serving them, and he answered with a lawyer. Morris Lowenthal argued that the Black Cat's customers had every right to gather there, and in 1951 the state's Supreme Court agreed: people behaving properly in a bar have a right to be served.

2. The state came back. One historian counts it as a fourteen-year battle, and just before the bar's Halloween party in 1963, the licence was taken. The party went ahead without alcohol. One witness remembers hundreds of people still there at six in the morning, drinking only soft drinks and juices.

3. I like to think that night says more about Sol Stoumen than the court case does: the licence was his power, it was gone, and he kept the room open for them until morning. That's the part of you people rarely see. Not the strength. What you do once the strength has run out.

4. The cocktail I've made for you is a Horse's Neck, an old classic that began life as a soft drink: ginger ale with a long, long twist of lemon, and a "stick" of spirit only for those who asked. An old drinks reference calls cognac with ginger ale a "winter warmer", so cognac it is: a deeper, older one, so that even with the ginger ale leading three to one, you can taste it clearly underneath. Strength, there the whole time, never raised. The whole lemon is peeled in one unbroken spiral and hung inside the glass before anything is poured, so it's already a drink before the cognac goes in. Nobody wrote down what was poured at the Black Cat that night. The soft drink comes first in this glass in honour of it.

5. There will be a day when being strong isn't enough: trouble you can't stop, happening to someone you'd stand in front of. Don't go home. Stay until morning, and give them whatever you've still got, even if it's only company. It was never your strength they came to you for. It was you.

## Resonance

**As the guest (a Gentle Giant).** Tagline: stops me; it names the thing everyone around me knows and nobody says, and it's flattering only in the way the truth is. Epigraph works cold (what night? why only soft drinks?) and doesn't echo the tagline. whoYouAre ¶2 is the line: *"People assume you were born gentle. You weren't. You decide it, again and again, and you've never told anyone how often."* The gift is said out loud ("nobody has to fight"). The fear lands without frightening because the title block came first. In the story I see myself not in the lawsuit but in six in the morning. The drink is the shape of me (soft drink leading, strength underneath). y5 tells me what to do with my worst fear, and ends by telling me it was me all along. Ready for the audit, pending Tomás's spec.
