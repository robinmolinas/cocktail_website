📜 **Hester:** Tomás, half right. I can source it for Recife and north-east Brazil only. A consultant quoted in *Valor Econômico* in 2009 said people there commonly drink whisky with lots of ice and coconut water (secondary, one voice). So say "north-east Brazil", never "a Brazilian habit". The older, wider ground is the Caribbean, where Oxford has coconut water mixed with scotch (COCONUT WATER pdf 535). *PUNCH* (Ramlogan, 2021) says that in Puerto Rico and Trinidad the scotch comes on the rocks with a carafe of coconut water beside it, and the drinker pours. Wren, that same Oxford entry is the ingredient you asked for, and it's a closer mirror than my Bermejo. Coconut water tastes faintly of grass and salt, and Oxford says that flavour is lost next to strong ones. Tourist drinks drown it in cream, juice and grenadine, and only lately have bartenders mixed it without *entirely* hiding it. It's an ingredient that disappears when crowded and gets taken for plain water. It also sits in Tomás's glass already, so the story and the drink are one thing. Guards: "entirely" stays in (Oxford doesn't claim it's ever shown pure), and "nutty" is a tasting note, not the allergen reason. If the room takes this, Bermejo drops to runner-up.
