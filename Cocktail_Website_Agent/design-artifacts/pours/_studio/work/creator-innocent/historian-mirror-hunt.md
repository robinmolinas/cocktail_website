# Mirror hunt: The Minimalist (creator-innocent), round 1 — Hester

Person (persona.py): true creativity is knowing what to leave out; stripped to the purest form; space for imagination; speaks quietly. Fear: status quo, mediocrity. Innocent side: purity, honesty, trust, wonder; fears punishment, danger, moral dilemma.

The shape I hunted for: **leaving out as the creative act, and as a kind of honesty**, done by someone the world might mistake for plain.

## A (my pick): Julio Bermejo and the Tommy's Margarita (person + drink)
**Story.** By the late 1970s the ordinary Margarita was sour mix, cheap triple sec and not much tequila (Oxford). At his family's unremarkable restaurant in San Francisco, Bermejo, back behind the bar after his own plans failed, decided "as a young person with no authority" to sell only 100% agave tequila at four times the cost, threw out the sour mix, and over years took the orange liqueur out too, sweetening with agave syrup instead (Proper pp. 81, 84). What was left was tequila, lime and agave, a drink that shows the spirit instead of hiding it; he called it the house Margarita, others gave it the restaurant's name, and it went round the world (Proper p. 84; Oxford).

**Why it mirrors.** The subtraction *is* the invention: nothing was added, three things were removed. And the removal is a form of honesty (the Innocent): he took out what was there to cover up. The fear of mediocrity is in the record too: the drink he stripped was the mediocre one.

**Spark for Tomás.** Agave syrup is *commonly* made from blue agave, the tequila plant (Oxford pdf 79): nearly everything in the glass but the lime comes from one plant. Our reading, hedged ("commonly"). Gesture on the record: Bermejo dilutes his syrup so none clings to the jigger (Joy pdf 397).

**Sources.** Oxford MARGARITA pdf 1236–1237, HERRADURA pdf 1001, AGAVE NECTAR pdf 79; *A Proper Drink* pp. 79–84; *Joy* pdf 397. Card: `_studio/fact-cards/tommys-margarita.md` (F1–F16, C1–C2).

**Risks.** Year contested (1990 / c. 1990 / early 1990s with syrup late 1990s): say "over years". "Invented" is too strong: it evolved and he didn't name it. His father's reaction ("slapped his son's ears back") is ambiguous: keep it out, or "wasn't impressed" only. The Denny's detail (Proper p. 84) cuts both ways for someone who fears the ordinary: Wren's call.

## B (runner-up): Dick Bradsell's Bramble (person + ingredient)
**Story.** At Fred's Club in Soho, Bradsell took a Singapore Sling he'd made elsewhere, dropped the soda and the Bénédictine, shortened the glass and gave it a British name (Oxford BRAMBLE pdf 336). In his own telling, tasting a blackberry liqueur put him "back in that blackberry patch on the Isle of Wight", and he built a plain gin sour around it when fashion was all vodka (Proper p. 31).

**Why it mirrors.** Subtraction plus a child's memory: the Innocent's wonder, kept simple. **Why it's second:** the "leaving out" is only in Oxford's account; Proper's (also Bradsell's own) tells it as a liqueur epiphany, and the dates differ (Oxford 1989; Proper menu 1991 or '92, "Bradsell was not sure"). The mirror rests on one source. Also London dry gin (the menu already has three; non-binding).

**Link.** Bermejo and Bradsell each asked the other to make his drink when they met (Proper p. 82).

## Considered, not offered
- **The Old-Fashioned** (Oxford pdf 1427–1429): drinkers asked for the "old-fashioned" cocktail to get the plain one; it was later cluttered with fruit and restored in the revival. True and nicely subtractive, but its shape is preference for the old way (status quo), the persona's fear. Legend caught: the Pendennis Club origin (Chicagoans ordered it before the club existed).

## Round 2 addendum: coconut water (answers Tomás; offers Wren her ingredient)
- **Tomás's question.** Partly true. Sourced for north-east Brazil only (Recife, "common", one consultant, *Valor Econômico* 2009, secondary). Oxford (pdf 535) has scotch with coconut water around the Caribbean; *PUNCH* (Ramlogan 2021) has it in Puerto Rico and Trinidad, served as scotch on the rocks with a carafe of coconut water beside it. Say "the Caribbean and north-east Brazil", never "a Brazilian habit".
- **Wren's ingredient.** Oxford, COCONUT WATER (pdf 535): subtle, lost next to strong flavours, usually drowned in tourist drinks, and only lately mixed without being entirely hidden. An ingredient that disappears when crowded. This is a truer mirror of "crowding gets to them" and "clear heard as plain" than Bermejo's story, and it belongs to Tomás's glass. The gesture on record: it comes on the side, and the drinker pours.
