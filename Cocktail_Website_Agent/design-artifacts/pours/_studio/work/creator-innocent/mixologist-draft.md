## Recipe (for the pour)

- **glassware:** rocks glass (a short, heavy tumbler), salt on half the rim, one large clear ice cube
- **contains:** `[]`

| amount | item | note (shown) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 60 ml | 100% agave blanco tequila | |
| 22.5 ml | fresh lime juice | |
| 20 ml | thinned blue agave syrup (below) | the same plant as the tequila |
| 1 | large clear ice cube (below) | made a day ahead, with the cloudy part cut off |
| a pinch | kosher salt, for half the rim | not needed; there if you want it |

**Thinned agave syrup:** stir equal parts blue agave syrup and water until they're one. Keeps two weeks in the fridge. (He thins his so none of it stays behind in the measure.)

**Clear ice** (a day or two ahead): fill a small picnic cooler with hot tap water, leave it on the counter to cool, then put it in the freezer with the lid off. It freezes from the top down, pushing the air and the cloudiness into the last water to freeze. Take it out before it's frozen solid, tip the block out and cut away the cloudy part. Let the block sit until it looks wet and clear, not frosty. Then score it with a bread knife and tap the back of the blade with a rolling pin until one large cube splits off. Keep the spares in a bag in the freezer.

## Method (v1.1; Wren to read)

1. A day or two ahead, make the clear ice (above).
2. Rub the cut side of a squeezed lime half around the outside of the rim (the outside only), halfway round the glass only. Roll that wet half in a saucer of salt. The other half stays bare.
3. Put your clear cube in the glass.
4. Pour the tequila, the lime juice and the thinned agave syrup into a shaker.
5. Fill the shaker with ice and shake hard for about ten seconds, until the outside is too cold to hold.
6. Strain it over the clear cube. Nothing on top.

**Salt:** on the *outside* of half the rim. The principle is the *Codex*'s (p. 180): a properly balanced drink doesn't need extra salt, so they rim only to honour a classic's heritage, only half the glass so the guest can choose each sip or skip it, wetting the outside only (with the drink's own citrus) so nothing falls in. p. 212 (the Paloma) is just one worked example of the method. It's on the glass, not in the drink, so it moves no numbers. Source for the idea: Regan's suggestion (*Joy* pdf 398), so "some bartenders", never "he".

**closingLine (settled with Wren, round 6):** *Cut the cloudy part off the ice. Then stop cutting.*
- Round 5 I gave it up (I thought it restated her ending); round 6 Wren gave it back, and her reason convinced me: *If nothing tastes missing, nothing is.* argues against her own last paragraph (keep one thing you love; don't cut until nothing's missing). A closing line has to agree with the position. Mine is an action that's true in every glass (method step 1) and says the ending in the drink's own words.
- Checked: no "light" (As It Was), no "take away" (the tagline), no echo of the epigraph.
- Dropped: *If nothing tastes missing, nothing is.* (argues against the position) / *Taste it before you add anything. You won't need to.* / *Nothing else goes in. That was the work.* (both contradict the salt).

## Image brief (draft)

> A single heavy rocks glass alone on a pale linen-grey stone counter, with wide empty space around it. In it, a shaken tequila-and-lime drink, pale straw-green and softly hazy, with one large, perfectly clear square ice cube whose top edge stands just above the liquid, sharp-cornered and glass-clear. No garnish. Coarse salt on the outer rim of one half of the glass only; the other half bare. Off to one side, at a distance, a small wooden board with a bread knife resting on it, and beside the knife the thin cloudy-white slab of ice that was cut away. One lime, a single half of it squeezed. Soft, even daylight from a window, light blue in the shadows. + HOUSE STYLE + AVOID

- **Glass and drink:** a heavy, thin-rimmed rocks glass. The drink is shaken lime, so it's hazy and pale straw-green, never neon or clear. One big clear cube, visibly clearer than the drink around it. No garnish. Coarse salt on the outside of half the rim only, the other half bare and clean (the name).
- **Props (story, three only):** the cloudy offcut on the board (what was cut away); the bread knife (the cut); the single squeezed lime (the one thing that isn't the plant). Nothing else. The empty space is part of the brief (the persona's imagery: single-object focus, air around it).
- **One impossible detail:** seen through the clear cube, the wall behind the glass is *sharper* than it is around it, as if the ice clears the view instead of bending it.
- **Must not appear:** bottles of sour mix or orange liqueur (don't show what was left out; the offcut says it); sombreros, cacti, papel picado or any Mexican-restaurant cliché; paint, brushes or design tools (the literal artist); a second drink; smoke; text or readable labels; clutter.
- **Palette:** the persona's soft light grey, calm light blue and warm beige (Pantone 7541 C, 290 C, 7527 C) in the room; the only colour in the glass is its pale straw-green.
