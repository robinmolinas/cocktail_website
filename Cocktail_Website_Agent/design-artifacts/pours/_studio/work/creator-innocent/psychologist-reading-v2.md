## whoYouAre

You rewrite a message three times, and every time it gets shorter. The last version looks like it took a minute. Most of what you make is what you said no to.

You don't want less. You want to feel each thing properly, and too much of anything drowns it out. Some people mistake the quiet for coldness, or for plain. The ones who know you know it's room: what you make leaves space, and people find their own meaning in it.

## yours

In the mid-1980s, Julio Bermejo was back behind the bar of his family's restaurant in San Francisco, an ordinary neighbourhood place, pouring Margaritas like everyone else's: sour mix, cheap tequila, orange liqueur. Then he tasted a tequila made only from agave, and decided the bar would pour nothing else, at about four times the cost. He made that call, he says, "as a young person with no authority".

Over the years the sour mix went, and then the orange liqueur, with a little agave syrup in its place. What was left was tequila, lime and nothing to hide behind. He called it the house Margarita. Other people gave it the restaurant's name.

I like to think he knew what you know: the hard part is what you take away, and nobody thanks you for what isn't there.

So this one keeps his three ingredients and his habit of thinning the syrup with water, so none of it clings to the spoon. The measures are mine. The syrup is blue agave, the plant tequila must come from by law, so everything in the glass except the lime and the water comes from one plant.

Sometimes you cut one thing too many, and it's usually the one that was only there because you loved it. The salt on this glass isn't needed. It's there on one side, in case you want it. Leave one thing like that in whatever you make next. Not because it's needed. Because you like it.
