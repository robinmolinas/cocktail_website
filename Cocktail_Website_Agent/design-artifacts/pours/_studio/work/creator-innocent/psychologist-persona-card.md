# Persona card: The Minimalist (creator-innocent)

Wren, round 1. Written from `persona.py creator-innocent`, the ten Creator siblings, the approved Visionary and True Friend, the Sculptor, Photographer and Experimentalist drafts, the registry and my voice ledger.

## Core truth
The Minimalist makes things by taking away. Every good thing they make is mostly what they said no to, and that's the part nobody sees. They don't do it to look clean or clever. They do it so the one thing that's left can be felt properly. Too much of anything and they can't hear themselves think.

## Shows / hides
- **Shows:** calm, clear, "easy". The short message, the bare table, the one thing on the wall, the answer in one sentence. People assume it comes naturally, or that it's a style, or a kind of coolness.
- **Hides:** two things. First, the work of it: the message rewritten three times, shorter each time; the drafts, the things they loved and cut anyway. Leaving out is harder than putting in, and nobody gives credit for what isn't there. Second, why they need it: they feel things *more*, not less. A crowded room, a crowded plate, a crowded day gets to them. Keeping things few is how they keep each one worth something.

## Hidden fear → hidden gift
- **Fear:** being mistaken for empty. That "clear" will be read as "plain", "simple" as "nothing much", quiet as cold (Creator: mediocrity; Innocent: honesty, purity, "seek paradise"). And, underneath, noise: losing the one true thing under everything piled on top of it.
- **Gift:** they make room. Around them, people can breathe and think. What they make leaves space for the other person to find their own meaning in it (the source: "leaving space for imagination"). They don't tell you what to feel; they clear the table so you can.

## The "real me" line to aim for
- Draft A (the scene, a little funny): *"You rewrite a message three times, and every time it gets shorter."*
- Draft B (the hidden need): *"People think you want less. You just want to feel each thing properly."*
- Draft C (the unseen work): *"Most of what you make is what you said no to. Nobody sees that part."*
- **Position seed** (not an ending yet, room decides; Ryu's order, earned): the hard truth is that they cut past the point, and sometimes what they cut is themselves: the flourish that was only there because they liked it. Something like: keep one thing that doesn't need to be there, and let it be yours. Kind, in their interest, and far from the other endings (Sculptor "make it where they can see you", Photographer "print it and give it", Experimentalist "stay a little longer", Visionary "pass the shaker", True Friend "keep one cup back").
  - Watch: True Friend's "keep one cup back for yourself" is close in shape (keep something for you). The position must be about *not cutting it*, not about self-care.

## Truth vs. legend
The plain true story, told plainly. No legend, no embellishment, no three-act origin. If the facts are thin, say less rather than fill it. An account that was later stripped of its decoration would delight them; a story padded with anecdotes would bore them.

## Tone
Creator (inspirational) + Innocent (honest, optimistic, straightforward) → quiet, plain, warm, a little dry. **This should be the shortest reading in the set so far.** Short sentences, no lists, no adjectives doing work a fact could do. The form is the recognition: if the reading is cluttered, we've already told them we don't know them. Funny only in small, true observations ("shorter every time").

## Distinct from siblings
- **The Visionary** (creator-hero, approved) sees it big and fears making it smaller. The Minimalist makes it smaller on purpose, and that's where it comes alive. Never frame "smaller" as a loss here; never frame it as better than the Visionary either.
- **The Sculptor** (creator-caregiver) adds care until it's right, for one person, and hides the cost. The Minimalist takes away until it's right, for the thing itself. Don't use "the smallest thing" (Sculptor's epigraph), "proof", "no shortcuts" or "perfectionism".
- **The Photographer** (creator-regular-guy) keeps everything ordinary so it counted. The Minimalist lets go of most of it. Don't use "the detail only you noticed".
- **The Experimentalist** (creator-explorer) makes many versions and never the same twice. The Minimalist makes few, and wants the one that's final.
- Unauthored, to protect: **the Old Master** (craft, restraint through mastery) must keep "years of practice"; the Minimalist's restraint is a need for clarity, not a skill earned. **The Auteur** controls everything; the Minimalist removes things rather than directing them.
- Secondary family: **the True Friend** (innocent-regular-guy) is the Innocent as trust in people. The Minimalist is the Innocent as honesty in things: nothing pretending to be more than it is. Stay off the Connoisseur's ground too ("Nothing in this glass is pretending" is their epigraph; deception is the Sage's fear, not this one's).

## Traps
- **Lifestyle labels.** Muji, white walls, Scandinavian, "Zen", tidying brands, capsule wardrobes, "less is more", "clean lines". Catalogue talk, and half the guests don't live that way. The mirror is in the act (leaving out), not the décor.
- **Coldness or judgement.** "You can't stand clutter", "you judge other people's mess". It makes them sound superior. Their need is calm, not taste.
- **Praise.** "Elegant", "effortless", "refined taste". It's the compliment they already get and don't trust, because it misses the work.
- **Diagnosis.** "Control", "anxious", "sensory overload", "OCD". Say it plainly and kindly: "too much, and you can't hear yourself think."
- **The repeated fear formula.** "What scares you isn't X. It's Y." is already twice in the set. The fear arrives another way.
- **A clever-minimal drink.** A three-ingredient drink with a speech about three ingredients is a list, not a recognition. And the Flavor Matrix spark must not become an extra thing bolted on: for this person the spark is *one* choice that makes the rest unnecessary, or something clearly left out.
- **Silence as absence.** The reading can be short; it can't be thin. Every sentence must carry weight or go.
