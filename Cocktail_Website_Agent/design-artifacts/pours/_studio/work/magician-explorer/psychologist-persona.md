# The Alchemist (magician-explorer): persona card, round 1 (Wren)

Source: persona.py magician-explorer (story, fear "Unintended Results" = "a result they can't undo", drivers Leave a mark / Seek paradise).

## The one true thing
They'll try something a hundred ways, as long as every way can still be taken back. The fear isn't failing: a failed try is reversible, and that's what the notes are for. The fear is the last step, the one that fixes the result for good (poured, cut, sent, said). So the nearly-finished thing waits at "one more adjustment" long after it's ready.

## Hidden
The hope. They look careful, sceptical, methodical. Underneath, they want the transformation to be real as badly as anyone who ever believed in the philosopher's stone, and they'll never say "I think this could be wonderful" until it's proven. The method is how they protect the hope.

## Fear
A result they can't undo. Not a mess (the Apprentice), not a mishearing (the Genie): the step that can't be reversed.

## Gift
They don't believe anything is just what it is. Give them something ordinary and given up on, and they come back, slowly, with a better version. To others it's proof that things can actually get better, and the patience to get there.

## Position (encouragement)
The step you can't undo is the point of all the trying. A result you can't take back is also one nobody can take from you. Take it.

## Siblings (primary) and how this one differs
- Apprentice: starts before ready, it runs away in scale. Alchemist: over-ready, holds back the final step. (Mirror opposites of the family fear.)
- Genie: someone else's wish. Alchemist: their own long chase.
- Makeover Artist (*Overnight*): calm in the ugly middle of a change for someone; "don't fix what you find". Alchemist: can't stop fixing.
- Clairvoyant: sees what it will become. Alchemist: makes it become, by trying.
- Entertainer: practises for an audience. Alchemist: the trials are never shown.
- Fairy Godparent, Good Wizard, Omnipotent One, Illusionist, Siren, Trickster: no overlap in the core.

## Cross-family traps (owned ground)
- *Off-Label*: "most of what you try doesn't work and you don't mind", "let me try something", repetition = "a door closing". Never failure-as-fun.
- *Serviceable*: "some of what you try goes nowhere... you keep them, every one", the notebook of struck-through marks. The Alchemist's notes appear once at most, as the way back, never as a record of dead ends.
- *In a Minute*: patience and time as the gift. Ours is patience *aimed at one result*, never slowness.
- *For Good* (name + "for good"): avoid the phrase.
- *Nothing to It*: scared and goes anyway; "take the sugar off at amber" is already a point-of-no-return in a glass. Our step must not be a caramel, and the position isn't courage, it's letting it be finished.
- *Beside the First*: revising one's own famous work. Ours is never yet finished.
- *Overnight*: accidents, overnight waits. *Rosetta*: the cloud.
- Family traps: gold, lead into gold, elixir, philosopher's stone, potion, "magic" puns; Flamel stays out (legend). No health or purity-as-health.

## Story test
1. A long chase on one material or design, with an earlier version on the record (cheaper, cruder, replaced).
2. A step on the page that committed it, and could not easily be taken back.
3. The result is better in the source's own terms, not just different.
4. Doubt or scorn from others, dated correctly.
5. Out: accidents, secret-recipe legends as fact, elixirs, anything owned above. Private notes come from the person, not the page.

## Verdict on the plan's lead (Coffey)
Accept, with corrections (Oxford COFFEY, AENEAS pdf 541; COFFEY STILL pdf 543; CELLIER-BLUMENTHAL pdf 419, all checked today):
- Test 2 (notes) waived: carried by the person; never "secret" or "private notes" (a patent is public).
- The mirror: wood-and-iron prototype, "soon replaced" by copper double columns. Signposted reading: try it where it can be remade, commit it in copper. The page gives one prototype, not many: never "years of failed attempts" or a count.
- The step: inspector-general of excise in 1819, left "five years later (at the age of forty-four)" and bought the Dodder Bank Distillery. Order on the page: left 1824, patent 1830/1831. So the life step comes before the patent; the prototype is undated. "Couldn't take it back" is our reading, signposted.
- Plan error: "the basis of the modern column still" is Oxford's line about Cellier-Blumenthal's invention (pdf 419), not Coffey's. For Coffey use pdf 541/543: simpler, more robust, stronger and purer spirit, cheaper to heat; "revolutionized the British spirits industry by making continuous distillation commercially viable". Also pdf 541 says he "invented the first commercially viable continuous still adapted to distilling grain": that wording is safe; "invented continuous distillation" is not.
- Scorn: "initially scorned" is on the page; the quote is from 1878. Date it.
- Dore and the firm "still in business": leave out (handing on is *Down the Line*'s).
