# Persona card: The Thrill Seeker (outlaw-explorer) — Wren, round 1

Sheet: needs to feel alive, and safe rarely does it; jumps first and checks the depth afterwards; danger into a game, fear into a laugh with friends; behind it, a hunger for freedom, proof nobody can fence them in; the padded, sensible life where nothing happens *and nothing is theirs* frightens them more than any drop. Fear: powerlessness (Outlaw), entrapment (Explorer).

## Why my plan sketch has to change
My sketch's one true thing ("scared every time; the fear is how they know they're alive") and its position ("tell them you were scared") are already *Nothing to It* (the Pioneer): whoYouAre "You're scared every time. Nobody's ever been able to tell." and y5 "tell them you were terrified, and that you went". Building this pour on hidden fear makes it the Pioneer's reading with a bridge in it. So point 3 of my story test (visibly scared) is withdrawn, and with it my condition on the ITV page.

## The one true thing
They're terrified of being helpless, and they fling themselves off things. That isn't a contradiction. The drop is the one kind of helplessness they choose, on their own terms, with nobody's permission. What they can't bear is the other kind: the life that gets decided for them, slowly, sensibly, by other people. The jump is how they prove, to themselves first, that their life is still theirs.

- **Hide:** that it isn't about the adrenaline or the showing off. It's about who's in charge. And that the quiet, managed days frighten them far more than the edge does. They'd never say that, because said out loud it sounds like a complaint, and they'd rather laugh.
- **Fear:** powerlessness: being kept, steered, cushioned, the choice taken off them "for their own good". (Quieter, not for the reading unless earned: the day they can't jump any more.)
- **Give (say it out loud, Robin's rule):** they turn other people's fear into a laugh. Near them, the scary thing becomes a game everyone's in on, and the people around them get nights they still talk about years later. They make being alive feel like something you do, not something that happens to you.

## Siblings (how this one differs)
- **Pioneer** (*Nothing to It*): scared, goes first *so others can follow*, shrugs. Thrill Seeker goes for the feeling, not for whoever's next. No "went first", no "so they knew they could", no fear-hidden-under-calm, no "tell them you were terrified".
- **Rescuer** (*Off Duty*): runs toward danger for someone else. This one runs toward it for themselves.
- **Mad Inventor** (*Why Not?*): "There's an easier way. It just isn't as much fun." Close to "the sensible way is boring": never frame the reading as safe-vs-fun.
- **Quest Seeker** (*Someone Else's Map*): "Far doesn't scare you. Finished does." Avoid the "X doesn't scare you, Y does" frame in the title block.
- **Rule Breaker** (*Asked In*): makes the thing without permission; distrusts the yes. Thrill Seeker makes nothing; it's the body and the moment.
- **Gangster** (*Where You Stand*): owns "being pushed around ... with no say at all". Powerlessness is family-wide; ours is *chosen* helplessness vs *imposed* safety, never the system/procedure grievance.
- **Gentle Giant** (*In One Piece*): owns the fear of "the day being strong isn't enough".
- **Fugitive** (unauthored): runs away with someone; ours runs toward with friends. **Adolescent** (unauthored): guards a self against becoming fake; ours guards a life against becoming padded.
- **Left Standing** owns "don't go alone. Take someone".

## Traps
- Injury as punchline; danger glamorised; anything readable as "do something reckless tonight"; "adrenaline junkie".
- Drink tied to risk (family trap): no champagne in the jumper's hand, no "night of partying" before, no sparkling wine, no stunt enacted in the glass.
- The bartender must never become the padded life. No "be careful", no "test it first" as a moral. Equally, never "go further".
- "First" is the Pioneer's ground: if the story is the first bungee jump, "first" gets one plain clause, and nothing about everyone who followed (the industry after).
- The arrest is the sensible world's answer, a cost; never glamour of crime, never the guest as lawbreaker.
- Kirke's later life and death, the club's later fatality, Clifton's other associations: out.

## Story test (revised)
1. Something others called reckless, done for the feeling, not money or a cause.
2. Friends in it: watching, jumping too, laughing along.
3. **The choice is visibly theirs, against the sensible authority's say**, and the authority's answer is on the page (stopped, fined, told "never again").
4. The danger made into a game or a joke (costume, date, a deadpan line).
5. Cost is ridicule, money or bruises; ends in laughter or survival.
Out: fatal stunts, injuries, drink-driving, medicine, fear-on-the-page as the hinge.
