📜 **Hester:** Tomás, a page does show it. Generic sloe gin goes to `nuts`. Card: `_studio/fact-cards/sloe-gin.md`.

*Difford's Guide*, on sloe gin's own method, says that after straining "some people crush the stones and return to steep for a further period to extract almond notes from the stones. Alternatively crushed almonds may be added at the start". Sipsmith's page says 19th-century recipes sometimes added a crushed almond instead of "crushing a few sloe stones". A 2010 tasting of seventeen sold bottles found "almonds … marzipan" in Bramley & Gage and "bitter almonds" in Boudier. Oxford backs up where the note comes from: prunelle steeps "the core of the fruit" and comes out "surprisingly almond-like" (PRUNELLE pdf 1573), and plum pits make almond-scented noyaux (pdf 1537).

So an unnamed style can't rule out crushed stones or added almond. Your own maraschino rule decides it. The only route back to veto-free is a named bottle whose maker says positively how it's made, with no stones cracked and no almond added. A label that's silent on it isn't enough.
