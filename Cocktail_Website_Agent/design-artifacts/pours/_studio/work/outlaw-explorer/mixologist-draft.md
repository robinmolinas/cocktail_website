The spark is Cynar, accepted by Wren in r3 with her guard (never "milder than its reputation", never a dare). Spec: `_studio/specs/outlaw-explorer.json`.

**Glass:** coupe (about 180 ml), chilled
**Contains:** nuts (from the sloe gin, on the safe side: see Checks)

## Recipe
| amount | item | note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 45 ml (1½ oz) | sloe gin | a traditional British sloe gin of 25% or more; it brings the colour and most of the sweetness. Bought sloe gins may contain almond: if you avoid nuts, don't make this one |
| 30 ml (1 oz) | London dry gin | any London dry gin of 40% or more |
| 20 ml (⅔ oz) | fresh lemon juice | squeezed just before |
| 7.5 ml (1½ tsp) | Cynar | a bitter liqueur with artichoke among its flavourings |

## Method
1. Put the coupe in the freezer for ten minutes, or fill it with ice and water while you mix.
2. Pour the sloe gin, the gin, the lemon juice and the Cynar into a shaker. There's no syrup: the sloe gin comes sweetened already.
3. Fill the shaker with ice, close it, and shake hard for 10 to 15 seconds.
4. Empty the coupe, then strain the drink into it through a fine strainer, so no chips of ice get in. No garnish.

## Wren's three checks, against the spec (y4)
- **"deep red":** no page (Hester, audit A23). The only colour word on a page is *Imbibe!*'s "pinkify" (pdf 122), so the reading can say it turns the drink pink, or name no colour. The finished drink, shaken with lemon, is lighter: a bright, slightly cloudy pinkish red (my estimate from the bottles, unsourced). "Turns it red" is safe; "deep red" needs a page.
- **"sweet on its own, so it needs no added sugar":** sweet because sugar goes in when sloe gin is made (Oxford pdf 1814: "sweetened with sugar"). So "no added sugar" is wrong strictly. Say "made by steeping sloes in sweetened gin … so it needs no syrup". The spec has no syrup (version H, not J).
- **"shaken hard":** yes (method 3, 10-15 seconds). **"small measure":** yes, 7.5 ml (1½ teaspoons) against 95 ml of everything else.
- Also in y4: "it gives the sweet sloe the bitter edge it needs" is a craft claim, and mine is fine. Keep it as the bartender's view, not as the Matrix's.
