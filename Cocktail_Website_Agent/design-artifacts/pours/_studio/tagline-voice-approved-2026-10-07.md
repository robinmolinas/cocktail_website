## Voice learned from Robin's review

- The tagline is the first recognition when the cocktail appears. Lead with a recognisable personality trait and make the guest feel welcomed and valued.
- Read the full persona and pour before drafting. The line should describe the person, rather than depend on a particular detail of the historical story.
- Make sense before the guest has read the narrative. “Remember with your whole body” felt strange without context.
- Save the reading's earned realisation and proposal for later. The Spiritualist's search for magic belongs in the tagline; recognising what already keeps them steady belongs in the reading.
- Celebrate what the person gives others. The Saviour's reliability and generosity should be apparent before addressing their difficulty receiving help.
- Gentle tension is welcome when it supports recognition. Avoid presenting a failing, burden or verdict as the guest's first impression of their cocktail.
- Describe the Gentle Giant through the safety their strength offers. The earlier emphasis on the effort of gentleness was at the limit of how much conclusion belongs in a tagline.
- For the Judge, connect willingness to question their beliefs to fairness. Avoid language about making their own side “look bad”.
- Distinguish nearby personas: the Sense Seeker wants someone to feel what moved them; the Tourist thinks of someone when making a discovery.
- Vary rhythm across the collection. The accepted lines are references for tone and perspective, not a sentence template to repeat across all 132.
- The Researcher's defining trait is sustained exploration driven by care: the grit to keep searching, beyond the initial decision to start. Robin corrected number 10 and then accepted the revised line on 2026-10-08.
- The Healer believes in people before they believe in themselves. Robin broadened number 15 beyond a prediction that someone will be all right.
- Warmth must preserve the power of the idea. Robin rejected several first-pass Creator rewrites as basic, weak or cheesy; the distinguishing insight should survive the edit.
- Keep a strong existing line when it works. Robin explicitly retained the Minimalist's original and preferred the Experimentalist's original to the first rewrite.
- Metaphor is welcome when it is natural and immediately understandable. Robin asked for the gravity of the Visionary's dreams and for the Surrealist's shifts in perspective delivered with humour. Plain language does not require flattening those ideas.
- Give the persona's motive and distinctive behaviour alongside the before and after during review, so Robin can judge the connection.
- “The things you love become worlds you never finish exploring” is a particularly strong accepted reference: Robin said “I love 25 it hits”. The image reveals the depth and ongoing discovery in devotion, and makes sense before the reading. Use that precision and emotional force as a reference, rather than repeating its world/exploration motif for other personas.

The previous review, `tagline-review-2026-10-02.md`, remains useful for diagnosing repeated cadence. These accepted examples supersede its suggested replacements wherever the pairing is listed above.
