# Cocktail menu words

Robin's latest instruction, 12 October 2026, governs these descriptions.

Use exactly three familiar single words that let a guest imagine the drink without interpreting its recipe. The reference menus freely combine flavour, texture, character and occasional playful drink-style words. At least one word should feel inviting or evocative.

The evocative word comes from the cocktail's ingredients, taste, aroma, texture, intensity, temperature, finish or sensory aura. Tomás owns this judgement. The guest's personality, the psychological reading and the story's lesson do not justify a menu word.

For every word, give a concrete explanation from the served drink. Examples: Dreamy from airy cream-and-blossom foam; Smouldering from mezcal smoke lingering behind orange sweetness; Glacial from freezer-cold vodka's slow, thick pour; Woodland from steaming pine and allspice aromas.

No descriptor repeats a meaningful word from either the cocktail name or the personality title. Check obvious derivatives too. Renaming the person through a synonym is equally unhelpful when the glass does not earn the word.

Use the final recipe and method ahead of discarded room proposals. Preserve distinctions between sweetness, bitterness, sourness and drying grip; between spirit warmth and hot service; between silky body and cream flavour. Include the timing when a note develops through melting ice or arrives in a separate glass.

Repetition is acceptable when drinks genuinely share a sensory profile. Do not force unique trios or substitute grand personality adjectives to make rows look different.

Descriptions remain recipe-based predictions until the cocktails are tasted. A permitted substitute or a faint aroma can change the sensory profile; record a real uncertainty plainly in the review explanation.
