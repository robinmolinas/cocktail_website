## What the rooms found hard

- **Names.** Two rooms split 1–1–1, even with picks held until the vote. Everywhere else a 2–1 vote settled it in one step. In sage-outlaw, the room's pick (*On the Record*) wasn't Wren's, so after step 4 she changed the name line in her title block, and nothing else.
- **The stirred style for drinks with no full-strength spirit.** balance.py reads both the verjus Sidecar and the port drink OUT by structure. Both rooms kept their drink by choice and gave the reason in Checks. The rule candidate from batch outlaw (a stirred-spirit style with no acid floor) covers spirit-only drinks, not these, so a wine-core or freeform style would be a separate question for you.
- **The two-web-visit budget against unread primary texts.** Hester spent her visits looking for Barnard's own text and for a period source on Forrester, and found only later books quoting them. The library gaps are listed above.
- **Contested history.** Two rooms had sources that disagreed: whether Forrester was right (his own trade was at stake), and why Barnard left out the casks. In both, the reading says what's on the record and leaves the doubt in plain view ("We don't know why"). Hester struck her own phrasing twice when it went beyond the page: a merged quote about Forrester, and "a flavour both grains share" in sage-magician.
- **Crossings.** Rounds run in parallel, so audits sometimes read a version already being replaced. Each crossing was named in a host turn and settled in round 4 without an extra round.
