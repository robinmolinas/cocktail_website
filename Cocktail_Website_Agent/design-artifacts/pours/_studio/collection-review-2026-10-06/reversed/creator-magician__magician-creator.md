# Reversed pair: creator-magician / magician-creator


## creator-magician · The Makeover Artist · *Overnight* (draft)

- tagline: You change everything except the part that makes it theirs.
- epigraph: Last night, this drink looked like a mistake.
- drink: daiquiri · freeform · rum_aged_tea, tamarind_water, lime_juice, demerara_syrup, milk · a small tumbler (rocks glass), chilled, no ice
- closing: Leave it overnight, and don't fix what you find.

**whoYouAre**

Show you almost anything and you see what it could be: the chair someone put out on the pavement, a friend's flat, the haircut they've had since school. Once you start on it, you'll change nearly everything, except the one thing that makes it theirs. You find that part first, and build the rest around it. So the words you're hoping for at the end aren't "I'd never have recognised it." They're "That's so you."

What nobody sees is the middle. There's always a point where it looks worse than when you started: the room stripped to the walls, the paint half on, a friend at the mirror not sure what you've done. You're the only one who isn't worried. You've been through enough middles to know the mess is part of how it works. What worries you comes later, once you've gone: that it all slides back, or that it turns out in some way you didn't mean. What stays is how you looked at them: as if the best of them was worth moving everything else for. Most people have never been looked at like that.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. Next time something you've changed comes out differently from the picture in your head, don't fix it straight away. Give it a night. Look at it the way you'd look at that chair on the pavement. Arnold was only trying to get rid of a harsh edge, and he got silk. Some of your best work will be the part you didn't mean.


## magician-creator · The Genie · *On One Condition* (draft)

- tagline: Nobody gets what they asked you for. They get what they meant.
- epigraph: There are no orange bitters in this glass. Two other bottles stand in for them.
- drink: daiquiri · collins · gin_london_dry, vodka, lemon_juice, rosemary_syrup, cointreau, angostura, soda_water · tall (Collins) glass, chilled, filled with ice
- closing: Mix the stand-in first: that's the ask. Clap the rosemary last: that one's for you.

**whoYouAre**

Someone asks you for a few words at their party, and you come back with a toast that has the room crying and then laughing. Someone calls with a broken heart, and by the end of the night there's a plan, a playlist and a joke they'll repeat for years. You don't do small, and it isn't showing off. You listen for what the person meant under what they said, and you give them that.

What worries you is getting it wrong in the most literal way: doing exactly what was asked and watching it land flat, because the words were never really the wish. So you listen hard, and you give more, just in case. And with all those ideas flying, nobody thinks to turn round and ask what you'd wish for. You don't tell them either. In your head, wishes are something you grant. It gives people something that lasts, though. When you take a small request seriously enough to make it wonderful, you tell someone their wish mattered, and they remember that longer than whatever you made.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. So this is my wish for you. Whoever asks for your help next, say yes the way you always do, and before you start, name the one part you'd like to make entirely on your own terms. It isn't taking. It's usually where the best thing you make for them comes from, and for once, one of the wishes granted will be yours.
