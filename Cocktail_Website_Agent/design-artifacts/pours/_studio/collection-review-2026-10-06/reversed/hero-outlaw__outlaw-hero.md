# Reversed pair: hero-outlaw / outlaw-hero


## hero-outlaw · The Lovable Rogue · *No Promises* (draft)

- tagline: You never said you'd come. You always do.
- epigraph: This glass holds grocery-store bitters. A Brooklyn liquor shop wasn't supposed to sell them.
- drink: old-fashioned · built · rye_40, sorghum_syrup, underberg · small rocks glass (single old-fashioned, about 200 ml), one large ice cube
- closing: Stir the sorghum in first: once it's cold, it clings. Then tell one person you'll be there, ahead of time.

**whoYouAre**

A friend asks if you can help on moving day. "Probably," you say, with a grin that convinces nobody. On the day you're the first one there, with a van you borrowed from someone you won't name, and you leave after the last box is in. People introduce you with a warning: don't believe a word they say. You've never minded the warning.

Here's what it hides. You don't say you'll be there, because saying it out loud is a leash to you, and breaking it would be worse than anything they could call you. So you keep the bar low and clear it every time. You'd rather surprise them than let them down. When something really matters, your friends stop asking who's sensible and call you. You're the proof that someone can be rough round the edges and completely dependable, and to them that beats a good name any day.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. Next time someone you'd go anywhere for has a big day coming, skip the grin and the "probably". Say yes, plainly, the week before. You won't lose an inch of your freedom: you were always going to go. What changes is theirs. For once they get to know, instead of hope. And you might find the warning was never what they believed about you.


## outlaw-hero · The Maverick · *Can't Watch* (draft)

- tagline: It's rarely your fight. That's never once stopped you.
- epigraph: In 1968, a non-drinker got himself arrested for one of the spirits in this glass.
- drink: martini · stirred · ogogoro_pedros, gin_london_dry, sweet_vermouth, demerara_syrup, orange_bitters · a Nick & Nora, chilled
- closing: Equal measures, no more of either. Then pick the rule that isn't fair, and break it where it can be seen.

**whoYouAre**

Someone sighs: it's a pity, but those are the rules. You've already stopped listening. You're working out which rule it is, who it's really for, and what it would take to get round it by Friday. Not for you. Usually it isn't even your fight.

People put it down to nerve, or a taste for trouble. It's neither. It's that you can't watch. Being told to wait, to go through the proper channels, to leave it to the people who run things while it carries on happening: that's the thing that truly frightens you. And there's a part you'd never say out loud: some of it is for you. Doing something, even something that costs you, is how you stop feeling helpless.

So you fix it, quietly, one person at a time: a rule bent just for them, a back way found. It works. What it gives people is real: with you around, "those are the rules" stops being the end of the matter, and that matters more than you let yourself think. But you know the other half. The rule is still there in the morning, and so is the next person it lands on.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. You'll go on fixing things one person at a time, and you should. But once, pick the rule that keeps sending you back, and stop working round it on the quiet. Do it where people can see who did it, so the rule has to answer for itself. Not for the thanks. So it changes for everyone, and you get the thing you've wanted all along: never having to watch that one again.
