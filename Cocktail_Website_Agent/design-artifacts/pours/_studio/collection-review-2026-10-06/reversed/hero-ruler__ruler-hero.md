# Reversed pair: hero-ruler / ruler-hero


## hero-ruler · The Warrior · *Dressed for It* (draft)

- tagline: Everything's gone wrong, and you still set the table at seven.
- epigraph: The Crusta is named for its sugar crust. Its 1862 recipe ends with two words: "Then smile."
- drink: sidecar · stirred · cognac_black_tea, rich_syrup, orange_curacao, lemon_juice, angostura · small stemmed wine glass (about 200 ml), chilled, fine sugar on the outside of the rim, lined inside with a collar of lemon peel, no ice
- closing: Fit the peel inside the glass, then take your hand away: it stays.

**whoYouAre**

The week everything goes wrong, you still put a proper dinner on the table. You still turn up on Monday ironed and on time, and you'd quite like everyone else to as well. People roll their eyes. Someone always asks whether this is really the moment.

It is, and you know why, even if you seldom explain it. In a crisis, people watch whoever's in front to see how serious it is, and a rule that's still being kept tells them it isn't over. You rarely admit that the rules hold you up too, or that some nights you've agreed with every person who told you it was a terrible idea. What you guard against is wobbling in front of them. But look at what your rules give people: in their worst week, someone still expects something of them, so they still count. That's respect, and most people never think to offer it.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. Next time you're keeping a standard and someone calls it pretentious, or too much, don't hear it as all or nothing. Find the part they're right about, admit it plainly, and fix that part. You'll worry it looks like wobbling. It doesn't. That's how the rest of what you stand for holds, and people will trust your rules more for it.


## ruler-hero · The Noble Ruler · *A Place Kept* (draft)

- tagline: When you say there's room for everyone, there is.
- epigraph: Red wine floats on this sour. In 1883, a bartender said wine made his sours better.
- drink: daiquiri · shaken · jim_beam_white_label, lemon_juice, simple_syrup, red_wine_cabernet · small stemmed wine glass (about 250 ml), chilled, no ice in the glass
- closing: Float the red so it shows. Once in a while, so should the cost.

**whoYouAre**

When you tell people there's a place for them, they stop worrying. They can, because you've taken the worrying on yourself. You keep your word the way other people keep a house: every day, mostly unseen, and without asking whether it suits you this week.

You said there'd be a place for all of them. You never said what it cost you. Something of yours goes missing along the way, a plan, a weekend, the thing you'd hoped to put your name to, and you act as if it were the obvious thing to do. Partly because it is. Partly because you're afraid that if they saw you mind, they'd ask themselves whether the promise still holds. It does. Because of you, the ones who rely on you get to stop worrying about tomorrow, and most people go a whole life without that.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. One request, then. When keeping a promise costs you something, say so, once, to someone you kept the place for. The promise won't look any weaker. It'll look exactly like what it is: something you care about enough to pay for. And they'll finally get the chance to thank you, which you so rarely allow.
