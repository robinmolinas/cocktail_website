- closing: Two cubes for everyone. For one person, ask how they take it.

**whoYouAre**

Something small goes wrong in a room full of people. Someone arrives soaked, a glass goes over, somebody's aunt doesn't know a soul. Before it's become a thing, you're there with a towel, a dustpan, a chair and a name. You didn't wait to be asked. Sitting there watching would have felt stranger.

And everyone gets the same you. The friend of twenty years and the new neighbour, the boss and the person clearing the plates: the same question about their week, the same cup, made the same way. Having favourites feels a bit selfish to you, a bit unfair, so you don't. What you'd rarely admit is that the people closest to you get exactly what everyone gets. Not a bit more. They'd never say so.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. Don't change a thing about how you treat everyone. But being fair to everyone was never meant to mean that nobody gets to be special. Somewhere there's someone close to you who has been getting the same you as the new neighbour, and has never once complained. Give them something nobody else gets. Not more work: an evening, a call that isn't checking in, the good cup. The first of these drinks was made for a few particular guests. Let someone be yours.


## regular-guy-caregiver · The Good Samaritan · *Whoever Comes In* (draft)

- tagline: Most people learn not to look. You never did.
- epigraph: Part of this drink is made days ahead. The rest waits until someone's there.
- drink: old-fashioned · shaken · brandy_aged_grape, espresso, parsnip_syrup, saline_solution · chilled coupe
- closing: Make the syrup before you know who it's for.

**whoYouAre**

Most people pick up a knack from living among strangers: looking through someone. The person sitting by the cash machine with a paper cup, the one talking to nobody on the bus, the tourist turning the map round for the third time. Everyone else's eyes slide past. Your eyes stop. You say hello. You ask where they're trying to get to. For a minute, someone everyone had stopped seeing is just a person in the queue again.

What people miss is the cost. The ones you walk past, because you were late or tired, follow you home for longer than all the times you stopped. You'd hate to be called good. You're just more afraid of a street where everyone has stopped looking, where anyone could end up being stepped round, you included. The people you stop for get to be someone, not a problem. And everyone watching learns that stopping is normal.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. You can't stop for every one. Nobody can, and the ones you miss will come back to you later. So leave something waiting for them: a coffee paid ahead at the place you go every morning, an hour given somewhere they'll come in after you've gone. Then, some morning when you're late and you hurry past, you'll know something's already there for them, and they won't follow you home.
