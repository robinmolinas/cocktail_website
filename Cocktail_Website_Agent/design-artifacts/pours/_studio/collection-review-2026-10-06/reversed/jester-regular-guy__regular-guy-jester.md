# Reversed pair: jester-regular-guy / regular-guy-jester


## jester-regular-guy · The Stand-up · *Is It Just Me* (draft)

- tagline: You say what everybody does. Suddenly nobody's the only one.
- epigraph: A whiskey with a beer. In 1896, a newspaper thought that was worth a story.
- drink: highball · freeform · apricot_steeped_rye, beer · small stemmed glass (about 90 ml) for the rye, room temperature; tall pilsner glass (about 400 ml), chilled, for the beer
- closing: A sip of the rye, then the beer. And when you tell your story, start with "I".

**whoYouAre**

You're the one who says it. Not the clever thing, the true one: that everybody waves back at someone who was waving at the person behind them, that everybody answers "you too" when the waiter says "enjoy your meal". There's half a second of quiet, then the whole table laughs, and somebody says, "I thought that was just me." That's the sound you're after. An evening where everyone stays polite and nobody admits to anything is your idea of a very long night.

You keep one part quiet. Every one of those starts with you. You're the one who waved back. You say "everybody does this" because it's kinder, and safer, than "I do". So you're always in the joke, and hardly ever what it's about. Somewhere under the laughing is a small worry that one day you'll say it and the table will go quiet, and it really will be only you. It hardly ever is. And people go home feeling a little less strange than when they came. You did that, with one true sentence.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. So swap one word. When you catch yourself saying "everybody does this", say "I do this" instead. The laugh still comes, I promise, and this time you're inside it, not telling it. People enjoy you for the joke. They feel close to you for the confession. And you'll find what that order in 1896 turned out to show: it was never just one person's.


## regular-guy-jester · The Prankster · *Got You* (draft)

- tagline: You only play jokes on the people you want to keep.
- epigraph: Built on Chicago's bitter liqueur for willing visitors. The face they pull has a name.
- drink: daiquiri · freeform · jeppsons_malort, grapefruit_juice, maple_syrup, saline_solution · coupe, chilled
- closing: Freeze the grapefruit hours ahead. Pour it for someone new, and let that be the only setup.

**whoYouAre**

You don't play jokes on just anyone. The new one at work, a friend's quiet cousin, the neighbour who's only just started coming to the barbecue: they're who you pick. Salt in the sugar bowl, a made-up house rule, a serious look held a beat too long. Then you watch. The moment their face cracks and they laugh is the moment they're in, and a week later they're telling it themselves: the day you got them.

Someone got you first, and you rarely mention it. That's how you got in, and every joke since has been you passing it on. You remember the one that didn't land, too: the face that never came round. It's why you can feel the line before you reach it. And there's a smaller worry you'd never admit: if nobody ever gets you back, you start to wonder whether you're really in. Here's what you give, and it should be said plainly: a stranger leaves with a story that makes them one of you. People feel claimed. That lasts a lot longer than the laugh.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. Keep playing your jokes. They work. But once, with someone you've decided to keep, do what that letter did: no joke to hide behind, just what you're glad they're here for. It feels more exposed than any prank. It's also the surest way to learn you're in, because people answer plain words in kind. And one day, one of them will get you back, properly.
