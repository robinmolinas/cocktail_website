# Reversed pair: outlaw-ruler / ruler-outlaw


## outlaw-ruler · The Leading Edge · *Before It Had a Name* (draft)

- tagline: Everyone sees the rebel. Nobody sees the plan.
- epigraph: Old Potrero began as a brewer's next idea, once other brewers had followed his lead.
- drink: sidecar · shaken · old_potrero_rye, cointreau, lemon_juice, quince_paste_loosened · a well-chilled coupe
- closing: Mash the quince until no lumps are left. Then pick what you'll steer yourself, and let the rest run.

**whoYouAre**

You catch it before anyone has a word for it: a sound, a look, an idea a few people keep circling. And you're the one who gives it a shape, a name, a place, a first version people can point at and join.

People see the rebel. They don't see the plan, and you prefer it that way, because wanting to steer sounds like the very thing you were all against. So you steer quietly. What worries you is what it turns into afterwards: run by people who never got it, smoothed out for everyone, your hands nowhere near it.

And here's the good of it: most new things die young, talked out of it or copied badly. The ones you start tend to grow up, because someone was steering while they were fragile.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. So let me steer, just once. When something you started takes off, don't try to hold on to all of it. Decide what you'll keep steering yourself, and do it properly. Let the rest run in other people's hands. That isn't it getting away from you. It's how you'll know it was real. And it leaves you free for the next thing nobody has a name for yet.


## ruler-outlaw · The Revolutionary · *Had to Be Serious* (draft)

- tagline: You didn't just tear it down. You stayed to run it.
- epigraph: In one rebel drink, these bitters replaced the Pimm's. In this glass, they share it.
- drink: highball · highball · strawberry_muddled, cucumber_muddled, lemon_juice, pimms_no1, peychauds, soda_water · Collins glass, filled with ice
- closing: Spoon the bitters in before the soda. When the next one comes, ask what they'd put in its place.

**whoYouAre**

You were the one who said the old way was finished. People laughed. Then they let you try, it worked, and the new way was yours to keep going: the rota, the budget, the wet Tuesday when nobody remembers why anything changed. Most people who start a fight are gone by then. You stayed.

You'd rarely admit it, but you never wanted a free-for-all. You wanted it run properly, by you, and you had the plan for afterwards before the argument was over. The day you dread is someone new standing where you stood, saying what you said, and you hearing yourself answer the way they once answered you. But staying is the hard part, and hardly anyone thanks you for it. You make the better way real, and keep it real, so the people who wanted it actually get to live in it.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

6. When someone you've taught comes back and tells you it's all got a bit boring, don't defend it first. Ask what they'd do instead, and give them something towards it. You had your answer ready once. So might they, and whatever they build will carry some of you in it.
