# Reversed pair: innocent-outlaw / outlaw-innocent


## innocent-outlaw · The Outsider · *Fresh Air* (draft)

- tagline: Nobody quite believes you when you say it's enough. It is.
- epigraph: In 1862 a famous bar book printed this drink with sweet cider. Others kept pouring it tart.
- drink: highball · highball · rum_aged, dry_cider · a heavy tumbler (about 300 ml), two or three small lumps of ice
- closing: Carry it out of doors before you taste it. Whoever follows you out will understand.

**whoYouAre**

At a wedding or a birthday, someone asks what you're up to these days. You tell them: the same place, the work you like, your usual walk in the morning. Then you watch them wait politely for the rest. There isn't any rest. Years ago you stepped out of the race everyone else is running, quietly, and you don't miss it.

Living with less was easy. Being loved by people who think you're wasting yourself is harder. "You could do so much more." They mean it kindly, which is why you don't argue. And under that sits a quieter fear. No one will drag you back in. But someone kind might ask, with a job you'd be good at or a favour "just for a while", and once you're back indoors, the part of you they liked would turn sour.

Your way does something for people, and someone should tell you. After an afternoon with you, they go home wanting a little less, and lighter for it. You make "enough" look like a choice, not a defeat, and you show them someone can say no and stay kind.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. You don't owe anyone a bigger life, or an explanation of this one. Thoreau once kept a smooth face while tempting a companion with one of those sour apples. By winter, when frost and thaw had turned them sweet, the two of them were filling their pockets together. So stop defending your life to the ones who worry about you. Have one of them come along. Some will taste what you taste, and you'll have someone to fill your pockets with.


## outlaw-innocent · The Adolescent · *Kept* (draft)

- tagline: You can smell a fake from across the room. The real thing, too.
- epigraph: The makers of one bottle in this glass chose not to make more.
- drink: old-fashioned · built · armagnac, yellow_chartreuse, rich_syrup, angostura · an old-fashioned glass, ice cubes
- closing: One level spoonful of the Chartreuse, and no more. Then pour one for someone who's only heard you refuse.

**whoYouAre**

Everyone at the table is nodding along to something that isn't true, and you can feel your face giving you away. Not because you love an argument. You can spot something put on, and you've never learned how to stop noticing.

Underneath sits a promise, made a long time ago, about the person you'd never turn into. You check on it more often than anyone would guess. What scares you is how gently the change would come: nobody forcing you, no moment you'd notice, only your own good reasons, one after another, until you're someone you once swore you'd never be.

You never got used to it, and the people who live and work beside you are better off for it. When you say so, they remember what they meant before they stopped noticing. Without someone like you, a room can talk itself into almost anything.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. Keep the promise. But most people know you by what you turn down. Let someone see what it's protecting: what you love, up close, before anyone gets the chance to argue. A refusal starts arguments. Something you love, shown to someone, tends to end them. And then you're known for what you love, not only for what you refuse.
