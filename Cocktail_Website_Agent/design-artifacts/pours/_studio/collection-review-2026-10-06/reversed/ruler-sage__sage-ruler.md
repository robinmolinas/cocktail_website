# Reversed pair: ruler-sage / sage-ruler


## ruler-sage · The Judge · *Either Way* (draft)

- tagline: Even the people you've ruled against trust you. Especially them.
- epigraph: There's whisky in this glass that rival distillers once insisted wasn't whisky at all.
- drink: old-fashioned · built · scotch_blended, malt_rich_syrup, angostura · old-fashioned glass (rocks glass, about 300 ml), filled with ice cubes
- closing: Stir until you can't see the malt. Then say what you've decided, and let the argument go on without you.

**whoYouAre**

Two friends fall out, and sooner or later each of them tells you their side. You hear both out to the end, and you ask for the bits that don't flatter them. Then you tell them plainly what you think happened, and it's rarely what either of them hoped. And here's the odd part: when trouble comes round again, it's the one you disagreed with who calls first.

People assume plain speaking is easy for you. It isn't, not once. You want to be liked as much as anyone, and later, alone, you replay the conversation, half hoping someone will show you what you missed. What you won't put up with is the kind-sounding fudge, the "let's not make a fuss", because you know who pays for it, and it's never the person saying it. It's the quietest one there. With you, the quietest voice gets the same answer as the loudest, and it matters more than they ever tell you.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. Once everyone's been heard and you've made the call, you've done the fair part. You don't also owe the room its agreement. Some of them will never come round, and that doesn't make the call any less fair. If the evidence ever changes, it'll find you, because you're the one who always asks for it. Until then, take the evening off from deciding. You've earned the nights when nobody needs an answer from you.


## sage-ruler · The Scientist · *Note to Self* (draft)

- tagline: Impressive is easy. Exact is what you're after.
- epigraph: A careful 1887 book on whisky hardly mentions casks. The vanilla here is for them.
- drink: highball · highball · scotch_single_malt_highland_unpeated, vanilla_extract, soda_water · tall highball glass (about 300 ml), chilled in the freezer, 1-inch ice cubes added one at a time
- closing: Ease the first cube in slowly. Then leave the hunch in the margin, where someone will find it.

**whoYouAre**

You know when the boiler was last serviced, what the meter read in March, and how long bread really takes in your oven, not what the packet says. When an argument starts about how things used to be, you don't join in. You fetch the notes.

Some people mistake that for coolness. It isn't. Writing it down is how you keep a messy world still enough to think in. And, though you rarely admit it, it's one of the things you enjoy most. You're better company than your notebook suggests.

What haunts you is the thing you never thought to write down, the one that mattered after all, in a column you didn't make. But when someone needs to know what really changed, nobody has to guess. They count from your numbers, and the count holds.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. A last thought, and it isn't about the numbers. Keep the readings, the dates and the bread times exactly as you do. But put yourself in the notes too: the joke, the hunch, the part you loved. The numbers are what people will check. You are what they'll want to read.
