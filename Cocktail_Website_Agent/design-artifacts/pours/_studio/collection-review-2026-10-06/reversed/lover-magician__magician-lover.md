# Reversed pair: lover-magician / magician-lover


## lover-magician · The Charmer · *Worth Finding* (draft)

- tagline: Everyone wants to know you better. You make sure there's always more to know.
- epigraph: It starts at a bar upstairs, behind a restaurant. Its shiso hides behind the gin.
- drink: martini · stirred · shochu_barley, gin_london_dry, blanc_vermouth, rich_syrup, shiso_leaf · small stemmed cocktail glass (about 150 ml), chilled, no ice
- closing: Put the leaf on the table as you serve it. Then give away the thing you'd have kept for last.

**whoYouAre**

You're easy to talk to and hard to finish. After an evening with you, people have said more than they meant to, and know a little less about you than they'd like. So they come back. Each time you give them a bit more: a story you don't tell at work, a look that says you noticed, a door left slightly open.

Here's the part they miss. You don't keep something back to be chased. You keep it back so there's still a reason to stay. At some point you decided that once someone has seen all of you, the finding is over, and they'll go. So there's always one thing you haven't said yet. And it works: people feel they've found something in you that no one else has. They're right. That's what you hand them: a sense of discovery most people never get from anyone. It's worth a great deal. But behind that last thing, you're on your own.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. So tell one person what you've been saving. Not when they've earned it: before. You're afraid there'll be nothing left to stay for. I don't think that's how it works with you. Being known completely is the one kind of closeness you've always kept from yourself, and it's the kind that doesn't need a reason to stay. They'll stay anyway. Let yourself find out.


## magician-lover · The Siren · *Undimmed* (draft)

- tagline: You never set out to change anyone. People change around you all the same.
- epigraph: Poured the way one London bar pours its Martini. That bar has a famous house rule.
- drink: martini · freeform · dry_vermouth, vodka · small stemmed cocktail glass (about 120 ml), kept in the freezer, no ice
- closing: Grate the lemon over the top and leave it floating. Then let people see the whole of you, and trust them with the choosing.

**whoYouAre**

You hardly said anything at that dinner. You asked one question and really listened to the answer. A few weeks later they'd left the job, or booked the flight, or finally said the thing they'd been holding back for years, and they told you it started that night. Everyone else heard a lovely story. You lay awake wondering whether it was them or you.

You know what you can do to people without even trying, so you come in quieter than you are. You stand nearer the door, make the joke at your own expense, leave a little early, save the big question for another time. Everyone thinks it's just your manner. It's a decision you make again every evening, because underneath it is one fear: someone losing their footing because of you. And here's what you seldom hear back: people come away from you more like themselves, with the nerve for something they were already half-ready to do. You give people that, and you never take anything for it.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. So stop coming in at half. Being careful never meant shrinking. It means being honest about who you are. Ask the big question at the dinner, and stay until the end. Then trust people with what they do next. That part belongs to them. Without that weight, I'd bet you enjoy the evening as much as everyone else does.
