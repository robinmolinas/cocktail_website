# Reversed pair: hero-magician / magician-hero


## hero-magician · The Superhero · *Day Job* (draft)

- tagline: You could make the whole room feel slow. You never have.
- epigraph: Its first maker put out booklets of the bar's drinks. This one's in none of them.
- drink: daiquiri · shaken · rum_white, sweet_vermouth, creme_de_cacao_white, orange_curacao, grenadine, lime_juice · nick-and-nora glass (about 150 ml), chilled
- closing: Half the lime, squeezed by hand. When you're next introduced, bring up something the introduction doesn't cover.

**whoYouAre**

Most days you're easy to miss: quiet in a meeting, with a joke that lands a second too late. Then something turns up that only you can sort out, like a friend's figures that won't balance the night before they're due, or a letter that has to be exactly right. For an hour you're someone else entirely. Afterwards you go back to being easy to miss, and that part is on purpose. You'd rather nobody near you felt outshone.

Ask which of the two is the costume, and you'd hesitate. Years ago, without quite noticing, you decided the quiet one was the disguise, and the ability was the real you. So you keep it sharp, every day, because going rusty would feel like disappearing. The people you're with get someone who could make them feel small and chooses, every time, to make them feel capable instead. That's worth more to them than the ability.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. One thing to take with you. People will keep introducing you by what you can do: "this is the one who can sort anything out." When it happens again, let them, and then add something about you that has nothing to do with it: the song you can't stop playing, the town you'd move to tomorrow, something you're hopeless at. Watch which one they ask about. And let the quiet one be introduced too. It was never the disguise.


## magician-hero · The Good Wizard · *Worth the Trade* (draft)

- tagline: Everyone remembers that you saved it. You remember what you had to change.
- epigraph: Cognac was replanted on a rootstock from Texas. The grape that couldn't follow is here too.
- drink: old-fashioned · stirred · cognac, folle_blanche_armagnac, gooseberry_syrup · old-fashioned glass (rocks glass, about 300 ml), one large ice cube
- closing: Keep the quarter of Folle Blanche, however small it looks. Once it's safe, find a place again for what you had to give up.

**whoYouAre**

Something is going under: a friend's business losing money every month, a plan for a big day that has come apart, a team about to lose the thing it built. Everyone else is afraid of making it worse, so nobody touches it. You're the one who says, quietly, "this is what has to change." Then you see it through, step by step, until it stands again.

Afterwards, while the others are relieved, a part of you isn't, quite. Something had to give so the rest could hold: the old name over the door, the bigger room, the side of the project someone loved most. It worked, and you still think about that part. What you fear is what it might do that you didn't mean. It's why people trust you with the change: you weigh what it will take before anyone else thinks to ask, then you make the call anyway, and carry it yourself. Because of you, things that would have been lost altogether are still here.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. Once you've pulled something through and it's safe again, look for whatever had to make way. More often than you'd think, it hasn't gone anywhere, and now there's room. Bring a little of it back in, and let yourself enjoy the whole of what you saved.
