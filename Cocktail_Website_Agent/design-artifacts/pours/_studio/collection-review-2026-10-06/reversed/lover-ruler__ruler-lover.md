# Reversed pair: lover-ruler / ruler-lover


## lover-ruler · The Sex Symbol · *By Design* (draft)

- tagline: Everyone knows the effect you have. Almost nobody knows you designed it.
- epigraph: Named in Havana for America's Sweetheart. This one's for the one behind the curls.
- drink: daiquiri · shaken · rum_white_50, pineapple_juice, lime_juice, thyme_grenadine · cocktail glass, chilled
- closing: Make the grenadine yourself, the day before. Then choose your next version before anyone asks for the old one.

**whoYouAre**

You know what you do to people, because you worked it out. The voice, the pause before you answer, the one thing everyone remembers you wore: none of it is an accident, and you'd be quietly proud if anyone asked. They don't. They think you were simply born like that.

People fall for what you made. Very few ask to meet the one who made it. That part is lonely, and you almost never mention it, because to everyone else it seems like having it all. And when you're steering, with no credit for it, an evening can turn warm, even daring, and never tip into trouble. People feel more alive around you, and safe. That's care too, though it looks like a show.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. A hard year shouldn't be what makes the change for you. Choose the next version of yourself while the choice is still yours, and let a few people watch you choose. Some will miss the old one for a while. The ones who stay will be the ones who wanted you, and that's the company you've been missing.


## ruler-lover · The Magnetic Leader · *Up Close* (draft)

- tagline: You know exactly what your attention does. And you mean all of it.
- epigraph: A Monte Carlo barman kept his Manhattans plain. This one borrows from a regular's cocktail.
- drink: martini · stirred · pineapple_juice_pressed, rich_syrup, rye, sweet_vermouth, angostura · stemmed cocktail glass (about 180 ml), chilled, no ice
- closing: Strain the pineapple out. It's still there at the rim. So are you, even when you're not trying.

**whoYouAre**

Someone comes back after a year away, and you ask about the interview they were dreading. You remember their sister's name. You stay with them a second longer than they expect. They go home feeling remembered.

You know exactly what your attention does, and you give it on purpose, because a lot holds together when you do. People who watch from further off call it charm, and sometimes mean it as a warning. They've got it backwards: it works because you mean it. You hardly mention what it costs, or the question underneath: if I stopped, would they still come? The fear is the evening it doesn't land, when someone looks past you and everything quietly drifts. And this is what your attention gives: people leave feeling they were the one who mattered, and sure enough to offer something of their own. On an ordinary evening, nobody expects to be given that.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. Soon, for once, don't be "on". Let someone else carry the conversation, and let them show you something they've learned. Then you'll see whether they'd still come, and I hope the answer surprises you, in the best way.
