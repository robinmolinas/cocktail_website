# Reversed pair: creator-outlaw / outlaw-creator


## creator-outlaw · The Trend Setter · *Still Yours* (draft)

- tagline: You liked it first. You get to like it last.
- epigraph: It was made for the staff. It didn't stay there.
- drink: sidecar · shaken · vodka_citrus, triple_sec, lime_juice, cranberry_cocktail, basil_leaf · a saucer coupe (a wide, shallow stemmed glass), chilled
- closing: Shake it hard, keep it sharp, and serve it to whoever rolls their eyes.

**whoYouAre**

You rarely set out to start anything. You just like what you like a little before everyone else does: a song, a way of dressing, a word, a place nobody goes yet. You wear it or play it or order it because it's yours. Then, a while later, you notice other people doing it too, and it honestly surprises you every time.

The part you keep to yourself comes next. Once it's everywhere, it's usually done a bit worse, and the people whose taste you respect start rolling their eyes at it. Quietly, you go off it. You've let go of things you loved just because too many people started loving them. Nobody gets to tell you what to like. That includes telling you to stop. And without meaning to, you hand people something they rarely name: the nerve to love a thing before anyone else does.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. The next time something of yours turns up everywhere, and the eye-rolling starts, don't quietly let it go. Wear it, play it, order it anyway, out loud. You never gave something up because everyone else did. Don't give it up because everyone else found it.


## outlaw-creator · The Rule Breaker · *Asked In* (draft)

- tagline: Nobody had to let you. It was good before they did.
- epigraph: Before anyone made it legally, a king's chamberlain was looking everywhere for whisky from this glen.
- drink: old-fashioned · built · glenlivet_12, elderflower_liqueur, rich_syrup, orange_bitters · a small tumbler, one large ice cube
- closing: Stir it for twenty seconds, then leave it be. It holds up on its own.

**whoYouAre**

Tell you no, and you'll make it anyway. Usually you'd already started. The best things you've made began without anyone's say-so, often on ground that wasn't yours, and they turned out better than what was allowed. A no has never stopped you. If anything, it shows you where to begin.

It's the yes you don't trust. When the people in charge finally want what you made, something in you goes cold, as if being let in were the first step to being owned. And underneath sits a question you've never quite asked anyone: is it good, or only good because it isn't allowed? What I see is this. Much of what everyone takes for granted now, someone like you made first, before anyone agreed it could be done. You show people what better looks like while they're still saying no, and it matters to far more people than ever say so.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. So, one proposal. Make something that isn't against anything: no rule in its way, nobody to prove wrong. Then show it to the people whose approval you've never wanted. If they want it, that isn't the moment you lose. It's the moment you find out it was good. And you'll still be the one who made it.
