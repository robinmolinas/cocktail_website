# Reversed pair: innocent-jester / jester-innocent


## innocent-jester · The Giggler · *There It Goes* (draft)

- tagline: You find it funny first. Then everyone does.
- epigraph: Behind this drink is one named for whatever song was on. One city adopted it.
- drink: sidecar · shaken · bourbon, amaro_nonino, aperol, lemon_juice, saline_solution · a coupe (about 180–200 ml), chilled
- closing: Five drops of salt water before you shake. And when something's funny, laugh out loud.

**whoYouAre**

A friend is halfway through a story, and you've already gone. Not a polite laugh: the real one, eyes watering, trying to say "sorry, go on" and not managing it. You're hardly ever the one telling the joke. You're the one who can't stop laughing at it, and that's usually the moment it gets funny for everybody else.

What people don't see is how many you hold in: in meetings, at the serious dinner, anywhere someone might think less of you for it. A laugh held in sits in your chest all afternoon. You've heard "you'd laugh at anything" more times than you can count. You wouldn't. You just find more of it funny. Now and then there's the second when you're the only one laughing and the room doesn't follow, and it stays with you longer than you let on. And the fear underneath is being told off for it so often that one day you stop. Please don't. When you laugh at something someone said, they leave feeling funny.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. Next time something's funny in a room that's gone stiff, don't hold it in. Look round first: there's nearly always someone else trying not to laugh. Let yours go, and you'll have given them theirs. Then enjoy it. It really was funny.


## jester-innocent · The Naïf · *Off the Tour* (draft)

- tagline: Nobody believes your stories. They all happened.
- epigraph: A Green Swizzle, Wembley, 1924. In a story, Bertie Wooster vowed to name a son after it.
- drink: daiquiri · shaken · rum_white, lime_juice, simple_syrup, absinthe_verte_tangerine, angostura · tall Collins glass (about 350 ml), crushed ice, the swizzle stick left standing in it, no straw
- closing: Spin the stick between your palms until the glass goes white. Then go back for whatever you were hurried past.

**whoYouAre**

You're somewhere you're meant to sit still: a hotel breakfast with its little rules, a waiting room, the long middle of a speech. Ten minutes, and something small has caught you. The sachet that won't tear. The toaster that sends the bread round twice. You give it all your attention, because right then it's the only problem in the world. It all goes sideways, and nobody there forgets how. You look up, and that's when you find the room.

You never set out to be funny. The part you laugh off is the hush: the "not now", the hand on your arm. Being told to sit still has always sounded like being told to be less, and what you dread is managing it, and nothing happening at all. Hushing you would cost everyone else their best stories, the dull mornings they still tell, fondly, years later. And after you, nobody needs to pretend the rules made sense.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. I like to think you'd have seen the man with the stick before anyone else on the tour did, and stopped. Next time you're being steered past the one thing you'd actually like to see, look round for the other person who's had about enough. There usually is one. Go together. Give it all your attention, and if something wonderful comes of it, make a promise far too big for it. Somebody will be telling that afternoon for years, and it'll all be true.
