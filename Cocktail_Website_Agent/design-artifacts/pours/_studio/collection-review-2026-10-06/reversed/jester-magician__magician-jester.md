# Reversed pair: jester-magician / magician-jester


## jester-magician · The Elf/Imp · *The Kind You'd Want* (draft)

- tagline: Everyone calls it trouble. Only you know the rules.
- epigraph: Poured from a bottle in the fridge, like anything else. It isn't anything else.
- drink: highball · highball · apple_brandy, dry_vermouth, rich_syrup, soda_water · a plain everyday tumbler (about 300 ml), filled with ice
- closing: Label the bottle before it goes in the fridge. Then plant your next surprise somewhere it's still going once you've left the house.

**whoYouAre**

Somebody's keys are in the fruit bowl, under the bananas, and you're helping them look, very seriously. This morning every mug in the cupboard was turned to face the wall. They call it trouble, and they keep letting you in anyway.

Nobody spots the rules. Every trick fits one person, and everything can be put right in seconds. The mugs go back. The keys turn up before anyone's late. For every trick people see, there are a few you decided against, because of who'd be on the end of it. What you're afraid of is the trick that lands wrong and becomes somebody's bad day. So you keep them small, and gone by teatime.

You make a house a place where being surprised is safe, and around you, grown-ups still get to play.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. Keep your rules. They're why people let you in. But make one that stays. Not a trick for this afternoon: a surprise built into something that lasts. A note in the pocket of a coat they won't wear until winter. A line written inside a book you lend. You won't be there when it lands, and that's the point. Months from now someone will find it, grin at nobody, and know exactly who it was.


## magician-jester · The Illusionist · *Even When You Know* (draft)

- tagline: You don't win arguments. You change what people see.
- epigraph: The darkest band is on top. The clear one underneath is the heaviest.
- drink: old-fashioned · freeform · creme_de_cacao_white, port_tawny_10, bourbon_beet · a small stemmed pousse-café or cordial glass (about 75 ml), narrow and flared; no ice
- closing: Pour the darkest layer last, and slowest. Then explain how it works: it'll still look upside down.

**whoYouAre**

Someone at dinner is certain of something. You don't argue. You put a thing on the table, say nothing, and wait. Then comes the moment you love: what they were so sure of tips over, and they laugh. A minute later they're showing the next person as if it had been their own idea, and you let them.

You always have a reason, though. You picked that thing because you wanted them to see something differently, and it mattered to you that they did. You keep how it works, and why, to yourself, because you think the fun would turn into a lesson once it's explained. And you watch their faces all along, because that moment can tip the wrong way, into feeling foolish or played. You'd never want that for anyone. It's why nothing you show is fake. It's all right there, and the eye still won't believe it. So with you, being wrong becomes a pleasure. They walk away with one certainty looser, and glad of it.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. Once, try it backwards. Show someone how it works first, before they've even looked, and then let them look. You're afraid that once it's worked out, the fun is over. It isn't. Knowing it's sugar doesn't move a single band, and knowing how you did it won't stop them seeing it. And they get something a surprise alone can't give them: they get to see it from your side, and still be amazed.
