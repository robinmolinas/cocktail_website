# Reversed pair: outlaw-regular-guy / regular-guy-outlaw


## outlaw-regular-guy · The Gangster · *Where You Stand* (draft)

- tagline: You know how things really get done. You also know what you'll never do.
- epigraph: A century ago, Canadian whisky was worth breaking the law for. On both sides of it.
- drink: old-fashioned · stirred-spirit · canadian_whisky, maple_syrup, orange_curacao, angostura · a coupe, chilled
- closing: Scrape the teaspoon of maple flat. Then show someone close where you draw the line.

**whoYouAre**

You know the difference between how things are meant to work and how they actually do: who to ask, what to say, which door opens if you knock the right way. When the official route stops answering, people come to you, and you sort it without a fuss, and without making them feel small for asking.

Behind all that, there's a line. You've got one, and you've never needed to write it down. There are things you won't do, whatever they'd win you, and you keep that to yourself, because out loud it would sound soft. But it's why the people near you feel safe. They may not know exactly where the line is. They know you won't cross it. That matters more than you'd guess: with you, nobody has to wonder where they stand.

What gets to you is the people who write the rules, break them when it suits, and call it procedure. Being pushed around by someone like that, with no say at all, is the nearest you come to being afraid.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. You make more of the rules around you than you'd ever admit: who gets helped, and how, and what's never done. So let one person you trust in on where your line is, and let them hold you to it, as you'd hold anyone else. It costs you nothing you'd ever spend. And once someone else can see it, your line isn't a habit any more. It's your word.


## regular-guy-outlaw · The Scoundrel · *Just This Once* (draft)

- tagline: You don't get away with things. You get let off. That's better.
- epigraph: The man sent to sell the gin in this glass said he never sold a bottle.
- drink: daiquiri · collins · plymouth_gin, lemon_juice, simple_syrup, champagne_brut, water · large wine glass (about 500 ml), filled with ice
- closing: Ice first, whoever's watching. Then pour the rest of the bottle for the table.

**whoYouAre**

It's ten past ten, and the kitchen closed at ten. You go up to the counter, make the cook laugh, and five minutes later there are chips for six. The doorman, the receptionist, the teacher at the gate: you've had a yes out of every one of them, and you asked to their face.

People say you get away with everything. You don't. You get let off, which is different: someone decided to, because it's you. Every "go on, then" is a small proof you're still wanted. What you dread was never the telling-off. It's the one who doesn't look up, says "rules are rules", and handles you like a stranger.

There's a code in it, though you'd laugh at the word: you only try it on someone who can afford to say yes. And here's what you never get credit for. When a rule bends for you, it bends for everyone you came with.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. Keep asking. It works, and whoever says yes enjoys it more than they let on. But now and then, ask for something you're owed, plainly, with no grin and no "go on". Watch the yes come anyway. The people who let you off were never doing it for the charm. They like you.
