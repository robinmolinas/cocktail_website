# Reversed pair: outlaw-sage / sage-outlaw


## outlaw-sage · The Devil's Advocate · *Hear Me Out* (draft)

- tagline: You'll argue the other side. Sometimes it's even yours.
- epigraph: One bartender made the case for this drink by admitting what was wrong with it.
- drink: daiquiri · shaken · amaretto, bourbon_cask_strength, lemon_juice, sherry_vinegar, egg_white, simple_syrup · an old-fashioned glass, fresh ice
- closing: Measure the vinegar, and be ready to defend it. Next time, let the view you defend be one you hold.

**whoYouAre**

Someone says it's settled, and something in you sits up. It isn't that you think they're wrong. Often you think they're right. But nobody has argued with it yet, and an idea nobody has argued with hasn't been tested. It's only been agreed with. So you take the other side.

You'd hardly ever admit how often you agree, or how glad you'd be to lose to a good answer. "Only playing devil's advocate" keeps your own view safe, where nobody can argue it down. The sigh is what you dread, the "there they go again": once your questions stop counting, you're stuck with decisions nobody was allowed to think about.

Nobody says thank you in the moment. They should. An idea that's survived you can survive almost anything, and when the real objection comes, from a boss, say, the others already have the answer, because you made them find it.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. Here's my side, and I mean it. Keep testing everything: people are better for it, even the ones who sigh. But now and then, argue for something you actually love, and say plainly that it's what you think. No escape hatch, no "just for the sake of argument". Let them push back on you for a change. You may find your own ideas survive you too, and everyone else finally gets to argue with the real you.


## sage-outlaw · The Lone Voice · *On the Record* (draft)

- tagline: You don't like being the only one. You like pretending even less.
- epigraph: Port goes in twice here: once in short, then again at greater length.
- drink: martini · stirred · port_ruby, port_tawny_10, black_tea · small stemmed port glass (about 150 ml), chilled, no ice
- closing: Ruby first, then twice the tawny. Make the second pour the bigger one.

**whoYouAre**

You looked into it properly: the figures behind the plan everyone's excited about, the story the family has told for years, the way things have always been done at work. And it doesn't hold. So you said so, plainly, with what you'd found. The room went quiet, and not the good kind.

Here's what you don't show. You don't enjoy standing there on your own, not at all. Part of you hopes you're wrong, because being wrong would make life simpler. So you check it again, usually late, and it's still there. Then you say it again, with more of it. What you can't live with is the comfortable version becoming the official one, with nobody having said a word.

And often, later, someone finds you, a little quietly, to say they'd wondered the same. Because you said it first, they get to say it second. That's a gift, though it rarely feels like one at the time.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. Let me leave you with this. When a room goes cold on you, it's tempting to read it as an answer. On bad nights it feels like proof you're wrong. On good ones, like proof you're right. It's neither. Forrester was attacked by the people with a stake and backed by the people of the wine country, and neither of those settled whether he was right. So the next time it goes quiet, let it, and go back to what you found. If it's still there, say it again, with more of it. And if one day it isn't, you'll be the one who says so, out loud. That's exactly why people can trust what you tell them.
