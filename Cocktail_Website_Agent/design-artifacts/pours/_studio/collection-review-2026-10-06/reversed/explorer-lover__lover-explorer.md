# Reversed pair: explorer-lover / lover-explorer


## explorer-lover · The Sense Seeker · *Straight Back* (draft)

- tagline: One taste, and you're somewhere else entirely. You always let it take you.
- epigraph: The cider man also brought three liqueurs. One of them tasted like an island.
- drink: daiquiri · shaken · gin_london_dry, lemon_juice, simple_syrup, creme_de_mure, balsamic_vinegar · an old-fashioned glass (rocks glass), filled with crushed ice
- closing: Pour the blackberry on last, so it's tasted first. Then slide the glass across the table, and wait.

**whoYouAre**

Someone opens a jar, and before anyone has said a word you're standing in a kitchen you haven't been in for years. A smell on a stairwell, the first bite of a plum, and you're somewhere else, completely. You can't always remember what year it was. You can remember exactly what the bread tasted like. And whatever is in front of you now, you're really tasting it.

What you play down is how hard it hits. You say "mm, lovely", because you've seen the eyebrow go up across the table: it's only bread. It's never only bread to you. What frightens you is a life with the senses turned down, on a menu somebody else chose. Or a meal with someone who isn't tasting, which is lonelier than eating alone. And look what you do with good things. You tear off the warm end of the loaf and hand it over. You crush a leaf under someone's nose: "smell this". Years later, the taste that takes them somewhere has you in it. That's a lovely thing to leave in someone's life.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. Find the taste that takes you somewhere, the one you could never quite explain, and give it to someone you love. Cook it, pick it, track it down. Don't tell them the story first. Just hand it over, and then let them tell you where it took them. You've been taking people into your places for years. This time you'll get to go into one of theirs, and you'll know that the person across the table is tasting too.


## lover-explorer · The Pleasure Seeker · *Curtain Call* (draft)

- tagline: You love every minute. Even the last one, if you let it.
- epigraph: The Bloody Mary once had a politer name, and no ice at all.
- drink: highball · freeform · vodka, tomato_juice, manzanilla, lemon_juice, celery_salt, salt_pinch · a stemmed wine glass (about 250-300 ml), chilled, no ice
- closing: There's no ice in it, so it ends. Choose the night's ending yourself, before the coats come out.

**whoYouAre**

You say yes first, every time. Yes to the cold sea in May, to the dish nobody else at the table dares to order (and then everyone wants a forkful), to walking on somewhere after dinner because the night's too good to end there. You enjoy things properly, out loud, and it's catching. With you around, people stop feeling guilty about the long lunch on a weekday, or the second pudding.

It's never the bill you mind. It's the lights coming up. You can feel the end of a good night coming long before anyone reaches for a coat, sometimes right in the middle of the best part, and that's partly why you enjoy things so hard. Nobody's there for the ride home, when everyone goes back to their own lives and the noise stops all at once. The fear under it is a plain one: that when the music stops, you'll be the one left in the quiet. Still, you make enjoying things feel safe, and people remember who did that for them.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. One night soon, when it's at its very best, the table loud and nobody watching the time, be the one who ends it. Not because you're tired, and not to be sensible. Say it out loud: this was perfect. Then name the next one, and the date. You'll leave on the best note instead of the last one, and you'll find out what happens when the music stops. Nobody vanishes. They ask you when. And may the next one be even better.
