## innocent-magician · The Dreamer · *Let's Try It* (draft)

- epigraph: Tasmania's new whisky began on a fishing trip: good water, good barley, and nobody making any.

- closing: The Glenfarclas goes in last. Then find out what's really in the way.

**whoYouAre**

Little things add up for you. The friend who lights up whenever baking comes up. The job advert you know would suit someone perfectly. The two people at a party who keep ending up talking to each other. Put enough of them together and you can see how something could turn out, well ahead of everyone else. To you, that isn't wishing. It's where the signs point.

But you learned early to keep it quiet. "Don't get your hopes up" was nearly always said kindly, by people who loved you. So now every hope comes with a little "probably not" in front of it, said first, to beat everyone else to it. What you dread is the look afterwards, and worse, someone telling you that you got their hopes up.

And still you don't stop. When a person close to you puts a hope down, you quietly pick it up and keep it for them until they're ready to have it back. Most people just tell them to be realistic.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

6. Whenever you can see how something could go, for yourself or for someone close, drop the "probably not". Say what you've added up, plainly, and then find out what's really in the way. It's often smaller than the hope: an old rule, a form, one person nobody has thought to ask. And sometimes the first answer is a yes.


## magician-innocent · The Sorcerer's Apprentice · *More Than One Head* (draft)

- tagline: You never worry it won't work. You worry it will.
- epigraph: A whisky merchant wrote he could almost wish his business would halve. This glass doubles instead.
- drink: highball · highball · strawberry_lemon_cordial, johnnie_walker_red_label, soda_water · tall tumbler (about 400 ml), filled with ice cubes
- closing: Thirty, sixty, a hundred and twenty. When it starts growing like that, say the "almost" out loud.

**whoYouAre**

You start things. A dinner that turns into a monthly fixture. A small idea that ends up as a group of forty. A side project that people you've never met now ask you about. Usually you start before you're quite ready, because the excitement won't wait, and finding out as you go is half the fun.

What hardly anyone guesses is this. Failure isn't what keeps you up. Success is: the kind that keeps going faster than you can follow, in directions you didn't pick, with your name on it. So you keep one small wish to yourself: that it would slow down for a week. You keep it quiet, because who complains when something's working? That wish doesn't betray the joy. It's the price of it. And here's what the joy gives everyone else: a lot of the good things around you are only there because you couldn't wait to begin them.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. So keep starting things before you're ready. Most of your good ones began that way. But when one of them starts to run ahead of you, don't keep the "almost" to yourself. Say it while it still makes you laugh, and give someone half of it to hold. It won't slow down. You just won't be carrying it alone, so you'll finally get to enjoy watching it grow.
