# Reversed pair: creator-lover / lover-creator


## creator-lover · The Obsessed · *Rosetta* (draft)

- tagline: Other people like things. You fall for them.
- epigraph: The French word for the cloud in this glass also means disreputable.
- drink: old-fashioned · freeform · cranberry_syrup, absinthe_lucid, water · a stemmed water goblet or a large wine glass (240 ml or more)
- closing: Wait for the cloud. Then bring the red up into it.

**whoYouAre**

There's one thing you keep going back to. A song, a recipe, a drawing you've started over so many times that nobody else can see the changes any more. People think you're repeating yourself. You're not. Every time, you notice something you missed. When someone asks why it matters so much, you explain the details, the how, the history, because "I love it" sounds like too much.

And when you finally show it to someone, you watch their face. You're not waiting for praise. You're waiting to see if they felt it too. The word that hurts you most isn't "no". It's "nice". Still, spend enough time near you and people start to care about things a little more than they planned to. You make loving something that much look like what it is: a pleasure.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

4. Next time you show someone the thing you love, leave out the how. Tell them what got you, the way one sentence got him. They may never taste the difference you can. But they'll hear it in your voice, and that's the part you've been waiting for them to feel.


## lover-creator · The Muse · *In Your Own Hand* (draft)

- tagline: People make their best things around you. So do you.
- epigraph: 'Juice of A Few Flowers' sounds like a joke. The handwritten card says otherwise.
- drink: daiquiri · shaken · gin_london_dry, orange_juice, grapefruit_juice, lime_juice, lemon_juice, mint_syrup · two nick-and-nora glasses (small, stemmed), chilled, each rim dipped in coarse sugar on the outside
- closing: Pour both glasses. Hand one over with something you've made, and put your name on it.

**whoYouAre**

Friends show you things first: the song before it has words, the first chapter, the dish they've never cooked for anyone else. Something in the way you pay attention makes them want to finish it, and often they do. People are braver with their own work when you're around. Sometimes you even turn up in it: a line in the song, a character who talks the way you talk.

You make things too, and there's usually something of yours, half done, that nobody has seen. It's lovely to be someone's reason, and it feels safer than being judged on your own work. But the you in their songs is their idea of you, and an idea is easy to love from a distance. People have made beautiful things out of you. Very few have asked what you're making.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. So when someone shows you what they're working on, show them something of yours back. Not the polished version, and not so they'll praise it: the unfinished one, in your own hand. Let one person see the maker, not the reason. That's how you get known up close. And since hardly anyone asks, I will: what are you making?
