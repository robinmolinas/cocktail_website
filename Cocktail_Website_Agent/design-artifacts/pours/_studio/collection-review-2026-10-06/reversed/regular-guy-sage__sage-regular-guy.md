# Reversed pair: regular-guy-sage / sage-regular-guy


## regular-guy-sage · The Everyman · *Tried and True* (draft)

- tagline: Most of what you know, somebody told you. You still remember who.
- epigraph: In 1947, one bar guide printed five different Blackthorns. This glass is built on one of them.
- drink: martini · stirred · sloe_gin, gin_london_dry, dry_vermouth, orange_bitters · small stemmed glass (about 150 ml), chilled, no ice
- closing: Sixty between the two gins, whatever the bottle. When you teach it, say how you split the sixty.

**whoYouAre**

A friend starts a new job tomorrow and is sure they'll get it all wrong. You tell them what an old neighbour used to say: learn everyone's name on the first day, and the rest can wait. It's exactly right, and they write it down. Half of what you know came like that, with somebody's name on it, and you still hear their voice when you say it.

Here's the bit you don't say: you didn't hold on to all of it. Some of what you were told turned out to be wrong, and you let those bits go quietly, because saying so would feel like letting them down. What's left, you've tried for yourself. You can't always say why it works, only who told you, and when someone asks for the reason, you doubt yourself. The real fear is the day nobody asks. If it stops with you, a little of them stops too.

But each time you say "as my neighbour used to say", that neighbour is in the room again, and someone leaves with something to use tomorrow. You do a kindness to two people at once.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. You're afraid it ends with you. It doesn't. Somewhere, someone already starts a sentence with your name, and you just haven't been there to hear it. So don't wait for someone to ask, or until you're old. This year, teach one thing you were taught to someone who'll keep it, and tell them the bit you changed, and why. That's how you end up among the people you quote.


## sage-regular-guy · The Dude · *Whatever They Call It* (draft)

- tagline: You let almost everything go. Just not the part that matters.
- epigraph: Built for a tequila that had to find a name twice. Stone still crushes its agave.
- drink: highball · highball · fortaleza_blanco, soda_water · tall highball glass (about 300 ml), three ice cubes
- closing: Pinch the oregano once and lay it on the ice. It's the smallest thing in the glass, and the one you'd miss.

**whoYouAre**

Two hours into the evening, the table is arguing about something that sounds important: who said what, whose fault it was, what to call it. You've let all of it go. It isn't that you can't follow it. You followed it once, a while back, and found out how little of it was worth the heat. But buried in all that noise there's one small thing nobody else noticed was the point, and that one you won't let go.

Your ease took effort, though nobody sees it. You've been pulled into someone else's story before, and found yourself carrying part of something that wasn't true. So now you step back early. And because you step back, people sometimes decide you don't care. You do. You care about very few things, and you care about those all the way.

That gives people something, and they rarely say it: with you, nobody has to win. The temperature drops. When someone wants an argument to go quiet, they bring it to you.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

6. Your way has one cost, though. Because you let so much go, people take your ease for not caring. Next time the table's arguing and the thing you care about is caught in the middle of it, don't just keep hold of it quietly. Tell them, out loud, that it matters to you. The people who thought you were easy about everything will be surprised. Then they'll trust your ease even more, because they'll know it was always your choice.
