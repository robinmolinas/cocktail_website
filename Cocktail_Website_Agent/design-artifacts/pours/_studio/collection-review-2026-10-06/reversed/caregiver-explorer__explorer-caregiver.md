# Reversed pair: caregiver-explorer / explorer-caregiver


## caregiver-explorer · The Researcher · *Serviceable* (draft)

- tagline: You care about people you'll never meet.
- epigraph: The fizz in this glass was once meant to cure sailors. It never did.
- drink: highball · highball · scotch_blended_hopped, soda_water · highball glass, filled with ice
- closing: Pour the soda slowly, down the side. And when something doesn't work, say so: someone out there needs to know.

**whoYouAre**

When someone you care about is told there's nothing more to be done, you don't quite accept it. That same evening you're reading, comparing, writing down questions for the next appointment, finding the one person who might know. Not because you think you're cleverer than the experts. Because "that's just how it is" has never once been enough for you when someone is hurting. Walking away feels like breaking a promise to others, but most importantly to yourself.

Some of what you try goes nowhere. Those tries never make it into the story you tell, but you keep them, every one. And here's something I'd guess you've never said out loud: you love it a little. The not-knowing, the chase. Some days that feels almost wrong, as if it made it about you. It doesn't. It's why you're still looking after everyone else has stopped, and why your care so often reaches people you'll never meet.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. Don't hide the tries that went nowhere. Someone else is about to set off down the same road, and what you found there could save them the trip. One wrong answer ended up in drinks all over the world. Post-it Notes were born from a failed experiment to create a super-strong adhesive. And stop treating the love of the looking as the selfish part. It's what carries the help out of the room.


## explorer-caregiver · The Conservationist · *Left Standing* (draft)

- tagline: You'd rather know it's there than have it.
- epigraph: The plant behind this glass flowers once in its life. Most are harvested first.
- drink: martini · stirred · siembra_azul_blanco, blanc_vermouth, creme_de_peche · a copita (a small tulip-shaped tasting glass), chilled, no ice
- closing: Nothing goes on top. Drink it while it's cold: you can miss it later.

**whoYouAre**

Every time you return to a place you love, you're bracing before you arrive. The café that might have closed. The field that might be houses now. The woods at the end of the lane, with a car park where the gate was. Everyone else sees the view. You're counting what's left.

You start missing things while they're still here. So you touch them as little as you can. You don't pick the flower. The stone stays where it was. The path looks the way you found it. People sometimes take that for strictness, and you'd rather they did. It's easier than saying how much it hurts to watch something thin out while everyone around you is still enjoying it. And easier than admitting the fear under that one: that some day you'll be part of the reason it went. But here's what your care does, whether anyone thanks you or not. When other people arrive, there's still something there for them to love.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. You don't have to be the only one watching. The next place you're afraid of losing, don't go alone. Take someone and show them what you see: the thing that's thinning out, the one nobody else has noticed yet. Then there are two of you who'd notice if it went. And I think you'll find that watching it with someone feels less like grieving and more like what it really is: loving it while it's here.
