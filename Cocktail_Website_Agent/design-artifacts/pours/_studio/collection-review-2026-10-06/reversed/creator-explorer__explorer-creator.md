**whoYouAre**

Hand you instructions and you'll follow them, right up to the moment you don't. You start things before you know what they'll be. That isn't a hole in the plan. It's the part you like best: finding out.

Some of what you try doesn't work, and you honestly don't mind. You rarely mention those ones, and there are more of them than anyone guesses. What gets under your skin is the opposite: something works, and everyone wants it exactly like last time. Being asked to repeat yourself feels like a door closing. Stand next to you long enough and people start saying what you say: "let me try something." You've shown them that something that didn't work isn't a disaster. It's just the next thing to change.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. It didn't win, Gonzalez says, and he kept making it anyway. Somewhere you've got something you left half-tried: a recipe, a plan, an idea you walked away from just as it started working. Go back to it. Not to make it the way it was. To find out what it turns into when you stay.


## explorer-creator · The Innovator · *For Good* (draft)

- tagline: You don't mind losing a weekend if nobody loses ten minutes again.
- epigraph: One idea behind this glass came from a convenience store.
- drink: sidecar · freeform · tequila_blanco, cointreau, simple_syrup, water, vanilla_extract, lime_juice · a large stemmed goblet (about 350 ml), chilled, no salt rim
- closing: Make it a day ahead. Then the evening is for your guests, not the blender.

**whoYouAre**

Somewhere in your life there's the proof of how you work: a thing that used to jam, drip or stick, until one afternoon you'd had enough of it. You took it apart there and then, and it hasn't jammed since. And you regret none of it.

What you keep quiet is how it starts. It sounds like moaning: the queue that's slow for no reason, the form that asks for the same thing twice, the way something has always been done that has never quite worked. You've tried sighing and getting on with it. It doesn't take. It stays with you until you've found a way round, and the way round usually comes from somewhere nobody thought to look. The sentence you can't bear is "it's always been like that". The one that frightens you is hearing yourself say it, and meaning it. And the things you fix stay fixed. People stop having the problem, forget it was ever there, and get their afternoons back. That's what your restlessness gives them, and it's worth far more than the grumbling it started with.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. So trust the annoyance. It isn't moaning, and it isn't ingratitude. It's usually the first sign that you've found something worth fixing. The next time someone tells you it's always been like that, take it as directions: they've just shown you where to start. And when it works and nobody says a word, enjoy the quiet. You made it.
