# Reversed pair: innocent-sage / sage-innocent


## innocent-sage · The Wise Kid · *Just So You Know* (draft)

- tagline: You tell people what you'd want to be told.
- epigraph: A Whiskey Sour, as it was ordered. For a while, one bartender made it with Scotch.
- drink: daiquiri · shaken · bourbon, lemon_juice, sugar · a small stemmed sour glass (about 180 ml), chilled in the freezer, no ice
- closing: Press the sugar into the peel until it smells of lemon. One little step, and the whole glass is fresher.

**whoYouAre**

There's a crumb on someone's chin at dinner, or a typo on the first slide that everyone has already read, or a colleague whose name someone has been saying wrong for a year. Most people saw it. Maybe they thought it wasn't their place, or that it was kinder to leave it. You go over, quietly, and say so, to that person and nobody else. It never felt brave to you. It just seemed like what you'd want, if it were you.

There's a second after that you never mention. You've said it, and for a second you wait to see whether you're in trouble. At some point someone told you the truth was rude, and part of you still listens for it. And what hurts more is the reverse: finding out that people you trust watched you get something wrong and said nothing, to be nice.

Your way hands people the chance to fix it while it's still small, and it counts for a lot. Being told by you never feels like being caught out. It's being trusted to take it.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. So when you spot the next one, say it, and don't stand there waiting for the verdict. I'd bet most people are glad, even if it takes them years to get round to saying so. And you've done this for plenty of people without ever asking for it back. Pick someone close and ask them to do the same for you. It's your turn to be told.


## sage-innocent · The Prodigy · *Nothing Escaped You* (draft)

- tagline: You got there first. You're still glad you did.
- epigraph: A Sidecar without its lemon. The sour comes from the grape itself.
- drink: sidecar · freeform · brandy_unaged_grape, cointreau, blanc_vermouth, verjus_blanc, simple_syrup, water · coupe (about 220 ml), chilled, no ice
- closing: Stir it until the outside is ice-cold. Next time you know something, leave off the "I think", and let them check.

**whoYouAre**

Someone is explaining your own subject to you, slowly, as if to a child. You've known it for years, better than they do. You smile, you let them finish, and you put "I think" in front of the part you're sure of. You learned that early, along with everything else: people hear who's talking before they hear what's said.

Being doubted doesn't hurt. A real "show me" is a kind of respect. The pat on the head does, from someone who never looked, and so does finding out that the confident voice was bluffing all along.

And the delight never went out of it. You give everyone the thing you once had to wait for: you never talk down to anybody. Ask you anything and you answer the real question. People leave a conversation with you feeling cleverer than before. Nobody forgets how that felt.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. So when someone doubts what you know, don't shrink and don't argue. Show them, the way he did, and let them look for themselves. The ones worth keeping will look. You've waited long enough to be believed. You don't have to wait to be heard. And I hope that more than once in your life, someone who checked takes your arm.
