# Reversed pair: hero-regular-guy / regular-guy-hero


## hero-regular-guy · The Firefighter · *Anyone Would Have* (draft)

- tagline: When it counts, you've already done it a hundred times.
- epigraph: Gin, cherry, herbs and lime. One day, nobody behind the bar could make it.
- drink: daiquiri · collins · gin_london_dry, cherry_heering, benedictine, lime_juice, soda_water, angostura · large rocks glass (about 400 ml), one large ice cube
- closing: One of each, so anyone can learn it. Someone beside you already has.

**whoYouAre**

The lights go out in the middle of a party, and you already know where the fuse box is. The car won't start on the way to the airport, and you've got the number saved. Afterwards, you shrug: anyone would have done the same. But you know that isn't quite true. You found the fuse box the week you moved in. You drove the route the day before. You don't trust yourself to be at your best on the day, so you don't leave anything to the day.

What you'd never say is why. It isn't pride. You're afraid of being the one who wasn't enough when someone was counting on you. So you practise, quietly, for things that may never happen, and when it goes well you hand the credit to everyone else. Being singled out has always felt a bit like being left out. But everyone near you gets through a bad day more easily because of a hundred ordinary ones you never mention. That's how you care for people, and it deserves more than a shrug.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. You've spent years getting ready for other people's hard days. What you forget is that the others have been getting ready too, and some of it was for yours. Being enough was never meant to be your job alone. So before the next day like that, practise as much as you like. Then look at who's around you, and trust their hours the way they've always trusted yours. You were never the only one who was ready.


## regular-guy-hero · The Have A Go Hero · *I'll Do It* (draft)

- tagline: You say yes before you know how.
- epigraph: The rye this drink calls for was first made in a barn, by amateurs.
- drink: martini · stirred · hudson_do_the_rye_thing, apple_brandy, sweet_vermouth, demerara_syrup, angostura · rocks glass, one large cube
- closing: Make it before you've practised, with someone watching. Ask for a hand before you need one.

**whoYouAre**

Someone says it in the pub, on the doorstep or in the group chat: somebody really ought to do something about it. The broken bench, the club with no treasurer, the school play nobody will run. Everyone nods. Before you can stop yourself, you've said "I'll do it", and only later, doing the washing-up, do you realise you've no idea how.

Nobody hears about the evenings: the videos, the forums, the cousin who might know. You're out of your depth most of the way through, and you know it. The words you dread are "who asked you?", and worse, the doubters turning out to be right. You go anyway, partly because if you thought about it any longer, you wouldn't. The thing everyone was waiting for somebody to fix gets fixed. And because you so plainly need a hand, people who'd never have volunteered end up holding the ladder. It counts for more than you know: what was everyone's complaint becomes something they did together.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. So, when "I'll do it" next comes out of your mouth, finish the sentence: "and I'll need a hand." Say it at the start, while you still don't know how, and say who you'd like. You worry it'll show how out of your depth you are. It will, a little. It's also the reason they'll come. The job gets done sooner, nobody waits for the experts, and the thing you started ends up belonging to everyone who helped.
