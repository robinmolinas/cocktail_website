# Reversed pair: explorer-ruler / ruler-explorer


## explorer-ruler · The Expedition Leader · *Far Enough* (draft)

- tagline: You'll take them anywhere. You'll bring every one of them back.
- epigraph: They turned round 180 km from the South Pole. This glass is for the way home.
- drink: old-fashioned · built · shackleton_blended_malt, plum_syrup, angostura · a heavy double tumbler, one large clear cube
- closing: Drink to the top you turned round from. It was still the right place to turn.

**whoYouAre**

It was your idea in the first place: the hill, the all-day walk, the trip nobody else would have booked. Then, a couple of hundred metres below the top, the cloud comes down, someone has gone quiet, and you hear yourself say it: "Right. Turning round." The groan. The long way down. In the car park you count heads, and nobody notices you're counting.

You'll go as far as everyone can get back from, and not a step further. You wanted that top more than any of them. You swallow it on the spot, so nobody has to feel they cost you anything, and later, privately, you file the day under "didn't make it". The fear is getting stranded somewhere you led people, with no plan that gets every one of them home. And that's what your turning round gives the people with you: they go further in your company than they'd dare alone, because they know you'll turn round in time. You earn that trust every time you turn.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. So stop keeping that file. Every turn-round in it is a reason people still say yes when you ask them to come. And one of these years, choose a top that's only for you, take people strong enough to get there and back, and go. They'll all come home, the way they always do with you. This time, I hope you stand on it.


## ruler-explorer · The Reformer · *Not the Same* (draft)

- tagline: People think you want to change it. You're the one who loves it most.
- epigraph: A Chicago bar guide of 1900 says to coat the glass. The drink came decades later.
- drink: daiquiri · shaken · vodka, simple_syrup, lemon_juice, apricot_eau_de_vie · cocktail coupe (about 200 ml), chilled, coated inside with apricot eau-de-vie, fine sugar on the outside of the rim
- closing: Coat the glass before you pour. And before you change anything, say what you're keeping.

**whoYouAre**

Every summer there's the same street party, and every summer a few more people find a reason to miss it. You're the one who suggests the Saturday instead of the Sunday, and the neighbours nobody ever thought to knock for. You keep the food everyone brings, the old songs, the raffle with the terrible prizes. Someone who's been coming for thirty years tells you it won't be the same. It isn't. The next summer, the street is full.

Here's the part nobody guesses: you love the old version more than the people defending it. You argue with dates and numbers, because "I'd hate to lose this" sounds soft, so the ones who love it too can hear someone wrecking it. The row doesn't scare you. What does is the change you pushed for turning out to be what finishes it off, and everyone remembering it was you. And look what you leave instead: the things people love are still there, with more people in them, because someone cared enough to change them. Nobody says so at the time. Someone should.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. One thing I'd like you to try. You keep the love behind the sensible reasons, and that's why the people defending the old version hear an enemy. Let them hear the love first, before the dates and the numbers. They'll find someone on their side, because you always were. And once the place is full again, don't stand by the wall checking that it held. Go in and enjoy it. It was always as much yours as theirs.
