# Reversed pair: regular-guy-ruler / ruler-regular-guy


## regular-guy-ruler · The People's Champion · *Word Gets Round* (draft)

- tagline: Nobody near you pays extra for not knowing.
- epigraph: Limes are packed by size, a bartender said. Odd ones go cheap. Measures don't mind.
- drink: daiquiri · shaken · apple_brandy, lime_juice, pomegranate_syrup · coupe (about 180 ml), chilled in the freezer
- closing: Juice the odd-sized limes. Then make one for the friend who always guesses.

**whoYouAre**

Your neighbour's parking fine came back cancelled, because you knew which sentence the appeal needed. A friend got their money back from the airline in one call, with you beside them saying what to ask for. You know which form actually gets read, and that you're allowed to ask for a name. All of it matters on the day a letter arrives and someone you love goes pale.

You'd rather not admit that you like knowing. It's a kind of power, though you wouldn't use the word. And when someone offers you a seat where things get decided, you come up with a reason to say no. Knowing this much already puts you near the side of the counter that says no. One step further, and you're afraid your own people would start to see you as one of them.

Because of you, people who'd have paid full price, signed the wrong thing or taken no for an answer didn't. You put what you know on the side of the people nobody told. That counts for a great deal more than you let it.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. Say yes when you're next offered a seat where things get decided. You're afraid it would put you behind that counter, on the side that says no. It needn't, as long as you keep telling your people everything you know. From there you'll have more to share, and more people to share it with.


## ruler-regular-guy · The People's Representative · *One Table* (draft)

- tagline: Everyone wants you on their side. You keep ending up on both.
- epigraph: A city gave up brandy for whiskey. Years later, one glass poured both.
- drink: martini · stirred · rye, cognac, sweet_vermouth_cardamom, benedictine, peychauds, angostura · large rocks glass (about 300 ml), one large ice cube
- closing: Put the cardamom in the vermouth, never in either spirit. Next time you cross over, bring all of yourself.

**whoYouAre**

A family stops speaking over a seating plan. A team splits down the middle over who did the work. Two neighbours each want you to say the other one's wrong. Everyone waits to see which table you'll sit at. You go and sit with the people who expected you not to, and you have whatever they're having.

You don't do it with a speech. You've never won anyone round with an argument, and you don't try. You just turn up on their ground and mean it. From outside it can look like sitting on the fence. It isn't. You have a side, and you know your own mind. The hardest question you get doesn't come from the other side. It comes from your own: whose side are you on? What you fear is being made to choose, and the day a row stops being a row and becomes the way things are. Because of you, nobody has to pick a table. Both halves end up in one room, and neither had to give itself up to get there.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. One thing, though. When you go over to the other side, you tend to leave a little of yourself at the door, so they feel safe. Your own side notices, and that's where the question comes from. Don't pour less of yourself. It was never your quietness they trusted. It's that you came as you are and stayed. And when your own side asks again, you'll have your answer: both, and still me.
