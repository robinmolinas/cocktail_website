# Reversed pair: explorer-jester / jester-explorer


## explorer-jester · The Mad Inventor · *Why Not?* (draft)

- tagline: There's an easier way. It just isn't as much fun.
- epigraph: Taverns heated drinks like this with a red-hot iron. This one burns its sugar instead.
- drink: old-fashioned · hot · sugar, water, abbey_ale, rum_aged, lemon_juice, orange_bitters, salt_pinch · a wide china teacup (about 200 ml), warmed with hot water first
- closing: Get someone to pour the ale in while you stir. Then you both built it.

**whoYouAre**

There's a perfectly good way to do it, and everybody knows it. Then there's your way: three extra steps, a part you made from something at the back of a drawer, and someone in the doorway asking what on earth you're doing. It lit you up the moment you thought of it, and that was reason enough.

Some of it falls over. You laugh first, and loudest. What you don't laugh at is the kind voice that says "you could just buy one". It's said with a smile, as common sense, and it lands harder than any collapse, because it turns the best part of your week into a waste of time. What you'd hate most is a life with nothing left to build. Here's what your long way gives people: the kitchen gets louder when you start, and an ordinary job becomes the most interesting thing in the house. When it works, nobody is more surprised than you, and the surprise is catching.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. So when you next hear "you could just…", don't explain. Explaining makes it sound like a defence, and it was never one. Hand them the other end instead: the spanner, the bit that needs holding, the ale to pour. Let them be there when it works. I'd bet they'll want to do the next one with you.


## jester-explorer · The Alternative Comic · *Unrehearsed* (draft)

- tagline: Anyone can get the laugh. You're after the second before it.
- epigraph: The most familiar cocktail there is, sealed inside ice. Nobody tastes it until the ice breaks.
- drink: old-fashioned · freeform · rye, rum_zacapa_23, demerara_syrup, angostura, water · rocks glass (about 300 ml), holding a shell of ordinary cloudy ice frozen in a plastic cup
- closing: Crack it in front of them. Then don't leave the table.

**whoYouAre**

It's a birthday, and you've been asked to say something short. You do: one sentence, read slowly from a very long sheet of paper. There's a second where nobody knows whether they're allowed to laugh. You let it run a little longer than is comfortable. Then it goes, and it's a laugh nobody planned, not even you. The evening where everyone laughs on cue, at the punchline they could see coming from the door, bores you to tears.

Here's the part that doesn't show: you're watching every face. You'd never explain one of your jokes, because explaining feels like taking it apart while everyone watches. So when one doesn't land, you keep a straight face and take it home with you. What you'd least like to hear is that it came across as cold, that someone thought it was on them, or felt left outside it. It almost never is. And it's a rare thing you give people: a real reaction instead of a polite one. For a second nobody has to know how to feel, and the night everyone's still describing weeks later is usually one you were at.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. So keep every strange thing you had planned. Don't explain it beforehand, and don't make it sensible. Just don't disappear straight after. Stay for the second that follows, and for the conversation after that. Nobody needed the joke taken apart. They needed someone in it with them. The ones who weren't sure the first time are the ones who'll come back for whatever you try next.
