# Reversed pair: caregiver-jester / jester-caregiver


## caregiver-jester · The Reassurer · *Worth the Trip* (draft)

- tagline: You always make them laugh at the wrong moment, but it's actually always the right one.
- epigraph: The story goes that the drink behind this one reached the front by taxi.
- drink: highball · highball · cognac, gin_london_dry, lime_juice, ginger_cumin_syrup, angostura, soda_water · tall glass (highball), full of ice
- closing: Add the soda when you get there. Then let them do the talking.

**whoYouAre**

When something goes badly for someone, you're the one who says the thing that makes them laugh. Not straight away. You wait. You read them first, you work out how bad it is, and sometimes you keep the line to yourself because it's too early. Then, at the right moment, you say it, and for a second everything weighs a little less. They laugh, they're half embarrassed to be laughing, and then they breathe out.

People sometimes take it to mean you don't take things seriously. It's the other way round. You don't joke because it isn't serious. You joke because it is. The worry underneath it all is that the joke was really for you, to make it easier to stand there. It wasn't. It was how you told them you'd seen exactly how bad it was, and that they weren't in it alone.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. The laugh is how you get in. Don't let it become how you get out. When the laugh comes, don't reach for the second line. Let the quiet after it happen. You're good company there too, without saying a thing.


## jester-caregiver · The Morale Booster · *Anyway* (draft)

- tagline: You decide it's going to be a good night. Then you make it one.
- epigraph: People tried everything to fix the orange in the Bronx cocktail. This is one more try.
- drink: martini · shaken-spirit · gin_london_dry, sweet_vermouth, dry_vermouth, orange_wheel_muddled, peychauds, coriander_seed · stemmed wine glass (about 250 ml), chilled
- closing: Muddle the whole wheel, peel and all. Then tell the story of the one that didn't take.

**whoYouAre**

It's a grey week, or a long wait, or a birthday nobody's quite in the mood for, and you've decided it's going to be good anyway. So you think of something: a silly game with proper rules, a theme, a time to turn up. Half the fun is that you took it seriously. People who would never start anything themselves find they've joined in, and even the one who swore they wouldn't ends up laughing.

There's a list, though, and only you have it: the ones that didn't take. The quiz nobody got into. The evening that stayed flat however hard you pushed it. Everyone else forgot them by the next morning. You didn't, because under all the fun is the thing you dread most: a gloom nobody could lift, and you the one who couldn't lift it. Here's what those nights gave people, even the flat ones. They could tell that someone wanted the evening to be good for them. That's what your fun was for all along, and it matters more than whether the game worked.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. So keep the list. Just stop reading it as a record of what went wrong. Every idea on it that didn't take is part of the one that will. When something you've planned falls flat, laugh about it with them, and start planning another. Throw it anyway. I'd come to both.
