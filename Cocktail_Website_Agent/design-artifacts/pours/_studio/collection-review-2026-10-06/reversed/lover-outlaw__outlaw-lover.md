# Reversed pair: lover-outlaw / outlaw-lover


## lover-outlaw · The Bad Influence · *Accomplices* (draft)

- tagline: You don't get people into trouble. You get them to drop the act.
- epigraph: A New York bar put curaçao in its Manhattan. This one adds beetroot. It stains.
- drink: martini · stirred · rye, sweet_vermouth_beet, orange_curacao, angostura · coupe (about 180 ml), chilled
- closing: Slice the beetroot yourself and leave the red on. Then ask someone to something dull, and keep whoever comes.

**whoYouAre**

Two hours into a polite evening, you lean over and whisper, "Coming?" Twenty minutes later the two of you are sitting on a kerb with chips, laughing too loudly. Then they tell you something they've never told anyone. You weren't getting them into trouble. You were getting through to them.

People call you a bad influence, and you let them, because it's half true and it makes a better story. The rest of it: polite feels lonely to you. It keeps everyone at arm's length, you included. You don't want an audience. You want accomplices. Under every "come on" there's a "please come", and you'd rather raise the stakes than say that plainly. What you dread is being kept for the noise: loved as the wild one and never really known, or made sensible and quietly dropped. That's your gift: around you, people drop the act. They say the honest thing, the funny thing, and they like who they are when they're with you.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. You don't have to keep raising the stakes to keep people. Some time soon, plan something with nothing to dare in it: a Sunday food shop, soup on the stove, a film you've both seen twice. See who's glad to be there. Those are your accomplices, and they always were. They'll still be around when nothing's happening, and you get to be known for more than the noise.


## outlaw-lover · The Fugitive · *Rent-Free* (draft)

- tagline: You love like it's a getaway. Even where you stop is a hideout.
- epigraph: Its wine spends a night in the cold, as two newlyweds kept theirs: in a mine.
- drink: highball · highball · white_wine_dry_california_bay, soda_water · a tall stemmed wine glass, filled with ice
- closing: Ice first, soda last. Then pour it where you mean to land, and unpack.

**whoYouAre**

A one-way ticket. A job left on a Friday. A family dinner where you finally told them, and the table went quiet. You've chosen people, places and whole lives like that: all at once, and usually against advice. And every time, choosing and getting free felt like the same thing. Whoever you fell for was also the way out.

You'd never tell anyone how much you'd like to land somewhere. It feels like giving in. Being parked is the real fear: someone else deciding where you stop, and the life you ran towards slowly picking up the rules of the one you ran from.

And whoever you choose never has to guess. They know they were picked over everything else. Next to you, love looks like courage, and anyone watching starts asking what they'd leave for theirs.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. When the pull to go comes again, ask yourself what you'd be leaving. If it's someone deciding for you, go. If it isn't, stop, on purpose, in a place of your choosing, and make that the adventure. Being caught means someone else chose where you'd end up. Stopping means you did.
