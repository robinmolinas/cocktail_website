# Reversed pair: hero-innocent / innocent-hero


## hero-innocent · The Initiate · *Work It Out* (draft)

- tagline: There's more to you than this. You just can't prove it yet.
- epigraph: Vodka, beef broth, and an orange someone found on a Chinese menu.
- drink: highball · freeform · vodka, beef_broth_homemade, orange_juice, sherry_medium_dry, salt_pinch · tall highball glass (about 400 ml), filled with ice
- closing: Squeeze the orange peel over it last. When nobody will say what's wrong, read every menu you're handed.

**whoYouAre**

Someone you look up to tries something you've worked hard on and says, "Not quite." Nothing else. You smile, say you'll have another go, and that night you're still turning it over, long after they've forgotten they said it. You'd never admit how much one sentence from the right person can move you. Or why: you're sure there's more in you than anyone has seen so far, and you don't have the proof yet.

What you dread is the day it's for real: being asked for it properly, in front of the very person you wanted to impress, and having nothing to show. So you listen to people who know more as if every word might be the key. It's also your gift to them. People love teaching you, because you take a hard word home and come back with something. But part of you is still waiting for them to hand you the answer. Some answers nobody can hand you.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. So if someone whose opinion matters to you says "not quite" and stops there, don't hold out for an explanation. Take it home, and let the answer come from wherever it turns up: a conversation on a train, a shop window, something you read for another reason. When you bring it back, it'll be yours, and so will everything you build on it. Nobody is going to tell you when you're good enough. You'll know it the day your answer is one they hadn't thought of.


## innocent-hero · The Underdog · *Straight Up* (draft)

- tagline: Everyone else could see how far you'd come. You were the last to hear.
- epigraph: Mango and juniper, from far apart, have a note in common. It was always there.
- drink: daiquiri · shaken · borovicka, lime_juice, mango_syrup · a thin stemmed coupe, chilled in the freezer
- closing: No garnish. And when good news comes, let it in first.

**whoYouAre**

You never set out to win anything. You wanted to get a little better at the thing in front of you, then a little better again. So you rarely turn round to see how far it has taken you. Other people do, and when they mention it, you're honestly surprised.

And when something good comes your way, there's a moment before the happiness. You replay how it happened, checking it was all fair, as if someone might ask. Somewhere along the road you learned that good things can be taken back, and you couldn't bear anyone thinking you hadn't got there honestly.

What you don't notice is that you never suspect anyone else. When someone you know gets the job or the prize, you're simply glad, and sure they got there fairly too. Not many people manage that, and it's a gift: people watch you take the straight road and start to believe it goes somewhere.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. So when something good finds you again, don't search it for the catch. You remember how you got there, and nobody handed you any of it. Someone near you is watching to see whether the long way pays off. Show them it does. The checking can wait, and I'd bet it never finds anything.
