# Reversed pair: jester-sage / sage-jester


## jester-sage · The Cynic · *Against My Better Judgement* (draft)

- tagline: Everyone hears the joke. Hardly anyone hears the hope.
- epigraph: Bourbon, stirred dry. A four-line poem about San Francisco's 1906 fire ends on whiskey.
- drink: martini · stirred · bourbon, dry_vermouth, pear_syrup, angostura · cocktail glass (about 180 ml), chilled, no ice
- closing: Take the peel away once its oil is on. Then, for once, say the hope first and the joke second.

**whoYouAre**

The letter says the changes are "for your convenience". You have a line about it before anyone's finished reading it, and the table groans, then nods, because it's true. People assume you expect the worst of everything.

Here's what they miss. You only notice the gap because you still know how it should be. Under every joke is a picture of something better, and you've never quite given it up. You keep that part quiet. A joke is hard to laugh at; a hope is easy. What wears you out is the pretending: a dinner where everyone agrees to call it fine, and it goes on and on. Further down sits a fear you rarely say: that one day it won't be funny any more.

This matters more than people know. When something's absurd, you make it possible to laugh at it without giving up on it. After your line, everyone breathes out.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

6. Keep the jokes. Plenty of people get through the week because of them. But once, say the hope out loud, as yourself, before a joke can get there first. Tell someone how you think things could be. It might get laughed at. I'd bet it gets listened to, and I'd love you to find out.


## sage-jester · The Wise Fool · *In Good Part* (draft)

- tagline: You'll be the silly one, so nobody has to be the wrong one.
- epigraph: A club that teased politicians over dinner had a drink of its own. It was pink.
- drink: daiquiri · egg-white · plymouth_gin, lemon_juice, raspberry_red_wine_syrup, egg_white · coupe, chilled in the freezer, no ice in the glass
- closing: Froth it before the ice goes in. Then tell one joke tonight that's for nobody's good.

**whoYouAre**

Someone at the table has started to believe their own story. A boss, an old friend who's done well, the relative everyone humours. You don't correct them. Correcting them would cost them something with everyone watching. So you make it funny, carefully, aimed at them and not at the room, and they're the one laughing hardest. Nothing else happens that night. A week later, quietly, they've dropped it. Nobody mentions the joke, least of all you.

It sounded like a quip. It took you most of the evening. Nobody guesses how much the untrue thing bothered you while you were smiling: you can sit with almost anything except a story everyone has agreed to believe. The room that frightens you is the one where nobody's allowed to laugh, because that's where it stays true for good. And there's a cost you rarely mention. When you mean something about your own life, people are still waiting for the funny part.

So here it is, plainly, because it's worth something: you let people be wrong and keep their dignity. It's kinder than honesty on its own, and much harder.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. Keep telling the truth like that. The people who've been wrong around you are luckier than they know. But every joke of yours is working for something: the truth, the face, whoever it's about. Now and then, make one that carries nothing at all, daft from start to finish, just because it makes you laugh. That club kept a baby's rattle, after all.
