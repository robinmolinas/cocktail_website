# Reversed pair: magician-regular-guy / regular-guy-magician


## magician-regular-guy · The Entertainer · *Cold Water First* (draft)

- tagline: You practise alone so that strangers can laugh together.
- epigraph: People came to watch the fire. This is what was underneath it.
- drink: old-fashioned · hot · caol_ila_12, irish_pot_still_whiskey, demerara_sugar, water, lemon_peel · a small heatproof glass tumbler (about 300 ml), warmed with hot water first
- closing: Lift the saucer yourself. Then practise the next trick with someone.

**whoYouAre**

A pack of cards on a kitchen table, or at a bus stop while the bus is late, and for one minute the ordinary world goes strange. Grown-ups turn back into children. Whoever happened to be standing there gets it.

People think you do it for the gasp. The gasp is lovely, but what you really practise for is the second after, when two people who've never met look at each other and laugh. You're the only one there who knows how it's done, so you don't get the wonder. You get their faces, and that's what you came for. What you'd rarely say is how much it matters, and how much you dread the silence where that laugh should be, in front of people who owe you nothing. That's what the hours are against, and what they buy is a minute in which you turn a bus stop full of strangers into a room. Very few people can do that with a pack of cards.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. So when you're learning the next one, don't wait until it's perfect. Let one person watch you practise, spills and all. You'll find they laugh just as much, and you'll be in the laugh instead of watching it from behind the cards.


## regular-guy-magician · The Mechanic · *Good for Years* (draft)

- tagline: Everyone says get a new one. You've already got the back off.
- epigraph: No shaker, no strainer, no syrup. Just the last of a jar.
- drink: old-fashioned · shaken-spirit · mezcal, orange_marmalade · small tumbler, one large cube
- closing: Strain it through the gap in the lid. Then leave the jar by the sink, and enjoy a room where nothing's broken.

**whoYouAre**

Someone's lamp has died, or the zip's gone on a coat, and they're already saying it's had its day. You've got the back off before they've finished the sentence. A hair grip, a folded bit of card, tape from the kitchen drawer: you use whatever's there, and it works. It looks a bit odd. It lasts for years.

Quieter, though, is this. At a party where nothing's broken, you're not quite sure where to stand, and a dripping tap in the kitchen feels like being let in. What gets to you is the shrug, and "just get a new one", because a world that throws things away has less room for you in it. When you mend something, people keep their own thing, the bike they learned on or a grandparent's lamp, and the piece of their life that comes with it. And you show them what was wrong, so the impossible turns out to be ordinary.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. Next time you're asked round and nothing's broken, leave the dripping tap alone. Stay in the room with everyone else; the tap can wait. They didn't invite you for what you can mend. They invited you because they like having you there. And for once, you can enjoy the evening too.
