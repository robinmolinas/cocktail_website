# Reversed pair: innocent-lover / lover-innocent


## innocent-lover · The Teenage Heart-throb · *The Big Words* (draft)

- tagline: You've never loved anything a little.
- epigraph: Manhattan has a cocktail. So does the Bronx. Brooklyn is still asking.
- drink: martini · stirred · rum_jamaican_aged_50, sweet_vermouth, amaro_ciociaro, angostura · a cocktail glass (about 180 ml), chilled
- closing: Squeeze the peel from high up, so nobody misses it. If they laugh, keep the words the same size.

**whoYouAre**

When you love something, people hear about it. A person, a band, a team, the town you grew up in: it gets all of you, out loud, usually before anyone has asked. You're the one who makes the playlist, writes the birthday card that runs onto the back, and says the big words before anyone else dares to.

People assume you don't notice how it looks. You do. Nobody knows the pause after the big words better than you: the half-second before the room decides whether to smile with you or at you. You step into it regardless. The part you hide is the walk home afterwards, going back over the one face that smirked. What stings is being laughed at for meaning it, because it tempts you to say it a little smaller next time. Still, there's a gift in it: everyone you love has proof. Most people go their whole lives without being loved out loud, in front of others. Whoever you love never has to wonder.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

6. Whenever you say the big words and somebody laughs, don't take them back, and don't trim them for next time. Same words, same size. Somebody usually joins in. In Brooklyn, it was thirty-three recipes in a day.


## lover-innocent · The Pure Heart · *Wide Open* (draft)

- tagline: You've been hurt. You still love like you haven't.
- epigraph: A Japanese whisky, a whole egg, and a love story that began in Scotland.
- drink: flip · flip · nikka_from_the_barrel, demerara_syrup, egg_whole_lavender · coupe (about 180 ml), chilled
- closing: The egg goes in whole. And when you want to reply, reply now: that's what they love you for.

**whoYouAre**

You say it when you feel it. "I missed you", at the door, instead of "long time no see". You answer a message the minute it arrives, because you were glad to get it, and you can't see why you'd hide that. When you love someone, they know. Usually within the week.

Most people call it innocence. They assume nobody's ever hurt you. Somebody has, more than once, and every time you knew exactly what you were risking. You've heard all the advice: be careful, make them wait, don't show your hand so soon. You decided long ago that being hurt costs less than being careful. The bruises stay private, because showing them only brings the advice back. The thing you're quietly afraid of is the evening you notice you've waited three hours to reply, on purpose. And next to you, nobody has to pretend to care less than they do. They say it back. Love you don't have to earn is the rarest kind, and you give it to everyone you love.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. My advice is the opposite of everyone else's: don't learn the lesson. When it goes wrong again, and sometimes it will, someone will tell you to be more careful. Hear them out. Then love the next person exactly as you would have before, and if you ever catch yourself waiting three hours to reply, on purpose, reply now. Not because it's safe. Because that's how everyone near you learns to say it too. And I hope the next one says it back before you've finished saying it.
