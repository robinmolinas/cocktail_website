# Reversed pair: creator-sage / sage-creator


## creator-sage · The Old Master · *Beside the First* (draft)

- tagline: Everyone else calls it finished. You call it the first edition.
- epigraph: Its first author came back fourteen years later and added an 'Improved' one.
- drink: old-fashioned · shaken-spirit · rye, simple_syrup, triple_sec, angostura, green_chartreuse · a small coupe, chilled, its rim wiped with lemon
- closing: Taste it next to the one you already know. Then decide.

**whoYouAre**

People bring you what they've made so you can tell them it's right. Often it is, and you say so. But alone with something you made years ago, the one people still quote back to you, you keep asking whether it could be better. Not because it failed. Because it didn't, and everyone stopped looking.

What you dread is settling into being right, becoming the way it's always been done, with everyone too respectful to mention it. So you question it yourself, first. And because you'll do that to your own best work, the ones who work with you feel free to do it to theirs.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. Here's where I'd take a stand. Take the thing you're best known for, the one everyone says not to touch, and change one thing in it. Don't replace it. Show the two side by side, and let people choose. You'll learn the most from the ones who choose the old one.


## sage-creator · The Architect · *What It Rests On* (draft)

- tagline: People lean on what you make without ever wondering why it holds.
- epigraph: Bourbon's grain is at least half corn. This glass hides a little more in the syrup.
- drink: old-fashioned · freeform · bourbon, popcorn_demerara_syrup, angostura, ice_water · small coupe, chilled, no ice
- closing: Add the water and the peel, and that's your last job tonight. Then be a guest at your own evening.

**whoYouAre**

By the time you say "that'll work", you've already watched it fall down a dozen ways. The trip for twelve, the move, the kitchen that has to feed everyone at once: you've walked through it in your mind, found where it gives, and fixed it while everybody else was still choosing dates. So your calm yes has a whole night of no behind it, and nobody ever hears that part.

You don't much mind how something looks. Someone else can pick the colours and the name. What you can't trust is a thing that only looks sound: the confident arrangement nobody has tested, the promise with nothing underneath. You'll say "not yet" to something everyone loves before you'll watch people stand on it.

They rarely know it, but this is what you give them. Nothing goes wrong, so nobody asks why. They get to enjoy the evening without once thinking about the floor, and that is care, even if it never gets called that.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. One thing I'd push you on, though. When it's for people who matter to you, you fill every gap, because a gap is one more thing that could go wrong. Next time you lay out a weekend for them, check every part as usual, and then put the gaps in yourself, as carefully as anything else: a chair nobody's been given, an afternoon with no name on it. You'll simply have given them some air, and in my experience that's where the best of a weekend usually happens.
