# Reversed pair: caregiver-hero / hero-caregiver


## caregiver-hero · The Rescuer · *Off Duty* (draft)

- tagline: Everyone else is still deciding. You've already gone.
- epigraph: Nobody makes this one for themselves. And it won't wait.
- drink: old-fashioned · hot · irish_whiskey, honey, water, thyme_sprig · a wide teacup (about 250 ml), warmed with hot water first; not a tall mug
- closing: Two minutes for the thyme. Spend them sitting down.

**whoYouAre**

Something goes wrong: a fall on the stairs, a phone call at two in the morning, a friend's voice that isn't quite right. By the time anyone else has decided what to do, you're already moving. You don't feel brave. You feel clear. You're the one saying "you call, you hold this, stay with them", and it's the surest you ever feel.

Nobody sees the part that comes later. When everyone's safe and home, your hands start to shake, or you lie awake until four. And the quiet weeks can be harder than the bad nights, because with nobody to go out for, you're not sure what you're for. Standing back has always felt to you like the worst kind of selfishness. So you've never let anyone come out for you. You'd rather be the one who comes.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. You won't ask for help in a crisis. I know that, and I'm not going to argue with it. So turn it round. On some ordinary evening, when nothing's wrong and nobody needs you, let someone make you this, and sit down while they do. Let them do the going, just once.


## hero-caregiver · The Saviour · *Next One's Mine* (draft)

- tagline: You'll take anything on for anyone. Except a favour.
- epigraph: The vermouth in this glass is there because hardly anyone stopped after the first.
- drink: highball · highball · rye, sweet_vermouth, angostura, soda_water · tall glass (highball, about 350 ml), filled with ice
- closing: Let someone get this one. Don't get the next.

**whoYouAre**

The bill nobody can split, the call nobody wants to make, the mistake that was only half theirs: it's landed on someone near you. You don't rush. You weigh it up, quietly, and then you say it: "Put it on me." And they go home lighter. That's the kindness in you, and it counts for more than you'll ever let anyone say.

The other half is harder. Someone does you a small kindness, a lift, a meal, an evening of their time, and it's paid back within the week, with a little extra. Owing anyone, even briefly, feels like weakness to you, like leaving a door open that someone could walk through. So your books are always in credit. Plenty of people owe you something. You've never asked for any of it back.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. So when someone does something for you, don't pay it back. No dinner in return, no bigger present, no "I'll get you back for this". Every time you square it straight away, you take something from them: the feeling you get every time you say "put it on me". Let them keep that. And for once, you get to be the one who's let off.
