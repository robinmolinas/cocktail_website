## magician-outlaw · The Trickster · *No Accident* (approved            # Robin, 2026-09-24)

- tagline: You never break a rule by accident.
- epigraph: The story was a trick. The drink never was.
- drink: old-fashioned · carbonated · sugar_cube, angostura, peychauds, bourbon, triple_sec, champagne_brut · champagne flute
- closing: Don't stir it. It tells you the truth at the end.

**whoYouAre**

You see the strings. Where other people see how things are, you see how they were decided, and who decided them, and you can't resist giving those strings a tug.

You say your truest things as jokes, because a joke slips past people's guard where an argument never would. What looks like chaos from the outside is, in your head, a plan three moves ahead. You would rather be misunderstood than boring, and you'd rather shake a room than disappear in it. Here's what most people miss about you: underneath the mischief, you care a great deal. Every trick is really an invitation to see what you see. What scares you isn't getting caught. It's being ignored.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. Here's what I'd ask of you. The next time you feel like pulling the rug out from under a room, make it a trick with a gift inside, and leave people with something better than what they walked in with. Then stay long enough to show them how it was done. The trick is what they'll remember tonight. The gift is what they'll keep.


## outlaw-magician · The Disrupter · *For Its Own Good* (draft)

- tagline: Everyone else keeps it going. You love it enough to end it.
- epigraph: This glass's grape made one winemaker famous. Then he demoted his own famous wines.
- drink:  · built · grappa, nebbiolo_syrup, orange_bitters · a heavy old-fashioned glass, one large cube
- closing: Warm the wine, never boil it. Then take apart what you love, and leave room for someone to put it back.

**whoYouAre**

Someone says "but that's how it's always been", and you feel it before you can explain it. It's still standing, but it stopped being alive a while ago: the trip nobody enjoys any more, the group that meets because it always has, the way of working everyone defends and nobody would choose today. Sooner or later you're the one who says so. Then you're the one who ends it, and the one they're cross with.

Here's the thing, though: you only ever break the things you love. Nobody fights that hard over something they don't care about. And afterwards you miss it, often more than the ones who were angry with you. You tell no one, in case it's taken for an apology. Your real fear is quieter than any row. It's something good being kept going out of politeness, year after year, until nobody remembers why it mattered, and you, who saw it, said nothing.

And it's worth saying plainly: because of you, the thing they loved gets a second life, the cross ones included. They rarely thank you at the time. But what grows back is usually better, and very often it's still theirs. That's love too: the kind that's willing to end things.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. When you next end something you love, say why while you're doing it: that it's for the thing's sake. Then leave the rebuilding to someone else, even if they bring back something that looks a lot like the old way. That isn't your break being undone. It's what it was for. And you get to love the thing again, without carrying it alone.
