**whoYouAre**

Someone you love mentions that the car's making that noise again. That's all. They'd never ask, and they don't need to: you heard it, because it's exactly how you'd have said it. By Saturday you're flat on your back underneath it, and neither of you makes a thing of it. A mention is enough. What comes back isn't a card or a speech. It's the thing done, for as long as it takes.

The sentence you've never quite managed is the other one, the one that says why. The feeling's there, full size. Said out loud, it sounds like someone else's words, so it stays put, and you tell yourself they know. Mostly they do. But now and then, on a long drive home, you think about the day it'll be too late to check. Here's what your way gives the people you love: they never have to find the words to ask, and they never owe you a thank-you. They always know where they can lean. That's worth more than any speech.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. Forget the speech. Nobody who loves you is waiting for one. You need one line, said while you're busy with something else. Under the car. Halfway through the job. On the drive home, eyes on the road. "I'd do this for you any day" is plenty. They've always known where they can lean. Now they'll know why.


## regular-guy-lover · The One Next Door · *First Choice* (draft)

- tagline: You're everyone's easy yes. Just once, you'd like to be someone's plan A.
- epigraph: Chicago's answer to New England's punch was made with New England rum.
- drink: daiquiri · freeform · rum_aged, sweet_vermouth, rich_syrup, lemon_juice, orange_bitters, water · coupe, chilled
- closing: Give the peach its few days in the brandy. Some things are worth being waited for.

**whoYouAre**

Half past eight on a Friday, your phone lights up: "free tonight?" You know what it means. Something else fell through. And you nearly always are free, and you nearly always go, and ten minutes after you arrive they've kicked their shoes off and they're telling you about their week. People are at ease with you in a way they aren't anywhere else. Families take to you on the first visit. New people get brought to meet you first, because with you nobody has to try.

What you don't tell anyone is that you'd like, now and then, to be the plan and not the fallback. Not the easy option: the one somebody picked on purpose. You don't say it, because wanting something out loud feels like a fuss, and you've never wanted to be the one causing it. So you stay gracious, and part of you wonders whether you'll always be liked by everyone and picked first by no one. With you, nobody has to earn their place, and that was never a consolation prize.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. In that story, Williams was a guest in someone else's house. He could simply have agreed. Instead he sent for something better, and let the room decide. When you next notice you're someone's easy option, don't be gracious about it. Let them see you'd like to be first. Whoever picks you then won't be settling. They'll know exactly what they've got, and you can simply enjoy being chosen.
