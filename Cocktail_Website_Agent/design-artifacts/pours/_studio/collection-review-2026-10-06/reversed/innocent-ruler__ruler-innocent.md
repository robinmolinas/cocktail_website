## innocent-ruler · The Little Prince · *Whole World* (draft)

- tagline: It looks small to everyone else. It's never been small to you.
- epigraph: Thrown tin to tin. Barcelona, it's said, was the last place still doing it.
- drink: martini · stirred · gin_london_dry, dry_vermouth · a small wine glass (about 120 ml), chilled in the freezer, no ice
- closing: Four or five throws, then stop while it's cold. Make it for someone, and watch their face at the second sip.

**whoYouAre**

Someone asks what you've been up to, and you say "nothing much, just the usual". You mean the herbs on the windowsill, the friend you've rung every week since school, the old bike you keep running. To anyone else it looks small. It has never once struck you that small makes it matter less. Other people ask how big it is. You ask whether it's all right today.

You keep the small jobs small by doing them early: the drip seen to the day it starts, the hurt feelings sorted out before dinner. Nobody notices those. They aren't meant to be noticed. And you never mention the fear. Miss one day, and it feels as if all of it could slip. So you shrug and call it your little thing, because the seriousness gets smiled at. But look at what that care does for everyone near you. Whatever you're looking after is safe, and anyone close to you learns that a small thing taken seriously is worth more than a big thing taken lightly. With you, nothing small is ever only small.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. You look after it so closely that you mostly look for what might be going wrong. Next time, before you check what needs doing, stop and look at it the way someone new would, as if you'd never seen it. It's been all right for years now. That isn't luck. It's every one of those days, and you're allowed to take pleasure in what they built.


## ruler-innocent · The Diva · *Say So* (draft)

- tagline: You won't settle for fine. Not with wonderful right there.
- epigraph: The bar behind this Martini was built to look as if it had always been there.
- drink: martini · stirred · noilly_prat_original_dry, gin_london_dry, tokaji_aszu, orange_bitters · large V-shaped cocktail glass (about 250 ml), chilled, no ice
- closing: Pour more vermouth than gin. Make the joke yourself, then ask for it anyway.

**whoYouAre**

You walk in and you know at once. Wrong table, the light's too harsh, and that song isn't helping. So you say so, kindly, usually. If nothing happens, a little less kindly. And it's never only your own evening you're asking for. You want all of it right: the room, the glass, the music, everyone at their best.

People hear a demand. What you're really saying is that you still think it can be lovely. Most people stopped expecting that years ago. You didn't. You rarely let that part show, because hope is easy to laugh at, and a demand at least sounds strong. And you mind being called difficult more than you let on. What you dread is asking, watching nobody move, and then the whole thing sliding, bit by bit, into a mess. So here's what your asking gives. Because you said something, everyone gets the better night, including the ones who'd never have asked. Most of them wanted it too. It matters more than anyone thinks to tell you.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. So keep asking. Please don't stop. But borrow Chalker's trick. You and I both know it isn't too much. Make the joke anyway, with a grin, before you ask: "I know, it's a bit much." Then ask. It's hard to call someone difficult when they've laughed first, and once people are laughing with you, they'll help you get it right. And when it's right, enjoy it as much as you knew you would.
