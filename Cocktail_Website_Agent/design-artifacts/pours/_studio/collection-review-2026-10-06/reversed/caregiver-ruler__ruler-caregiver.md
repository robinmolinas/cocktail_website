# Reversed pair: caregiver-ruler / ruler-caregiver


## caregiver-ruler · The Nanny · *Fair Measure* (draft)

- tagline: You were never against the fun. You're why it lasts.
- epigraph: Most of this glass is plain water. In 1740, that was an order.
- drink: highball · highball · rum_navy, lime_juice, demerara_sherry_vinegar_syrup, water · tumbler or highball glass (about 300 ml), no ice
- closing: Four of water to one of rum. The lime and the sugar are theirs.

**whoYouAre**

It's late, everyone's having a good time, and you're the one who says "one more, then home." You're the one who checks what's left in the account before the trip gets booked, who asks for a text when they're home, who keeps the Sunday call going long after the others have let it slide. Some people hear a rule. It isn't one, not really. It's what keeps the good thing there tomorrow.

People call you the sensible one, and on worse nights, something less kind. It stings more than you show, because you were never against the fun. You're the reason there's any left in the morning. Everyone else gets to forget the time because you never do. And somewhere under the plans is a fear you wouldn't say out loud: that if you stopped holding it all, it would come apart, and it would be your fault for wanting one night off.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. Next time you set the limit, set it, and then let the rest be theirs to make. Don't check how they did it. Focus on enjoying the fun yourself. The taste was always theirs. It'll still hold, and you might even get that night off.


## ruler-caregiver · The Caring Leader · *Go On* (draft)

- tagline: Most of the fights you've stopped never happened.
- epigraph: This drink can sit on its ice while someone finishes talking. Someone once timed it.
- drink: martini · stirred · cognac, sweet_vermouth, mandarin_liqueur, angostura · cocktail glass (about 180 ml), chilled
- closing: Let it wait on the ice until they've said everything. Then strain it, and give your answer.

**whoYouAre**

You hear it before anyone else does. A joke at dinner that lands a little too hard. A voice going up half a note in a meeting. A group chat going quiet in the wrong way. By the time the others notice, you've already pulled up a chair, asked the one who's upset what they meant, and let them say all of it.

People take you for easygoing. You aren't. You're listening the whole time, because when people are in your care, you'd rather hear the hard thing tonight than watch it break something later. You could settle most of it with one word, and you rarely use it. You'd rather they finish. It matters more than you think: people say the real thing around you, because they're sure you'll hear all of it. You never mention how hard you work at it, or the fear underneath: that the one time you miss it, it all comes apart, and someone you were looking after pays for it.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. You're right to hear people out, and they're right to trust you for it. But one day the kind thing will be the no: the tab stopped, the plan called off, the decision made before everyone agrees. Say it plainly, once they've finished. The people you listened to won't walk out. They stayed because you listened, and that's exactly why your no will hold.
