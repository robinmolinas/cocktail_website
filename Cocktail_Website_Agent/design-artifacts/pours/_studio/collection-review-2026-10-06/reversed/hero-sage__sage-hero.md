# Reversed pair: hero-sage / sage-hero


## hero-sage · The Problem Solver · *Leave It With Me* (draft)

- tagline: The plan's gone. Now it gets interesting.
- epigraph: The first was made in a pitcher, a family story goes, when the gin ran out.
- drink: daiquiri · shaken · lime_juice, sugar, makrut_lime_leaf, rum_white · six small tumblers (about 180 ml), chilled in the freezer; a pitcher (about 1.5 litres)
- closing: Pour all six in one go, along the row and back. And the next time the plan falls apart, admit you're having fun.

**whoYouAre**

The oven gives up an hour before twelve people arrive. Someone says the evening's ruined, someone else starts looking up takeaways, and you're already at the open fridge, asking what you've got. You'd never wish it on anyone. But when the plan breaks and every face turns to you, a small part of you lights up. You keep that quiet. It hardly seems fair to enjoy someone else's emergency.

The bit you never mention is the blank before the answer comes. They're all looking at you, and there's nothing there yet. That blank is your nightmare, and somehow the whole fun of it too, because a puzzle that couldn't beat you wouldn't be worth solving. So you keep a level face through it, and no one learns how close it felt. And here's what it does for everyone else: the moment you ask what there is to work with, the room stops panicking and starts looking. Everyone gets something to do with their hands, and a little of your nerve. That's a real gift, and most people never notice who started it.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. When every face next turns to you and nothing has come yet, tell them the truth, smiling: this is the bit you love most. Watch what that does. They stop waiting for you to produce an answer and start hunting with you, and the blank isn't yours alone any more. It becomes a game the whole room is playing, and you can love it in the open.


## sage-hero · The Grandmaster · *At the Time* (draft)

- tagline: You'll let a good move look bad, and not say a word.
- epigraph: Rye, sugar, bitters, peel. The coffee is in the ice, and arrives later.
- drink: old-fashioned · built · rye_barrel_proof, demerara_syrup, angostura, water · heavy double old-fashioned glass (about 350 ml), one large block of coffee ice
- closing: Swirl it once the coffee starts to show. When you miss one, make the next move just as steadily.

**whoYouAre**

You turned down the offer everyone said you'd regret. Or you let the first chance go by, or gave up a point in an argument you could easily have won. People shook their heads, kindly, and it looked as if you'd got it wrong. You let it look that way. You couldn't prove it yet, and you won't promise what you can't show.

It costs you, too. Letting go of the sure thing never feels clever. It's a loss, and you carry it, saying nothing, until the move plays out. You don't mind looking as if you've got it wrong for a while. What you mind is the move you didn't see, after people trusted you to have read it all.

When everyone wants the quick win, you can sit through the slow one, and whoever is with you can stop panicking. That steadiness is a kindness, even on the days it gets called stubbornness.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. Now, the thing I'd want you to hear. One day there'll be a trap you don't see. Not because you weren't paying attention: you were, more closely than anyone at the table. Some things can't be read from where anyone is sitting. When it happens, it doesn't mean the people who doubted you were right. Take that loss as plainly as you take the ones you choose, and carry on. You'll have lost a piece you didn't mean to give. You'll still have everything that made you good at this.
