# Reversed pair: jester-outlaw / outlaw-jester


## jester-outlaw · The Scandalmonger · *Quote Me* (draft)

- tagline: Rooms get more honest around you. Rarely politer.
- epigraph: The cocktail's earliest known definition in print comes with a dig at the other side.
- drink: old-fashioned · freeform · rum_gold_jamaican, banane_du_bresil, angostura, ice_water · small tumbler (about 200 ml), chilled in the freezer, no ice
- closing: Name the banana as you pour it. Then ask who else thinks so, while everyone's still at the table.

**whoYouAre**

Dinner, eight of you, and everyone has spent the starter praising something nobody there actually likes. You put your fork down and say so, in the bluntest words available. There's a gasp. Somebody laughs and turns it into a cough. Then you watch for whoever will say it back.

Hardly anyone hears it right. It comes out like a verdict, but you mean it as a question. You enjoy the gasp, and you won't pretend you don't. And often you go too far on purpose: once you've said the outrageous version, the honest one everybody's been sitting on suddenly sounds mild, and someone dares. What wears you down is good manners doing their quiet work: the gasp smoothed over, someone offering round the potatoes, and the same people agreeing with you afterwards, by the coats, where it doesn't count.

It's time someone told you what that's worth: you take the blame for the first line so the honest conversation can happen in the room. You turn "how dare you" into "hang on, is that true?" Not everyone thanks you for it. They should.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. Keep saying it. A room needs someone who will. But when the gasp next gets smoothed over and the potatoes start going round, don't reach for a bigger line. Ask it as the question it always was, out loud, and then wait. Someone usually answers. Then you get the conversation you came for, there and then, while it still counts.


## outlaw-jester · The Subverter · *With the Bite In* (draft)

- tagline: Said straight, they'd stop you. So you make them laugh.
- epigraph: A song made rum and Coca-Cola famous. The song was never really about the drink.
- drink: highball · highball · rum_angostura_1919, lime_juice, coca_cola, angostura · a tall glass, filled with ice
- closing: Squeeze the lime and drop it in. If they sing it back wrong, write a new one.

**whoYouAre**

There's a meeting, or a family dinner, or a rule nobody's supposed to question, and everyone's nodding along. Then you say something, lightly, and the table laughs, the person at the head of it too, a beat late. Nobody can quite tell you off. It was only funny. That was the idea.

Nobody notices that you chose it. The joke isn't a reflex, and it never punches down. It's aimed at whoever's at the head of the table, because that's the one place a plain sentence would be stopped. So you keep quiet about how much of it you mean: the moment they know, they stop laughing and start stopping you. A telling-off you can live with; it means it landed. What you dread is hearing your line from somebody else, everyone laughing, with the point quietly taken out.

And what you give is more than a laugh. When a room laughs at whatever's in charge, it sees how ordinary the thing really is, and fears it less. That's how you hand people their nerve back, and it's a real gift, whatever you call it.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. One day you'll hear a line of yours from someone else, to a big laugh, with the point gone. Don't spend the evening proving it was yours. Come up with another, and aim it a little higher than the last. A joke can be borrowed; the person it came from can't, and every room that laughed with you is a little braver for it.
