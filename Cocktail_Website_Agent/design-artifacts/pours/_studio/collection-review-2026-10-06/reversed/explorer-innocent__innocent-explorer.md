# Reversed pair: explorer-innocent / innocent-explorer


## explorer-innocent · The Discoverer · *In a Minute* (draft)

- tagline: Most people need a good reason to stop. You need one to hurry.
- epigraph: Someone once put drinks like this in bottles for years, to see what time would do.
- drink: martini · stirred · rum_white, blanc_vermouth, madeira_rainwater, orange_curacao, pomegranate_syrup · a small stemmed wine glass (about 200 ml), chilled, no ice
- closing: Try a spoon of the Madeira on its own before it goes in. It took its time getting here.

**whoYouAre**

On any walk, you're a few steps behind. Something stopped you: frost that's stayed only in the shadow of a fence, a bee climbing into a flower the wrong way round, a sign you've passed a hundred times and have only now read properly. Somewhere up ahead, someone says "come on". You've been hearing "come on" for as long as you can remember.

You were never dawdling. You wanted to see what it would do. You don't often say how much a small thing can make your day, because it can sound naive, and there's an old, faint guilt about holding everyone up. It isn't being slow that scares you. It's getting busy, and sure, and one day walking straight past something without feeling the tug, and not even missing it. The people who walk with you see more than they would on their own. The ordinary comes back to them new. That's what your slowness gives them, and every so often the thing you stopped for turns out to matter to everyone.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. Busy comes for everyone, and the stopping goes first. You won't hear it go. So have one thing going that can't be hurried: a cutting in a jar on the windowsill, a question you haven't looked up yet. Look in on it now and then. And when someone up ahead says "come on", take the minute. It's usually the best one of the walk.


## innocent-explorer · The Free Spirit · *The First Guess* (draft)

- tagline: Your questions are how everyone else starts looking.
- epigraph: From around Cognac, where something growing on the walls was misnamed for over a century.
- drink: martini · stirred · cognac, cognac_sichuan_pepper, pineau_des_charentes_blanc · a small tulip-shaped glass (about 180 ml), chilled, no ice
- closing: Ask what's in it before anyone tells you what it is.

**whoYouAre**

When someone says "actually, nobody's really sure", most people sigh. You light up. You ask why: why the form needs that box, why a thing is called what it's called, why everyone does it this way. You're not making a point. You'd honestly like to know, and you're secretly hoping the answer will be stranger than you expected. It usually is.

Not everyone hears it that way. Over the years a few of your questions got you told off: "because I said so", the sigh, the look across the table. So now you ask lightly, with a smile, as if it hardly matters. It matters. The worst answer, to you, is a label: "oh, it's just a…", handed over to close the subject, so that nobody looks again. And yet around you, people do look again. With you, "I don't know" stops being embarrassing and becomes the start of the fun, and people need that more than they'll ever tell you.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. So when a question next comes to you, don't make it small. Ask it properly, and offer your guess, even if you suspect it's wrong. Being wrong didn't keep Baudoin's name off it. It got the whole thing looked at. And I'd bet the real answer turns out stranger than your guess. That's the part you'll enjoy most.
