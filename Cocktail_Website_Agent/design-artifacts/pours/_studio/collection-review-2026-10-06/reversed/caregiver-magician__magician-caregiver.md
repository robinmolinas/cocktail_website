# Reversed pair: caregiver-magician / magician-caregiver


## caregiver-magician · The Healer · *Till Spring* (draft)

- tagline: You believe they'll be all right before they do.
- epigraph: Bitter orange and sweet orange, side by side. Neither one is hiding the other.
- drink: highball · highball · bigallet_china_china, pomegranate_syrup, soda_water, cognac · a tumbler (about 300 ml), filled with ice
- closing: Pour the brandy last, over a spoon. How it lands was never all up to you.

**whoYouAre**

You can tell when someone is going to be all right, usually a good while before they can. A friend swears they'll never get over it, and you think: give it till spring. You don't say so, because it would sound like you weren't listening. You just keep turning up with something easy to say yes to: a walk, an errand, a Sunday that needs a second pair of hands. And one day they notice they've been getting better for a while, somewhere in all those Sundays.

You know that help can taste bitter. Being looked after can feel like being told you're not coping, so you make yours easy to take. But the few who didn't get better stay with you. You go back over them, wondering what you missed, as if their mending were yours to finish. That's the price of believing in people before they do, and you've never once thought it wasn't worth paying.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. The ones who didn't get better: put them down. Their mending was never yours to finish. You were the sweet beside the bitter, the reason the hard part got taken at all, and that was always the job. Next time you catch yourself going back over one of them, remember the Sundays instead.


## magician-caregiver · The Fairy Godparent · *Their Night* (draft)

- tagline: It was never your night. You made sure it was theirs.
- epigraph: Reportedly, a 1911 surprise party got its own drink. The name probably went elsewhere.
- drink: old-fashioned · shaken-spirit · anis_dulce, anis_seco, peychauds, angostura · cocktail glass, chilled
- closing: Shake it until the tin is ice-cold. This time, don't slip out early.

**whoYouAre**

You notice what someone needs before they've said it, and you act before anyone asks. By the time their big day comes, it's done: the call made, the table booked, the right people quietly in the room. And you're at the back, or not there at all, because it was never meant to be about you.

What you'd never admit is the question that comes after: did I have the right? Help nobody asked for can land wrong, and you know it, so part of you is always listening for the moment a kindness turns out to be meddling. Most of the time it doesn't, because you only do it for people you've watched closely enough to know. Help nobody has to thank you for is help nobody has to repay. You hand someone a moment that is completely their own.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. Keep doing it without asking. The people you do it for are the ones you know well enough. Only, when you've arranged someone's moment, don't leave before it arrives. A surprise is the one kind of help you can see land: the door opens, the room shouts, and for a second they can't speak. Be there for that second. That's how you'll know you had the right.
