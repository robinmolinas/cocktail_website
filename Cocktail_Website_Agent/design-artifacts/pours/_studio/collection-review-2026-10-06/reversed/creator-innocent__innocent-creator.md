# Reversed pair: creator-innocent / innocent-creator


## creator-innocent · The Minimalist · *Half a Rim* (draft)

- tagline: You take things away, and somehow there's more.
- epigraph: Its maker called it the house Margarita. Other people named it after the restaurant.
- drink: daiquiri · shaken · tequila_blanco, lime_juice, agave_nectar, water · rocks glass (a short, heavy tumbler), salt on half the rim, one large clear ice cube
- closing: Cut the cloudy part off the ice. Then stop cutting.

**whoYouAre**

You rewrite a message three times, and every time it gets shorter. The last version looks like it took a minute. Most of what you make is what you said no to.

You don't want less. You want to feel each thing properly, and too much of anything drowns it out. Some people mistake the quiet for coldness, or for plain. The ones who know you know it's room: what you make leaves space, and people find their own meaning in it.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. Sometimes you cut one thing too many, and it's usually the one that was only there because you loved it. The salt on this glass isn't needed. It's there on one side, in case you want it. Leave one thing like that in whatever you make next. Not because it's needed. Because you like it.


## innocent-creator · The Naive Artist · *The Way It Felt* (draft)

- tagline: You never saw what was wrong with it. Nor did anyone it was for.
- epigraph: Half red wine, half cola. There's a right bottle to mix, apparently.
- drink: highball · highball · red_wine_young_spanish, coca_cola, kalimotxo_ice · tall glass (about 400 ml), filled with the frozen Kalimotxo cubes
- closing: Stir once, over its own ice. How it's usually done can wait until they've tasted it.

**whoYouAre**

What you've made, you hand over straight away: a cake iced the colour the day felt, a card you drew instead of buying one, a birthday song with one verse too many. It's obviously lovely. It hasn't crossed your mind that there's a proper way to do it.

Then someone explains, kindly, how it's usually done. You nod. You still don't see what was wrong with it. The day you'd dread is the one when the explanation starts to make sense. Because what you make rarely looks like the thing. It looks like how the thing felt, and the people it's for can always tell. That's rarer than getting it right, and it's worth more.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. So when someone next explains the proper way, listen. Some of it will be useful. Then make the next thing the way it looked to you, and hand it straight to whoever you made it for, before anyone else sees it. They'll see what you meant. And you'll get to enjoy making it again, the way you did before anyone explained.
