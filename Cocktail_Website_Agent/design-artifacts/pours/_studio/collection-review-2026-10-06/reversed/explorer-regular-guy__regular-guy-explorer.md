## explorer-regular-guy · The Tourist · *Brought Home* (draft)

- tagline: Half the people you know have eaten somewhere because of you.
- epigraph: Two places go into this glass: a village in Mexico, and wherever you live.
- drink: highball · highball · local_honey_syrup, lime_juice, grapefruit_juice, mezcal_del_maguey_chichicapa, soda_water · a tall highball glass (about 300 ml), filled with ice
- closing: Buy the honey on a street near home you've never walked down. It still counts.

**whoYouAre**

You're halfway through the best meal of the trip, down a street the guidebook didn't mention, and you're already working out who you'll tell. You'll come home with the name of the place, what to order and who to ask for.

Truth is, you like the guidebook. Around people who brag about never booking anything, you leave the return ticket out. Softer still: the best night of a trip is sometimes the one after it, at your own table, telling it. Not because you didn't love being there. Because a find isn't finished until someone you love has heard about it. So the one trip you'd never book is the one with no way back: stuck somewhere, even somewhere beautiful, with no one to tell. You bring it home instead, and everyone who stayed gets to go a little. That's what you give them: the world, handed round, with directions.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. You don't need a ticket to go one street further. Away, you give a place everything: the market, the side street, the tip from someone who lives there. At home, you take the same road to the same shop. Try the street after it. Ask the person behind the counter where they'd eat. I'd bet there's a find twenty minutes from your door that nobody you know has heard of yet, and you're the one who'll tell them. That's a whole trip, and you'll be home in time to tell it.


## regular-guy-explorer · The Entrepreneur · *Loose on Top* (draft)

- tagline: You never wanted to be the boss. You just couldn't bear having one.
- epigraph: One restaurant owner chose the brands in this drink. Whoever's pouring chooses the top.
- drink: sidecar · shaken · tequila_blanco, giffard_triple_sec, lime_juice, simple_syrup · cocktail glass (about 200 ml), chilled, no salt
- closing: Measure the bottom exactly. Then hand the top to someone else, and join the people you made room for.

**whoYouAre**

You started something of your own. Maybe a Sunday football team, a stall at the weekend market, a supper that turned into a monthly thing, or a whole business. When people ask why, they expect a big reason. The real one is smaller, and you rarely say it: you wanted to live your way, and pay your own way doing it.

There's something else you keep quiet. Somewhere along the line, other people started treating it as theirs. They turn up early, bring friends and save each other seats. And part of you worries about ending up in the corner, keeping the whole thing running for everyone else to belong to. The freedom you went out for always turns out to have room for other people in it.

**yours (proposal paragraph)**

5. Don't leave it as late as he did. Whatever you've started, the team, the stall, the thing with your name on the door, hand someone the piece you've kept hold of and let them do it their way. It won't be how you'd do it. It'll be theirs, and that means it's working. Then turn up the way everyone else does: early, with a friend, for no reason except that you like it there. You went out on your own to be free. You don't have to stay on your own to keep it.
