## 1. jester-hero, *The Wink* (Robin D6, option A)

### First, my error, struck

Card C5 and the dossier's guard row say "the men were a Texian raiding force taken at Mier". **That was only partly true. It came from me, and D6's "Why" (and its example clause, "Texans taken on a raid into Mexico") were built on it.** Green's own pages show that two groups were in the room at the bowl in April 1843:

- **The Bexar prisoners:** "fifty of our countrymen" taken "the September previous" by General Woll (Green p. 235). Woll's Mexican army took San Antonio on 11 September 1842. About fifty-three Anglo townsmen fired on it first and then surrendered as prisoners of war (pp. 29–30). Woll pulled out of the town afterwards (p. 39). Appendix I counts "Sixty-seven Bexar Prisoners captured by General Woll" (p. 447). TSHA's "Perote Prison" (Connor) has "about fifty men captured in San Antonio by Adrián Woll" placed in Perote in December 1842.
- **The Mier men:** Green "and fifteen companions of the Mier men", lodged in the next room (p. 243). They were part of a force that crossed the Rio Grande, occupied Mier, levied supplies, took the alcalde and attacked the town in December 1842 (Green pp. 74–82). TSHA's "Mier Expedition" (Nance) calls it "the last of the raiding expeditions from Texas".
- **All hands came to the bowl.** "We went around to the prison rooms, and summoned all hands" (p. 259).
- **Trimble, the owl man, was a Bexar prisoner, not a Mier man.** He is listed among "Those who died in Prison in Mexico" inside the Bexar list (Appendix I p. 448, "7 Trimble (Tecolote)"). The list ends with "the Remainder of the Bexar Prisoners alive". He appears nowhere in the Mier rosters.

So "Texans taken on a raid into Mexico" would be false for most of the room, including the man the story is about. The clause has to cover both groups. It does that by naming the raids both ways and taking no side. Something else changes in the same sentence: "five months from home" is Green's figure for his own party (p. 256). The Bexar men had been taken in September, seven months before, so the reading now says "months".

### y1, sentence 1 (the replacement)

**Before:**
> In April 1843, prisoners from Texas were being held in the fortress prison at Perote, in Mexico, five months from home, with chains riveted round their ankles.

**After:**
> In April 1843, the fortress prison at Perote, in Mexico, held prisoners from Texas: men taken in the raids the two had been making on each other the year before, months from home now, with chains riveted round their ankles.

- **Sources, claim by claim:** Perote, prisoners from Texas: Green pp. 235, 243; TSHA "Perote Prison". "taken in the raids … on each other": the Bexar men were taken when Woll's army seized San Antonio, 11 Sept 1842, which it then left (pp. 29–30, 39); the Mier men were taken on the Texan raid on Mier, Dec 1842 (pp. 74–82; TSHA "Mier Expedition"). "Raids" is our word for both moves (Green calls Woll's an "invasion", p. 29; Nance calls Mier a "raiding expedition"). "the year before" = 1842, true of both. "months from home": Mier men "five months" (p. 256), Bexar men about seven. "chains riveted round their ankles": p. 259.
- **Fences, all held:** no war named (the word "war" doesn't appear), no side ("the two … on each other"), no deaths, no battle named, no anniversary, no toasts, no Green.
- **Checks:** the sentence grows from 29 to 40 words, so history goes to about 47% of yours (Wren's count was 225 of 490), still under half. The new 4-grams were grepped against every pour ("raids the two", "making on each other", "months from home now", "the two had been"), and none are shared. The next sentence ("They called the chains their jewellery.") still follows on.
- **Wren's call:** "the two" means Mexico and Texas. If she wants to name them, she can write "the raids Texas and Mexico had been making on each other", which is equally true but puts "Texas" in the sentence twice. Don't use "the two countries": that makes Texas's sovereignty in 1842 a claim.
