# Corpus review (2026-10-06)

Read across all 132 pours (`corpus/*.md`), all 66 `reversed/` files and all twelve family reviews. These are recommendations only. Robin's own words (`robin-edits.md`: every caregiver edit, plus *Four Shares* y4) are never touched or proposed for change below. Counts come from a scratch parse of the corpus files. They're there to start a reading, and none of them justifies a change on its own.

Summary: the five things that matter most across the collection, ranked.

1. **Two pours serve the same cocktail.** *Somewhere to Land* (jester-ruler) and *Word Gets Round* (regular-guy-ruler) are both a whiskey Rickey: whiskey, lime, sparkling water, no sugar. Both say "The cocktail I've made for you is a Rickey, a (renowned) classic", and their methods share a line word for word ("No sugar, and nothing on top."). `signals.md` missed the pair (rye vs bourbon, Apollinaris vs soda). This is the one recipe pair that meets Robin's own exclusion ("only the exact same cocktail is off-limits"). *Word Gets Round*'s story is about limes, not the Rickey, so it's the one that should move.
2. **The whoYouAre engine is audible, more than any phrase.** About 40 pours turn the fear the same way: "X doesn't scare you. Y does." or "What you dread isn't X. It's Y.". About 30 end whoYouAre on a rarity stamp ("That's rarer than you think", "Very few people can", "no small thing"), and about 25 announce the kindness before giving it ("Here's what that gives…", "it deserves to be said"). Each one does what the rulebook asks. Together, they mean a guest comparing two or three pours with friends will hear the same three moves in the same order. This is the main subject of the variety rules below.
3. **One reversed pair is interchangeable, and the family reviews disagree on the fix.** *Making the Calls* / *On Their Behalf* (creator-ruler / ruler-creator) open on the same observation ("slightly off… a word on a page"). Both family reviews propose rewriting their own side's ¶1. Only one change is needed: creator-ruler's, from the creator review.
4. **Of the 66 reversed pairs, 52 are distinct, 13 close and 1 interchangeable.** Of the close ones, five collide on a specific sentence rather than on territory, and one sentence fixes each: *I'll Tell You Later* / *Only Half Joking* (both "not the no, the flat answer"), *Hold the Shark* / *The Wink* (both pay off on "It'll be you" / "It was you"), *By Design* / *Up Close* ("simply born like that" / "simply like that"), *Already There* / *Plain to See* (both "check my reasons"), and *The Big Words* / *Wide Open* (both "don't shrink it next time").
5. **The rest is habits of position, and none is wrong alone.** "I like to think" is in yours 3 in 108 pours, and opens yours 3 in 42. "So…" opens 55 of the 132 proposals. "I hope…" ends 19 readings. Ten closing lines repeat the proposal as "Next time, …". Repeated anchors are well fenced. The one repeated authority is the *Oxford Companion*, named in the guest text of 9 readings under four different names.

**Proposed variety rules.** These are for the editor, applied only when a pour is already being edited for another reason or as part of the calibration batch. The aim is to reach the targets across the collection, not to rewrite every instance. Each "before" is verbatim.

**V1. The fear is said straight in most pours.** Keep the "not X, but Y" turn only where X is something people really assume about the guest. The frame "X doesn't scare you. Y does." goes in no more than one pour per archetype row or column. Target: the turn in about 1 pour in 6 (from about 1 in 3).
- *Dressed for It* (hero-ruler) · before: "The hard part doesn't frighten you. Wobbling in front of them does." → after: "What you guard against is wobbling in front of them."
- *The Long View* (ruler-magician) · before: "The storm doesn't frighten you. Being the one in charge when it wins does, and being remembered for that." → after: "What you lie awake over is being the one in charge on the day the storm wins, and being remembered for that."
- (third, if wanted) *Just Knew* (explorer-magician) · before: "The looking doesn't scare you. A life that stops asking what it's for does." → after: "What would scare you is a life that stops asking what it's for."

**V2. Say the kindness the way Robin did: in the sentence, without an announcement and without a rarity grade.** State what the guest's way gives (the rule stands, once per reading), and say its worth through the people who receive it or through a consequence. "Here's what… / it deserves to be said / someone should tell you" goes in at most 1 pour in 4. "That's rare / Very few people / no small thing / Hardly anyone can" goes in at most 1 in 5.
- *Whoever Comes In* (regular-guy-caregiver) · before: "Here's what it gives, and it deserves to be said: the people you stop for get to be someone, not a problem." → after: "The people you stop for get to be someone, not a problem."
- *Built to Hold* (magician-ruler) · before: "A whole room gets to be carefree for a night, and that's your doing. Hardly anyone can give that." → after: "A whole room gets to be carefree for a night, and that's your doing. It's worth every minute you spent on duty."
- (third) *Note to Self* (sage-ruler) · before: "They count from your numbers, and that's rarer than you think." → after: "They count from your numbers, and they're right to."

**V3. "I like to think" stays the signature, but it doesn't always open the reveal.** In no more than about 1 pour in 4, the phrase is the first words of yours 3. Elsewhere, start from the fact or the image and let the signpost arrive mid-sentence. Keep every signposted claim inside the signpost.
- *Anyone Would Have* (hero-regular-guy) · before: "I like to think he never quite forgot that guest at the bar, waiting for a drink nobody there could make." → after: "That guest at the bar, waiting for a drink nobody there could make: I like to think he never quite forgot them."
- *Note to Self* (sage-ruler) · before: "I like to think two things kept it on the shelf:" → after: "Two things kept it on the shelf, I like to think:"

**V4. A proposal starts on the instruction, not on an announcement of one.** Cut "So here's my request / position / side." preambles. No more than about 1 proposal in 3 opens on "So". Ryu still takes a position: the instruction *is* the position.
- *No Promises* (hero-outlaw) · before: "So here's my one request. Next time someone you'd go anywhere for has a big day coming, skip the grin and the "probably"." → after: "Next time someone you'd go anywhere for has a big day coming, skip the grin and the "probably"."
- *Still Yours* (creator-outlaw) · before: "So here's my position. The next time something of yours turns up everywhere, and the eye-rolling starts, don't quietly let it go." → after: "The next time something of yours turns up everywhere, and the eye-rolling starts, don't quietly let it go."

**V5. End on the guest's choice or gain more often than on a wish.** "I hope…" ends at most 1 reading in 8 (from 19). Where the sentence before it is already the gain, stop there. "It was never X. It was you." is used once in the collection; keep *In One Piece*'s, where it answers the fear of strength not being enough.
- *Just This Once* (regular-guy-outlaw) · before: "They like you. I hope they keep saying yes." → after: "They like you."
- *Only Half Joking* (lover-jester) · before: "Plenty of people you like have no idea how much, and the ones you tell will play back hardest of all. I hope they tease you for it." → after: "Plenty of people you like have no idea how much, and the ones you tell will play back hardest of all."

**V6. The closing line stays with the glass, or turns outward in an image of its own. It's never a second copy of the proposal.** *Further Than Me*'s "Put the two sprigs in together. Then find out what one of them knows now that you don't." is the model: one gesture, then one new step. At most about 1 closing line in 12 uses "Next time".
- *Dressed for It* (hero-ruler) · before: "Fit the peel inside the glass, then take your hand away: it stays. Next time a critic has a point, grant it first." → after: "Fit the peel inside the glass, then take your hand away: it stays."
- *First Choice* (regular-guy-lover) · before: "Give the peach its few days in the brandy. And the next time you want to be chosen, let it show." → after: "Give the peach its few days in the brandy. Some things are worth being waited for."

Every V-rule edit gets a lint run and a quick swap read against its reversed pair. None of them changes a fact, so none needs Hester, except where a V3 reorder moves a clause out of the signpost (then it does).
