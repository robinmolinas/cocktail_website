# Family review: Lover (2026-10-06)

Reviewer notes in one paragraph: the family's stories are its strength: Willard's numbered doors, Murphy's handwritten card, Nellie Bly's "was my hair tossed", Rita Taketsuru, the extra "e" in Jasmine, Siegal's thirty copies, McMillian reciting the julep ode. They're tender without being sentimental, and nearly every reading ties its twist to the story in one clause ("as my tease played back to him", "Thirty again: a coincidence, nothing more"). The drinks are distinct from each other: a cold toddy that waits, a juice-led 7% sour, a room-temperature claret-and-rye, a lavender-scented Flip, a Mint Julep made in the ode's order. No P1 in the family: every allergen line matches the tool. What recurs: (1) **taglines in the public/private shape**: *Worth Finding*, *By Design*, *Any Day*, and *By Design* sits almost word for word beside its reversed pair *Up Close* ("Everyone knows the effect you have…" / "You know exactly what your attention does…"). (2) **Two open wording questions on spoon measures**: *Hoping You'd Come*'s "spoonful and a half" (the known one) and the same pattern in *The Wink* and *The Big Words*. The family needs one rule: say "teaspoon" when the recipe says tsp. (3) **History weight**: *Out of Your Way* is the one reading clearly over half. (4) "Here's what your way gives…" and "Here's the part they miss" signpost the kindness again, as in the other families. And a note for the editor: "keeps in the fridge" appears in six Lover methods. Robin's "last, not keep" edit was made in a reading paragraph, so whether it applies to recipe notes is a collection-level call, not something to change pour by pour.

## Revision proposals (ranked, most important first)

### lover-caregiver · P2 · ground: Tomás · routine
- **Where:** recipe row "sugar", note; `yours 4`
- **Current (verbatim):** note "Willard used as much sugar as brandy; I cut it to a spoonful and a half, or it would be more than four times too sweet"; y4 "so I kept it to a spoonful and a half."
- **Proposed:** note "Willard used as much sugar as brandy; I cut it to a teaspoon and a half, or it would be more than four times too sweet"; y4 "so I kept it to a teaspoon and a half."
- **Why it improves the pairing:** the amount column says 1½ tsp, and Tomás's own note (Open items) says "a spoonful" can be read as tablespoons, which would push the drink over the sugar ceiling. It's a guest-safety-of-taste fix that changes no meaning. My answer to the open question: teaspoon.
- **Checks before applying:** none (6 g is unchanged).

### lover-hero · P2 · ground: Hester · Robin
- **Where:** `yours 2`; `yours 4` (last sentence)
- **Current (verbatim):** y2 "It nearly came to nothing. It all hung on a special mail train that night, and when they pulled into Amiens they were locked in their compartment and thought they'd be carried past." y4 "I made it warm for the fire Verne's wife lit with her own hands when they arrived, and for the two of them standing bareheaded in the cold courtyard to wave her off."
- **Proposed:** y2 "It nearly came to nothing: at Amiens they were locked in their compartment and thought they'd be carried past." y4 "I made it warm for the fire Verne's wife lit with her own hands when they arrived."
- **Why it improves the pairing:** the story runs well past half. The y4 clause repeats y3's courtyard scene, which already lands ("Nobody there was worried about anyone's hair"), and the mail-train mechanics add suspense without adding to the person. These cuts take about 30 words of story out and bring the reading close to half. If Robin wants it fully under, the next candidate is y1's "She asked only whether she'd miss any connections."
- **Checks before applying:** none factual (cuts only); keep the anchors in the dossier.

## Reversed pairs and siblings

- **lover-caregiver / caregiver-lover** (*Hoping You'd Come* / *Standing By*): distinct. The host who hopes without asking vs the partner who understands their way round a hurt.
- **lover-creator / creator-lover** (*In Your Own Hand* / *Rosetta*): distinct. The muse hiding their own work vs the one who falls for one thing and explains it in details.
- **lover-explorer / explorer-lover** (*Curtain Call* / *Straight Back*): distinct. Dreading the night's end vs a taste that takes you back.
- **lover-hero / hero-lover** (*Out of Your Way* / *One Line*): distinct. Going the whole way to be there vs keeping cool for someone. Both are "for someone", but the behaviour differs.
- **lover-innocent / innocent-lover** (*Wide Open* / *The Big Words*): close. Both love out loud. *The Big Words* is public, loud, and fears ridicule; *Wide Open* is private, immediate (reply now), and fears turning careful after hurt. Distinct enough, and both rooms fenced each other. The tagline proposal above keeps them apart.
- **lover-jester / jester-lover** (*Only Half Joking* / *I'll Tell You Later*): close. Both are teasers. Compliments sent sideways vs good news held back for timing. Distinct in behaviour and glass.
- **lover-magician / magician-lover** (*Worth Finding* / *Undimmed*): see `reviews/magician.md`. Distinct: holding one thing back vs changing people without trying.
- **lover-outlaw / outlaw-lover** (*Accomplices* / *Rent-Free*): distinct. Pulling someone out of a polite room vs love as a getaway.
- **lover-regular-guy / regular-guy-lover** (*Any Day* / *First Choice*): distinct. Shows love by doing and can't say why vs always the easy yes, wanting to be plan A.
- **lover-ruler / ruler-lover** (*By Design* / *Up Close*): close. The readings differ (Pickford's authored image vs a barman's remembered details), but the taglines are near-interchangeable, and both are about knowing your effect on people. The tagline change above is the fix; the readings can stay.
- **lover-sage / sage-lover** (*In Kind* / *A Brother's Care*, approved): distinct.
- **Inside the family:** (1) "Here's what your way gives the people you love" (*Any Day*), "Here's the part they miss" (*Worth Finding*), "Here's the thing you'd never tell the table" (*In Kind*), "Here's what people take home from your evenings" (*Hoping You'd Come*): four "Here's…" signposts in one family. Vary when next edited; no proposal on its own. (2) Spoon wording: settle "teaspoon" for tsp across the collection (*Hoping You'd Come* here; *The Wink*, *The Big Words* in other reviews). (3) Seven of the eleven proposals end on a warm wish, five of them "I hope…" (*Out of Your Way*, *Wide Open*, *Only Half Joking*, *By Design*, *In Kind*), plus "And I wish you a full house" and "And may the next one be even better". That's Robin's "end on a warm wish" working, but in one family it's becoming the ending. When an editor is next in two of the "I hope" pours, end one on the guest's choice instead.
