# Family review: Regular Guy (2026-10-06)

Reviewer notes in one paragraph: the warmest family in the collection, and the one where whoYouAre does its job best. The opening scenes are observed, not described (the cash machine, the bread at the wedding, half past eight on a Friday, ten past ten at the kitchen counter, the back off the lamp), and almost every pour says the kindness out loud, as Robin asked. The drinks are honest and homely (a marmalade jar as shaker, a caipirinha by the law's three things, a Rickey measured not counted), and the three structural outliers are justified against Arnold's own precedents: the shakerato, the frozen-grapefruit Malört and the stirred Cohasset Punch. What recurs: the proposals nearly all resolve the same way (stop hiding behind your usefulness and join the room: *Loose on Top*, *Good for Years*, *Word Gets Round*, *Right Here*, *First Choice*). That's true to the archetype's belonging motive, but the editor shouldn't push any further in that direction. The whoYouAre opening scenes are so concrete that the taglines keep borrowing from them. Four current lines repeat a whoYouAre or proposal phrase almost word for word (*Whoever Comes In*, *Right Here*, *Good for Years*, *Just This Once*), and several of the 2 October recommendations do the same. One unsourced claim (*Word Gets Round*'s "official cocktail of Washington, DC") is the family's only P1.

## Revision proposals (ranked, most important first)

### regular-guy-ruler · P1 · ground: Hester · routine
- **Where:** `yours 4`, first sentence
- **Current (verbatim):** "The cocktail I've made for you is a Rickey, a classic that spread across America in the 1890s and is now the official cocktail of Washington, DC: bourbon, fresh lime and soda water."
- **Proposed:** "The cocktail I've made for you is a Rickey, a renowned classic that was already famous in the 1890s: bourbon, fresh lime and soda water."
- **Why it improves the pairing:** neither "spread across America" nor "the official cocktail of Washington, DC" is in this pour's anchors, fact audit or the Heugel card. Hester's Rickey card (`george-williamson-joe-rickey.md`) lists both phrases under **Fenced (never in guest text)**, naming *Word Gets Round*'s y4. The replacement rests on card F15 ("By the mid-1890s the gin version was the best known", Oxford pdf 1664). If Hester can source the 2011 DC designation from Oxford RICKEY (pdf 1661–1664) and add an anchor row, the original sentence can stay instead. That's Hester's call.
- **Checks before applying:** Hester to verify "already famous in the 1890s" against F15's wording; anchors row; lint.

### regular-guy-caregiver · P2 · ground: Wren · Robin
- **Where:** `whoYouAre 1`, last sentence
- **Current (verbatim):** "For a minute, someone who'd turned into part of the street is just a person in the queue again."
- **Proposed:** "For a minute, someone everyone had stopped seeing is just a person in the queue again."
- **Why it improves the pairing:** the studio's worry is that the cash-machine figure reads as someone sleeping rough. I'd keep the figure. The suspended coffee exists for exactly the person who can't afford one, and the reading's whole point is seeing them as "someone, not a problem". What makes it uncomfortable is "turned into part of the street", which describes the person as scenery in the reading's own voice. The new wording puts the blindness on everyone else, where ¶2 already puts it ("a street where everyone has stopped looking"), and keeps the person a person.
- **Checks before applying:** lint; Wren's resonance re-read of ¶1.

## Reversed pairs and siblings

- regular-guy-caregiver / caregiver-regular-guy (*Everybody's*): close. Both are coffee with a spirit (a cold brandy shakerato vs a hot Irish-coffee flip), and both put impartiality at the centre ("for whoever comes in" vs "everyone gets the same you"). They separate on the fear: the ones you walked past, vs the closest getting no more than anyone. The y3 tweak above widens the gap.
- regular-guy-creator / creator-regular-guy (*As It Was*): distinct. Hidden talent shown at home vs keeping the ordinary Tuesday.
- regular-guy-explorer / explorer-regular-guy (*Brought Home*): distinct. The Entrepreneur's freedom becomes others' belonging; the Tourist brings the world home.
- regular-guy-hero / hero-regular-guy (*Anyone Would Have*): distinct, and a good mirror (leaping before knowing how vs rehearsing everything in advance).
- regular-guy-innocent / innocent-regular-guy (*Four Shares*, approved): distinct. Plain pleasure said out loud vs the shared bowl.
- regular-guy-jester / jester-regular-guy (*Is It Just Me*): distinct. Pranks as admission vs confession as comedy.
- regular-guy-lover / lover-regular-guy (*Any Day*): distinct. Wanting to be chosen vs never needing to be asked.
- regular-guy-magician / magician-regular-guy (*Cold Water First*): close in the proposal only (both say step out from behind the craft and into the room, and both open "So when you're…"). The rest is distinct: mending vs performing. Opening tweak above.
- regular-guy-outlaw / outlaw-regular-guy (*Where You Stand*): distinct. Being let off vs the fixer's private line.
- regular-guy-ruler / ruler-regular-guy (*One Table*): distinct. Sharing what you know with your own side vs standing between two sides.
- regular-guy-sage / sage-regular-guy (*Whatever They Call It*): distinct. Handed-down sayings with names attached vs letting everything go but what matters.
- **Siblings:** the proposals share one resolution (join in rather than serve from the edge) in *Loose on Top*, *Good for Years*, *Word Gets Round*, *Right Here* and *First Choice*. Each is tied to its own scene, so none is interchangeable, but together they make a recognisable family formula. Don't add another. Three warm wishes to close ("I hope plenty of them say yes", "I hope they keep saying yes", "I hope you enjoy every bit of it") plus "I hope you let yourself enjoy being chosen" and "I hope one of them gets you back": five "I hope…" endings in eleven. That's Robin's taste, but at this density it starts to read as a template. If the editor touches any proposal, vary one. *Tried and True* and *Whoopee* (outlaw family) share the sloe-gin card and base. That's fine: a sour vs a stirred Blackthorn.
