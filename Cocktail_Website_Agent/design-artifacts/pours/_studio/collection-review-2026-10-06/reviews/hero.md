# Family review: Hero (2026-10-06)

Reviewer notes in one paragraph: the Hero family is the most even in quality, and has the fewest real problems of my four. The stories are fenced with real care (Barney Gallant's "Yes, his name really was Gallant", Trader Vic's shark granted before it's corrected, Fleming's "For Ivar, who mixed the first Vesper"). Every twist is tied to the story in words ("as a nod to the rule she bent", "as a nod to the cook he was"). Every allergen line matches the live tool, and all three out-of-range numbers are honest: *One Line* is a dry Martini (0.61 g sugar, by design), and *Hold the Shark* and *Dressed for It* are measured at their acid edges on purpose. I'd recommend Robin accepts *One Line*'s flag as justified. There's no P1. What recurs: (1) three taglines restate their own whoYouAre (*Work It Out*, *Dressed for It*, and *Anyone Would Have*, which is also a 2 October row). (2) **Five whoYouAre openings are a domestic mishap scene** (the oven before twelve guests, the lights out at a party, the icy bend, the flat tyre on the motorway, the friend's figures). *Anyone Would Have* and *Leave It With Me* both set theirs at a party, though the moves are opposite (prepared vs improvising). (3) Two coincidental names in one family (Gallant, Dionysos), and Trader Vic in two readings. Each is owned, but together they're noticeable. (4) Small home-kit gaps (keeping times; a "contains nuts" on a pine liqueur that the guest can't see the reason for).
