# Tagline calibration batch — 8 pours (Wren, 2026-10-06)

Before any tagline changes go collection-wide, this is a small test of register. No pour has been edited.

**What I aimed for.** Your one "yes" on 2026-10-02 was *"You don't mind losing a weekend if nobody loses ten minutes again."* It names a trade-off the person keeps making. It's true of their whole life, not of one story. It's in plain grammar, and it recognises a cost they choose. So **option A** is always in that register. **Option B** tries a deliberately different cadence: one long sentence, a question, or a very short line. Neither leans on a domestic prop (the reviewers' tic: first-aid kits, biscuits, keys, tea), and neither uses British-only words.

**How I checked.** Each option was checked against the pour's epigraph, the key sentence of its whoYouAre, its proposal (yours 5) and its closing line. I used the lint's own `content()` words and 3-grams. I also read each one by ear for echoed ideas, which the script can't catch. Then I checked each against all 132 current taglines in `signals.md`, and against the lint's 14-word limit.

**Shape of my eight picks:** 3 open with "You", none is two sentences, and 1 is a question. Lengths run from 6 to 13 words. The openings are You'd, You'll, Every, Being, Waiting, You'd, Why and Precision.

## 1. explorer-lover · *Straight Back* · The Sense Seeker (your mark: retry)

- **Current:** One taste, and you're somewhere else entirely. You always let it take you.
- **A (trade-off):** You'd trade a famous view for one perfect mouthful.
- **B (one long sentence):** Ask you about any city, and you'll start with what you ate.
- **Best reviewer candidate** (family review, `reviews/explorer.md`): "Every market you walk through costs you an hour and a bag of things to try." It's a real habitual cost and close to your yes. But it's 16 words, over the lint's 14, and "a bag of things" is the prop list I'm trying to avoid. Not better.
- **My pick: A.** It's the Explorer's freedom and the Lover's senses in one choice, and it's true of the person without the bread or the Bramble. The 10-02 line you marked retry ("You forget the year and remember the bread") borrowed the reading's own image.
- **Echo check:** A shares no word with the epigraph, the key line ("You can remember exactly what the bread tasted like"), y5 or the closing line. My first B, "…what it tasted like", echoed the epigraph's "tasted like an island" and would have failed the lint, so I rewrote it to "what you ate". Clean.

## 2. explorer-magician · *Just Knew* · The Spiritualist (your mark: retry)

- **Current:** You've been doing it for years. You just never called it that.
- **A (trade-off):** You'll travel a long way for one sentence you can live by.
- **B (one long sentence):** Happiness is something you'd rather go out and find than wait for.
- **Best reviewer candidate** (`reviews/explorer.md`): "You keep a notebook of lines worth living by, and lose it every spring." It's warm and funny, but it's a prop, and "lose it every spring" asks the guest to laugh at themselves before they've read a word. Their other candidate, the candle, brushes the reading's no-faith fence. Not better.
- **My pick: A.** It names what this person spends (distance, time, retreats) and what they come back for. That's the persona, not Gary Regan's hour, and it isn't abstract, which is why I think the 10-02 line ("The things that keep you steady rarely have names") came back retry.
- **Echo check:** whoYouAre ¶1 has "you return with one thing, a line or a habit". That's the same idea in different words. I'd call it a set-up, not an echo, and I'd like your ear on it. No shared words with the epigraph ("build it in any order"), the key line ("You'd never call those a practice…"), y5 or the closing line. B shares only "you'd rather" with whoYouAre.

## 3. explorer-regular-guy · *Brought Home* · The Tourist (your mark: "too specific to the story, not the persona")

- **Current:** Half the people you know have eaten somewhere because of you.
- **A (trade-off):** You'd rather a trip you can tell than one you can't come back from.
- **B (one long sentence):** Every trip you take is partly for the friends who couldn't come.
- **Best reviewer candidate** (`reviews/explorer.md`): "You're the friend people text before they book anything." It's clean and not a prop, but it's a social role ("the friend people text"), the shape you cut in the Connoisseur ("the unofficial sommelier"). It also echoes whoYouAre's "people who brag about never booking anything". Not better.
- **My pick: B.** The Tourist's truth is that adventure is better shared, and B says it as a gift, with no meal and no mezcal. A is closer to your yes, but it leans on the fear ("can't come back from"), and the tagline should intrigue, not frighten.
- **Echo check:** A **fails**: it shares "trip", "tell", "come back" with whoYouAre's "the one trip you'd never book is the one with no way back". B shares only "trip" and "come", and it doesn't echo the key line ("the best night of a trip is sometimes the one after it…"). whoYouAre's "everyone who stayed gets to go a little" is the nearest idea, and B's wording stays clear of it. Nothing shared with the epigraph, y5 ("one street further") or the closing line.

## 4. caregiver-lover · *Standing By* · The Understanding Spouse (current line is whoYouAre's own sentence)

- **Current:** You've seen them at their worst. You're still here.
- **A (trade-off):** Being right matters less to you than knowing why they said it.
- **B (very short):** Their bad days are safe with you.
- **Best reviewer candidate** (`reviews/caregiver.md`): "You can tell what kind of day they've had from the way the keys go down." It's well observed, but it's whoYouAre ¶1 again ("you can hear it in the first word"), so it repeats the echo it's meant to fix, with a prop added. Their other candidate (tea on a good day and a bad one) is the British prop. Not better.
- **My pick: A.** It's the trade this person makes every time (understanding over winning), and it's the habit the reading later turns into a gift and a risk. That's the Spouse, not Twain or Livy.
- **Echo check:** A shares only "less" with whoYouAre. It doesn't echo the key line ("It never changed your mind about them. It completed the picture."), the epigraph (Twain's four things), y5 ("say it back") or the closing line. B shares "bad" with ¶1's "a bad day", and "bad days" is close to the current line's "at their worst" idea, which is why it isn't my pick.

## 5. caregiver-hero · *Off Duty* · The Rescuer (mass contrast, and it echoes whoYouAre)

- **Current:** Everyone else is still deciding. You've already gone.
- **A (trade-off):** Waiting it out has always cost you more than going in.
- **B (very short):** First out the door. Last to sit down.
- **Best reviewer candidate** (`reviews/caregiver.md`): "You keep a first-aid kit in the car, and you've used it on strangers." / "Your phone is never on silent, just in case." Both are props, the exact tic in question, and the first assumes a car. Not better.
- **My pick: A.** Standing back is the Rescuer's real cost and the reading's fear ("the worst kind of selfishness"). A says it as a choice they keep making, with no stairs or two in the morning.
- **Echo check:** A shares only "always" with whoYouAre. "Standing back" in ¶2 is the same idea in other words, which I'd keep as a set-up. It doesn't echo the key line ("You don't feel brave. You feel clear."), the epigraph ("Nobody makes this one for themselves…") or y5. I first wrote "You'd rather be in the middle of it than safe…", but *Making the Calls* already owns "You'd rather be … than … of it", so I rewrote it. **B fails the proposal check:** "Last to sit down" sets up y5 ("sit down while they do") and the closing line ("Spend them sitting down"). It's a tease, but it's an echo.

## 6. lover-ruler · *By Design* · The Sex Symbol (near-duplicate of ruler-lover *Up Close*)

- **Current:** Everyone knows the effect you have. Almost nobody knows you designed it.
- **A (trade-off):** You'd give up half the applause to be really known by one person.
- **B (very short):** Every head that turns is your own work.
- **Best reviewer candidate** (`reviews/lover.md`): "You've practised the things people think you were born with." It's good, but it restates whoYouAre ¶1 ("They think you were simply born like that"), and "practised / looks effortless" is already owned by *Not Too Polite*, *Nothing to It* and *Half a Rim*. Not better.
- **My pick: A.** The duplicate with *Up Close* comes from both lines being about knowing your effect. A moves to this persona's secret (admired by many, known by few), which *Up Close* never touches. B keeps the duplicate, so it shows the problem rather than fixing it.
- **Echo check:** A shares "give" and "you'd" with whoYouAre. It doesn't echo the key line ("People fall for what you made. Very few ask to meet the one who made it."), though it carries the same need forward. That's the closest call in this batch, and I'd like your ear. It shares nothing with the epigraph ("the one behind the curls"), y5 ("Choose the next version…") or the closing line. Nothing like it among the 132.

## 7. jester-explorer · *Unrehearsed* · The Alternative Comic (reviewer says take the 10-02 recommendation)

- **Current:** Anyone can get the laugh. You're after the second before it.
- **A (trade-off):** You'd rather puzzle a room than please it.
- **B (question):** Why tell a joke they already know the end of?
- **Best reviewer candidate** (`reviews/jester.md`, taking the 10-02 recommendation): "The silence before the laugh is your favourite part." It removes the mass contrast but keeps the echo. whoYouAre's key moment is "a second where nobody knows whether they're allowed to laugh", and y3 says "You know that second better than anyone". The 10-02 line and the current line both spend the reading's best beat. Not better.
- **My pick: B.** It's the persona's boredom with the expected, asked the way this person would ask it. It's also the only question in the batch, so it tests whether a question can be a tagline for you. A is the safer, Robin's-yes register if you'd rather not have questions.
- **Echo check:** B shares only "know" with whoYouAre. It stays clear of "on cue" and "could see coming from the door", which I rewrote around. Nothing shared with the epigraph (ice), y5 ("don't disappear straight after") or the closing line. A shares only "you'd". *Off-Label* owns "never … the same thing twice", so I didn't use the persona sheet's "can't tell the same joke twice".

## 8. sage-ruler · *Note to Self* · The Scientist (abstract symmetry)

- **Current:** Impressive is easy. Exact is what you're after.
- **A (trade-off):** You'll measure all evening so the argument can end in a minute.
- **B (very short):** Precision is your idea of fun.
- **Best reviewer candidate** (`reviews/sage.md`): "You'd rather write it down than remember it wrong." It's the strongest reviewer line in the batch, in your yes register. But "write it down" is the reading's key sentence ("Writing it down is how you keep a messy world still enough to think in") and appears twice in whoYouAre. Close, but not better.
- **My pick: B.** It names the thing this persona rarely admits, that they enjoy it, which is what whoYouAre turns on and y5 asks them to put in the notes. And it's six words, a different length from everything else here.
- **Echo check:** B shares no word with the epigraph, whoYouAre, y5 or the closing line. Its idea is whoYouAre's "it's one of the things you enjoy most", carried forward, not repeated. A shares "argument" with whoYouAre's scene ("When an argument starts… you fetch the notes"), and it borrows your yes's time trade (an evening for a minute) a little too closely.

## What I'm testing

1. **A or B as a register?** Five picks are A (a trade-off, like your weekend line), and three are B (a different cadence). If you mark the A's yes and the B's no, I'll write the collection in A, varied by length and opening rather than by cadence.
2. **Can a tagline be a question?** (*Unrehearsed*, B.) If not, I'll keep questions inside the readings.
3. **Is carrying an idea forward from whoYouAre allowed, as long as the words are new?** (*Just Knew* A, *By Design* A, *Note to Self* B.) Or does the tagline have to come from somewhere the reading never goes?
4. **Is the reviewers' concrete-prop style a tic to you, or a strength?** If you like "the keys go down" or the first-aid kit, I'll stop steering away from props and just ration them.
