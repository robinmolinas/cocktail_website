## What the rooms found hard

- **Classic specs rarely balance as printed.** Every room that started from a named classic found it OUT on something:
  - equal-parts Hanky Panky, Ensslin's 1916 Aviation, Gonzalez's orgeat, Bermejo's acid, Engel's Knickebein
  - the equal-parts Last Word (OUT on sugar and acid, and it contains nuts)

  The drinks moved off the page, and the readings had to stop calling them "his recipe".
- **Hester's own shorthand kept reaching the reading, and she audited it out each time.** Examples:
  - "a little bit" of syrup (a quote that doesn't exist)
  - "new bottle of the decade" (absinthe was on record in 1843)
  - "printed beside" (the Improved went in a supplement at the back)
  - "In 2005 … on one condition"
- **Sibling echoes.** With ten pours in one family, the swap test and the lint's motif check did real work. Phrases moved off *Overnight*, *The Other Berry*, *A Brother's Care*, *Down the Line*, *Still Yours* and *As It Was*. Two story shapes were refused outright because a sibling owned them: the lineage chain (*Down the Line*) and the rescued-from-a-book revival (*As It Was*).
- **Crossing turns.** Parallel rounds meant agents often answered a version that had already moved on. In creator-sage the story swapped twice (Stenson ↔ Thomas) before it settled. Host turns named each crossing without taking a side, and every room still closed inside 8 rounds.
- **The spark rule against the person.** Twice (*Half a Rim*, *Beside the First*) the room consulted the Matrix and judged the surprise a costume on the person. Both are flagged, not hidden.
