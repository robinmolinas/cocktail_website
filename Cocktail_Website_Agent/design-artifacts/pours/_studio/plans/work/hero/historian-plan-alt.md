## hero-caregiver · The Saviour
- **Lead: Barney Gallant takes the plea (1919).** *Joy* pdf 59 (Regan, relaying Stanley Walker). Under wartime Prohibition his Greenwich Village Inn was raided; arrested with half a dozen waiters, he agreed to plead guilty if the waiters were set free; sentenced to thirty days, "he served far less"; by Walker's account the first person in Manhattan jailed under Prohibition laws. Passes: the cost is his own, taken in their place, for specific others. Guards: never "served thirty days"; "first jailed" is Walker's, scoped to Manhattan and wartime Prohibition; no refusing help on the page; crime never the point; his club's gendered house rules out.
- **Backup: none free that passes.** Tried: Stenson (Innocent's), Shackleton's biscuit (Explorer's; best fit: ask for it if Explorer drops Shackleton), Cipriani's loan (Lover's), Ann Rogers (no specific person spared). Open.

## hero-creator · The Idealist
- **Lead: Thad Vogler.** *Proper* pp. 176–179. Brand work only for liquors he approves of, done for free; dropped spirits whose integrity he doubted; "everything had a sense of place and time and a link to agriculture"; fired from Presidio Social Club, by his account, for an interview on the harm big drinks companies did; "the Thad Vogler school" (Cate, p. 176); Bar Agricole "a bit alienating". Passes: a made thing carrying a belief, and he didn't go quiet, at a cost. Guards: "fired because" is his account; no companies named; not a taste story; farm-to-glass is Explorer's Beattie ground.
- **Backup: the Browns' sealed bottle (Old Forester, 1870).** Oxford OLD FORESTER pdf 1430, BROWN-FORMAN pdf 365. First American whisky sold only in a bottle sealed by its maker, when sellers cut whisky; they refused to sell it any other way. Guards: no medicine; Oxford's scope only; weaker on "refused to be reduced".

## hero-regular-guy · The Firefighter
- **Lead: TGI Fridays' blindfold test.** *Proper* pp. 17–20. Busser, waiter, nine months barback; 400 drinks, 25 blindfolded against time, "muscle memory"; 15 jokes; by 2000 their name "was mud", but "the veterans and insiders knew their training systems were solid", and London's leading craft bartender hired their alumni as "clean and efficient". Guards: credit withheld, not given away; Bradsell unnamed; the gendered founding story out; no military, no fire words.
- **Backup: the Club de Cantineros (1924).** Oxford BARTENDER CULTURE pdf 227, MIXOGRAPHY pdf 1323, SLOPPY JOE'S pdf 1815. Training, English lessons, manuals; then the Prohibition tourists, eleven bartenders on a shift. Guards: credit not on page; Constante, Mary Pickford, Boadas out.

## hero-explorer · The Quest Seeker
- **Lead: Greg Boehm and the old books.** *Proper* pp. 60–61. About six hundred antique titles through dealers ("some were already pretty high"); "got bored and packed them away"; two years later "museum pieces were now working manuals"; reprinted from 2007, six volumes first, unsure of the market; tracked down Embury's surviving daughter for the copyright; so anyone could own what others "clawed and dug for". Guards: risk on the page, fear isn't; Cocktail Kingdom and Don Lee out; Jerry Thomas's book at most one of six.
- **Backup: Eric Seed's pallet of Zirbenz.** *Proper* pp. 240–241. One pallet of sixty cases, no distributor, it struggled; then he asked bartenders what they needed. Guards: violette and Jamaican rum out; never "mild-mannered".
