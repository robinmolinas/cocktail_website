# Hero family: Wren's sketches (2026-09-30)

All eleven are unauthored. The sheet was rewritten today, so every read below comes from the new names and stories in my brief, not from memory. The existing ground I read against: **Off Duty** (caregiver-hero, the Rescuer), **Down the Line** (creator-hero, the Visionary, approved), **Not Too Polite** (the Campaigner), **Worth the Trip** (the Reassurer), **Not Only the Way** (the Craftsman), **Instead** (the Doctor), **Still Yours** (the Trend Setter), **Beside the First** (the Old Master), **Fair Measure** (the Nanny), **No Accident** (the Trickster), and **Four Shares** (the True Friend).

## Family-wide first

- **All eleven fear weakness.** So "you fear looking weak" is Barnum inside this family. Each reading has to name its own kind of weakness:

| Pairing | The weakness they fear |
| --- | --- |
| Saviour | needing to be helped |
| Idealist | going quiet when it counts |
| Firefighter | not being good enough on the day |
| Quest Seeker | a life with nothing left to go after (they're afraid all the time, and it isn't the fear they fear) |
| Initiate | not being ready when it counts |
| Storyteller | the plain version being the true one |
| Dashing Hero | feeling too much, and losing someone they couldn't protect |
| Superhero | being nothing special without the ability |
| Lovable Rogue | being thought soft |
| Warrior | faltering while others are watching and depending on them |
| Problem Solver | having no answer while everyone watches |

- **The flattery trap is this family's biggest risk.** Hero readings slide into praise ("you're brave, you step up"). Praise isn't recognition. Every whoYouAre has to name the fear plainly, and then say the kindness out loud (Robin's caregiver rule).
- **Words to ration across the family:** brave, courage, hero, save, strong, fight. The reading uses at most one of them, and never the word the persona's name is built on.
- **The sheet's examples are not the mirror stories.** Jesus, Dylan, 9/11, Indiana Jones, Luke Skywalker, Big Fish, Bond, Superman, Jack Sparrow, Hector and the Genius Bar are fiction, brands, faith or real tragedy. The mirror comes from drinks history. An example may be used as the reading's single outside touchstone, and only if it lands faster. **Never Jesus and never 9/11** in the guest text: faith and real mass death aren't ours to borrow.
- **Already taken by close pours (out for the whole family):**
  - "you're already moving", "By the time anyone else has decided…", "Nobody sees the part that comes later", "the one who comes" (Off Duty)
  - hands shaking afterwards, now used twice (Off Duty, Not Too Polite)
  - "You don't feel brave. You feel clear." (Off Duty)
  - doubting alone "because other people are building on your belief", and passing the shaker (Down the Line)
  - "it was nothing" and "stop saying it was nothing" (Not Only the Way)
  - asking "since when?" (Instead)
  - "being ignored" and "people rally behind a great story" (No Accident)
  - "You joke because it is [serious]" (Worth the Trip)
  - grog, navy rum and Admiral Vernon (Fair Measure)
  - "keep one cup back" and "You're allowed to be looked after too" (Four Shares)
- **Proposals, family-wide:** under-receiving is shared by every Hero and every Caregiver. "Let someone else go / carry it / look after you" is already said twice. Each Hero proposal below takes a different angle. Keep them apart.
- **Stories that fail every Hero test:** anything that glamorises violence, war, danger or crime, and any "died for it" ending told as glory.

## hero-caregiver · The Saviour
- **One true thing:** they take the cost *in someone's place*: the blame, the bill, the bad news to deliver, the harder half. And being helped back feels like a debt they can't stand to owe. Their books are always kept so that they're in credit.
- **Hides:** how humiliating it feels to need anyone. **Fears:** being the one who has to be carried. **Gives:** someone else gets spared, often without knowing it was them.
- **Close siblings and existing pours:**
  - vs **the Rescuer / Off Duty** (the closest risk in the whole family): the Rescuer goes *toward* danger in the crisis and lives on the rush. The Saviour takes a cost *instead of* someone, often slowly, in ordinary life, with no rush at all.
  - vs the Warrior: the Warrior holds a line in front of people. The Saviour gives something up.
  - vs Four Shares: not about being looked after. It's about owing.
- **Traps:** religious framing (no cross, no martyr, no "sacrifice" as holiness); death-as-glory; health or medicine.
- **Story test:**
  - Must show a person who gave up something real of their own (money, credit, comfort, a place, a name) so that a *specific* other person was spared.
  - It must cost them, visibly.
  - Best if the source also shows them refusing help, or being unable to repay a kindness.
  - Out: crisis rescue in danger (the Rescuer's), snow or mountain rescue, hot drinks served to a helper, anyone dying for it, anything medical.
- **Position direction:** accept one favour and leave it unpaid. Stay in someone's debt.

## hero-creator · The Idealist
- **One true thing:** they'd rather *make* the point than argue it. They're braver with a song, a page or a picture than in a row. And they refuse to become the slogan of the thing they made.
- **Hides:** the worry that making something instead of fighting is a way of hiding. **Fears:** going quiet when it matters. **Gives:** a made thing lets a whole crowd say it together, and reaches people an argument never would.
- **Close siblings and existing pours:**
  - vs **the Campaigner / Not Too Polite**: the Campaigner speaks up first in the room. The Idealist makes something that speaks.
  - vs **the Trend Setter / Still Yours**: both move on once something spreads. The Trend Setter moves on out of taste. The Idealist moves on to keep the belief from being owned.
  - vs **the Old Master / Beside the First**: the Old Master questions the work. The Idealist keeps the belief and lets the form change.
  - vs the Visionary: no monument, no chain handed on.
- **Traps:** "artist", "activist", "voice of a generation"; party politics; Dylan puns.
- **Story test:**
  - Must show a person whose *made thing* (a drink, a book, a label, a song, a menu) carried a belief well beyond them.
  - It must show them refusing to be reduced to it, at the cost of disappointing people who wanted it kept the same.
  - The belief has to be one a guest of any politics can respect: fairness or welcome, not a party line.
  - Out: aggression, protest turned violent, plain quality stories.
- **Position direction:** it isn't hiding. When it matters, sign your name to it.

## hero-regular-guy · The Firefighter
- **One true thing:** "anyone would have done the same" isn't modesty. They trained for years so that it *would* feel like that. The courage is practised, done as a crew, and never shown.
- **Hides:** the hours of drill nobody saw. **Fears:** being the weak link on the one day it counts. **Gives:** the people around them are safe *because* of dull, repeated work.
- **Close siblings and existing pours:**
  - vs **the Rescuer / Off Duty**: the Rescuer runs on instinct and goes first alone. The Firefighter runs on drill, and always as part of a crew.
  - vs **the Craftsman / Not Only the Way**: the Craftsman hides pride in a method. The Firefighter hides the fear of not being enough, and hands the credit to the crew. So the position is never "take the credit".
  - vs the Warrior: a crew of equals, not a leader in front.
  - vs Down the Line: the shakermen's relay is taken.
- **Traps:** real fires, 9/11 or any death toll; uniforms and job labels ("first responder"); the word "hero".
- **Story test:**
  - Must show a *team* that practised something unglamorous, over and over, for a moment that might never come.
  - The moment came and the practice held.
  - Afterwards, they gave the credit away.
  - The practice has to be visible in the source, not only the result.
  - Out: solo geniuses, danger as spectacle, medicine.
- **Position direction:** on the bad day you won't be alone. Being enough is the crew's job, not yours alone.

## hero-explorer · The Quest Seeker
- **One true thing:** they aren't fearless. They're scared the whole way and go anyway. And the prize was always partly an excuse for the proof that they could.
- **Hides:** the fear itself, and the flat feeling the day after they arrive. **Fears:** a life with nothing left to go after. **Gives:** they bring back the thing *and* the proof that it could be done, so others try.
- **Close siblings and existing pours:**
  - vs the Explorer family (wandering and freedom): this person needs a *named* target, and freedom isn't the point.
  - vs the Initiate: this one already has proof.
  - vs the Researcher / Serviceable: that one works for strangers. This one goes for the prize.
  - vs the Rescuer: the Rescuer goes for others.
- **Traps:** relics taken from other people's temples (no looting told as glory, no colonial "discovery"); Indiana Jones puns; "adventurer" as a label.
- **Story test:**
  - Must show a person who went after one *specific, hard-to-get* thing (a lost recipe, a vanished bottle, a plant, an answer) and got it.
  - The source must show a fear or a real risk on the way.
  - The story must reveal what the prize was really for, and ideally what they went after next.
  - Out: taking from others without credit, stories where the prize is the whole point.
- **Position direction:** tell someone you were scared. The fear is the part that makes the proof worth anything.

## hero-innocent · The Initiate
- **One true thing:** they already feel who they're going to be, with no proof at all, and they're a little embarrassed to be that sure.
- **Hides:** that certainty, and how fast they trust whoever teaches them. **Fears:** the moment it counts, and finding they aren't ready. **Gives:** the teacher remembers why they started. Beginners' willingness is catching.
- **Close siblings and existing pours:**
  - vs the Quest Seeker: no proof yet.
  - vs the Firefighter: not trained yet.
  - vs the Innocent family: wonder there is about the world. Here it's about who they'll become.
- **Traps:** age. Never "young" or "youth": a 55-year-old starting over is an Initiate too. Also Star Wars words ("chosen one", "padawan"), and a mentor-betrayal ending that frightens.
- **Story test:**
  - Must show a person at the *very start* (first job, first bar, first recipe, apprentice) with a teacher.
  - They face a first real test before they felt ready.
  - The story must make the unreadiness part of how they became themselves.
  - Being trusted too quickly may show, but gently.
  - Out: prodigies born finished, stories told only once someone was already famous.
- **Position direction:** you won't feel ready. Nobody does the first time. Go when it counts anyway.

## hero-jester · The Storyteller
- **One true thing:** the tall tale is how they get through. They tell their own hard things bigger and funnier, so the hard thing doesn't win, and so others believe they'll come through theirs.
- **Hides:** the plain version they never tell. **Fears:** that the plain version is the true one, a small life nobody remembers. **Gives:** people leave braver about their own troubles.
- **Close siblings and existing pours:**
  - vs **the Trickster / No Accident** (the big collision): the Trickster's story is a trick. The Storyteller's is a gift, about their *own* struggle. Keep "being ignored" and "people rally behind a story" out.
  - vs **the Reassurer / Worth the Trip**: the Reassurer lifts someone else in the moment. The Storyteller retells their own past, later, and bigger.
  - vs the Surrealist: the Surrealist notices the world is strange. The Storyteller makes their own life larger.
- **Traps:** calling them a liar or a show-off; any "deception" frame; Big Fish puns.
- **Story test:**
  - Must show a drinks figure who told *their own* hardship as a tall tale, where the legend and the record differ.
  - The legend must be kinder or braver than the truth, and the true core underneath must be sourced.
  - The room grants the legend first, then shows what's under it, and never makes the teller a fraud.
  - This is the one family member where a famous legend is an asset, not a problem.
- **Position direction:** tell one person the plain version too. It's worth remembering as it was.

## hero-lover · The Dashing Hero
- **One true thing:** the polish is a lid. The calm, the joke and the perfect exit keep people from seeing how much they feel for the ones they'd fight for.
- **Hides:** the love. **Fears:** feeling too much, and losing someone they couldn't protect. **Gives:** the people they love feel safe and never have to carry the worry.
- **Close siblings and existing pours:**
  - vs the Superhero: the Superhero hides an ability. This one hides a feeling.
  - vs the Lovable Rogue: the Rogue hides kindness behind a joke to avoid seeming soft. This one hides *love*, behind style.
  - vs the Lover family: here the feeling is disguised, not given freely.
  - vs the Connoisseur: not a truth kept for love.
- **Traps:** seduction and womanising; gender (never a gentleman or a Bond girl); danger glamour; spy puns. The Vesper will be the obvious reach: Bond names it after a woman he loses. It *does* mirror the wound, but it's fiction, it's gendered, and it's a betrayal story. Only if Hester sources it and the room owns the fiction.
- **Story test:**
  - Must show a person whose public style (cool, witty, immaculate) covered a private devotion to one person or a few.
  - Best if the source shows the devotion under the style, and any loss is told gently.
  - The style must be real and the feeling must be real. Neither one is a trick.
  - Out: seduction, violence, a death told as spectacle.
- **Position direction:** say it plainly to one of them before you ever have to protect them.

## hero-magician · The Superhero
- **One true thing:** they keep their best ability deliberately small, and underneath, they fear it's the only lovable thing about them.
- **Hides:** how much they could do, and the fear of being ordinary without it. **Fears:** being loved for the cape, not the person. **Gives:** power used quietly for others, and held back when it would only show off.
- **Close siblings and existing pours:**
  - vs the Dashing Hero: a hidden ability, not a hidden feeling.
  - vs the Firefighter: a gift, not drill.
  - vs the Problem Solver: not the thinking under pressure, but the double life.
  - vs the Sculptor: not made for one person.
- **Traps:** capes, kryptonite, "super", "genius"; any line that praises the ability (compliment trap).
- **Story test:**
  - Must show a person with an extraordinary skill who lived an ordinary-looking life (a day job, a quiet name) and used the skill for others without taking the credit.
  - Best if the source shows the ordinary side mattering to someone, apart from the skill.
  - Out: stories where the skill *is* the whole person, and fame stories.
- **Position direction:** let someone meet you without the cape, and notice that they stay.

## hero-outlaw · The Lovable Rogue
- **One true thing:** they come back. The rule-bending, the improvising and the wink all cover loyalty they'd be embarrassed to have named.
- **Hides:** a real sense of honour. **Fears:** being thought soft. **Gives:** when it counts, the least likely person turns up.
- **Close siblings and existing pours:**
  - vs **the Trickster / No Accident**: the Trickster breaks rules on purpose, for a story. The Rogue breaks them on the way to keeping a promise.
  - vs the Campaigner: not a cause, a friend.
  - vs the Dashing Hero: kindness hidden, not love behind polish.
- **Traps:** pirate puns; grog, navy rum and Vernon (Fair Measure's); Seelbach (No Accident's); making crime glamorous; "bad boy" (gendered).
- **Story test:**
  - Must show a person with a doubtful reputation (a smuggler, a Prohibition bartender, a rule-breaker) who kept faith with someone at a real cost to themselves.
  - The kindness has to be sourced and real, and ideally disguised as something else.
  - Out: crime as the point, harm to anyone, Trickster-style reveals.
- **Position direction:** once, let a kindness go out without the wink.

## hero-ruler · The Warrior
- **One true thing:** they can't falter in front of the people behind them. So they falter alone, beforehand, where nobody sees, and then walk out first.
- **Hides:** the tiredness and the doubt before. **Fears:** faltering where the people who depend on them can see. **Gives:** others can be afraid, because this one stands in front.
- **Close siblings and existing pours:**
  - vs **the Visionary / Down the Line**: the Visionary doubts the dream. The Warrior doubts their own nerve on the day.
  - vs the Rescuer: a group over time, with order to keep, not a lone rush.
  - vs **the Nanny / Fair Measure**: the Nanny keeps rules for the fun to last. The Warrior goes first into difficulty.
  - vs the Firefighter: leads from the front, not as an equal.
  - vs the Saviour: holds a line rather than giving something up.
- **Traps:** war glory, violence, a death ending (Hector dies at the gates); military ranks as labels.
- **Story test:**
  - Must show a person responsible for a group who went first into something hard and kept order when things fell apart.
  - The source must show a private moment of fear or doubt the group didn't see (a letter, a diary, a witness).
  - It must cost them. Out: battles, deaths told as glory.
- **Position direction:** let them see one crack. They'll close ranks around you.

## hero-sage · The Problem Solver
- **One true thing:** they secretly *enjoy* the crisis a little. Pressure makes them calm, and a problem with a clock is the best puzzle there is. That pleasure is the part they hide.
- **Hides:** the delight. **Fears:** standing in front of the problem, everyone watching, with no answer. **Gives:** their calm slows everyone else's heart, and turns panic into a next step.
- **Close siblings and existing pours:**
  - vs **the Rescuer / Off Duty**: the Rescuer acts, physically, for people. This one thinks. Avoid "clear".
  - vs **the Doctor / Instead**: the questioning frame is taken. Here it's the clock and the audience, not diagnosis.
  - vs the Researcher: works fast and in front of people, not slowly for strangers.
  - vs the Superhero: no double life.
- **Traps:** "genius", Apple, tech words; "invented by accident" myths.
- **Story test:**
  - Must show a person who solved a practical problem under real time pressure, with people waiting and watching.
  - They solved it with what was at hand (a shortage, a broken plan, a missing ingredient).
  - The source must show the clock and the audience. Best if it also shows their pleasure in it.
  - Out: slow research, pure luck.
- **Position direction:** say "I don't know yet" out loud once. It's the first useful question, and nobody thinks less of you.
