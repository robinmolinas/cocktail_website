# Sage family: Wren's sketches (2026-09-30)

Read fresh from Robin's rewritten personality sheet (`brief.py psychologist --plan sage`). Ten unauthored pairings. The Connoisseur (sage-lover, *A Brother's Care*, approved) is existing ground, and so are the other families' pours that carry Sage as the secondary.

## The family in one page (read this before any row)

**All eleven fear deception.** That's the family, not the person. So the row's test is always *whose lie, where, and what it costs*. My map, one line each:

| pairing | the lie they fear | what they do about it | what it costs them |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Connoisseur (approved) | the flattering label on a pleasure | find the words for why it's good | (owned: "being fooled", "the true story over the famous one") |
| Mentor | **their own** advice turning out false, in someone else's life | listen, ask, then step back | letting go of someone they helped grow |
| Architect | the beam that only looks load-bearing | test it in their head before anything exists | being the one who says "not yet" to a thing everyone likes |
| Dude | other people's lies and drama pulling them in | refuse the bait, keep the one real thing | being taken for someone who doesn't care |
| Philosopher | the easy answer | ask one layer deeper | never getting to be finished |
| Grandmaster | the trap they didn't see | read the whole position, give up a piece now | looking like they're losing, for a while |
| Prodigy | the grown-ups bluffing; being talked down to | keep asking with open eyes | being "too much", or "too young", long after they aren't |
| Wise Fool | a lie nobody dares laugh at | tell the truth sideways, with a joke | the risk of the joke landing on the wrong ear |
| Game-Changer | a revolution built on a flawed idea | make it so simple it seems obvious | the doubt nobody sees before the reveal |
| Lone Voice | a comfortable lie becoming the official truth | keep saying what the evidence shows | loneliness |
| Scientist | the result that turns out false (especially their own) | measure, write it down, test again | a messy world they can only keep in order by checking |

**Three triangles to keep apart** (most sibling collisions will be here):
- *Asking vs joking vs declaring:* Philosopher asks and never settles; Wise Fool slips it past with a laugh; Lone Voice says it straight and pays for it.
- *Testing things:* Scientist tests the result, after; Architect tests the structure, before; Grandmaster reads an opponent, over time.
- *Changing minds:* Game-Changer persuades by making it obvious; Lone Voice isn't believed; Mentor changes one person, from the inside, and then leaves.

**Already owned, outside the family (don't reuse the frame):** the Doctor (caregiver-sage) owns the hard question to a friend, "since when?", saying no rather than being wrong, *Instead*, Wondrich, Old Tom, Ransom. The Old Master (creator-sage) owns questioning your own best work, "settling into being right", Jerry Thomas's 1876 "Improved". The Researcher (caregiver-explorer) owns "that's just how it is is never enough", the kept dead ends, "Some of what you try goes nowhere", Priestley's soda. The Auteur (creator-ruler) owns "can't leave it when something's off", "hard to please", **"let someone else make the second"**, Saunders/Pegu. The Reassurer (caregiver-jester) owns "you don't joke because it isn't serious, you joke because it is", Scialom. The Surrealist (creator-jester) owns "this is going to sound weird". The Campaigner (caregiver-outlaw) owns speaking up with shaking hands for someone who isn't in the room, Heyrick. The Makeover Artist owns "the ugly middle". The Visionary owns "others build on your dream". Stock whoYouAre openers already taken: "What you can't forgive…", "What you dread isn't…", "What nobody sees…", "What you can't stand…", "What you give people…", "You're allowed to…", "Go back to it".

**Stories and motifs already taken (Hester, please don't bring these back):** the Rob Roy, Hoboken/Orphal, the lost 1873 recipe, oloroso solera, the two sherries, **the sage leaf** (and it's an archetype pun anyway: out for every row); Wondrich/Old Tom; Thomas 1876; Priestley; Saunders/Pegu; Arnold's milk-washed tea; Scialom/Suffering Bastard; the Nix tomato case; Heyrick; Bermejo/Tommy's; the St Bernard; Angostura as a base; Cecchini's Cosmopolitan; Twain/Livy; Picon; Uyeda; the Sheridan's/Shannon coffee; Vernon's 1740 grog (and "water as an order"); the Kir/cassis.

**Family-wide traps:** the personality names that are professions (Architect, Philosopher, Scientist, Grandmaster, Mentor) must never become the reading's label: the guest who has never built a thing or played chess must still see themselves. "Scientist of the senses" is the Connoisseur's opening line: nobody else gets "scientist" as a metaphor. No "books and footnotes" flattery: every Sage reading must say what the person *does* with knowing, not that they know a lot. Health is out for all ten (Curie's radium, "cures", medicine) unless a row says otherwise.

## sage-caregiver · The Mentor
- **The one true thing:** the only Sage whose feared lie is *their own*. They'd forgive being fooled; they'd never forgive having steered someone wrong. And the success they want is to be needed less: they're happiest when someone they helped outgrows them.
- **Hides:** how much they check their advice before they give it, and how long they turn it over afterwards ("was that right for *them*?"). Also, quietly: the small ache when the person no longer calls. **Fears:** false advice, given with confidence, lived by someone who trusted it. **Gives:** courage that belongs to the other person (they ask the question that makes the penny drop, so the answer is the other person's own).
- **Siblings:** vs the Connoisseur (approved): the Connoisseur's joy is someone discovering *the thing* through them; the Mentor's is someone discovering *themselves*, and then not needing them. The Mentor gives a question, not a recommendation. vs the Doctor (caregiver-sage): the Doctor asks the hard question to stop a mistake ("no"); the Mentor asks it so the other finds their own way ("go on"). vs the Visionary: others build on the Visionary's dream; the Mentor's people build their own. vs the Auteur: "let someone else make the second" is the Auteur's position. The Mentor's ending can't be "hand it over".
- **Traps:** teacher, coach, boss, parent (a label; and "parent" assumes). Oprah as the story (fine as one touchstone, never the mirror). "Wise", "guide" as praise. Making it about being thanked.
- **Story test:** the mirror must show (1) someone who taught or backed another person, where (2) the other person went on to outgrow them, surpass them or leave, and (3) the teacher was glad of it, on the record or in a signposted "I like to think". It must also show (4) honesty at a cost: advice that could have been flattering and wasn't, or a teacher who corrected their own earlier teaching. **What must hold:** the student's success is the student's; the story can't make the teacher the hero of it. **Out:** a master who kept the secret (the opposite), credit fights, health, quality-only stories ("the best bartender of his day"). Best if the drink itself travelled through the student, not the teacher.

## sage-creator · The Architect
- **The one true thing:** they see how it stands before it exists, and what they can't abide is something that only *looks* sound. The deception they fear is structural: the beam that was never carrying anything.
- **Hides:** the run-through already done in their head, including every way it falls down, so their calm "yes, that'll work" has a whole night of "no" behind it. Also that they'd rather be thought slow than build something that fails someone later. **Fears:** the hidden flaw, found by someone else, after people rely on it. **Gives:** things others can lean on without thinking about it (the holiday for twelve that simply works; nobody knows why it didn't go wrong).
- **Siblings:** vs the Scientist: the Scientist checks the result after; the Architect tests the plan before a single piece exists. vs the Grandmaster: no opponent and no clock; it's weight and time. vs the Game-Changer: the Architect builds one thing that stands; the Game-Changer wants to change how everybody lives. vs the Old Master (creator-sage) and the Auteur (creator-ruler): not revisiting old work, and not control of every detail; it's load-bearing vs decorative. They'll happily leave the decoration to anyone.
- **Traps:** architect, engineer, builder as labels; blueprint imagery in the text; praise of "precision". Keep "the plan in your head" as a scene (the kitchen, the holiday), never a job.
- **Story test:** the mirror must show (1) something designed before it was made (a drink, a bar, a method, a recipe built on paper or in the head), where (2) the design was about what holds it up, the hidden structure, not the look, and (3) it still stands: it's poured the same way today, or its frame is. Best if (4) the story has a false twin that looked sound and didn't last. **What must hold:** the structure is real and on the page (ratio, method, a template that others built on). **Out:** "it took years to perfect" (quality-only), the Old Master's return to one's own work, anything about fame.

## sage-regular-guy · The Dude
- **The one true thing:** they know exactly what matters, and it's small. Everyone else is scheming and shouting; all they want is the one real thing back (the rug that tied the room together). Their wisdom is refusing the bait.
- **Hides:** that the calm is learned, not lazy. It took living to find out most drama isn't worth it. Also that they do care, a lot, about the few real things, which is why they won't spend care on the fake ones. **Fears:** being dragged into someone else's lie and made to carry it. **Gives:** a lower temperature. People come to them when they want the room to go quiet. (Say that out loud: it's worth something.)
- **Siblings:** vs the Wise Fool: the Fool steps into the drama with a joke to puncture it; the Dude steps out of it. vs the Philosopher: the Philosopher goes deeper; the Dude goes simpler. vs the Minimalist (creator-innocent): the Minimalist takes away to feel each thing; the Dude just won't pick up what isn't theirs to carry (no reduction as craft). vs the Connoisseur: no words for why, no ceremony.
- **Traps:** "slacker", stoner, laziness; gender (the name is slang: never "he", never "guy", never "man" about the guest); quoting the film at length (one touchstone at most; the film's lines are not the reading's words); making "easy-going" a compliment.
- **Story test:** the mirror must show (1) someone or something surrounded by fuss, fashion or pretence that (2) kept to the plain, real thing, and (3) turned out to be right, or lasting, because of it. It must show (4) what they refused, not only what they kept. **What must hold:** the plain thing has to be honestly good, not settled for. **Out:** fan service (the White Russian *only* if Hester's story in it shows the refusal and the one real thing; a drink from the film isn't a mirror on its own), laziness as the joke, anything that sneers at people who do care.

## sage-explorer · The Philosopher
- **The one true thing:** they can't leave a question alone, and the truth is always one layer deeper. They'd rather have an unfinished truth than a finished answer that's false. They think best out loud with others, until very late.
- **Hides:** that the questions sometimes cost them company: they've watched people get tired, and they've learned to keep the next "but why?" to themselves. Also that they enjoy not knowing more than they admit. **Fears:** settling for the easy answer, being satisfied too soon. **Gives:** the long conversation. People leave a dinner with them thinking about something they'd never have thought about.
- **Siblings:** vs the Wise Fool and the Lone Voice (the triangle above): the Philosopher never reaches a verdict to declare or joke about. vs the Prodigy: not early brilliance, but a habit of depth. vs the Researcher (caregiver-explorer): the Researcher chases for someone who's hurting and "loves the chase" is already said there: the Philosopher's pleasure is the *conversation*, not the hunt. vs the Old Master: not doubting one's own work, but doubting the question.
- **Traps:** "philosopher" as label; the symposium as "drinking party" gag; making them sound argumentative or endlessly indecisive (it's depth, not dithering). Plato's Symposium as the mirror is allowed but crowded: wine mixed with water by an appointed person echoes the Nanny's grog ("water as an order"): Hester, own it or avoid it.
- **Story test:** the mirror must show (1) a question that stayed open, where (2) people kept asking it, in conversation or across generations, and (3) the obvious answer turned out wrong, or the question mattered more than any answer. Best if (4) the answer is still unsettled today, and the story says so plainly (an honest "we don't know" suits this guest better than any legend). **What must hold:** the openness is real on the page, never manufactured. **Out:** a neat solved-mystery story (that's the Scientist or the Connoisseur), the Researcher's chase, anything where one person "finally found the truth".

## sage-hero · The Grandmaster
- **The one true thing:** they're brave in a patient way. They'll give up something now, a piece, a position, even the look of winning, for what matters ten moves later. And what they fear isn't losing; it's the trap they didn't see.
- **Hides:** that the calm is work. By the time they move they've already played out the bad lines in their head, including the ones where they lose. Also that the sacrifice hurts while they're making it. **Fears:** being outplayed by the one thing they didn't read. **Gives:** steadiness others lean on in a rush (everyone else panics; they say "wait"), and the long view that turns out right.
- **Siblings:** vs the Architect: an opponent and a clock, not a structure. vs the Scientist: they act on reading, not proof; they must decide before they can know. vs the Game-Changer: the Game-Changer's move is one big reveal; the Grandmaster's is a quiet sacrifice whose meaning shows later. vs the Rescuer (caregiver-hero): the Rescuer moves before anyone decides; the Grandmaster waits on purpose.
- **Traps:** chess player as label; killer instinct, beating people, "winning" as the point; war metaphors; "master" wordplay.
- **Story test:** the mirror must show (1) a choice that looked like a loss at the time (something given up, delayed, left out) that (2) was chosen on purpose for a later gain, and (3) paid off in a way that's on the record. It must show (4) the moment others thought it was a mistake. **What must hold:** the sacrifice is real and costly, and the patience is visible (time passes in the story). **Out:** a lucky win, cleverness alone, triumph over a rival as the payoff (the gain should be the thing lasting, not someone losing), health.

## sage-innocent · The Prodigy
- **The one true thing:** they knew things early, and the delight never went out of it. What they can't stand is being patronised, or finding out the grown-ups were bluffing.
- **Hides:** the old sting of being talked down to, which still flares when someone explains a thing they already know. And the moment, long ago, they realised an adult they'd believed didn't actually know. They kept asking anyway; they just learned whom to ask. **Fears:** condescension, and bluff passed off as knowledge. **Gives:** the question that silences the table, asked with wide-open eyes and no agenda, which makes it honest for everyone.
- **Siblings:** vs the Philosopher: not depth for its own sake, but speed and wonder together. vs the Wise Fool: the Prodigy's question isn't a tactic; it's sincere. vs the Game-Changer: they want to know, not to transform. vs the Connoisseur: no curation, no taste; it's appetite.
- **Traps:** making it about age (the guest may be seventy; "early" is the start of a life, not the state of it); child genius clichés; "gifted"; praising intelligence (the reading recognises the delight and the sting, never the IQ). Tavi Gevinson is a gendered example: fine for Robin's sheet, never in the reading's pronouns about the guest.
- **Story test:** the mirror must show (1) someone who was young or new when they got something right, (2) who was doubted or talked down to by people with more standing, and (3) who kept the curiosity, not the grudge. Best if (4) the elders turned out to be bluffing or wrong on the record. **What must hold:** the young person's knowledge is on the page, not a legend of "a boy who invented it". **Out:** revenge, "proved them all wrong" gloating, fame as the payoff, anyone whose youth is only asserted.

## sage-jester · The Wise Fool
- **The one true thing:** they tell the truth sideways. They get it past people's defences with a joke, especially the defences of whoever is in charge. They're cleverer than they let on, and that's on purpose.
- **Hides:** that every joke of that kind is a small risk, aimed and timed, and they know exactly who might not laugh. And that being taken for "just the funny one" is sometimes useful and sometimes lonely. **Fears:** a room full of lies nobody dares laugh at. **Gives:** permission. After their joke, everyone can say what they were thinking.
- **Siblings:** vs the Reassurer (caregiver-jester): the Reassurer jokes to comfort someone hurting, and already owns "you joke because it is serious". The Wise Fool jokes to deflate pretence, aimed up, not down or across. vs the Surrealist (creator-jester): the Surrealist notices strange things; the Wise Fool notices lies. vs the Dude: steps in, not out. vs the Lone Voice: gets away with it, where the Lone Voice doesn't.
- **Traps:** the word "jester" (archetype name: say fool, clown, or nothing); the joke *about* the guest; stand-up comic as a label; King Lear as the mirror (fine as a touchstone, not a drinks story).
- **Story test:** the mirror must show (1) a truth told through humour, a name, a prank or a mocking drink, (2) aimed at someone powerful or something pompous, where (3) the joke carried a real point that stuck. It must show (4) the risk: it could have cost them. **What must hold:** the joke and its target are on the record; the point it made is real. **Out:** a drink that's merely silly, a joke at the weak's expense, Prohibition-era "bootlegger outlaw" romance (that's another row's ground), anything already in the Reassurer's or the Surrealist's pour.

## sage-magician · The Game-Changer
- **The one true thing:** they study a problem until they see what everyone missed, and then make it so simple it looks obvious. They want the knowledge to change how people live. What scares them is being wrong at that scale.
- **Hides:** the years of work behind the "obvious" thing, and the doubt the night before it goes out: if this is wrong, it's wrong for everyone. Also that "it seems obvious now" is both the proudest thing anyone says to them and a little galling. **Fears:** a revolution built on a flawed idea. **Gives:** a new normal that makes life easier for people who'll never know who changed it.
- **Siblings:** vs the Makeover Artist (creator-magician): one person's thing, kept theirs; the Game-Changer changes everyone's. vs the Visionary: the Visionary's dream outlasts them; the Game-Changer's change has to be *right*, and they check. vs the Architect: not one sound structure, but a changed habit. vs the Lone Voice: they persuade by showing, and they're believed.
- **Traps:** Steve Jobs hagiography; tech-launch language ("disruptive", "iconic"); magic wordplay (archetype pun: no "trick", no "spell"); genius worship.
- **Story test:** the mirror must show (1) one clear insight that (2) changed how many people drink or make drinks, (3) so completely that today it looks obvious or is simply taken for granted. It must show (4) the risk of being wrong, or a check before it spread. **What must hold:** the spread is on the page, and the "before" is shown (what everyone did until then). **Out:** a lone inventor myth without sources, a change that was only fashion (the Trend Setter's), a flop, health claims.

## sage-outlaw · The Lone Voice
- **The one true thing:** they say what the evidence shows, even when everyone important wants them to stop. Not to provoke: because the alternative is a comfortable lie becoming official. Loneliness is often the price, and they pay it.
- **Hides:** that they'd love to be agreed with. They aren't contrary for fun. Being right alone is not a pleasure; it's just better than being wrong in company. **Fears:** the comfortable lie written down as the truth. **Gives:** the record. Because they said it, later people could find it.
- **Siblings:** vs the Campaigner (caregiver-outlaw): the Campaigner speaks for someone who isn't in the room, about what's unfair, and owns the shaking hands. The Lone Voice speaks for the evidence, and the cost is being alone, not nerves. vs the Wise Fool: says it straight, gets no pass. vs the Philosopher: has found something and says it; doesn't keep asking. vs the Scientist: the Scientist's fight is with their own result; the Lone Voice's is with authority.
- **Traps:** martyrdom and melodrama; Galileo as the mirror (touchstone at most); rebel or contrarian as praise; outlaw wordplay (archetype pun).
- **Story test:** the mirror must show (1) someone who stated a thing they had evidence for (2) against an official or popular version, (3) and paid for it in standing, company or credit. It must show (4) the evidence, not only the courage. Vindication is welcome but must not be the point; the cost has to be felt. **What must hold:** the official version and the dissent are both on the page. **Out:** a righteous cause for others (the Campaigner's), a story with no real cost, someone later proved *wrong*.

## sage-ruler · The Scientist
- **The one true thing:** they trust only what can be tested, and the result that haunts them is a false one, above all their own. Rigour is how they keep a messy world in order: it's calming, not cold.
- **Hides:** that the checking is how they settle themselves. Measuring and writing it down makes the world hold still. And that they check their own work harder than anyone else's. **Fears:** a result that looked true and wasn't, with their name on it. **Gives:** things people can rely on without re-checking, because they already did.
- **Siblings:** vs the Researcher (caregiver-explorer): the Researcher chases a way through for someone hurting and loves the chase; the Scientist wants the thing to be *true*, and the pleasure is the order. vs the Auteur (creator-ruler): the Auteur sends things back until they're right by taste and control; the Scientist accepts a result they dislike if it's true. vs the Old Master (creator-sage): not doubting a celebrated work, but testing before anything is claimed. vs the Architect: after the fact vs before.
- **Traps:** "scientist" as label or metaphor (the Connoisseur's opening already says "a scientist of the senses": this row can't reuse it); lab coats and beakers; Curie's story (radium is health: out); "ruler" or measuring-stick wordplay (archetype pun); "obsessive".
- **Story test:** the mirror must show (1) someone who measured, recorded or tested where others guessed, (2) and whose record corrected a false belief, or who corrected their own earlier claim in public. It must show (3) the dull, careful part (the weighing, the writing down), not only the finding. Best if (4) their numbers are still used. **What must hold:** the method is on the page, and the false belief is named. **Out:** health, a lucky discovery, the Researcher's Priestley ground, Arnold's (Makeover) ground, anything "perfected" (quality-only).

## Notes for Hester and Tomás
- **Every row needs a cost or a risk in the story.** Nine of these ten fail as mirrors if the story is only "someone was very good at it". That's the test most likely to kill a candidate late, so I've stated it in every row.
- **The rows most likely to collide with each other:** Mentor and Game-Changer (both "a change that spread", but the Mentor's spreads through one person); Lone Voice and Wise Fool (both against authority); Scientist and Architect (both method). If two leads fit two of these rows, give each to the row whose *cost* it shows.
- **Positions (not binding, for the rooms):** none is decided here. The only fence: the Mentor's ending can't be "let someone else make it" (the Auteur's), and the Scientist's can't be "question your best work" (the Old Master's).
