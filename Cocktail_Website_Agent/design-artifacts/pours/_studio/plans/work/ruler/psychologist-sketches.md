# Ruler family: Wren's sketches (2026-09-30)

All eleven Ruler pairings are unauthored. Each persona was read fresh from Robin's rewritten sheet (persona.py, via brief.py). Ground they have to differ from: the Nanny (caregiver-ruler, *Fair Measure*), the Auteur (creator-ruler, *Making the Calls*), the Doctor, the Campaigner, the Rescuer, the Nurse, the Trend Setter, the Old Master, the Visionary, the Trickster and the True Friend.

## Family-wide tests (apply to every row)

- **The shared fear isn't the true thing.** All eleven fear chaos ("Revolution"). If a reading rests on "you hold it all together and fear it falling apart", it fits all eleven, and the Nanny already owns it ("if you stopped holding it all, it would come apart, and it would be your fault"). Each row below names **its own kind of chaos**. The story has to threaten that kind, not chaos in general.
- **The guest is an ordinary person.** Every example on the sheet is a head of state, a tycoon or a star. Show the power in a family, a group of friends, a kitchen table, a shared house. Never assume status, money, staff or a job title. No "boss", "manager", "CEO", "president", "judge" (the job), "king/queen".
- **No archetype puns:** rule, ruler, reign, crown, throne, "you rule", "rule of thumb", "royal treatment", and a ruler as a measuring stick ("to the millimetre"; the Auteur already owns "to the millilitre").
- **Gender:** the examples are mostly men, King Arthur is a king, and "Diva" is coded feminine. None of it reaches the guest. No knight, chivalry, sir, lady, queen, princess, statesman or "man of the people".
- **The sheet's examples are out as mirrors** (Gandhi, Wintour, Mandela, Lincoln, Arthur, Carey, Branson, Clinton, Churchill, Che, Judge Judy): no politics, war, race, religion or scandal. Churchill's drinking legends (the vermouth-glance martini) are legends and glorify drinking, so they're out.
- **Out for the whole family:** health or cure stories (Caregiver ground), violence, drinking contests or strength as power, and any story that only praises quality.
- **Existing ground to keep clear of:** grog or watering the rum, Admiral Vernon, 1740, "one more, then home" (Nanny). Sending it back with a note, credit and blame, redoing others' parts, Pegu (Auteur). "See the finished thing first" (Down the Line). Asking the hard question, saying no (Doctor). Speaking first for the absent, sugar as argument (Campaigner). "Serve it to whoever rolls their eyes", the staff drink (Trend Setter). Change one thing and keep the old beside it (Old Master). A shared punch bowl in equal cups (Four Shares). Everyone gets the same you (Nurse). Moving first in a crisis (Rescuer). Being ignored, rather misunderstood than boring (Trickster). A taxi (Reassurer). A rowdy kitchen going quiet (Angel). A broken promise "most importantly to yourself" (Researcher).
- **Hester, a flag:** Petraske and Milk & Honey already appear in the Trend Setter's and the Nanny's files, and tiki or the Beachcomber in the Nanny's and the Auteur's. Check how before a Ruler row leans on them.
- **Tone:** the Ruler tone is confident and decisive, so readings may take a firm position. The Diva and the Charismatic Leader can be funny, and should be.
- **Close pairs inside the family** (the room reads each against its pair): Caring Leader / Judge (both listen first). Perfectionist / Diva (both insist it be right). Charismatic / Magnetic (both charm). Noble / People's Representative (everyone gets a place). Reformer / Revolutionary (both change the order). Legend / Noble (both stand up in the storm).

## ruler-caregiver · The Caring Leader

- **The one true thing:** when everyone else raises their voice, they lower theirs, and the room follows. Their authority is quiet by choice, and the calm is work. It isn't temperament.
- **Hides:** they're as frightened as anyone in the heat. They take everyone's shouting and carry it home. **Fears:** that someone in their care gets hurt because they were too gentle, or drew the line a moment too late. **Gives:** a room safe enough that people do their best in it.
- **Their chaos:** a moment of heat (a row, a panic, a scene) that hurts someone on their watch.
- **Differs from:** the Nanny (a limit kept over time, "one more, then home"; this one is the heat of a single moment, and a boundary spoken calmly rather than a curfew). The Rescuer (moves and takes over; this one stays still, listens, and lets others do the work). The Judge (listens in order to decide; this one listens so people calm down). The Noble Ruler (stands in front; this one stands among).
- **Traps:** saint or martyr talk, Gandhi, "servant leadership" jargon, any parent-and-child reading, "safe space" jargon.
- **Story test:** the story's person must **have real power and choose not to use force or volume**. They must **face a moment of heat** (an angry crowd, a panicking crew, a fight at the bar) and **hold a line, said calmly**. It must **cost** them: patience, being taken for weak, or a price of their own. What must hold: **nobody in their care gets hurt, and the people around them do their best work because of the calm.** Out: rescues, rationing and water, hunger or health, and a calm that's only a quality of the drink.
- **Listening for:** the guest recognising their own quiet voice as a decision, and the reading saying out loud that it's worth something.

## ruler-creator · The Perfectionist

- **The one true thing:** their "no" becomes other people's good taste. They choose and refuse. They don't make everything themselves (that's the Auteur). A careless detail doesn't feel untidy to them. It feels **wrong**, a small crack in the world.
- **Hides:** it isn't coldness, it's care, and every no costs them a little too. They'd rather be thought cold than let it go out wrong. **Fears:** something careless going out with their name on it. **Gives:** a standard the people around them rise to and remember.
- **Their chaos:** mediocrity let through, in their name.
- **Differs from:** the Auteur, the closest ground in the studio ("the one wrong word", "send it back with a note", "blamed for all of it", redoing others' parts at night). The Auteur makes it all and owns it all. The Perfectionist **curates and edits**: the standard is theirs and the hands are other people's. The Visionary owns "see the finished thing first". The Minimalist takes away. The Old Master questions their own work. The Diva wants it beautiful; the Perfectionist wants it **right**.
- **Traps:** editor, curator or stylist as a label. The "ice queen" (gendered, and an insult). *The Devil Wears Prada* as the touchstone. "Hard to please" (the Auteur's line).
- **Story test:** a person whose **taste set the rule for others** (a menu, a book, a house style, a list of what's allowed) and who **refused things to protect it**. It must **cost** them warmth or being liked. What must hold: **the standard was care for whoever would drink or read it, not vanity**, and other people's work got better under it. Out: redoing others' work, revising their own classic, taking away, a vision handed on, a story that's only about quality.
- **Position direction:** their yes is rare, so say it out loud, as loudly as the no. Not "let it go out imperfect", which sits too close to the Auteur's "let someone else make the second".

## ruler-regular-guy · The People's Representative

- **The one true thing:** when a group splits, they cross to the other side's table and put on its colours. They lead by belonging to the people who expected them to be the enemy.
- **Hides:** crossing costs them with their own side ("whose side are you on?"). **Fears:** being made to choose a side, and the split becoming permanent. **Gives:** both halves end up in one room.
- **Their chaos:** division, a family, team or group pulling apart.
- **Differs from:** the True Friend (makes sure nobody is left out; kindness, forgiveness). The Nurse (the same you for everyone; this is about two camps, and a gesture across). The Noble Ruler (equal places under their word, set up from the top; this one walks over, from the ground). The Caring Leader (calms the heat; this one bridges the sides).
- **Traps:** Mandela, apartheid, race, sport. Don't make them political. "Common touch" and "down to earth" read as condescending. No "man of the people".
- **Story test:** someone with standing **crosses to the other side's symbol, drink or ground** to hold a divided group together. They must **face distrust from the other side and suspicion from their own**. It must **cost** them their own side's easy approval. What must hold: **both sides end up in the same room, drinking the same thing**, and the person loses nothing of who they are. Shapes that would pass: a drink two rivals both claim, or someone who poured the rival's drink. Out: charity, a limp compromise that pleases nobody, war.

## ruler-explorer · The Reformer

- **The one true thing:** they change the rules while the thing is still running, one beam at a time, so that more people fit inside. They're the one who opens the door and keeps the house standing.
- **Hides:** they're slow on purpose. The bold ones call them timid, and the old guard calls them reckless. **Fears:** pulling the wrong beam and bringing it all down, on their watch. **Gives:** room for people who were outside.
- **Their chaos:** collapse halfway through a change.
- **Differs from:** the Revolutionary (tears the whole thing down and wants the new one to be theirs; this one keeps what works and wants it to be everyone's). The Old Master (changes one thing in their own work; this one changes the shared rules, for others). The Makeover Artist (transforms a thing for someone, "calm in the ugly middle": keep clear of that phrase). The Campaigner (raises a voice; this one rewrites the rules).
- **Traps:** Lincoln, slavery, civil war, US politics. "Change-maker" corporate speak. Reformer as a label.
- **Story test:** a person or drink that **changed a rule or an institution from inside** (a guild, a bar's door policy, a law about drink) and **kept the parts that worked**. The whole must **not collapse during the change**. It must **cost** them attacks from both sides, or the slowness. What must hold: **the change let more people in, and the old thing still stands.** Out: an overthrow, one person revising their own recipe, a story where the change stays unfinished.

## ruler-hero · The Noble Ruler

- **The one true thing:** duty is how they love. They keep their word and ask only that others keep theirs. At their table nobody's seat is higher, and they take the hard call so nobody else has to.
- **Hides:** the doubt. Standing in front means nobody sees their face. They carry decisions nobody thanks them for. **Fears:** failing the people who trusted them. **Gives:** everyone around them has a place and knows it will still be there.
- **Their chaos:** disorder breaking into what they built, and a trust broken on their side.
- **Differs from:** the Rescuer (a sudden crisis, moves first; this one is the long weight of promises). The Caring Leader (calm and listening; this one decides and stands in front). The People's Representative (crosses to the other side; this one keeps its own house). The Legend (words in a storm, the verdict of history; this one keeps its word and cares little for fame). The Researcher owns the broken promise "most importantly to yourself". Four Shares owns the equal shared bowl, so no punch-bowl mirror.
- **Traps:** King Arthur is a legend, never a fact. Knights, kings, chivalry, the military, statues (they'd hate being a statue). "Duty" can read cold, so show it as love.
- **Story test:** someone who **made a promise, or a rule of equal places, and kept it at a real cost**, and who **took a hard decision alone that protected others**. What must hold: **the word is kept, and everyone keeps their place.** The cost is carried without thanks. Out: a rescue in the moment, a legend passed off as fact, sacrifice or death, war, a shared equal bowl.
- **Position direction:** let someone else carry one decision, or let the people at the table see the doubt. Keep it apart from the Rescuer's "let them do the going".

## ruler-innocent · The Diva

- **The one true thing:** they insist on beauty on everyone's behalf. They're the one who asks for the lights down, the song changed, the drink remade, and everyone's evening gets better for it. Only they get called difficult.
- **Hides:** it isn't vanity, it's hope, an almost childlike belief that life should be lovely and that someone has to say so. **Fears:** being ignored, and the night sliding into ordinary mess. **Gives:** occasions that feel like occasions. People remember their nights with them.
- **Their chaos:** a mess nobody bothered to stop, and a request nobody heard.
- **Differs from:** the Perfectionist (a standard, the right result, in their name; the Diva wants it **beautiful**, for the moment, from wonder rather than rigour). The Trickster owns "being ignored" as its line, so the Diva's reading must reach it another way (the request that goes unheard). The Connoisseur (truth in taste). The Trend Setter (liking it first).
- **Traps:** gendered coding (queen, princess, "she"), "spoiled", "high maintenance", "petulant" said to the guest's face, mocking them. A funny reading is welcome. A reading that laughs **at** them breaks the First Law.
- **Story test:** someone who **insisted on how a drink or a room should be** (the glass, the ice, the light, the song), sent things back, and **was thought difficult for it**. What must hold: **the insistence came from wanting it beautiful for everyone, and people ended up loving the result.** Comedy is allowed. Out: snobbery, quality for its own sake, a tantrum with nothing beautiful behind it, a story where they're punished or humiliated.

## ruler-jester · The Charismatic Leader

- **The one true thing:** the fun is the plan. They make it look improvised, but they've already found the exits, and they're steering the whole time everyone thinks they're only having fun. They turn the stranded night into the best one of the trip.
- **Hides:** nobody sees the steering, and some days they wonder whether people follow them or the show. **Fears:** the group stalling on their watch, going flat, or tipping into real chaos. **Gives:** people do things with them they'd never have dared.
- **Their chaos:** a show that falls apart, or one that goes dull. Both are theirs to prevent.
- **Differs from:** the Trickster (mischief against rules, the fear of being ignored, "rather misunderstood than boring"; the Charismatic Leader's fear is the **group** losing momentum, not their own invisibility). The Reassurer (humour as comfort at the wrong moment; the taxi is theirs). The Magnetic Leader (one person, up close; this one is a crowd and an adventure). The Surrealist.
- **Traps:** Branson ("the story goes" is a legend), billionaire or stunt-man labels, "life of the party" (Barnum). Don't make them shallow.
- **Story test:** someone who **turned a setback into an adventure others joined**, with a big gesture, while **quietly still in charge** (the logistics, the rules, the safety). What must hold: **it was genuinely fun and genuinely under control.** Cost: nobody saw the work, and it could have tipped over. Out: pranks for their own sake, comfort humour, a stunt nobody followed, a story that only shows the showman and never the steering.

## ruler-lover · The Magnetic Leader

- **The one true thing:** they know exactly how well their warmth works, and the warmth is still real. The name they remember, the second of eye contact: it's attention, and they worked at it. It isn't magic, and it isn't a trick.
- **Hides:** being "on" is tiring, and there's a quiet doubt whether people follow them or the spell. **Fears:** the night the spell breaks, losing the room. **Gives:** people leave feeling they were the only one in it.
- **Their chaos:** the room turning away.
- **Differs from:** the Nurse, its mirror image. The Nurse gives everyone the same; this one gives each person the feeling of being the only one. Neither reading may echo "everyone gets the same you". The Charismatic Leader (a crowd and an adventure; this one leads face to face). The Obsessed (falls for things; this one draws people). The Trickster and the Makeover Artist (tricks and transformations; this is not a con).
- **Traps:** Clinton, affairs, anything sexual about the guest, manipulation framing ("you use people"), "ladies' man", saxophone or performer labels. Seduction stays out entirely.
- **Story test:** someone who **won people one at a time, face to face** (a host, a bartender famous for remembering every regular's name and drink), and **knew what that warmth did**. What must hold: **the warmth was real, and the order of the room rested on it.** Cost: always being on, and the fear of the one night it didn't work. Out: seduction, a con artist, charm as manipulation, a performance on a stage.

## ruler-magician · The Legend

- **The one true thing:** they were written off before. In ordinary times they're a bit too much: too intense, too dramatic, too long a view. When everything shakes, everyone turns to them, and they find the words.
- **Hides:** the years of being dismissed still sting, and part of them knows they come alive in a crisis. **Fears:** being remembered for the one time chaos won. **Gives:** the sentence people hold on to at the worst moment.
- **Their chaos:** the verdict of history, being the one who let it happen.
- **Differs from:** the Rescuer (hands, fast, in the hour; this one is words, over decades). The Visionary (a dream others build on; this one is a turning point in a storm). The Noble Ruler (the steady keeping of promises; this one waits out the wilderness years). The Old Master (questions their own best work). Beware the "forgotten, then revived" arc that many drink stories share, and the Photographer's "a book that never caught on". The mirror must be dismissed **and then needed in a crisis**, not simply rediscovered.
- **Traps:** war, glorifying Churchill, his drinking, cigars. "You're a legend" as a pun on the name. Assuming the guest is famous.
- **Story test:** someone **dismissed or out of favour for years** whose moment came **in a crisis**, and whose **words or single decision turned it**. What must hold: **the long patience, the years of being dismissed, and the right words at the right time.** Cost: the wasted years. Out: war as the story, drinking as strength, the Churchill martini legend, fame for its own sake, a plain revival with no crisis in it.

## ruler-outlaw · The Revolutionary

- **The one true thing:** they never wanted no rules. They wanted the right rules, theirs. They fought the old way and won, and now they're the ones laying down the new way, and they can see the next rebel coming. That rebel looks a lot like them.
- **Hides:** the flicker of recognising their younger self in whoever challenges them, and the fear of becoming what they fought. **Fears:** the next revolution. **Gives:** proof that the old order can change, and conviction when everyone else hedges.
- **Their chaos:** being overturned in turn.
- **Differs from:** the Reformer (keeps what works, changes from inside, wants room for everyone; this one tears it down and wants it to be theirs). The Campaigner (a voice for others, for fairness). The Trickster (strings and jokes). The Trend Setter ("serve it to whoever rolls their eyes": keep the proposal clear of welcoming the eye-roller).
- **Traps:** Che, violence, executions, glamorising revolution, "comrade", flags. The irony must be told **gently**, never as hypocrisy (Robin: be gentle about failings).
- **Story test:** someone or something that **overturned an establishment** (a drink, a bar, a bartender who defied the old rules) and **then became the establishment**, facing the next challenger. What must hold: **the conviction was real, the win was real, and the irony is owned in one kind clause.** Out: violence, a story that ends in their downfall, a reading that calls them a hypocrite. (The craft-cocktail revival turning into its own orthodoxy fits the shape, but see the Petraske flag above.)

## ruler-sage · The Judge

- **The one true thing:** they decide. Plenty of people listen to both sides. They're the one who then says who's right, even when it's the friend they like more, and they stand by it. They distrust the kind-sounding fudge that avoids deciding, because it hurts more people later.
- **Hides:** ruling against someone they love costs them. They'd rather be fair than liked, but they do want to be liked. **Fears:** chaos dressed up as kindness, and the lie nobody bothered to check. **Gives:** people know where they stand, and clear rules protect the quiet ones from the loud ones.
- **Their chaos:** an unchecked lie, and a muddle passed off as kindness.
- **Differs from:** the Doctor, the closest ground ("the one question they were hoping nobody would", "you say no anyway", "go over it all the way home wondering if you were too hard"). The Doctor asks and fears being wrong. The Judge **weighs two sides and settles it**. So the Judge's hidden cost can't be the Doctor's replay on the way home. The Connoisseur (truth kept gently, fear of deception). The Caring Leader (listens to calm people, not to decide).
- **Traps:** law or courtroom as the guest's job, TV snark, "judgemental" and "verdict" wordplay on the name, an arrogant reading.
- **Story test:** someone who **settled a dispute or protected ordinary drinkers by checking what was real** (a rule written against adulterated spirits, a disputed origin settled on the evidence, an honest measure enforced). What must hold: **both sides heard, a decision made and stood by, and the rule protecting the ordinary drinker rather than the powerful.** Cost: being unpopular with someone. Out: questioning a plan (Doctor), a truth lovingly preserved (Connoisseur), deception as a trick (Trickster).
