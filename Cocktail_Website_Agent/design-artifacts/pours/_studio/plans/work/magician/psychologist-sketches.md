# Magician family: Wren's sketches (2026-09-30)

Ten unauthored pairings. The Trickster (magician-outlaw, *No Accident*, approved) is existing ground. Read fresh from Robin's rewritten sheet.

## For the whole family (read before any row)

**All eleven fear "unintended results".** That fear alone never tells a guest it's them. Each row names its own version, and the story has to mirror that version, not the family's:

| personality | their version of the fear |
| --- | --- |
| Trickster (approved) | caught before the trick has done its work; underneath, being ignored |
| Fairy Godparent | meddling: help nobody asked for that goes wrong |
| Genie | a wish granted too literally: giving what was said, not what was meant |
| Entertainer | the flop: the trick that falls flat in front of people |
| Alchemist | a result they can't undo |
| Good Wizard | the protection that fails someone who was counting on them |
| Sorcerer's Apprentice | a spell they can't stop once it's started |
| Illusionist | being figured out completely, with nothing left to surprise |
| Siren | a pull that works too well on someone and does them harm |
| Omnipotent One | one piece that moves on its own and undoes the plan |
| Clairvoyant | the thing they didn't see coming |

**Ground other pours already own. No row may lean on it:**
- *No Accident* (Trickster): hoaxes and invented origins, "strings", "a trick with a gift inside", "the truth at the bottom of the glass", "no accident", "three moves ahead", the Champagne Cocktail/Seelbach shape (sugar cube, flute).
- *Till Spring* (Healer, caregiver-magician): help made easy to take, bitter beside sweet, believing someone will be all right before they do, the ones who didn't mend ("put them down"), amer/Picon, medicine.
- *Overnight* (Makeover Artist, creator-magician): the ugly middle, the wait overnight, the "unintended side benefit", the happy accident as the best part ("some of your best work will be the part you didn't mean"), milk punch and clarification.
- Elsewhere: never the same thing twice (*Off-Label*); already gone while others decide (*Off Duty*); credit and blame, "let someone else make the second" (*Making the Calls*); "why the fun lasts" (*Fair Measure*); the Tuesdays (*As It Was*); "made like X with no X in it" (*The Other Berry*); absinthe and falling for things (*Rosetta*); "you're allowed to…", "keep one back for yourself", "sit down" (*Four Shares*, *Off Duty*); "don't apologise" (*The Other Berry*).

**Family traps:** no puns on the archetype name ("magician", "spellbinding"); the persona's own name is fine, used once, never as a joke ("your wish is my command", "abracadabra", wands, lamps, crystal balls). "Magic" may appear sparingly, as *No Accident* used it. The sheet's examples (Cinderella, Aladdin, Mickey, Harry Potter, Gandalf, Escher, Flamel, the Sistine ceiling, the Sirens) can be a touchstone, at most one per reading. They are never the mirror story. Never quote them, and never let their gender pass to the guest. **Out for every row:** health or cure claims, hoaxes (Trickster's), a happy accident as the payoff (Overnight's), and quality-only stories ("it was simply excellent").

**Cross-family cousins** (being planned in parallel; flag collisions to the host): Explorer's Spiritualist, Hero's Superhero, Innocent's Dreamer, Jester's Elf/Imp, Lover's Charmer, Outlaw's Disrupter, Regular Guy's Mechanic, Ruler's Legend, Sage's Game-Changer.

## magician-caregiver · The Fairy Godparent
- **The one true thing:** they fix what someone needs before it's been said, and then they leave before the big moment, so they never find out whether they had the right. Slipping away is exactly why the midnight worry never gets an answer.
- **Hide / fear / give:** they hide that they acted without asking. They fear it was meddling, a kindness that turns out wrong. What they give: the other person gets to feel the night was their own doing.
- **Versus siblings:** the Genie is *asked* and delivers loudly. The Godparent is never asked and stays unseen. The Omnipotent One runs the whole picture for everyone; the Godparent changes one person's evening. **Versus the Healer (*Till Spring*):** the Healer makes help easy to say yes to and waits for it to take. The Godparent never offers a yes at all, so the question is permission, not patience. Keep "easy to take" and "believing before they do" out. **Versus the Sculptor (*Quite Alive*):** the Sculptor makes a thing to measure for someone. The Godparent arranges a moment and isn't in the room for it.
- **Traps:** "godmother" or any gendered echo of the example; Cinderella as the mirror, or a romance/matchmaking reading; martyrdom ("never thanked, poor you"); job labels (organiser, fixer); pumpkin, wand and "bibbidi" clichés; midnight once at most.
- **Story test:** (1) someone quietly changed another person's big moment, unasked and uncredited at the time; (2) the other person's moment has to be real and theirs, and the helper absent from it; (3) it has to show a decision made without permission, where it could have been overstepping; (4) what holds: the help did no harm; (5) the cost: they never saw it land, or learned late. Out: cures and remedies (the Healer's), anything handmade for one person as the gift (the Sculptor's), a matchmaking plot.
- **Position direction:** stay once until the moment arrives. See it land. That answers the worry better than doing less. (Not "you're allowed to…".)

## magician-creator · The Genie
- **The one true thing:** they can't do a small favour. Every request comes back bigger, funnier and more than anyone asked for, and all that overflow means nobody ever thinks to ask what *they'd* wish for.
- **Hide / fear / give:** they hide their own wish. It's the thing they'd make if nobody asked (the Creator half: freedom here means their own project, not rest). They fear granting it too literally: doing exactly what was said and missing what was meant. So they listen past the words. What they give: they take your small ask as seriously as a big one.
- **Versus siblings:** the Fairy Godparent works unasked and unseen; the Genie is asked, and arrives loud. The Entertainer does small tricks for anyone; the Genie does a big one for the person who asked. **Versus the Makeover Artist (*Overnight*):** that's transforming what someone already has while keeping what makes it theirs. The Genie makes something out of nothing. **Versus the Visionary (*Down the Line*):** the Visionary's dream is their own and others build on it. The Genie builds other people's, which is the wound. Cross-family: the Mechanic (can fix anything) is fixing; the Genie is overflowing.
- **Traps:** lamp, "three wishes", "phenomenal cosmic power" (a film line: never quote it); "your wish is my command"; party-planner or fixer labels; reading the overflow as people-pleasing (it's joy, not anxiety). The freedom wish must not turn into "rest" (*Off Duty* owns sitting down) or "being looked after" (*Four Shares*).
- **Story test:** (1) a modest request that came back as far more than was asked; (2) the joy of the overflow is on the record, not only its size; (3) the maker's own idea stayed unmade or unasked for, or came much later; (4) a near-miss of "too literal" is welcome, a disaster isn't; (5) what holds: the person who asked was delighted. Out: a drink "made for the staff" (*Still Yours*), a film crew (*Overnight*), a happy accident.
- **Position direction:** make one thing nobody asked for, for yourself, and say out loud what it is.

## magician-regular-guy · The Entertainer
- **The one true thing:** hours of private practice for one minute of a stranger's wonder, and the practice is the secret. It has to look effortless, so nobody ever knows how much they care.
- **Hide / fear / give:** they hide the hours. They fear the flop, the silence where the gasp should be. What they give: for one minute at a bus stop, grown-ups turn back into children, and it's for anyone, strangers included.
- **Versus siblings:** the Illusionist wants your certainty to wobble and stays a mystery; the Entertainer wants the laugh and the connection. The Genie goes big for one person; the Entertainer goes small, for everyone. **Versus the Trickster (*No Accident*):** the Trickster's trick has a point and fears being ignored. The Entertainer's has no point but joy, and fears a fumble, a skill failing in front of people. Never write "ignored". Cross-family: the Elf/Imp also has "a coin behind an ear", but theirs is mischief with a target; the Entertainer's is a gift with no target. **Versus the Photographer (*As It Was*):** keep the "ordinary day" out as the hook. The Entertainer's hook is the practised minute.
- **Traps:** "card-trick nerd" or "party magician" labels; a coin behind the ear as the reading's image (it's in two sheets); showing off as the motive (it's the gasp turning into a laugh).
- **Story test:** (1) a showpiece that looks easy in public and took long private repetition (practised with water, for years, alone); (2) the audience is ordinary people, not an elite; (3) the payoff is shared delight; (4) a fumble, a risk or a failed night is admitted somewhere; (5) what holds: the joy was real and it spread. Out: hoaxes, exclusive or royal audiences, spectacle only for the teller's glory. (Hester to judge: a showman's flaming or thrown pour, if the practice is on the page.)
- **Position direction:** let someone see a fumble. It gets the same laugh the gasp does. Or: let one person see the practice.

## magician-explorer · The Alchemist
- **The one true thing:** they'll try something a hundred times, failing and adjusting in notes nobody else can read, and the only line they won't cross is a step they can't take back. Bold in trying, careful about permanence.
- **Hide / fear / give:** they hide how many attempts failed (the notebook). They fear the irreversible result. What they give: the patient belief that almost anything can become better, and being the one who tries the twelfth time.
- **Versus siblings:** **the Sorcerer's Apprentice** fears a spell they can't *stop* (speed, a beginner). The Alchemist fears a result they can't *undo* (permanence, a veteran). Keep those two apart in every word. **Versus the Experimentalist (*Off-Label*):** never the same thing twice. The Alchemist makes the *same* thing over and over until it turns. **Versus the Researcher (*Serviceable*):** that's public honesty about what failed ("say so"). The Alchemist's notes are private, so the position can't be "share your notes". **Versus the Makeover Artist (*Overnight*):** no clarification or overnight turn, and no happy accident. Cross-family: the Spiritualist transforms the mind; the Alchemist transforms matter.
- **Traps:** lead into gold, the philosopher's stone, the elixir of life (a health claim); Flamel as fact (the sheet says his legend grew after his death; if used at all, it's a legend, signposted); Harry Potter's Flamel; "mad scientist"; a chemist or lab label.
- **Story test:** (1) one formula chased over many attempts, with the failures on the record as the method; (2) private or coded notes, or a closely held formula; (3) a real point of no return: a step that commits it (years in a barrel, a batch distilled, a vintage laid down); (4) what holds: the patience paid, and what it became is better, not just different. Out: accidental discoveries, "secret monk recipe" legends stated as fact, any "elixir" or long-life claim, and anything *Overnight* or *Off-Label* already used.
- **Position direction:** take the step you can't take back. Some things only turn once.

## magician-hero · The Good Wizard
- **The one true thing:** when everyone else is frightened, they're the one who turns the fear into a plan, and they judge their gift only by who it kept safe, never by how impressive it looked.
- **Hide / fear / give:** they hide that the years of learning were years of learning restraint. They fear the plan that fails the person relying on it. What they give: a plan in the room at the moment hope runs out.
- **Versus siblings:** the Omnipotent One plans to control; the Good Wizard plans to protect. The Clairvoyant sees ahead; the Good Wizard steps in. **Versus the Rescuer (*Off Duty*):** speed ("you've already gone"). The Good Wizard is deliberate: the gift aimed, the plan made. **Versus the Healer (*Till Spring*):** carries the ones who didn't mend and is told to "put them down". "The friend they could not save" is in this sheet too, so it may be named but can never be the core or the position. Cross-family: the Superhero fears being loved for the cape; the Good Wizard doesn't care about the cape at all.
- **Traps:** Harry Potter (quotes, "chosen one", houses); saviour grandiosity; a soldier, firefighter or doctor label; a war-hero glamour reading; making the lost friend the headline.
- **Story test:** (1) someone with a rare, hard-earned skill who used it to protect others in a real crisis; (2) what they did was *a plan*, not a lucky save; (3) the skill was learned over years and chosen for others' sake; (4) it cost them something (risk, or reputation), and a loss may be admitted in a clause; (5) what holds: people were safer because of it. Out: medicine and cures (Healer, Doctor), speed and instinct as the point (the Rescuer's), bravery for show.
- **Position direction:** say the plan out loud before it's perfect. Hearing there *is* one is what gives people their nerve back. Keep it clear of *Till Spring*'s "how it lands was never all up to you".

## magician-innocent · The Sorcerer's Apprentice
- **The one true thing:** they start before they're ready, on purpose, because the excitement *is* the point. What scares them isn't failing but succeeding faster than they can steer.
- **Hide / fear / give:** they hide that they're a step ahead of their permission (the borrowed hat). They fear the thing that keeps going once it's started. What they give: the energy that gets things moving, and a hopeful certainty that one day it'll change the world.
- **Versus siblings:** the Alchemist is a patient veteran who fears permanence; the Apprentice is new, in a rush, and fears momentum. The Good Wizard has already learned restraint; the Apprentice is learning it now, mid-flood. **Versus the Experimentalist (*Off-Label*):** confident curiosity. The Apprentice is someone new, with an elder in the picture. **Versus the Trend Setter (*Still Yours*):** "liked it first" is taste; the Apprentice's is ambition. Cross-family: the Dreamer is hurt by being scolded for hoping; the Apprentice fears the flood, not the telling-off. The master should come back and *not* take the magic away.
- **Traps:** **age.** "Fresh-faced" and "young" are out: the guest may be sixty and new at something. Mickey and Disney (the older poem can be a touchstone, never quoted at length); an "apprentice" or trainee label; turning the mishap into shame or a lesson lecture.
- **Story test:** (1) someone new at something who tried an ambitious thing before being fully trained or allowed; (2) it ran away from them in scale (it spread, multiplied, got out of hand); (3) an elder or mentor is in the story; (4) the cost is a visible mess, never harm to anyone; (5) what holds: they learned, and the joy survived. Out: disasters with injury, stories that end in disgrace, prodigy-genius stories.
- **Position direction:** keep starting early. Learn the word that stops it as eagerly as the one that starts it, or call the master in before the water rises.

## magician-jester · The Illusionist
- **The one true thing:** they fool you in plain sight, with your permission. You know it's a trick and you still can't see it. And they like being a little unsolvable themselves.
- **Hide / fear / give:** they hide that being fully figured out would feel like the end of them being interesting. They fear no surprises left. What they give: people leave seeing a little differently, and it lasts longer than the laugh.
- **Versus siblings:** **the Trickster (*No Accident*)** fools experts in the dark with an invented story, and the point is exposure. The Illusionist hides nothing and lies about nothing; the puzzle is out in the open. So no hoax, no fake history. The Entertainer wants the gasp-into-laugh; the Illusionist wants the wobble and the second look. **Versus the Surrealist (*The Other Berry*):** "made like X, with no X" is taken. A drink that is secretly something else is not this row. Cross-family: the Charmer hides one card out of fear of being left; the Illusionist hides for the fun of it. So the position can't be "let someone see all of you" (that's the Charmer's wound). The Elf/Imp pranks; the Illusionist puzzles.
- **Traps:** "nothing is as it seems" (a horoscope line); Escher as the mirror; optical-illusion gimmicks as the whole drink; "mysterious", "enigma", "a riddle wrapped in…".
- **Story test:** (1) something that openly fools the senses even once you know how it works (a layer that shouldn't hold, a colour or clarity that looks impossible, a shape that reads two ways); (2) the maker's delight in the double take is on the record, or can be signposted; (3) knowing how it works leaves the wonder intact; (4) what holds: completely honest, and nobody was deceived about what it is. Out: hoaxes and invented pasts, secret substitutions, deceiving experts.
- **Position direction:** show one person how it's done and watch them still be amazed. Being understood isn't the end of the surprise.

## magician-lover · The Siren
- **The one true thing:** they know exactly what effect they have on a room and have made themselves responsible for it. The pull isn't the interesting part. The care is.
- **Hide / fear / give:** they hide how often they turn it down on purpose. They fear someone falling too far, changing course for them, and getting hurt. What they give: when they aim it well, people lean in and become bolder and more alive for an evening.
- **Versus siblings:** the Illusionist plays with what you see; the Siren changes what you feel, and knows it. The Omnipotent One's power is structure; the Siren's is presence. **Versus the Obsessed (*Rosetta*):** the Obsessed is the one who falls; the Siren is the one people fall towards. Cross-family: **the Charmer** draws people in by holding back and fears being left. The Siren isn't hiding anything: the risk runs the other way, to *them*. **Versus the Trickster:** no deception at all.
- **Traps (the heaviest in the family):** a sexual, seductive or femme-fatale reading; looks, bodies, "beauty"; the myth's sailors dying (harm as romance); anything that reads the guest as a woman; "dangerous" as a compliment. Allure means presence, the air in a room. Drinks: absinthe and "the green fairy" (*Rosetta*); cherry-red clichés.
- **Story test:** (1) a drink, ingredient or person whose pull on people was real and could be ruinous; (2) someone handled that pull with care (a rule, a measure, a warning, a limit set on purpose); (3) the handling is the character, not the pull; (4) what holds: well handled, it did more good than harm, and people were better for it. Out: absinthe and the louche, addiction and health harm as the mirror, seduction or romance plots.
- **Position direction:** aim it on purpose. Use the pull to bring in the one person at the edge of the room. (Not "don't apologise"; not "turn it down".)

## magician-ruler · The Omnipotent One
- **The one true thing:** they carry the whole map so everyone else can enjoy the day without one. What feels like magic to the others is months of structure nobody sees, and they're watching every piece while the others are having fun.
- **Hide / fear / give:** they hide the vigilance (they rarely get to be a guest at their own plan). They fear the one piece that moves on its own. What they give: other people get to feel carried, the "it just worked".
- **Versus siblings:** the Fairy Godparent does one person's moment, unseen; the Omnipotent One does the whole picture for everyone. The Clairvoyant foresees; the Omnipotent One arranges. **Versus the Auteur (*Making the Calls*):** authorship, credit and blame for a creative work, and "let someone else make the second" is taken. The Omnipotent One's is design and logistics for others' benefit. **Versus the Nanny (*Fair Measure*):** limits and fairness so the fun lasts. This row isn't about limits. It's about a world built whole. Cross-family: the Legend holds a country in a crisis; the Omnipotent One builds the ordinary day to perfection.
- **Traps:** God, the Creation, the Sistine ceiling as the mirror (never cast the guest as divine); a "control freak" diagnosis; project-manager or event-planner labels; "omnipotent" puns.
- **Story test:** (1) someone who designed a whole system (a bar, a menu, an event, a way of serving) with every piece planned; (2) the scale of the unseen planning is on the record; (3) others experienced it as effortless; (4) one piece moved on its own (a person, a guest, a change) and it held, or went well; (5) the cost: the planner watching, not enjoying. Out: sole creative credit (the Auteur's), rules for safety (the Nanny's), a happy accident as the payoff (*Overnight*'s).
- **Position direction:** some of the pieces are people. When one moves on its own, let it, and be a guest for an hour. (Frame it as a person surprising them, not "the part you didn't mean".)

## magician-sage · The Clairvoyant
- **The one true thing:** they see how it'll turn out long before they can prove it, because they've noticed small things nobody else did. Their tenderest foresight is about people: they see a part still to play in someone everyone else has written off.
- **Hide / fear / give:** they hide how much of their calm is lying awake running it forward. They fear the thing they didn't see coming. What they give: advice that sounds strange now and obvious later, and a refusal to write anyone off.
- **Versus siblings:** the Omnipotent One arranges; the Clairvoyant understands and nudges. The Good Wizard steps in; the Clairvoyant sees in advance. The Trickster's whoYouAre already has "a plan three moves ahead", so never write "moves ahead". **Versus the Doctor (*Instead*):** diagnostic questions ("since when?"), and its close is "tell someone what they haven't heard of yet". So "say it plainly, sooner" needs care. **Versus the Healer (*Till Spring*):** "believes they'll be all right before they do" is about recovery. The Clairvoyant's is about a *role*: someone will matter. Cross-family: the Game-Changer turns knowledge into a revolution at scale. **Versus the Trend Setter (*Still Yours*):** "liked it first" is taste, not foresight.
- **Traps:** a psychic, fortune-teller or mystic label; crystal balls; "knows what you're thinking" (it reads as invasive); Gandalf and Tolkien quotes; a smug "I told you so".
- **Story test:** (1) someone saw early what a drink, ingredient or person would become, from small signs, while others dismissed it; (2) the signs themselves are on the record, not just the result; (3) there was a wait with no proof; (4) the foresight was right, but not complete: one thing they didn't foresee, owned; (5) best of all, it was about a person others had written off. Out: prophecy legends, hindsight myths told as foresight, a trend-spotting story, quality alone.
- **Position direction:** keep a door open for the one you still see something in, even when you can't prove it yet. (A second option, "forgive the one you didn't see", sits too close to *Till Spring*; avoid it.)
