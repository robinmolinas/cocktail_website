# Regular Guy family: Wren's sketches (2026-09-30)

All eleven pairings are unauthored. Every persona below was read fresh from today's `brief.py` (Robin's rewritten sheet). These are starting points: each room tests its row against my round-1 read and can overturn it.

## The ground the whole family shares (it belongs to no single sibling)

- **Primary drives:** belonging, altruism, respect, fairness. **Primary fears:** exclusion, discourtesy, *individuality* (standing out from your own people). Every sibling carries these. "One of us", "nothing fancy", "everyone's welcome", "the local", "down to earth" are family-wide colour and can never be one sibling's one true thing.
- **The modesty shrug is already taken twice.** The Photographer has "it's nothing, just a picture… would feel like showing off". The Firefighter's sheet (hero-regular-guy) has "anyone would have done the same". No sibling here gets a shrug as its recognition.
- **Under-receiving is taken:** True Friend ("You're allowed to be looked after too", "keep one cup back"), Rescuer ("never let anyone come out for you"), Saviour's sheet ("finds it very hard to be saved"). No position in this family should be "let someone look after you".

## Tests that apply to every row

1. **True and on the page.** The act at the heart of the mirror, and any motive it rests on, is sourced. "I like to think" can colour the story but can't carry it.
2. **An ordinary person the guest can stand next to.** No billionaires, celebrities, politicians or saints. Ingredient and drink histories are welcome (a humble bottle can be the person).
3. **The drink comes out of the story.** No bridging ingredient. If the story's drink is famous, the reading says so.
4. **Out for every row:** health, medicine and cures (closed studio-wide, and folk wisdom and caregiving both pull toward them); terror, war and disaster; any person or story already in the registry (list above); the sheet's broken examples (*Attentats de Londres*, the hitman film *The Mechanic*, "Jo the Plumber", Brin & Page). Fiction is never the mirror: a sheet example (Billy Elliot, Huck Finn, *Wedding Crashers*, Jim Halpert, Forrest Gump) may appear at most once, as the one touchstone from outside the bar.
5. **Nobody is laughed at.** This family fears discourtesy. A story that makes its ordinary person the butt of the joke fails every row.
6. **Gender-free recognition.** The story's person can be anyone, and the recognition must never depend on the guest matching them.

## regular-guy-caregiver · The Good Samaritan
- **One true thing:** what they can't bear is a crowd watching someone be alone. A stranger struggling in plain sight while everyone waits for someone else: they're the one who crosses the road. The trigger is the aloneness, not the danger.
- **Hides:** that they aren't fearless. Every time there are two seconds of "is it my place?" (the family's discourtesy fear), and they go anyway. **Fears:** turning into someone who walks past, and a world where nobody stops. **Gives:** a stranger stops being a stranger. And once one person moves, others do too (the sheet's "ripples").
- **Differs from:** Nurse (people in the room, no favourites; this one is about people outside every circle). Rescuer (emergencies and clarity; this one is small and ordinary, and awkward). Angel (calm brought into a room). True Friend (friends, staying). Have A Go (a fight, not a kindness). Saviour (self-sacrifice).
- **Traps:** a helping-profession label; "hero"; the shrug; a position of "let someone help you" (taken three times); "What you can't stand…" is The Other Berry's frame, so the words must differ.
- **Story test:** the story's person helps a **stranger** who had no claim on them, and it wasn't their job or their customer. Others were there or passed by, or the stranger was plainly on their own. It **costs** something small and real (time, money, a plan, some dignity), and they sought no credit. It **held**: the source shows the help helped, and ideally someone followed or the habit spread. **Out:** emergencies and rescues (Rescuer), warming arriving travellers with a hot drink (Nurse's Foynes), official hospitality (Kir), philanthropists and galas, anything medical.
- **Line that might land:** "You weren't brave. You just couldn't leave them on their own in front of everyone."

## regular-guy-creator · The Hidden Talent
- **One true thing:** the gift doesn't match where they come from, and it's not the world's applause they want. They want their own people in the front row. (Billy Elliot's final scene is his dad in the audience, not the critics.)
- **Hides:** the gift itself, or how much it matters, from the people they belong to. Being good at it could sound like "who do you think you are?" **Fears:** that showing it will make them one of *them* and not one of *us* (individuality meets exclusion). **Gives:** proof that it can come from anywhere. When they show it, they bring their people with them.
- **Differs from:** Photographer (keeps the ordinary; the shrug is theirs). Obsessed (outsider in love with the making; Breaux's story is taken). Sculptor (made for one person; Ada Coleman taken). Everyman (knowledge, not a gift). Visionary (a dream for others to build).
- **Traps:** praise ("you're a genius") is flattery, and many guests won't believe they have a talent. Frame it as "the part of you that doesn't fit what people expect of you". No class condescension ("even a humble…"). No shrug.
- **Story test:** the story's person was **ordinary in their world's eyes** (a job, a place or a background nobody linked to this gift) and had a **real, documented** gift. It was **unseen, doubted or kept private** at first, and there's a sourced moment it's **seen**. Best of all, the people they came from see it, or they bring it back home. It **costs** the risk of seeming to get above themselves, or years in private. **Out:** rags-to-riches fame as the point, genius myths, a gift for drinking, and the stories of Coleman, Breaux, Ensslin and Bermejo.
- **Line that might land:** "Strangers clapping was never the point. You wanted the people you grew up with to see it."

## regular-guy-explorer · The Entrepreneur
- **One true thing:** they'd rather struggle at their own thing than do well at someone else's. And what they start is a place for people like them, the thing they looked for and couldn't find.
- **Hides:** the loneliness of the start. Everyone hears "it's going great". **Fears:** that striking out means belonging nowhere: the old crowd thinks they've gone, and the new one was never theirs. Being boxed in, answering to someone (the Explorer's side). **Gives:** somewhere that didn't exist before, where others now belong. They left so they could build a place to belong.
- **Differs from:** Visionary (a dream bigger than one life that others build on; this is small, now, and every job theirs). Auteur (control of every detail). Tourist (keeps the return ticket; this one tore it up). Trend Setter (taste first).
- **Traps:** "entrepreneur" and "business owner" are profession labels, so a guest who never started a company must still see themselves (a club, a stall, a move, the first one to leave town). No money as the happy ending. The sheet's billionaire example is never used.
- **Story test:** the story's person **started something from nothing**: little money, no backing, no permission, leaving a safer path. The source shows **when, where and with what**, and that it was a risk (doubters, lost security). It was made for **ordinary people**, not the rich. It **held**: it lasted and was used. Best if they kept their people close (first staff, neighbours, first customers). **Out:** tech and billionaire founders, garage legends, luxury brands, buyouts as the ending, and Bermejo, Saunders, Ramos and Ransom.
- **Line that might land:** "You didn't leave to be on your own. You left to build somewhere the rest of them could come."

## regular-guy-hero · The Have A Go Hero
- **One true thing:** they don't wait to be qualified. A problem too big for someone like them, and they get stuck in anyway, with more enthusiasm than expertise.
- **Hides:** that they feel out of their depth nearly every time. They move fast partly because thinking too long would stop them. **Fears:** failing in public, being told "who asked you?" and "leave it to the professionals" (the Hero's fear of looking incompetent meets the family's fear of standing out). **Gives:** permission. Once one ordinary person has a go, everyone else is allowed to.
- **Differs from:** Rescuer ("you don't feel brave, you feel clear"; this one doesn't feel clear and goes anyway). Firefighter (trained, a crew; this one is untrained and alone). Campaigner (argues and writes; this one does, with their hands, time and money). Samaritan (one stranger, one kindness).
- **Traps:** "hero" as praise; tackling criminals or violence; celebrity crusades (the sheet's Jamie Oliver is never the example).
- **Story test:** the story's person had **no title, training or authority** for what they took on. They took on a problem **bigger than themselves**, practically rather than by argument, and were **doubted or laughed at**. They **struggled or failed at least once and kept going**. The source shows the **result**, something changed for many. **Out:** pamphlets, speeches and fraud campaigns (Campaigner, Doctor), rescues (Rescuer, Firefighter), official experts, temperance or health crusades.
- **Line that might land:** "Nobody asked you. That's never once stopped you."

## regular-guy-innocent · The Bumpkin
- **One true thing:** they still say "wow" out loud. They enjoy things without checking first whether they're supposed to: the view, the first warm day, a plain thing done well.
- **Hides:** the wince when someone smiles at their delight as if it were childish, and the times they've dimmed it to fit in with the knowing crowd. **Fears:** being taken for simple, which is exclusion by the clever. Getting told off (the Innocent's fear of punishment). **Gives:** people rediscover how to enjoy things plainly around them.
- **Differs from:** True Friend (the reverse twin: believes the best, forgives, stays; none of that here). Photographer (keeps the ordinary; this one *savours* it, out loud). Minimalist (takes away). Everyman (advice, not delight).
- **Traps:** the name is a put-down on the sheet (the personality lint says DECIDE). The reading must never make the guest feel mocked: never "simple", "naive" or "country" about the guest, and the name appears only as the tag. No moonshine or hillbilly stereotypes, and no purity or health claims.
- **Story test:** the story's person, drink or ingredient is **plain, rural or unfashionable**, and **looked down on by the fashionable**. The story vindicates the simple pleasure **as simple**, not as "secretly sophisticated all along". Joy is visible, rooted in nature or the seasons. The person is **never the butt of the joke**. Optional: a small, honest "the emperor has no clothes" moment. **Out:** moonshine, the Photographer's kept-record ground, the Minimalist's "stripped back", Burgundy cassis (Angel).
- **Line that might land:** "You never learned to act less pleased than you are. Please don't."

## regular-guy-jester · The Prankster
- **One true thing:** every good prank they've pulled ended with the target telling the story. The joke is how they make an "us", and being in on it is the point.
- **Hides:** that they know exactly where the line is because they once crossed it, and it still stings. **Fears:** the joke that hurts instead of includes (the family's discourtesy fear), and being on the outside of the plan. They'd rather gatecrash than wait to be invited (*Wedding Crashers*). **Gives:** a room of strangers becomes a gang with a shared "remember when…".
- **Differs from:** Reassurer (a joke timed for someone's worst day; comfort). Stand-up (words, noticing; this is physical and raucous). Trickster (rule broken on purpose, a story people rally behind, fear of being ignored; that fear is taken). Scoundrel (cheek toward rules).
- **Traps:** "What scares you… being ignored" is the Trickster's line. No cruelty, nothing punching down, no drunken chaos as the joke.
- **Story test:** a **documented practical joke, stunt or piece of mischief** (who, when, what), raucous and down to earth. The target and the onlookers **ended up in on it**, and the group came out **closer**. Best if a line visibly wasn't crossed. **Out:** cons for gain, invented origin stories and hoaxes on the public (the Trickster's Seelbach), humiliation, a victim who was vulnerable, a bar legend told as fact.
- **Line that might land:** "Nobody's ever stayed cross with you. That isn't luck. You only ever pick on the ones you love."

## regular-guy-lover · The One Next Door
- **One true thing:** everyone is glad when they're there, and what they'd like, once, is to be someone's first choice, not their safe one.
- **Hides:** the wanting. Being easy to be around is how they belong, and asking for more feels like standing out. **Fears:** being liked by everyone and chosen by no one (loneliness meets exclusion). Being the familiar, taken-for-granted option. **Gives:** home. People relax with them in a way they can't anywhere else ("familiarity and safety").
- **Differs from:** Rough Diamond (the reverse twin can't say it and fears losing someone for that; this one's issue is being taken for granted, not being unable to speak). Understanding Spouse (sees the worst and stays). True Friend (forgives, stays). Nurse (the same you for everyone).
- **Traps:** the reading must never require a romance or a partner (first choice of a friend, a team or a family counts). The source text still says "Boy/Girl Next Door": gendered, and never echoed. No seduction or sensuality. A position of "say it" edges onto the Rough Diamond, so look elsewhere (e.g. let people see what you want; don't always be the easy yes).
- **Story test:** the story's person, drink or ingredient is the **familiar, everyday one**, always there and taken for granted. A source shows it **chosen or loved precisely for that**, over a showier rival, or a steady, quiet affection kept over years that was finally returned. **Warmth, not drama.** **Out:** grand passion, jealousy, tragedy, unrequited endings, marriage as the frame (Twain and Livy are the Spouse's), the Obsessed's "falling for" things.
- **Line that might land:** "People feel at home with you. You'd just like, now and then, to be the one they came for."

## regular-guy-magician · The Mechanic
- **One true thing:** nothing is written off with them. Where others see scrap they see "not yet", and they'll make it go again with whatever's to hand.
- **Hides:** how big the pleasure is when a dead thing hums back to life, bigger than any thanks. And the quiet recheck the next day. **Fears:** the fix that fails later (the Magician's fear of unintended results), and giving up on something that could have been saved. The family's exclusion fear lives here too: nothing, and nobody, should be thrown out. **Gives:** second lives. The thing you loved, back.
- **Differs from:** Craftsman (a way of doing things as care; "this is the way I know how to look after you" is taken). Makeover Artist (changes how it looks, keeps what's theirs). Healer (people and belief). Entertainer (tricks for wonder). People's Champion (leads people in a crisis; the Mechanic fixes things).
- **Traps:** trade labels (mechanic, engineer, handyman); the hitman film on the sheet; "magic" without the how. Fixing is not curing.
- **Story test:** the story's person **made something work again, or at all, with what was at hand** (the wrong parts, a makeshift), where others had given it up. The source shows **how**, so the magic is explained. The fix **held**, and ordinary people used it, and the maker's delight shows. **Out:** cures and remedies, glamour inventions and patents, lab kit (the drink can't need it), Arnold's milk wash, Priestley's bowl over the beer vat, Uyeda's method.
- **Line that might land:** "You've never once believed something was finished just because someone said so."

## regular-guy-outlaw · The Scoundrel
- **One true thing:** they don't want to get away with it. They want to be *let off*. They bend the rule in plain sight, grinning, because being forgiven is proof they're loved.
- **Hides:** that they count on the charm and watch it closely. They always know how far it'll stretch. **Fears:** the day the grin stops working and the door stays shut (exclusion), and being tamed into someone who only asks permission (the Outlaw's fear of restriction). **Gives:** they make rules feel human. The room breathes out, and nobody resents them for it.
- **Differs from:** Gangster (the reverse twin owns "a code, not the law", loyalty to the crew, Capone; stay off codes and crews). Trickster (never by accident, a trick with a reason, fear of being ignored). Prankster (a shared joke that makes an "us"). Campaigner (argues against the rule).
- **Traps:** "lovable rogue", "lad" and "cheeky chappie" are gendered. No violence and no organised crime. Never praise harm. No invented stories (the Trickster's).
- **Story test:** a **sourced rogue** who **openly bent official rules** (a licence, a law, a customs line, house rules) with cheek, and whom **ordinary people and even the authorities let off or indulged** because they liked them. The charm is documented (an anecdote, a quote), not just a rap sheet. **Nobody ordinary was hurt.** **Out:** bootleg empires and gangsters, cons on ordinary people, invented origins, protest campaigns, drunkenness glorified.
- **Line that might land:** "You've never been sneaky about it. You wanted to be caught, and let off."

## regular-guy-ruler · The People's Champion
- **One true thing:** on ordinary days they let the loud ones talk. When it goes wrong, everyone looks at them, and they take charge. Then they give the room back.
- **Hides:** that they don't want to be in charge; leading every day would set them apart from the people they belong with. And a small sting when the loud ones take the credit afterwards. **Fears:** chaos with nobody competent at the wheel, and failing the one time it counts (the Ruler's fears). **Gives:** calm. People breathe because they're there, and afterwards they share the credit.
- **Differs from:** Firefighter (training, courage, a crew, "not good enough on the day"; keep off training and readiness). Rescuer (instinct, going out to one person). People's Representative (the reverse twin: power worn with belonging, symbolic gestures). Mechanic (fixes things, not rooms). Auteur (wants the calls every day).
- **Traps:** trade labels (the sheet's plumber); politicians and campaign props; "quiet hero" as praise; the Rescuer's quiet weeks.
- **Story test:** the story's person had **real, hard-won expertise** and was **unnoticed or talked over in ordinary times**. In a **decisive moment** (not medical) they **took the lead calmly** and it worked. Then they **stepped back and shared the credit**. The source shows both the ordinary obscurity and the moment. **Out:** politicians, self-promoters, war heroics, emergencies of rescue, the sheet's "Jo the Plumber".
- **Line that might land:** "You never fight to be heard. You don't have to. When it matters, the room goes quiet and turns to you."

## regular-guy-sage · The Everyman
- **One true thing:** half of what they tell people, they heard from someone who's gone. Their wisdom is handed down (a grandparent's saying, a neighbour's rule of thumb) and they carry those voices ("Mama always said…" is the sheet's own example).
- **Hides:** that they go quiet in rooms full of qualified people, even when they know better. **Fears:** that it ends with them and the chain breaks (exclusion across time), and being written off as old-fashioned. **Gives:** people bring them the complicated thing and leave with something plain they can use tomorrow. And by quoting the people who taught them, they keep those people in the room.
- **Differs from:** The Dude (the reverse twin owns "learned by living", stay easy, don't take the bait). Doctor ("since when?", "because you know to ask from experience"). Old Master (revises their own work). Connoisseur (truth versus legend). Bumpkin (delight, not advice).
- **Traps:** the name collides with the branding's own word for the whole archetype, so it's the tag only, never used as a description. Never show them as simple (no Gump IQ). "Old wives' tales" pull straight to remedies: health is out.
- **Story test:** a piece of **handed-down know-how** (a family recipe, a rule of thumb, a saying, a method) that passed **person to person, not taught formally or written down**, kept by ordinary people, and that **outlasted the fashions or proved right**. The source shows the **handing down** (from whom, to whom). **Out:** folk medicine of any kind, superstition sold as truth, gloating that the experts were fools, Jerry Thomas's revisions, the Dude's "keep it real".
- **Line that might land:** "Most of what you know, nobody sat you down and taught you. You just kept it, and now you're the one people ask."
