# Wren's sketches: Outlaw family (2026-09-30)

All eleven are unauthored. Existing ground to differ from: the three pours with this family as their **secondary**: the Campaigner (*Not Too Polite*: the voice that goes first against unfairness, hands shaking after, "count the ones you've won"; Heyrick, the sugar boycott), the Trickster (*No Accident*: sees the strings, tugs them, truest things said as jokes, fears being ignored; the Seelbach's invented story), the Trend Setter (*Still Yours*: likes it first and keeps it when everyone arrives; Cecchini's Cosmopolitan). The nearest caregiver pours: the Rescuer (moves first in a crisis; the St Bernard), the Reassurer (the joke that comforts at the right moment), the Nanny (grog, 1740, navy rum). Personas read fresh from the brief (Robin's rewrite).

## Family-wide, before any row

**All eleven fear powerlessness.** The sheet gives each its own version, and that version is where each reading's distinctness starts:

| pairing | what powerlessness looks like to them |
| --- | --- |
| Gentle Giant | unable to protect someone smaller |
| Rule Breaker | being bought, framed and hung on the establishment's wall |
| Gangster | being pushed around by a system that never worked for people like them |
| Thrill Seeker | the padded, sensible life where nothing happens |
| Maverick | being told to stand by while something unfair happens |
| Adolescent | growing up into someone they'd despise |
| Subverter | being silenced, the joke taken away |
| Fugitive | getting caught, pinned down, made to settle |
| Disrupter | the slow decay of things nobody dares touch |
| Leading Edge | losing the wheel: it runs off without them, or gets tamed |
| Devil's Advocate | the room where everyone nods |

**How they differ at a glance (what each one works on):** the Giant holds back their own power; the Maverick acts for strangers and leaves; the Gangster stays and looks after their own; the Rule Breaker makes a thing; the Adolescent guards a self; the Subverter jokes; the Devil's Advocate argues; the Disrupter breaks a situation so it can be rebuilt; the Leading Edge organises other people's rebellion; the Thrill Seeker runs toward danger with friends; the Fugitive runs away with someone.

**Family traps (apply to every row):**
- **Never make the guest a criminal.** Several examples are real criminals (Capone, Bonnie and Clyde) or real tragedies (Peaches Geldof died of an overdose; Jackass has injuries). No violence, no killing, no drugs, no deaths, no injury as a punchline, and no glamour of crime as the gift. The reading honours the code, the nerve or the love, never the crime.
- **No alcohol-as-rebellion.** No drinking dares, no "strong because you're bold", nothing that ties risk or defiance to the drink itself. The guest reads, not drinks.
- **Vocabulary the whole family will reach for:** "rules", "break", "rebel", "wild", "fire". None in a title block without a swap test. The Trickster owns "You never break a rule by accident"; the Campaigner owns "Someone had to say it" and the voice that goes first; the Trend Setter owns "liked it first" and the eye-rolling.
- **Story magnets (at most one pairing each, and say which):** Prohibition, bootlegging and speakeasies (natural home: the Gangster); a banned drink (absinthe is already Rosetta's base and "disreputable" cloud); smuggling and rum-running; navy rum and grog (the Nanny's); an invented origin story (the Trickster's); "made for the staff, then it spread" (the Trend Setter's); a boycott or pamphlet (the Campaigner's); punk and the King's Road (the Leading Edge's own sheet example, so best left to it or to nobody).
- **Religion:** Shiva (Disrupter) and the Church office (Devil's Advocate) are the sheet's own examples. Respectful, never a pun, and the story needn't be religious at all.
- **Gender:** no "big man", no couple assumed to be a man and a woman, no bad-boy/bad-girl framings.

## outlaw-caregiver · The Gentle Giant
- **The one true thing:** they know exactly how much they could do, and they spend their whole life doing it softly. The gentleness isn't weakness; it's a strength kept on a short lead, on purpose, every day.
- **Hide:** how aware they are of their own effect: the voice they lower, the way they sit down to be at eye level, the first impression they have to undo. **Fear:** the moment the small one needs protecting and they can't. **Give:** the small feel safe near them, and bullies go quiet without a word said.
- **Siblings:** the Maverick acts and vanishes; the Giant stays and stands there. The Gangster protects their own; the Giant breaks with their own kind (the other strong ones) for anyone small. The Rescuer (caregiver-hero) is the crisis hour; the Giant is the ordinary week, the presence that makes the crisis less likely. The Campaigner speaks first; the Giant rarely needs to speak.
- **Traps:** literal body size (strength can be rank, reputation, temper, a voice); gendered "big man"; the BFG pun on "giant"; "soft touch" as a compliment. Never write "you're gentle"; show what they do with their hands.
- **Story test:** the story's person must (1) have real power over someone smaller: size, rank, strength, a reputation that frightens; (2) choose gentleness when force was expected or easier; (3) break with their own kind to do it, and pay something for it (standing apart, mockery, a lost place among the strong); (4) protect without shouting: the holding back must be visible. **Out:** crisis rescues (the Rescuer's), animals rescuing people (Barry), medicine and cures, children in danger or harmed, any violence even "justified".
- **Position direction:** not "be gentle with yourself" (Barnum). Maybe: they can let someone see the strength too; the small ones feel safer knowing it's there.

## outlaw-creator · The Rule Breaker
- **The one true thing:** they make their best work where they weren't allowed to, and they're quietly afraid that without a wall to push against they wouldn't know what to make.
- **Hide:** that being welcomed tempts them, and they distrust themselves for wanting it. **Fear:** being bought, framed, made official, the work tamed into decoration. **Give:** something better left in the crack; the proof that permission was never the thing that made it good.
- **Siblings:** the Trend Setter (creator-outlaw) is taste, not making, and its position is "keep it when everyone loves it": **the Rule Breaker must not end there.** The Adolescent guards a self, the Rule Breaker makes an object. The Subverter's material is laughter, the Rule Breaker's is the thing itself. The Disrupter breaks so something can be rebuilt; the Rule Breaker breaks in order to make. The Experimentalist and the Surrealist (creator family) make freely; this one makes against.
- **Traps:** vandalism as virtue; anonymity as a secret (secrets are out); Banksy's shredded painting (the sheet's example, and famous enough to feel borrowed); "street" or "stencil" aesthetics in words.
- **Story test:** the story's person must (1) make something without permission, against a rule or on ground that wasn't theirs; (2) have it turn out good, better than what was allowed; (3) then face the establishment trying to adopt it: bought, licensed, made the official version; (4) and the story must show how they answered that. It must be a making, not only a breaking. **Out:** the absinthe ban (Rosetta), the invented origin (Trickster), "made for the staff" (Trend Setter), destruction for its own sake.
- **Position direction:** a hard truth, kindly: make one thing that isn't against anything, and see that it's still theirs.

## outlaw-regular-guy · The Gangster
- **The one true thing:** their word is the only contract they trust, and they've never forgiven anyone who treated their people as if they didn't count.
- **Hide:** that the distrust of the official way is learned, not chosen: they watched it fail people like them. **Fear:** being pushed around, disrespected, made small by people with forms and titles. **Give:** fierce, practical loyalty. Their people eat, get work, get a phone call back. Nobody on their side is left to the system.
- **Siblings:** the Maverick serves strangers and disappears; the Gangster serves their own and stays on the corner. The Gentle Giant protects anyone small; the Gangster draws a clear line round "ours". The Nurse (caregiver) gives everyone the same; the True Friend brings everyone in; the Gangster has an inside and an outside, and means it. The Campaigner speaks for strangers; the Gangster acts for the family.
- **Traps:** calling the guest a criminal, a boss, a godfather; money and intimidation as the gift; Capone (murders) as a hero. If Capone is used at all, only a sourced fact, with the dark granted in a clause. "Crew" and "family" as job-title labels.
- **Story test:** the story's person must (1) live by a code outside the official rules; (2) look after a specific community (a street, a trade, a family, a regular crowd) that the official world failed; (3) be seen by those people as one of their own; (4) keep the code when it cost them. The mirror is the loyalty and the code, never the crime. **Out:** violence, killings, glamour of money or menace. **This is the natural home of Prohibition** if the family uses it at all; claim it here or nowhere.
- **Position direction:** maybe let one outsider earn a place; or a rule that could work for them, not only against.

## outlaw-explorer · The Thrill Seeker
- **The one true thing:** they aren't fearless. They're scared every time, and the fear is how they know they're alive. The padded life frightens them more than the drop.
- **Hide:** the fear itself, under the laughing; and that it isn't showing off, it's refusing to let life get decided for them. **Fear:** the sensible, cushioned life where nothing happens and nothing is theirs. **Give:** they make other people braver; the story everyone tells for years started with them going first, laughing.
- **Siblings:** the Rescuer runs toward danger for someone else; the Thrill Seeker runs toward it to feel it. The Fugitive runs away with someone; the Thrill Seeker runs toward something with friends. The Maverick takes risks for fairness, this one for aliveness. (Watch the parallel Explorer family: discovery and freedom are theirs; the Thrill Seeker's is the jolt, not the map.)
- **Traps:** injury as a punchline, danger glamorised, drinking dares, anything a guest could read as "do something reckless tonight". "Adrenaline junkie" as a label. Never tell them to be careful (patronising) or to go further (irresponsible).
- **Story test:** the story's person must (1) do something others called reckless, for the feeling of it, not for money or a cause; (2) do it with others watching or laughing along: the thrill is shared; (3) be visibly scared and do it anyway; (4) pay a cost that's bruises, ridicule or money, never death or lasting harm; the story must end in laughter or survival, not tragedy. The sensible alternative should be visible in the story. **Out:** fatal stunts, injuries, drink-driving, medicine.
- **Position direction:** tell them you were scared; the story's better told that way.

## outlaw-hero · The Maverick
- **The one true thing:** they'd rather get the fair result the wrong way than lose it the right way, and they leave before anyone can thank them, because staying would mean answering for how.
- **Hide:** that the vanishing isn't modesty; being thanked makes them feel owned, pinned to the system they went round. And that it's lonely out there. **Fear:** being told to stand by while something unfair happens. **Give:** results. The unfair thing actually gets fixed, while the rest are still asking permission.
- **Siblings:** the Campaigner (caregiver-outlaw) speaks up first; the Maverick doesn't argue, it acts. The Rescuer moves first in a crisis; the Maverick moves against an order, often without a crisis. The Gangster stays and serves their own; the Maverick serves strangers and disappears. The Gentle Giant stays and stands; the Maverick goes. **Watch the family-wide "let yourself receive" position** (Four Shares, Off Duty): "stay for the thank-you" is close; find another angle or own the kinship in one clause.
- **Traps:** Robin Hood's arrows and forest as words; vigilante violence; theft as virtue; "rogue", "loose cannon" labels; the "hero" pun (archetype name).
- **Story test:** the story's person must (1) disobey an order or rule (2) to get a fair result for other people, not themselves; (3) it must actually work, the results are the point; (4) and they must not stay for the credit: unthanked, uncredited or gone. The cost of disobeying should show. **Out:** speeches and pamphlets (Campaigner), crisis rescues (Rescuer), violence, stealing for gain.
- **Position direction:** candidates: let one person know it was them; or tell someone how they did it, so it can be done again.

## outlaw-innocent · The Adolescent
- **The one true thing:** under all the noise is a promise they made to themselves early on, that life should be more honest than this, and they're still keeping it.
- **Hide:** how earnest they are; they care so much about what's real that it embarrasses them, so it comes out as a slammed door. **Fear:** growing into someone their younger self would despise. **Give:** they call out what's fake, and everyone else feels allowed to be honest too.
- **Siblings:** the Rule Breaker guards a work, the Adolescent guards a self. The Subverter jokes; the Adolescent means it, loudly. The Devil's Advocate argues on purpose; the Adolescent rebels on instinct, from a smell of falseness. The Trend Setter has taste; the Adolescent has a conscience.
- **Traps:** **the guest may be fifty.** Never call them young, never "a phase", never "you'll grow out of it", never condescend. Peaches Geldof's life (drugs, death, grief) is entirely out. No teenage mental health, no family trauma. Hair dye and slammed doors are the sheet's images, not facts about the guest.
- **Story test:** the story's person must (1) be young or at the start, and refuse what the grown-ups offered because it was false; (2) be right about something honest; (3) and the story must show whether that honesty held later, so the guest sees the promise kept (or tested). It's about refusing to become, not about wrecking. **Out:** drugs, overdose, death, a sneer at adults as the gift, punk and the King's Road (the Leading Edge's).
- **Position direction:** affirmation with an edge: check yourself against the younger one now and then, and notice you're still keeping the promise.

## outlaw-jester · The Subverter
- **The one true thing:** they don't mind the room going cold. What they couldn't stand is the day nobody lets them make the joke.
- **Hide:** that they privately check who laughed and who didn't, and dread the joke that lands on someone smaller. They're the most serious person in the room about what a joke is for. **Fear:** being silenced. **Give:** the comfortable start to squirm, and the room has to think again: they make space for something freer.
- **Siblings, carefully:** **the Trickster is the closest pour in the whole studio.** Trickster = mischief, a game, an invitation to see the strings, truest things said as jokes, fears being ignored. Subverter = satire aimed at power, the squirm is the point, they'll be disliked rather than muzzled, fears being silenced. The reading must not use "strings", "ignored", "truest things as jokes" or "invitation". The Reassurer (caregiver-jester) jokes to comfort; the Subverter jokes to unsettle. The Surrealist notices strangeness; the Subverter aims. The Devil's Advocate argues; the Subverter mocks.
- **Traps:** the Joker (chaos, cruelty, violence); "sarcastic" as a label; jokes at the vulnerable; "you're funny" as the recognition.
- **Story test:** the story's person must (1) make a joke aimed at power, something treated as sacred; (2) be punished or pressured for it: banned, fined, sacked, censored; (3) keep joking anyway; (4) and the joke must have changed how people thought. It must punch up. **Out:** pranks without a target (Trickster), comfort humour (Reassurer), invented stories (Trickster), cruelty.
- **Position direction:** encouragement with a condition: keep making it, and aim it up; the squirm means it worked.

## outlaw-lover · The Fugitive
- **The one true thing:** they're not running from the person. They're running from the moment it becomes ordinary, because somewhere they believe that stopping is what kills love.
- **Hide:** that they can't always tell love and freedom apart, and it scares them. **Fear:** being caught, pinned down, made to settle. **Give:** whoever is with them feels chosen against the whole world.
- **Siblings:** the Thrill Seeker runs toward danger with friends; the Fugitive runs away with someone. The Obsessed (creator-lover) falls for things and the making; the Fugitive falls for a person and a road at once. The Understanding Spouse stays and sees the worst: the Fugitive's opposite, and a useful foil. The Adolescent refuses to become someone; the Fugitive refuses to be kept somewhere.
- **Traps:** **the reading never requires a partner** (same guard as the Spouse): passion can be for a place, a plan, a life. No couple assumed man-and-woman. Bonnie and Clyde's crimes and deaths are out, and so is Bonnie's poem about how it ends. No infidelity, no secret affair (secrets out).
- **Story test:** the story's person must (1) choose a love (or a passion) against the rules or the world: an elopement, a border crossed, a family defied; (2) run for it; (3) **and the story must include what happened when the running stopped**, because that's the guest's real question. It must not end in death or ruin. **Out:** crime, violence, tragic endings, affairs.
- **Position direction:** a hard truth, warmly: stop the car once and see that it's still there. (Avoid "Tuesday": the Photographer owns it.)

## outlaw-magician · The Disrupter
- **The one true thing:** they break what everyone else has been politely tolerating, and they get blamed for the rubble instead of thanked for the rot they cleared.
- **Hide:** the doubt afterwards: lying awake wondering if they broke the right thing; and that they miss the old thing too. **Fear:** the slow decay nobody dares touch. **Give:** they do the hard thing nobody else would, so something can live again.
- **Siblings:** the Makeover Artist (creator-magician) keeps what makes it theirs and stays calm in the ugly middle (*Overnight* owns "looked like a mistake" and "don't fix what you find"); the Disrupter clears the ground. The Old Master questions their own work; the Disrupter touches what isn't theirs: a team, a habit, a relationship. The Rule Breaker breaks in order to make; the Disrupter breaks so it can be rebuilt, often by others. The Devil's Advocate keeps it to argument; the Disrupter acts. The Campaigner speaks against unfairness; the Disrupter acts against staleness.
- **Traps:** destruction for fun; anger; fire and apocalypse imagery; Shiva as a pun or decoration; "you love chaos" (the sheet says the opposite: not out of anger, because change is overdue).
- **Story test:** the story's person must (1) tear down or overturn an established thing that everyone else tolerated and that was quietly decaying; (2) do it deliberately and with care, not in anger; (3) take the blame for the damage at the time; (4) and the story must show what grew back, and that it was better. The doubt should be visible if the source allows. **Out:** disasters, wars, violence, the makeover shape (Overnight), religious ritual as the story.
- **Position direction:** say out loud what you're sorry to lose, then break it anyway.

## outlaw-ruler · The Leading Edge
- **The one true thing:** they love the new and they want the wheel, and people call that a contradiction. It isn't to them: they steer it to keep it from being tamed.
- **Hide:** that "opportunist" and "manipulator" sting, because they loved it before there was anything to gain. **Fear:** watching the thing they started run off without them, or get tamed by the mainstream. **Give:** they turn a mood into a movement, something others can actually join.
- **Siblings:** **the Trend Setter (creator-outlaw) is the closest**: taste, no steering, fear of the eye-rolling, "keep liking it". The Leading Edge names it, organises it, dresses it, runs it. The Visionary (approved) hands the dream on and fears it stopping; the Leading Edge fears it going on without them, so its position can't be "hand it on" (*Down the Line*'s) or "let someone else make the second" (*Making the Calls*'). The Auteur controls one work; the Leading Edge steers a crowd. The Rule Breaker makes their own; the Leading Edge shapes other people's rebellion.
- **Traps:** McLaren and the Sex Pistols (the sheet's example, and punk is the family's magnet); "tastemaker", "impresario", "svengali" labels; control framed as the villain.
- **Story test:** the story's person must (1) spot something new before it had a name; (2) give it shape: a name, a place, a look, a rule set; (3) steer it as it grew; (4) and face the moment it outgrew them or got tamed. Both halves must show: rebel and strategist. **Out:** "made for the staff, then it spread" (Trend Setter), a single creator's masterpiece (Auteur), a chain handed on (Visionary).
- **Position direction:** the edge is them, not the thing: let the old one go mainstream and go and find the next one.

## outlaw-sage · The Devil's Advocate
- **The one true thing:** when they push hard against someone's idea, it's because they think the idea is worth it. The argument is their form of respect.
- **Hide:** how often they actually agree with the thing they're attacking; and that being "exhausting" is lonely. **Fear:** the room where everyone nods and nobody is allowed to think. **Give:** ideas that hold up because they survived them; people treated as strong enough to be argued with.
- **Siblings:** the Subverter mocks, the Devil's Advocate argues. The Disrupter acts, the Devil's Advocate tests. The Adolescent rebels on instinct, the Devil's Advocate on purpose. The Campaigner (caregiver-outlaw) argues for a cause they believe; the Devil's Advocate argues the side they may not believe. The Doctor (caregiver-sage, "since when?") and the Connoisseur (fear of deception) are sage-family neighbours: the Devil's Advocate's fear is consensus, not being fooled.
- **Traps:** "contrarian", "troll", "debater" labels; cruelty; the literal Church office as a pun on the personality name (the name is shown, so a story about the real office would need to own the coincidence in one clause).
- **Story test:** the story's person must (1) argue the other side on purpose, by role or by choice; (2) argue it hard and honestly, not as a stunt; (3) be resented or found tiresome for it at the time; (4) and the argument must have made the outcome stronger or truer, whether they won or lost. **Out:** a cause they personally believed (Campaigner's shape), exposing a fraud (sage-family deception), invented stories (Trickster).
- **Position direction:** once, before the argument, tell them where you agree.

## Notes for Hester and the rooms
- The whole family will be pulled toward Prohibition, bans and smuggling. One pairing at most (the Gangster fits best); the rest need other ground, ingredients and people especially.
- Three rows carry examples whose real stories are off-limits: Adolescent (Geldof), Fugitive (Bonnie and Clyde's end), Thrill Seeker (injuries). The personas stand without them.
- The Subverter vs the Trickster, and the Leading Edge vs the Trend Setter, are the two collisions most likely to sink a draft late. Their rooms should run the swap test on the round-1 read, not at the resonance test.
