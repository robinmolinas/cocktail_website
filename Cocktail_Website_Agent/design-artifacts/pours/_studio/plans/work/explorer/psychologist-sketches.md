# Explorer family: Wren's sketches (2026-09-30)

All eleven are unauthored. Every persona here was read fresh from Robin's rewritten sheet (names and stories as printed by `brief.py psychologist --plan explorer`). Ground to differ from: the 26 pours in the registry, above all the two that have the Explorer as their *secondary* archetype, **The Researcher** (*Serviceable*, caregiver-explorer) and **The Experimentalist** (*Off-Label*, creator-explorer). Their two pairings are the reverse of the Conservationist's and the Innovator's.

## Family-wide (read before any row)

**The family's shared fear is entrapment, so no reading can rest on it.** All eleven want freedom and fear the cage. "You need to be free" is a horoscope sentence here. Each sibling needs its *own* cage, stated in its own word. I've given each one below, and no two readings should use another sibling's word as their key frame:

| sibling | its cage (its word) |
| --- | --- |
| Conservationist | not a cage but a loss: coming back and it's **gone** |
| Innovator | living inside someone else's **settled** answer |
| Tourist | **stuck**, with no way back |
| Pioneer | having been **ready** and not gone |
| Discoverer | too **busy** and too sure to be surprised |
| Mad Inventor | being told to be **sensible** |
| Sense Seeker | a **grey** life where every day tastes the same |
| Spiritualist | an ordinary life that never asks what it's **for** |
| Trailblazer | being **told where to go** (the signposted route) |
| Expedition Leader | **trapped**, and then they organise |
| Genius | a place where thinking isn't **allowed** |

"Curious" is family-wide too (all eleven are). Never let a reading lean on it.

**Already used by other pours, out for every row unless the room owns the overlap in one clause:**
- **Medicine and cures:** Angostura as a stomach remedy (*Off-Label*), Priestley's soda for scurvy (*Serviceable*), quinine and bark (the Doctor, the Healer). Medicine keeps offering itself to this family. Refuse it.
- **The sea, sailors, the navy, rations:** *Serviceable*, *Not Only the Way* (lime cordial for voyages), *Fair Measure* (Vernon's grog, 1740), *Four Shares* (sailors spread punch). The Expedition Leader's sheet story is at sea. This is the family's biggest collision risk.
- **The failures you keep:** "Some of what you try goes nowhere… you keep them" (the Researcher) and "Most of what you try doesn't work, and you honestly don't mind" (the Experimentalist). The Mad Inventor and the Innovator will both be pulled there. Neither may land on it.
- **"Go back to it"** (*Off-Label*), **"people you'll never meet"** (*Serviceable*), **the Post-it Note** (*Serviceable*), **"You never break a rule by accident"** (*No Accident*: the Trailblazer must not take a "rules" tagline), **"You don't make things strange. You notice they already are."** (*The Other Berry*: see the Discoverer).
- People taken: Priestley, Siegert, Gonzalez, Vernon, Uyeda, Thomas, Saunders, Cecchini, Picon, Livy Clemens.

**Out for the whole family (the tests that surfaced one per round in caregiver-sage, stated now):**
- **Quality-only stories.** "It was simply the best" mirrors nobody. The story's person has to *do* something the guest would do.
- **Secrets and secret recipes.** A kept secret is ownership, which is the wrong shape for people who want every door open (and Angostura's secret is already told).
- **Health, cures, wellness, "good for you".**
- **Legends as anchors.** Exploration stories breed them ("the first", "the lost recipe", "found under the ice"). Grant the legend, then correct it, and never build the recognition line on it.
- **The sheet's own example as the story.** Attenborough, Dyson, Armstrong, Newton, Shackleton, Hillary, Einstein and Slater are the persona's own illustrations, and the guest may have just read them on the reveal. Use one only if the library ties that person to a drink and the room owns the echo in one clause. A lesser-known person who *acts* like the guest is better.
- **Brands on the sheet** (Jeep, Persil, Red Bull, The North Face): never.

**Traps shared by all eleven:**
- **Gender.** Nine of the eleven sheet examples are men, and so is Wallace. The reading never borrows a pronoun from them. Second person only.
- **Archetype puns.** No "explore/explorer" used knowingly, and no "hero", "magic", "sage", "rule", "lover", "outlaw", "innocent" in a way that winks. Travel words (journey, map, compass, horizon, uncharted) are family-wide, so no sibling owns them. Allow at most one per reading, and never in an epigraph.
- **Profession labels:** traveller, adventurer, inventor, scientist, astronaut, naturalist, foodie, guru, rebel, captain. Show a scene, not a job.

## explorer-caregiver · The Conservationist

- **The one true thing:** they love things they refuse to own. Their care takes the form of leaving it as it was: no souvenir, no claim, sometimes not even a photo. What only they carry is grief *in advance*. They notice what's disappearing while everyone else is still enjoying it.
- **Hide / fear / give:** they hide that it hurts. It looks like principle, but it's closer to mourning, and they'd rather be thought strict than sad. Fear: coming back and it's gone, and underneath that, being part of the reason it went. Give: they make other people *see* a thing while it's still there, and they hand the place back whole to people who come after.
- **Versus the Researcher** (the reverse pairing): the Researcher's care chases an answer for people far away, and it's *searching*. The Conservationist's care is *restraint* aimed at a place or a living thing: knowing when not to take. **Versus the Photographer** (*As It Was*, keeps the Tuesdays): the Photographer keeps. The Conservationist leaves it where it is. **Versus the Minimalist:** the Minimalist cuts away in order to make something. The Conservationist doesn't take in the first place. **Versus the Tourist:** the Tourist brings the place home to share. The Conservationist leaves no trace of having been.
- **Traps:** preachiness, climate guilt, politics (the reading must never lecture the guest). A doom story whose point is extinction: grief with nothing holding. An Attenborough voice pastiche. "Leave only footprints" (a cliché). "Someone had to say it" belongs to the Campaigner.
- **Story test:** the story's person (or an ingredient's keepers) found something precious and chose **not to own, enclose or exploit it**, or kept it going for others to find. They must **face the pull to take it**: profit, credit, the easy harvest. It must **cost them** something they gave up. What must hold: **the thing survives**, and ideally it's still there today, so the guest could go and meet it. Out: rescue-from-the-brink heroics (that's rescue, not keeping), a secret held back (that's owning), anything at sea, medicine.
- **Position direction (for the room):** you don't have to carry every loss by yourself. Take someone with you, so there's one more person who'd notice.

## explorer-creator · The Innovator

- **The one true thing:** their invention runs on **irritation**. What everyone else puts up with, they can't, and the annoyance is the engine, not a flaw. They don't set out to create. They set out to get *out*, and the new thing is the door.
- **Hide / fear / give:** they hide how much of it starts as plain annoyance, because it sounds like complaining or ingratitude ("it was fine as it was"). They'd rather present the calm vision. Fear: being handed a finished world and told to live in it; "that's just how it's done". Give: the door they built for themselves becomes everyone's. People now walk through it without knowing it was ever a wall.
- **Versus the Experimentalist** (the reverse pairing, *Off-Label*): the Experimentalist plays and never makes the same thing twice. For them, finding out is the point. The Innovator has **one target**, one specific annoyance, and doesn't stop until it's gone. It's a destination, not play. **Versus the Visionary** (*Down the Line*): the Visionary carries a dream of size and time. The Innovator carries a gripe. **Versus the Mad Inventor:** that one builds for joy and needs no problem. This one builds to escape. **Versus the Campaigner:** the Campaigner says it. The Innovator builds the fix instead. **Versus the Genius:** the Genius holds the question in their head. The Innovator holds it in their hands.
- **Traps:** inventor, entrepreneur and "disruptor" labels. Dyson and vacuum echoes. "Thousands of prototypes": persistence is fine, but "the tries that went nowhere" is the Researcher's and the Experimentalist's. "Think outside the box." The Post-it.
- **Story test:** the story's person was **annoyed by an everyday shortcoming that everyone else accepted**, in a tool, a method, an ingredient or a drink, and **the annoyance must be on record**, not inferred. They made **a new way that others now use without thinking**. Cost: time, and being told it was fine already. What must hold: **the fix is still in use today**. Out: a lucky accident (the Discoverer's), play for its own sake (the Mad Inventor's), a dream of scale (the Visionary's), a failed competition entry they kept making (*Off-Label*'s), medicine.
- **Position direction:** stop apologising for being annoyed. Trust it: it's usually the first sign of your next door.

## explorer-regular-guy · The Tourist

- **The one true thing:** the trip isn't finished until they've told it at a table back home. The return ticket isn't a lack of nerve. It's part of the adventure, because the adventure is meant to be *shared*. Their daring is exactly one street further than the guidebook, and they come home glowing about that street.
- **Hide / fear / give:** they hide a small embarrassment. They like the guidebook and the way back, and they've felt looked down on by people who make a show of "real travel". Fear: being stuck somewhere, even somewhere beautiful, and being left out. Give: they bring the world back to people who didn't go. Their "one street further" gives others permission to try it, and their stories turn a dinner into a trip.
- **Versus the Trailblazer:** the Trailblazer refuses the approved path. The Tourist loves the approved path *plus one step*. **Versus the Sense Seeker:** the Sense Seeker goes in close for the sensation itself. The Tourist goes for the story it'll make, told to others. **Versus the Photographer** (*As It Was*): the Photographer keeps the ordinary days. The Tourist keeps the holiday and hands it round. **Versus the True Friend** (*Four Shares*): the True Friend's gift is inclusion at home. The Tourist's gift is the world, brought home.
- **Traps: condescension is the big one.** The personality's name can read like a put-down, so the reading must give the return ticket its dignity and never sneer (Robin: never insult the guest). Also: "globetrotter" and "travel blogger" labels, souvenirs as kitsch, a tiki drink told as a "tourist trap", foreign places as quaint scenery, TimeOut.
- **Story test:** someone **went somewhere, found one thing a step beyond the usual, and brought it home to share**, and it became loved at home. It must show **the going, the one-step-further find, and the coming back and telling**. The risk is small and taken knowingly, never heroics. What must hold: **a way back existed, and nobody vanished**. Out: exile, emigrating for good or getting stranded (that's the fear itself), plunder told as fun, a drink that's only a holiday joke, anyone ending up alone.
- **Position direction:** stop apologising for the return ticket. Next time, go two streets further. You're coming home anyway, and that's why you can.

## explorer-hero · The Pioneer

- **The one true thing:** they're afraid every time, and they go anyway, and they make it look calm. **The calm is for the people watching.** What decides it is regret: the fear of having been ready and not gone is bigger than the fear of the thing itself.
- **Hide / fear / give:** they hide the fear and the years of preparation nobody sees. Then they play it down afterwards ("one small step") so the next person isn't frightened off. Fear: ready and didn't go. Give: steadiness in a room that's scared, and proof. Once they've stood there, it's ground other people can stand on.
- **Versus the Rescuer** (*Off Duty*, "You've already gone"): the Rescuer moves in a second, for someone in danger. The Pioneer's moment was **prepared for years**, and it's about going *first*, not about saving anyone. Keep "already gone" out: that's the Rescuer's. **Versus the Trailblazer:** the Trailblazer is uninvited and irreverent. The Pioneer is disciplined, accepts being watched and honours the mission. **Versus the Expedition Leader:** the Leader carries the others home. The Pioneer carries only the first step. **Versus the Visionary:** the Visionary's dream is carried on by others. The Pioneer stands on the new ground in person.
- **Traps:** calling them "fearless" (they aren't, and that's the whole point). Astronaut and hero labels, moon puns, the "small step" quote itself (it's on the sheet, and it's worn). Bravado. Rescue imagery.
- **Story test:** a **first**, in public or under watching eyes, with **real risk of failing in front of people**. It must show **the preparation beforehand**, **the fear** (on record, or honestly signposted), **the steady doing**, and ideally **the understatement afterwards**. What must hold: it worked, and **others followed because it was now proven possible**. Out: luck or an accident (the Discoverer's), defiance (the Trailblazer's), rescue (the Rescuer's), a team brought home (the Leader's). **Guard for Hester:** "the first" is the most legend-prone claim in drinks history. If the first isn't solid on the page, the story fails.
- **Position direction:** tell one person you were scared. The calm will still work, and you won't be so alone behind it.

## explorer-innocent · The Discoverer

- **The one true thing:** they **give ordinary things time**. The discovery comes from patience and attention, not from cleverness. They stay open long enough to be surprised by something everyone else has already seen. They trust the world to be interesting, and they're willing to be slow about it.
- **Hide / fear / give:** they hide how often small things delight them, because it can come across as naive or childlike. Fear: growing busy and certain, becoming someone who already knows. Give: they hand other people the ordinary back, new. "Look at that" is the whole gift, and the "why?" that follows sometimes changes everything.
- **Versus the Surrealist (*The Other Berry*): DANGER, near-identical on the page.** Its tagline is already "You don't make things strange. You notice they already are." The Surrealist notices the strangeness and *makes a strange thing* from it, playfully (the tomato daiquiri). The Discoverer's heart is **unhurried openness and trust**, and the **"why?" that leads somewhere true**. So the Discoverer's reading never uses "strange" or "notice" as its key word. Its own word is **time** or **busy**. **Versus the Genius:** the Genius chases one question for years. The Discoverer isn't chasing: they're receptive, and they get surprised. **Versus the Experimentalist:** that one finds out by trying. This one finds out by stopping. **Versus the Sense Seeker:** that one hunts sensation. This one is open to wonder.
- **Traps:** Newton's apple. It's the sheet's own story, it's half-legend (the "told a friend late in life" version is the careful one), and the apple on the head is a myth. "Eureka" (a cliché, and it's Archimedes). Infantilising ("childlike", "innocent"). "Curious" as the point.
- **Story test:** a discovery **made by stopping for something ordinary that many had seen and passed**. It must show **the everyday thing everyone overlooked**, **the person who took the time and asked why**, and **that it turned out to matter**. Cost: time, and being thought slow or odd for dawdling. What must hold: **the moment is on record, not legend** (fitting, since this personality's own emblem is half-legend). Out: engineered effort (the Innovator's), play (the Mad Inventor's), a strange object made on purpose (the Surrealist's), medicine, the sea.
- **Position direction:** protect the slow part. Leave one gap in the week that nothing is allowed to fill.

## explorer-jester · The Mad Inventor

- **The one true thing:** **the ridiculous route is the point.** They'd rather build a machine to make breakfast than make breakfast, and they want someone there when it goes off. When it collapses they laugh loudest, and the laugh is shared. When it works, nobody is more surprised than they are, and they love that too.
- **Hide / fear / give:** they hide how much "be sensible" stings. It deflates them faster than any collapse, because it turns their joy into a waste of time. They'd never admit it, but when it works, they're quietly proud. Give: delight. When they laugh at their own collapse, it makes trying safe for everyone watching.
- **Versus the Experimentalist (*Off-Label*): DANGER.** "Most of what you try doesn't work, and you don't mind" is already theirs, and the Researcher's. The Mad Inventor's own thing is **over-building for fun**: the absurdly elaborate way to a simple end, done as a show, **with an audience and a laugh**. It's not trying again, and it's not keeping the failures. **Versus the Innovator:** the Innovator escapes a problem. The Mad Inventor doesn't need a problem at all. **Versus the Surrealist:** the Surrealist sees the oddness that's already there. The Mad Inventor builds it, with springs. **Versus the Reassurer** (*Worth the Trip*): the Reassurer's laugh eases other people. The Mad Inventor laughs at their own heap of springs. **Versus the Trickster:** there's no deception here. Everyone is in on it.
- **Traps:** "mad" said to the guest (a mental-health word; the persona's name can stay a tag, but the reading never calls them mad or crazy). "Mad scientist", Wallace and Gromit pastiche (cheese, the dog). "Genius" (a sibling's name). A gimmick drink that's clever for its own sake. A flourish that isn't true every time the drink is made.
- **Story test:** someone who **chose the elaborate, silly-looking way on purpose, for the joy of it**, while **the sensible way was available** (or someone told them to take it), and **people laughed**, ideally with them. It must show **the fun of the building** and **the surprise when it worked** (or glory even when it didn't). What must hold: **joy, not a hard-won persistence arc** (that's the Researcher's, the Experimentalist's and the Innovator's). Out: failures kept, an irritation fixed, a secret, medicine, a competition lost and repeated. Theatrical preparation in drinks history is a natural hunting ground (Hester to verify; legends abound).
- **Position direction:** never let anyone talk you into the sensible version. Build the silly one, and invite people to watch it fall over.

## explorer-lover · The Sense Seeker

- **The one true thing:** **their memory is made of sensations.** They can't tell you the year, but they can tell you what it smelled like. Their map of life is bakeries, one peach, a stranger's kitchen. And they want **an appetite nobody has to approve**. That's the freedom this sibling wants.
- **Hide / fear / give:** they hide how much they want, because they've been called "too much" or indulgent, and they've learned to play it down. Fear: grey sameness, and having to justify pleasure. Give: they make other people taste their own lives again. They bring someone the peach, and they turn an ordinary meal with others into the one that gets remembered.
- **Versus the Connoisseur** (*A Brother's Care*): the Connoisseur wants to *understand* and fears being fooled. The Sense Seeker wants to *feel*: no notebook, no rating, appetite rather than expertise. **Versus the Obsessed** (*Rosetta*, "you fall for them"): the Obsessed goes deep into one thing it makes. The Sense Seeker takes it in, then moves on to the next sensation. **Versus the Minimalist** (hidden need: to feel each thing properly): the Minimalist gets there by taking away. The Sense Seeker gets there by abundance and variety. **Versus the Tourist:** the Tourist collects sights to share. The Sense Seeker goes in close, sometimes alone, for the thing itself.
- **Traps:** on a cocktail site, "you love taste" is a Barnum sentence, true of every guest. The reading must be about *memory made of sensation* and *unapproved appetite*, not about liking flavour. Also: innuendo (sensual, never sexual). "Foodie", "gourmet", "epicure". Nigel Slater pastiche. Pleasure-as-alcohol (the reading isn't about drinking). **The drink-in-hand trap is strongest here** ("taste this now"). Gendered sensuality.
- **Story test:** **one sensation that set someone's course or stayed with them for life**. Either an ingredient that carried a place in its smell or taste to someone far away, or a person who **followed an appetite others disapproved of**. It must show **a close-up sensory detail on record** (a smell, a texture, a temperature), **the freedom of following it without permission**, and **someone else at the table**. What must hold: **the sensation the story names is really in the glass** (Tomás: the drink must deliver it, every time). Out: connoisseurship, ranking and "knowing" (the Connoisseur's), making (the Obsessed's), restraint, anything sexual, health.
- **Position direction:** stop asking whether it's too much. Take someone to the market before it opens.

## explorer-magician · The Spiritualist

- **The one true thing:** **they keep the one line.** They read everything, scripture to self-help, and keep the sentence that changes them. They go out looking, outward and inward at once, because they believe a mind really can be changed, and that happiness is something you go and find.
- **Hide / fear / give:** they hide the eye-rolls they've had (The Secret gets teased), and a fear of being thought gullible or flaky. Maybe, gently, a fear that the looking could become a way of never arriving (hold this lightly, and only if the room finds it true). Fear: an ordinary life that never asks what it's for. Give: they ask the question others avoid, and they believe people can change, which gives others permission to.
- **Versus the Healer** (*Till Spring*): the Healer believes *others* will be all right. The Spiritualist believes a mind can be *transformed*, their own first. **Versus the Genius:** the Genius trusts evidence and distrusts unexplained authority. The Spiritualist will keep a true line from any source, ancient or popular. That's the opposite stance, and the reading must honour it, never correct it. **Versus the Old Master** (*Beside the First*): the Old Master changes one thing in their work. The Spiritualist wants to change themselves. **Versus the Discoverer:** wonder at the world, versus a search inward.
- **Traps: the "spirit/spirits" pun is the cheapest line in the building. Never.** Never mock their beliefs, and never assume a faith, or no faith. New Age props (crystals, incense) as a joke. "Magic". Monk-made liqueurs: their origin stories are often marketing legends (Bénédictine's is), and green Chartreuse is already in *Beside the First*. Absinthe's hallucination myth (a legend, drug-adjacent, and absinthe is *Rosetta*'s). Wellness claims.
- **Story test:** a **search undertaken on purpose**, whether a journey, a retreat, a text or a teacher, from which the story's person **kept one thing that changed how they lived**. It must show **the going out to look**, **the one thing kept**, and **the change that lasted** (on record). Cost: leaving the ordinary, and being thought odd. What must hold: **the change is inward**, not fame or success. Out: miracles, hallucination, a religious claim stated as fact, a monk legend used as an anchor, any "spirit" wordplay, a drink sold as a cure.
- **Position direction:** you've kept enough lines. Live one of them for a month before you look for the next.

## explorer-outlaw · The Trailblazer

- **The one true thing:** **they don't ask, because asking means someone can say no.** They make their own way, openly and without permission, and then they refuse to make it grand. Nerve on the way up, irreverence at the top. They'll puncture the solemnity with one plain, blunt sentence.
- **Hide / fear / give:** they hide what's underneath the shrug. They often came in from outside (the beekeeper, not the officer), and being told where to go feels like being put back in a box they climbed out of. Fear: the fenced field, being made small. Give: the path they cut stays open. Because they won't make it sound grand, other people think "I could do that", and then they do.
- **Versus the Pioneer:** the Pioneer is disciplined, watched and dutiful. The Trailblazer is uninvited, informal, and allergic to ceremony. **Versus the Trickster** (*No Accident*, "You never break a rule by accident"): the Trickster's move is a trick with a story. The Trailblazer tricks nobody and simply walks off the path in plain sight. **No "rule" in this tagline.** **Versus the Campaigner** (*Not Too Polite*): the Campaigner has a cause. The Trailblazer has a route. **Versus the Trend Setter** (*Still Yours*, liked it first): that's taste. This is direction. **Versus the Innovator:** the Innovator builds a door. The Trailblazer climbs the fence.
- **Traps:** aggression (the sheet says "without aggression"). "Rebel" and "outlaw" labels. Summit and peak puns. Reproducing Hillary's quote (it's the sheet's). A mild swear is fine in a drink's name, but not a borrowed one. **Prohibition and bootlegging glamour:** likely claimed by the Outlaw family. Check the claims, and don't make breaking the law the cool part.
- **Story test:** someone **without permission or credentials** who **went their own way and left a route others then used**. It must show **the missing permission** (they weren't the approved one), **the nerve**, and **an unpompous attitude on record** (a blunt, funny or plain remark, or a refusal of the fuss). Cost: going unsupported, and the establishment's disapproval. What must hold: **it was done openly (no trick, no secret), and the route stayed open**. Out: a cause (the Campaigner's), a trick (the Trickster's), a first with everyone watching (the Pioneer's), taste-first (the Trend Setter's), crime as glamour.
- **Position direction:** leave the route marked. Someone behind you is waiting for proof they don't need permission either.

## explorer-ruler · The Expedition Leader

- **The one true thing:** **they bring everyone home, and they do it by organising hope.** When it goes wrong, they don't give speeches. They give people routines, jobs and a next step, and morale is part of the plan. They love the wild edge *and* the discipline it takes to survive there, and they'd never lead anyone somewhere they wouldn't go first.
- **Hide / fear / give:** they hide the weight: counting heads, the one face they're worried about, the doubt they can't show because everyone reads theirs. Maybe a private guilt that they love the edge they're leading people towards. Fear: trapped, and losing one. Give: people dare to go into the unknown *with* them, and the order they bring is a kind of safety.
- **Versus the Nanny (*Fair Measure*): DANGER.** Structure, orders, rum and water on Vernon's ships. The Nanny's structure keeps the fun lasting at home. The Leader's structure keeps people *alive in the unknown*, and they share the danger themselves. **No rationing story, no navy.** **Versus the Rescuer** (*Off Duty*): the Rescuer runs in alone, fast. The Leader stays, plans, and brings *all* of them out. **Versus the Auteur** (*Making the Calls*): the Auteur does everyone's part at night. The Leader hands everyone a part and trusts them to do it. **Versus the Pioneer:** the first step alone, versus the whole group home. **Versus the Nurse:** the same for everyone, versus everyone accounted for.
- **Traps:** captain, boss and commander labels; military cosplay. **The sea and ice:** the sheet's own Shackleton story is at sea, and four pours already sailed. Keep the sea out of the anchor if at all possible, and certainly out of the image. "Endurance" as a name or pun. "No man left behind" (gendered). Shackleton's 1907 whisky left under a hut is the obvious drinks story. Tempting, but its shape is *preserved and found*, which mirrors the Conservationist more than a leader. If Hester brings it, it must show the people coming home, not the bottle.
- **Story test:** someone **responsible for a group in the unknown or in danger** who, when they were **stuck**, **kept them together through organisation and morale**, and **brought all of them back**. It must show **the others depending on them**, **the moment of being trapped**, and **the response being structure, not heroics or panic**. Cost: the weight they carried unseen. What must hold: **everyone came home, sourced** ("not one died" must be on the page). Out: navy rations and grog (the Nanny's), a lone rescue (the Rescuer's), personal glory, a leader who lost people, sea imagery.
- **Position direction:** let someone else count heads one night. You're allowed to be one of the people brought home.

## explorer-sage · The Genius

- **The one true thing:** **their freedom is inside their head.** They can sit in a dull job or a rigid room and still be free, because they carry one question with them for years, turning it over. They think in pictures, "what would it be like to…". And they won't take "because I said so" from anyone who can't explain themselves.
- **Hide / fear / give:** they hide that rote and drill are close to unbearable. They can look idle or absent exactly when they're doing their real work. Fear: a place where thinking isn't allowed, and being made to stop. Give: they explain. When they turn a question over long enough, they turn it over for everyone, and they refuse unexplained authority on other people's behalf too.
- **Versus the Doctor** (*Instead*, "since when?"): the Doctor asks for someone who's suffering. The Genius asks for the question's own sake, over years. **Versus the Old Master** (*Beside the First*): the Old Master questions their *own* best work. The Genius questions *received* answers. **Versus the Connoisseur:** the Connoisseur fears being fooled and keeps truth out of love. The Genius fears being fenced in, and trusts evidence over authority. **Versus the Discoverer:** the Discoverer is surprised. The Genius is persistent. **Versus the Innovator:** a question held in the head, versus a fix built by hand.
- **Traps: flattery is the big one.** The personality's name is already praise, so the reading must recognise, never compliment ("you're brilliant" fails). Also: genius, scientist, nerd and prodigy labels. Einstein tropes (the hair, E=mc², "it's all relative"). Assuming the guest hated school (many didn't). The "patent clerk" day job as a template.
- **Story test:** someone who **held one question for years** (on record), often **from outside the establishment or inside an ordinary job**, whose answer **overturned what the authorities said, on evidence**.
