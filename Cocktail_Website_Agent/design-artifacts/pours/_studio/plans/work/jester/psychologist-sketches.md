# Jester family: Wren's sketches (2026-09-30)

All eleven are unauthored. Every persona was read fresh from Robin's rewritten sheet (brief of 2026-09-30).

## Family-wide (read before any row)

- **What all eleven share, so it can't be anyone's one true thing:** fear of boredom, love of a laugh, "the joke hides something serious". Each row below names what *only* that one carries. If a draft's recognition line would fit "someone funny", it isn't finished.
- **Existing ground to differ from (drafted or approved, Jester as secondary or Jester-like):**
  - *Worth the Trip* (the Reassurer, caregiver-jester): one person, the worst moment, waits before the line, "you joke because it is serious", fear "the joke was for me", position "let the quiet happen". Joe Scialom, hangover cure, taxi to the front. **The Morale Booster's near-twin.** Medicine and cures are Caregiver-wide anyway: out for this family.
  - *The Other Berry* (the Surrealist, creator-jester): notices strangeness that's already there, hides the strangest things, "this is going to sound weird", tomato vs. strawberry. Near the Wacky One and the Naïf.
  - *No Accident* (the Trickster, magician-outlaw): sees the strings, truest things said as jokes, "a trick with a gift inside", "being ignored", "the truth at the bottom of the glass", invented history (Seelbach). Near the Elf/Imp, the Scandalmonger and the Cynic.
  - *Fair Measure* (the Nanny): "You were never against the fun. You're why it lasts." Near the Straight Man.
  - *Not Too Polite* (the Campaigner): "Someone had to say it. It's usually you." and "the sugar is the argument". Near the Scandalmonger and the Elf/Imp.
  - *Still Yours* (the Trend Setter) closes on "whoever rolls their eyes". *Rosetta* (the Obsessed) owns "wait for the cloud".
- **Jester-secondary cousins being planned right now in other families** (read their plans' Claims before picking): the Giggler (innocent), the Illusionist (magician), the Subverter (outlaw), the Prankster (regular-guy), the Charismatic Leader (ruler), the Wise Fool (sage), the Storyteller (hero), the Mad Inventor (explorer), the Flirt (lover). Near-twins to police: Wise Talker/Storyteller, Cynic/Wise Fool, Scandalmonger/Subverter, Elf-Imp/Prankster/Illusionist, Tease/Flirt, Naïf/Giggler, Wacky One/Mad Inventor, Straight Man/Charismatic Leader.
- **Family traps:** the persona's famous example is never the story or the name (Seinfeld, Kaufman, Benigni, Mr Bean, Popeye, Dita Von Teese, Laurel & Hardy, Colbert; Russell Brand is never referenced at all). No "clown", "comedian", "comic", "fool", "jester" labels about the guest. No puns on the personality names ("straight", "imp", "wacky", "naive"). Funny is allowed (tone as far as human), but the reading must also be *true* under the laugh. Mild drink-name swears are fine.
- **Family story rule:** each mirror must show humour *doing something for someone*, and cost or risk something. A story that is only "a funny drink name" or "a legend corrected" passes nobody.

## jester-caregiver · The Morale Booster

- **One true thing:** they treat a room's mood as their job. Not one person (that's the Reassurer): the whole heavy room, and they plan it. The silly game they "happen" to have, the song they start, the ridiculous tradition they invented for the dreary office Monday. It looks spontaneous; it rarely is.
- **Hide:** the effort, and the tiredness afterwards. **Fear:** the gloom nobody can lift, and being the one who couldn't; they take that night home as a personal failure. **Give:** permission for everyone else to feel better, together.
- **Differs from the Reassurer (drafted):** room vs. one person; making the fun vs. timing the line; they fill the silence, the Reassurer was asked to leave it. Its y5 ("let the quiet happen") and its "wrong moment / right moment" tagline are off-limits. **From the Wise Talker:** everyday heaviness (a dull party, a sad week), not life-or-death; weather, not a shield. **From the Stand-up:** they bring fun in; the Stand-up finds it in what's already there.
- **Traps:** "hospital clown" or any care profession; medicine, cures, hangovers (the Reassurer and Caregiver family own them); "you're allowed to be looked after" (the True Friend's).
- **Position I'm reaching for:** the nights it didn't lift still counted: people remember that someone tried. Or: let someone else make the fun for you once.
- **Story test:** someone who deliberately made joy for a gloomy *group* (a crowd, a town, a bar full of people in a bad time), and put work and planning into it. It must show the cost or a time it didn't work. Out: cures and medicine, taking a drink to one person, the front line and the taxi, a lone joke, a hangover.

## jester-creator · The Wacky One

- **One true thing:** they are completely at home in being ridiculous, and they commit. The invented word gets used for years until everyone uses it. They don't apologise for it (the Surrealist does); they wear it.
- **Hide:** that it's a choice they keep making, and that "tone it down, just for tonight" hurts more than they show. **Fear:** being made normal; the important occasion where they're asked to leave it at home. **Give:** freedom: when they're ridiculous, everyone else stops checking themselves.
- **Differs from the Surrealist (drafted):** makes nonsense vs. notices strangeness; wears it vs. hides it. **From the Naïf:** means it to be funny; the Naïf never does. **From the Alternative Comic:** the Wacky One wants the delight, not the puzzled silence; it's an identity, not an experiment. **From the Mad Inventor (cousin):** the pointless machine is for joy and identity, not "will it work?".
- **Traps:** Popeye, spinach, "I yam what I yam"; any "weird" framing (the Surrealist's); the tomato/berry idea; calling them eccentric.
- **Position:** bring it to the one room you usually leave it out of.
- **Story test:** someone who made something deliberately absurd, owned it fully and publicly, kept at it, and it caught on or became theirs. Must be on purpose (not a mistake, not a trick, not a hidden oddness revealed). Something must have pushed back (mockery, a "be serious"), and they kept going.

## jester-regular-guy · The Stand-up

- **One true thing:** they say the small thing everyone does and nobody mentions, and the joke is usually on themselves first. It makes people feel normal: "oh, you do that too."
- **Hide:** how closely they watch people; the observation is a kind of attention, even love. And there are things about themselves they haven't turned into a bit yet. **Fear:** the dull table where everyone pretends. **Give:** belonging, laughing at being human together.
- **Differs from the Surrealist:** common things everyone sees vs. strange things only one person sees. **From the Cynic:** small, domestic and affectionate, not the news or lies. **From the Scandalmonger:** never a gasp or a taboo; they never punch. **From the Morale Booster:** finds the fun already in the room rather than bringing it in.
- **Traps:** "comedian", "stand-up", microphone and stage; "a show about nothing" (the persona's own line); "among your friends" (a banned template); "at the table, not the head of it" as a quote.
- **Position:** tell one of the ones you haven't made funny yet, straight.
- **Story test:** an ordinary, everyday drink or habit that everyone shared and nobody thought worth recording, until someone named it, and people recognised themselves. It must be small and common, not grand. The person in it is one of the crowd, not above it. Out: fame, the big stage, mocking anyone.

## jester-explorer · The Alternative Comic

- **One true thing:** the easy laugh feels like nothing to them. They'd rather get a real reaction (bewildered, silent, delighted) than a polite laugh they could have predicted.
- **Hide:** they aren't trying to be difficult; they care a great deal whether it lands, just not the way the crowd expects. **Fear:** being asked to do "the one you did last time". **Give:** they wake a room up; nothing with them is on autopilot.
- **Differs from the Experimentalist (creator-explorer, drafted, "never the same thing twice", Off-Label):** the Experimentalist experiments on things; this one experiments on the audience and the format itself. Keep the "twice" frame out. **From the Scandalmonger:** plays with form, not taboo. **From the Wacky One:** willing to puzzle, not only delight. **From the Trickster:** never deceives.
- **Traps:** Andy Kaufman, The Great Gatsby reading; "avant-garde" labels; calling them awkward or difficult.
- **Position:** once in a while, let them see why: tell them afterwards what you were trying.
- **Story test:** someone who broke a drink's expected form or ritual on purpose, knowing it might puzzle, and it did before it worked (or it didn't, and they tried again differently). The risk must be real and the aim a real reaction. Out: shock for its own sake, hoaxes and invented history, novelty for marketing.

## jester-hero · The Wise Talker

- **One true thing:** when things are really bad, they keep joking *for someone else*, and they're just as frightened as everyone. The joke is how they stay standing and keep another person going.
- **Hide:** the fear. **Fear:** the moment the joke stops working and they have nothing else. **Give:** hope that lasts; people keep going because they did.
- **Differs from the Morale Booster:** high stakes, in the middle of it, a shield, not everyday weather. **From the Reassurer (drafted):** they don't wait; they joke *during*, as defiance, and don't stop. **From the Storyteller (hero-jester, other family, near-twin):** in the moment vs. told afterwards; the Storyteller tells struggles as adventures; the Wise Talker jokes while the struggle is still happening.
- **Traps:** anything about the Holocaust or Life Is Beautiful; war as spectacle; "hero" or "brave" as labels; the Reassurer's front line and taxi.
- **Position:** tell one person you're scared too. It won't break the spell.
- **Story test:** someone who kept humour and spirit going for others through real hardship, in the middle of it, not in the retelling. Their own fear or cost must be visible. The humour must protect someone else, not only themselves. Out: medicine and cures, the El Alamein story, a triumph told afterwards (the Storyteller's), a battle "won" by a drink.

## jester-innocent · The Naïf

- **One true thing:** they're completely serious. They try things in earnest, sometimes gloriously wrong, and ask "why do we do it this way?" because they really don't know, and it turns out nobody else did either.
- **Hide:** it stings a little to be laughed at when you meant it; they've learned to laugh along first. (Careful: the Surrealist's "stings when taken as a joke". Keep the Naïf's about *doing*, not *seeing*.) **Fear:** being told to sit still and behave, to follow a rule nobody can explain. **Give:** they show how strange our rules are, and make it safe not to know.
- **Differs from the Giggler (cousin):** the Giggler finds it funny; the Naïf doesn't. **From the Wacky One:** never means to be funny. **From the Surrealist:** acts in earnest vs. notices.
- **Traps:** anything that calls the guest foolish, childish, clumsy or "naive"; Mr Bean and the turkey; condescension. The reading must leave them proud, not patted on the head.
- **Position:** keep asking why. Don't learn to sit still.
- **Story test:** someone who came to a drink or ingredient without knowing the rules, did it their own earnest way (not by accident or as a trick), and the "wrong" way turned out delightful, or showed the rule was silly. They must have been sincere and laughed at. Out: happy accidents (the Seelbach "accident" is the Trickster's), mockery of them, cleverness in disguise.

## jester-lover · The Tease

- **One true thing:** they know the wait is the best part, and they choose the timing. They give a little, hold back the rest, and enjoy watching it land.
- **Hide:** they need to see the effect; if nobody's paying attention, it's hollow. And they only tease people they like. **Fear:** the spell becoming routine; being taken for granted. **Give:** people feel noticed, wanted and alive around them.
- **Differs from the Flirt (lover-jester, cousin):** the Flirt reaches people without risking too much; the Tease is in control and savours the delay. **From the Obsessed (Rosetta):** the Obsessed falls for things; the Tease plays with other people's anticipation. **From the Trickster:** withholding, never deceiving.
- **Traps:** burlesque, striptease, anything sexual or gendered; Dita Von Teese; "seductive" labels. Keep it about anticipation and style.
- **Position:** let one person see how much you enjoy them, straight, without the game.
- **Story test:** a drink, ingredient or person where the pleasure was in the delay or the reveal, withheld on purpose and with style, and the anticipation made it. The one in control must enjoy the effect on others. Out: "wait for the cloud" (Rosetta's), the drink that changes to a truth at the bottom (the Trickster's), deception, crudeness.

## jester-magician · The Elf/Imp

- **One true thing:** their mischief is affection. They prank the people they love, and they always know exactly where the line is. The innocent face afterwards is half the gift.
- **Hide:** how carefully they watch that line, and the one time it went too far, which they still remember. **Fear:** a house where nothing unexpected ever happens. **Give:** an ordinary Tuesday made to feel enchanted.
- **Differs from the Trickster (approved):** no systems, no rules, no truth to expose, no plan three moves ahead; small, domestic spells for delight. Its "trick with a gift inside" and "being ignored" are off-limits. **From the Prankster (cousin):** sly and small, not raucous. **From the Illusionist (cousin):** a grin, not a mind puzzle.
- **Traps:** elves, fairies, childishness; "Tim-Tam"; any pun on "imp". **Sugar-for-salt is in the persona but collides with the Campaigner's sugar ("the sugar is the argument").** Keep it out of the drink's twist.
- **Position:** let them catch you once, and laugh with them.
- **Story test:** a small, harmless piece of mischief in drink history that delighted rather than fooled, with the maker keeping a straight face and nobody hurt. It must be affectionate and aimed at people they liked. Out: grand hoaxes and invented history (the Seelbach), jokes at a victim's expense, deception about a drink's past.

## jester-outlaw · The Scandalmonger

- **One true thing:** the gasp tells them what people really think. They say the unsayable to see who laughs, who pretends to be shocked, and who's lying.
- **Hide:** sometimes it really is just for the gasp, and they know it (the persona admits it; be gentle and honest about it). **Fear:** good manners covering a room where nobody says anything real. **Give:** permission to admit what you actually think; the pretending ends.
- **Differs from the Cynic:** personal provocation and taboo in a room vs. irony about the world's lies. **From the Subverter (outlaw-jester, cousin):** the Subverter unsettles power; the Scandalmonger tests the room. **From the Campaigner:** no cause; "someone had to say it" is off-limits. **From the Trickster:** "rather hated than ignored" is the persona's own line but echoes the Trickster's "It's being ignored". Don't land there.
- **Traps:** Russell Brand, never, in any form; flattering shock as bravery; cruelty. Tabloid imagery stays out of the reading.
- **Position:** choose who it's for: the pompous, never the one who can't answer back.
- **Story test:** a drink, name or person that scandalised polite company on purpose, where the scandal exposed some hypocrisy, and that paid a price for it. The target must be the pious or the powerful, never the weak. Out: secrets, hoaxes, cruelty, a cause (the Campaigner's).

## jester-ruler · The Straight Man

- **One true thing:** they *are* funny, but their funny is restraint: the pause, the raised eyebrow. They rarely get the credit, because people think the wild one is the funny one.
- **Hide:** they'd like someone to notice they're part of the joke, not the brakes on it. **Fear:** chaos with nobody to play it against: no partner, no frame. **Give:** the calm that lets others be wild safely, and shine.
- **Differs from the Nanny (drafted, "You were never against the fun. You're why it lasts."):** not limits or safety; a double act, timing, a partner. That tagline's frame is off-limits. **From the Auteur:** shares the stage and the credit rather than owning it. **From the Charismatic Leader (cousin):** runs the fun from the side, not the front.
- **Traps:** never "man" or any gendered word; no "straight" pun; "the sensible one" as a label; "killjoy"; Laurel & Hardy.
- **Position:** take the bow once. Let them see you laugh.
- **Story test:** a pairing in drink history (two people, or two ingredients) where the steady one made the other famous and got less credit. The steady one must have enjoyed it and been essential. Out: rules and measures (the Nanny's), one sole author (the Auteur's).

## jester-sage · The Cynic

- **One true thing:** the cynicism is disappointed hope. They joke because they still care what's true; they'd go quiet only if they stopped caring.
- **Hide:** that they're an idealist. **Fear:** lies everyone pretends to believe, and the tiredness of pointing at them. **Give:** truth with a laugh, so people can swallow it.
- **Differs from the Trickster (approved):** "you say your truest things as jokes" is the Trickster's line; the Cynic is the one reading the news, not staging tricks. **From the Connoisseur:** fear of being fooled told with a groan, not kept gently. **From the Wise Fool (sage-jester, cousin, near-twin):** the Wise Fool is allowed to mock the king; the Cynic is weary and disappointed about the world. **From the Scandalmonger:** irony, not shock.
- **Traps:** "cynic" as an insult in the reading; Colbert; eye-rolls (the Trend Setter closes on "rolls their eyes"); despair.
- **Position:** say the hopeful thing once, without the joke.
- **Story test:** a lie the drinks world went along with (a false claim, a sham, a marketing myth), exposed through wit by someone who plainly cared about the truth, not only about scoring. The hope under the joke must show. Out: the Seelbach hoax (the Trickster's), a legend corrected as the whole point (every pour does that), bitterness with no care underneath.
