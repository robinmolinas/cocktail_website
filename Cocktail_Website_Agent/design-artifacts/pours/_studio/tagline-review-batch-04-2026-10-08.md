## 27. Still Yours — The Trend Setter

Pairing: creator-outlaw (Creator × Outlaw).

**Persona:** You trust your own taste before a song, style or place catches on. Your influence is accidental: people follow something you loved for yourself. Later popularity can make you doubt that taste; the tagline stays with the original instinct.

**Cocktail and why you:** A basil-scented Cosmopolitan: a personal addition to a drink that went from a staff favourite to a worldwide fashion.

**Before:** You liked it first. You get to like it last.

**Accepted:** You love what you love, and somehow everyone else starts loving it too.

**Robin's review and response:** Robin: good. Accepted without change.

## 28. As It Was — The Photographer

Pairing: creator-regular-guy (Creator × Regular Guy).

**Persona:** You notice that an unplanned kitchen-table evening is already worth remembering. You preserve ordinary life faithfully, so people can see how much was there before it becomes a blur.

**Cocktail and why you:** An Aviation with its pale violet colour restored: an overlooked record preserves a detail others had lost.

**Before:** Everyone keeps the big days. You keep the Tuesdays.

**Accepted:** You see the moments worth keeping in the days nobody thinks to remember.

**Robin's review and response:** Robin found the original unclear. Replace the context-dependent Tuesdays image with explicit recognition of ordinary days as worth remembering.

## 29. Making the Calls — The Auteur

Pairing: creator-ruler (Creator × Ruler).

**Persona:** You have a clear picture before anyone starts, and care about how every part contributes to the whole. Your standards can be demanding; people working with you often produce their best work. The line leads with your vision and purposeful attention.

**Cocktail and why you:** A precisely measured Cognac sour with honey and one discreet dash of olive brine: a small detail changes how the whole drink comes together.

**Before:** You'd rather be blamed for all of it than praised for part of it.

**Accepted:** You'd rather answer for every detail than take credit for just your part.

**Robin's review and response:** Robin liked the first rewrite but missed the original's sharpness. Restore the choice to take responsibility for the whole outcome rather than seek praise for an individual contribution.

## 30. Beside the First — The Old Master

Pairing: creator-sage (Creator × Sage).

**Persona:** People trust your judgement, but success keeps you curious about your own work. You question what others have begun treating as definitive, giving them permission to question their work too.

**Cocktail and why you:** An Improved Whiskey Cocktail with Chartreuse in place of absinthe: a new version sits beside the established one rather than erasing it.

**Before:** Everyone else calls it finished. You call it the first edition.

**Accepted:** What everyone else calls finished, you call the first edition.

**Robin's review and response:** Robin preferred the original and requested one sentence. Preserve its first-edition observation and combine it into one sentence.

## 31. Left Standing — The Conservationist

Pairing: explorer-caregiver (Explorer × Caregiver).

**Persona:** Your love for a place comes with responsibility for what survives your visit. You leave the flower and stone where they are, so someone arriving later still has something to love.

**Cocktail and why you:** A tequila Martini with a little peach liqueur and no garnish, linked to growers leaving some agaves to flower.

**Before:** You'd rather know it's there than have it.

**Accepted:** You'd rather know it's there than have it.

**Robin's review and response:** Robin: good. Original retained and accepted.

Pairing: explorer-creator (Explorer × Creator).

**Persona:** An unnecessary inconvenience stays with you until you find a lasting fix, often borrowed from somewhere unexpected. You happily do the work once so others get their time back.

**Cocktail and why you:** A frozen Margarita prepared in advance for consistent results, drawing on a solution borrowed from a convenience-store machine.

## 34. In a Minute — The Discoverer

Pairing: explorer-innocent (Explorer × Innocent).

**Persona:** Frost, a bee or a familiar sign can still make you stop and look. Your curiosity gives ordinary things time to reveal themselves, and people walking with you see more than they would alone.

**Cocktail and why you:** An El Presidente with already-aged Madeira, drawn from the question of what happens when a familiar drink is given more time.

**Before:** Most people need a good reason to stop. You need one to hurry.

**Accepted:** The smallest things can stop you in your tracks. You let them, they're here for a reason.

**Robin's review and response:** Robin supplied the final wording, adding the belief that the small things are here for a reason. Apply the wording exactly as supplied.

## 35. Why Not? — The Mad Inventor

Pairing: explorer-jester (Explorer × Jester).

**Persona:** Building and experimenting are the pleasure, even when a simpler solution is available. Your extra steps turn an ordinary task into a lively shared adventure, with laughter when it fails and contagious surprise when it works.

**Cocktail and why you:** A hot ale-and-rum flip, sweetened by cooking the sugar dark: a more involved method gives both enjoyment and a different flavour.

**Before:** There's an easier way. It just isn't as much fun.

**Accepted:** There's an easier way, but it just isn't as much fun.

**Robin's review and response:** Robin liked the original and requested one sentence. Keep its playful comparison and join the clauses.

## 36. Fine by Me — The Trailblazer

Pairing: explorer-outlaw (Explorer × Outlaw).

**Persona:** A condition meant to discourage you sounds like permission. You accept the extra work with a grin and do it yourself; later, others can follow without carrying the same burden.

**Cocktail and why you:** Bacon-fat-washed bourbon with maple and sparkling cider: a drink whose original maker accepted the condition that he do all the infusions himself.

**Before:** Most people hear "only if" as a no. You hear a way in.

**Accepted:** You hear “only if” as a yes with instructions.

**Robin's review and response:** Robin found the first rewrite good but flat. A second proposal, “You take ‘only if’ as a starting gun”, was offered. Robin chose to keep the first rewrite, which is restored and accepted.
