# Pour — Four Shares · The True Friend (innocent-regular-guy)

## Cocktail

- **name:** Four Shares
- **tagline:** The world is kinder with you in it.
- **glassware:** a punch bowl with one big block of ice, ladled into small cups
- **contains:** `[]`
- **serves:** 14 (small cups, ladled)

**recipe**

| amount | item | note (shown) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 3 | lemons, peeled in long strips (no white pith) | |
| 150 ml | sugar | made the night before |
| 240 ml | fresh lemon juice | |
| 700 ml (a bottle) | aged rum | |
| 500 ml | cold black tea | |
| 1 | large block of ice | |

**method**
1. The night before: bury the lemon peels in the sugar in a sealed jar and leave them alone. By morning the sugar will have pulled the oil out of the peels and turned into a fragrant syrup.
2. Stir the lemon juice into the jar until the sugar dissolves, then remove the peels.
3. Pour the lemon syrup, the rum and the cold tea into a punch bowl over one big block of ice. Stir.
4. Ladle into small cups.

**closingLine:** *Ladle it out, and keep one cup back for yourself.*

## Reading

**epigraph**

**whoYouAre**
You believe the best of people, and you'd rather be disappointed now and then than live any other way. You forgive quickly. It isn't that you don't notice. It's that holding on to it costs more than it's worth. When things go wrong for someone, you don't make a speech. You just stay. **yours** (authored base; the live tailoring keeps these paragraphs, anchors and order)

1. Nobody knows exactly where punch was born. What we do know is that in the 1600s, English traders in India were mixing the local spirit with water, sugar, citrus and spice, and that sailors carried the habit to every port they reached. For almost two hundred years, a shared bowl of punch was the drink of choice across much of the world. It was the first mixed drink with spirits to travel the globe, and it's the ancestor of drinks you know, like the Daiquiri and the Margarita.
2. Even its name is a small mystery. The story most people tell is that "punch" comes from the Hindi word for five, for its five ingredients. Maybe. But one of the first Englishmen to write the drink down, a merchant in India around 1630, recorded it under a Hindi name that means "four shares". I like to think that's the truer name: not what goes into it, but how many people get some.
3. Punch was never a drink for one. It was made in a bowl and passed around. Small bowls went from hand to hand, in taverns and grand houses alike, and anyone in the room could drink. Some bowls even had words painted inside that only appeared once everyone had had their share: "Drink fair." "One bowl more." That's you, isn't it? You're the one who makes sure the bowl comes round to everybody, and that nobody is left standing at the edge of the room.
4. So I made your punch the old way, the way that takes time, to honour the time you give to others. It starts the night before: strips of lemon peel buried in sugar and left alone until the sugar has drawn out all their oil and turned into a fragrant syrup. You can't rush it. You can only give it time. Then lemon juice, a bottle of aged rum and cold black tea, poured over one big block of ice and ladled into cups. It isn't clever. It's generous. It's a drink that says "I was thinking of you yesterday."
5. Here's what I'd ask of you. Make this one day for people you love, and don't wait for a reason. And the next time you're the one who's struggling, let someone pass the bowl to you. You've spent a long time making sure nobody is left out. You're allowed to be looked after too.
