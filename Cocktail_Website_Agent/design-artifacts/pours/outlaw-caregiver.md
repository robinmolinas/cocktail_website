# Pour — In One Piece · The Gentle Giant (outlaw-caregiver)

## Cocktail

- **name:** In One Piece
- **tagline:** You make people feel safe without making them feel small.
- **glassware:** a tall glass, filled with ice
- **contains:** `["spice"]`

**recipe**

| amount | item | note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | whole lemon, unwaxed | for the peel only: one unbroken spiral |
| 45 ml | VSOP cognac | any VSOP; deeper than a VS, so you taste it under the ginger ale |
| 120 ml | pale dry ginger ale, cold | not ginger beer, and not a sweet golden ginger ale |

**method**
1. Peel the whole lemon with a vegetable peeler in one long, unbroken spiral, from top to bottom, taking as little of the white as you can. Go slowly: if it breaks, it still works, but the one long piece is the drink.
2. Lower the spiral into a tall glass and hook its top end over the rim. Fill the glass with ice, which holds the peel against the sides.
3. Pour in 45 ml of cognac.
4. Top with 120 ml of cold ginger ale, then lift the drink once, gently, with a long spoon.

**closingLine:** *Take your time over the peel. When strength can't help, your time still can.*

## Reading

**epigraph**
*In honour of a night when the bar could pour only soft drinks.*

**whoYouAre**
You walk into a room and it shifts a little. People notice you, and some of them are wary before you've said a word. So you've learned to go slowly and speak softly, and let them find out for themselves.

People assume you were born gentle. You weren't. You decide it, again and again, and you've never told anyone how often. There's a temper in you, and you know just what it could do, most of all when someone is picking on a person who can't answer back. So you don't shout. You go and stand beside them, and the room settles. Here's the gift in it: when you're there, nobody has to fight.

And there's a fear you never mention: the day being strong isn't enough, when it's happening to someone who matters to you, and nothing you've got can stop it.

**yours**

1. In 1949, the California board that issued liquor licences ruled that the Black Cat Café in San Francisco was a "disorderly house". Its reason for that was who drank there: gay men and women met at the Black Cat. The bar's owner, Sol Stoumen, stood to lose his licence. He fought it with a lawyer. Morris Lowenthal argued that the Black Cat's customers had the right to gather there, and in 1951 the state's Supreme Court agreed: people behaving properly in a bar have a right to be served. A right, not a favour.

2. He went on serving them, and the state came back. One San Francisco writer counts the whole fight as fourteen years, and just before the bar's Halloween party in 1963, the licence was taken. The party went ahead without alcohol. One witness remembers hundreds of people still there at six in the morning, drinking only soft drinks and juices.

3. I like to think that night says more about Sol Stoumen than the court case does: the licence was his power, it was gone, and he stayed open for them and poured soft drinks. People rarely see that side of you. Not the strength. What you do once the strength has run out.

4. The cocktail I've made for you is a classic, the Horse's Neck. In turn-of-the-century Atlantic City it was simply ginger ale with a long, long twist of lemon, and many liked theirs with a "stick" of rye or gin in it. A leading drinks reference calls cognac with ginger ale a "winter warmer", so cognac it is: a deeper, older one, which still comes through clearly underneath, even with a pale, dry ginger ale leading nearly three to one. Strength, there the whole time, never raised. The whole lemon is peeled in one unbroken spiral and hung down into the glass: with only ginger ale around it, that was once the whole drink. We don't know which soft drinks were poured at the Black Cat that night. The soft drink leads this glass for that night's sake.

5. There will be a day when being strong isn't enough: trouble you can't stop, happening to someone you'd stand in front of. Don't go home. Stay until morning, and give them whatever you've still got, even if all that's left is being there. It was never your strength they came to you for. It was you.
