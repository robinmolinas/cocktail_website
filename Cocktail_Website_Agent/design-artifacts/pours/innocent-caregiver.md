# Pour — Night Light · The Big Sibling (innocent-caregiver)

## Cocktail

- **name:** Night Light
- **tagline:** You were small too. You looked after them anyway.
- **glassware:** a small stemmed cocktail glass, chilled, no ice
- **contains:** `[]`

**recipe**

| amount | item | note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 60 ml | fino sherry, a fresh bottle | from the driest end of sherry, made under the flor. Look for a recent bottling date. A manzanilla (a fino from the seaside town of Sanlúcar) works too |
| 15 ml | dry (white) vermouth | |
| 15 ml | blanc (sweet white) vermouth | |
| 1 tsp (5 ml) | chamomile honey syrup, homemade (below) | fino is so dry that a little honey gives the drink more body |
| 2 dashes | orange bitters | |
| 1 strip | lemon peel | |
| | *for the syrup:* 50 ml just-boiled water, 1 chamomile tea bag (or 1 tablespoon dried chamomile flowers), 50 ml light runny honey (acacia or a light clover) | makes about 100 ml, enough for about twenty drinks |

**method**
1. Make the syrup ahead. Pour the just-boiled water over the chamomile and leave it for 5 minutes, then take the tea out and squeeze it. Stir the honey into the warm tea until it's smooth, and let it cool. Keep it in a closed jar in the fridge and use it within about two weeks.
2. Put the glass in the freezer, or fill it with ice and water while you mix.
3. Pour the fino, both vermouths, the syrup and the bitters into a mixing glass or a jar, and give it a stir so the syrup dissolves.
4. Fill it with ice and stir with a long spoon for about 30 seconds, until the outside is very cold. The melting ice is part of the drink: it adds about a third to what you poured.
5. Empty the serving glass if you filled it, and strain the drink in. No ice in the glass.
6. Cut a strip of lemon peel, just the yellow part. Twist it over the glass so its oil falls on the drink, then drop it in.
7. Cork the fino and put it back in the fridge. Once it's open it fades quickly, so use it within a week or so.

**closingLine:** *The fino needs only quiet company. Let one of them know you were scared too.*

## Reading

**epigraph**
*Part of the taste in this glass comes from something that stayed behind in the barrel.*

**whoYouAre**
You were small too. People forget that part. You were the one holding the smaller hand at the crossing, saving the last biscuit, staying awake because someone littler was frightened of the dark. No one had asked. It just seemed obvious that anyone you loved should be safe, and if nobody else was watching, you would. You were never less scared of the dark. You just had someone to be calm for.

You still do it. When something goes wrong, the calm voice comes out before you've decided anything. And underneath it, whenever someone you love gets hurt, part of you is already asking what you should have seen, as if it had been your watch. Here's what that gives them: the ones who grew up near you learned from you that the dark can be got through. Some of them are calm in it now, and they couldn't tell you where they got it.

**yours**

1. Fino sits at the driest end of sherry, and it's made in a way I still find surprising. Its barrels aren't filled to the top. Once the young wine is in the barrel, a pale layer of yeast grows across its surface. It forms by itself: the people who make sherry say it comes from yeasts already in the air of that part of Spain. It's called flor, and it stays over the wine for years.
2. What it does is keep the air off. There's always air in the space left at the top, and a little more comes through the wood, and air would turn a fino into a different wine, darker and heavier. The flor sits between that air and the wine and breathes up most of the oxygen itself, so the wine underneath stays protected. The air that would change the wine reaches the flor first. I like to think that's the whole job. Not a rescue. Just being the one it reaches first, every day, for years.
3. That's what you've done since before you were tall. The fright, the bad news, the thing that would have spoiled their night: it gets to you first, and by the time it reaches them, it's smaller. Most of the time they never knew it was there. That's how you've always cared. Everyone close to you has felt safe without ever having to think about it, and that was you.
4. The cocktail I've made for you is built on fino. It's delicate, and beside anything loud it can get lost, so no spirit goes in with it, just a little dry and a little sweet white vermouth around it, the way the Bamboo is built, a famous classic already on record by 1886. The flor itself is left in the barrel, but you can still taste its work: the dry, yeasty edge and the hint of almond in the smell are mostly the flor's. I sweetened it with a teaspoon of honey stirred into chamomile, the tea people make at bedtime, for all the nights you stayed awake so someone smaller wasn't alone in the dark.
5. Pick one of the people you watched over, one who's grown now, and tell them about one time back then when you were scared too, while you were the one being calm. They can carry it now. I think they'll be glad to know what reached you first, and prouder of you than you'd guess.
