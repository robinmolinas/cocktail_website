# Pour — Worth the Trip · The Reassurer (caregiver-jester)

## Cocktail

- **name:** Worth the Trip
- **tagline:** You always make them laugh at the wrong moment, but it's actually always the right one.
- **glassware:** tall glass (highball), full of ice
- **contains:** `["spice"]`

**recipe**

| amount | item | note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 30 ml | brandy (cognac or any VS grape brandy) | the brandy and gin together are the Suffering Bastard's; the rest is mine |
| 30 ml | London dry gin | the other half |
| 20 ml | fresh lime juice | squeezed the same day |
| 35 ml | cooked ginger and cumin syrup | homemade, below, adapted from the *Flavor Matrix*'s ginger syrup; anything from 30 to 40 ml keeps it balanced |
| 2 dashes | Angostura bitters | |
| 100 ml | soda water, cold | poured on last |
| 1 sprig | fresh mint | give it a clap between your hands so it smells as you drink |

**method**
1. **The syrup (the day before, or an hour ahead).** Toast a teaspoon of cumin seeds in a dry saucepan for about a minute, until you can smell them. Add a 10 cm piece of fresh ginger, peeled and thinly sliced, plus 480 ml water and 200 g sugar. Bring to a boil, then simmer for 5 minutes, stirring until the sugar has dissolved. Take it off the heat, cover it and let it cool. Strain into a clean bottle and keep it in the fridge. It makes about 560 ml.
2. Fill a tall glass with ice. Pour in the brandy, gin, lime juice, syrup and bitters, and stir for a few seconds.
3. Pour the cold soda water on top, then give one slow stir, lifting from the bottom.
4. Clap the mint sprig once between your palms and tuck it in beside the ice.
5. **To take it to someone:** mix everything except the ice and soda in a clean bottle and keep it cold. Add the ice and soda when you get there, because soda goes flat in a bottle. The lime is best the same day.

**closingLine:** *Add the soda when you get there. Then let them do the talking.*

## Reading

**epigraph**
*The story goes that the drink behind this one reached the front by taxi.*

**whoYouAre**
When something goes badly for someone, you're the one who says the thing that makes them laugh. Not straight away. You wait. You read them first, you work out how bad it is, and sometimes you keep the line to yourself because it's too early. Then, at the right moment, you say it, and for a second everything weighs a little less. They laugh, they're half embarrassed to be laughing, and then they breathe out.

People sometimes take it to mean you don't take things seriously. It's the other way round. You don't joke because it isn't serious. You joke because it is. The worry underneath it all is that the joke was really for you, to make it easier to stand there. It wasn't. It was how you told them you'd seen exactly how bad it was, and that they weren't in it alone.

**yours**

1. Joe Scialom trained as a chemist. In 1938 he left a career in pharmaceuticals to tend bar at Shepheard's Hotel in Cairo, and his signature drink there was a cure for the morning after. It was called the Suffering Bastard. I've never found out who named it. I like to think it was someone fond of the people drinking it, because that's the only way you can call someone that.

2. As Jeff Berry tells it, one morning in October 1942 his regulars from the Eighth Army were sent out to El Alamein after a long night. From the front, they cabled Scialom for the cure. He sent four gallons of it in Thermoses, by taxi.

3. The journalists at the bar seized on it, and the version they printed was that his drink helped win the battle. Maybe, but it almost certainly didn't. The battle is the part that gets repeated. I'd rather tell you about the taxi.

4. The taxi is the part I think is yours. People remember that you made them laugh, it's your method of delivery. They don't always notice that you came to where it was worst, with exactly what they needed, and that the laugh was how you delivered it. So this one is long and bright, and the soda goes on last, so the lift is the first thing anyone notices. Under it, the ginger is cooked, which takes some of the bite out, and I like to think that's what makes it feel warm rather than sharp. There's a little toasted cumin under the ginger. The gin and brandy together are his. The rest is mine, and it's made to travel: you bottle everything but the soda, and the fizz comes last, once you're there.

5. The laugh is how you get in. Don't let it become how you get out. When the laugh comes, don't reach for the second line. Let the quiet after it happen. You're good company there too, without saying a thing.
