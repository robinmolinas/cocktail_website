# Pour — Just Knew · The Spiritualist (explorer-magician)

## Cocktail

- **name:** Just Knew
- **tagline:** You've been doing it for years. You just never called it that.
- **glassware:** a rocks glass, filled with ice
- **contains:** `[]`

**recipe**

| amount | item | note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 45 ml | London dry gin | |
| 22.5 ml | Campari | |
| 15 ml | sweet vermouth | |
| 15 ml | dry vermouth | half the vermouth dry, so the bitter and the sweet sit level |
| 1 | dried bay leaf, torn in half | from the jar in the kitchen cupboard; scent and a little taste |
| 1 | orange peel | squeezed over the top |

**method**
1. Fill a rocks glass with ice.
2. Put in the torn bay leaf, the gin, the Campari and both vermouths, in any order.
3. Stir for a few seconds.
4. Squeeze the orange peel over the top so its oils fall on the drink, then drop it in.

**closingLine:** *Take the bay leaf from the jar you never think about. Then name one habit that's quietly kept you going.*

## Reading

**epigraph**
*A famous bartender's recipe for the classic behind this drink: build it in any order.*

**whoYouAre**
There's a shelf somewhere in your home that would surprise people. Very old books next to very new ones, and a couple you'd rather nobody picked up. You've gone further than the shelf, too: the silent weekend, the long walk, the course your friends teased you about. Each time you return with one thing, a line or a habit, and you want it to last through an ordinary week.

Hardly anyone knows how often it slips. By Wednesday the inbox has it, and part of you decides the real answer must be somewhere else, so you start looking again. The looking doesn't scare you. A life that stops asking what it's for does. But some of it has been working for years without your noticing. The walk you take before a hard day. The song you play in the car before you go in. You'd never call those a practice, and they've been holding you up the whole time. People feel it, too. You arrive more present than most, and sometimes you hand someone exactly the line they needed, with no lecture attached. That's a rare kind of kindness.

**yours**

1. In 1973 Gary Regan, a young bartender from a small English seaside town, started a new life in New York, where the customers seemed larger than life and made him nervous. So he began arriving at least an hour before his shift. He ate dinner alone at a table, then sat on, taking in the room, telling himself the night would go well.

2. The customers stopped intimidating him, and they still teased him, which he said was how he liked it. Years later he looked back and called that hour meditating, in effect, though he hadn't known it at the time: "I just knew it worked." The idea of mindfulness for bartenders came to him at workshops, and he gave talks on it, saying plainly that he was no qualified teacher. Writing about it, he told bartenders never to take things too seriously, and said that when he was setting up a bar alone, loud music could be his meditation.

3. I like to think the part of his story that belongs to you isn't the workshops. It's the hour before them, when he had no name for what he was doing. The search mattered, I think: it gave him words for it, and with words he could pass it on. But what he was doing was already there, on a working night like any other, doing its job. I'd guess something in your week does that for you, and you've never given it a name.

4. He wrote a book about the Negroni, a renowned classic. In his best-known book, he printed it with the Campari first, then sweet vermouth and gin, the same measure of each, and one odd instruction: build it in any order. The recipe doesn't say why. I like to think there's a whole outlook in those three words. In the cocktail I've made for you, his instruction stays. I've changed two things, so the gin leads and half the vermouth is dry, which makes it brighter and less sweet. Then one addition: a dried bay leaf, torn in half, out of the kitchen cupboard. I put it in for that hour: something ordinary you already had, doing more than anyone gave it credit for.

5. Keep going away. It's where you find the words. But before you plan the next retreat, look at what already carries you through an ordinary day, the walk, the song, the hour you keep for yourself, and give it its proper name. It counts. You're further along than you think, and I'm glad of it for you.

### Open items

- **The true order (Hester C1, C2):** the 1973 hour before shifts at Drake's Drum came first, with no name ("I just knew it worked"); decades later he looked back and called it "in effect, meditating", and the mindfulness-for-bartenders idea came to him at workshops. Nothing says he kept the hour every night for years (Simonson has him moving bar to bar).
- **Advice vs practice (C3, C4):** "I'm breathing in. I'm breathing out" and the five-to-ten minutes are his advice to readers, not his 1973 habit; the Thursday regular is his example, not a memory. The breath-stir was dropped for that reason, and because a Negroni tolerates a wide range of dilution (*LI* pdf 107–108).
- **"Any order" is in *Joy*, not verified in *The Negroni* (C10):** the 2015 book is not in the library or on archive.org; the reading says "in his best-known book". Counterweight (C11): PUNCH prints his Jägermeister riff stirred and strained, and Simonson has him stirring with his finger, so it's "the way he printed it", never "how he always made it". A gap for Robin: *The Negroni* (2015).
- **Fenced against siblings (C7, C8):** *Anyway*'s tagline is his "great night" in other words, so it gets one clause; the Innocent family's Wise Kid row keeps *Joy* pdf 133 (the Scotch Sours, the waitress), all of which this pour leaves out. No death year, no faith, no wellness claim; Oxford never mentions mindfulness (C9), so the inward story rests on his own account.
- **The drink is ours:** gin leading and half the vermouth dry (45 / 22.5 / 15 / 15) is our balance, not Arnold's allowance, which keeps vermouth one-to-one with Campari. Sugar sits at the sweet edge on purpose (5.95 g; Campari's bitterness, which balance.py can't hear); equal parts reads 9.49 g. The bay leaf is ours, not his. The cacao nib tincture was ruled out (a jar made specially, the *Codex*'s move, and it would have cost the veto-free floor: shared-plant nut and milk warnings).
