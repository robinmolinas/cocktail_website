# Step 8: Define Success Criteria

**Completed:** 2026-06-11
**Session:** 1

---

## Opening Question

**Agent asked:**
Dionysus is a non-commercial, high-ambition showcase project. What does success look like from the angles of Experience Quality (landing, quiz, output), User/Showcase Behavior (quiz length vs. retention trade-off), and Timeline/Portfolio Impact?

**User's response:**
Success is primarily about **experience quality**—ensuring users enjoy it, find it beautiful, and want to stay. The website should feel like a piece of art that visitors want to look at and admire, across three distinct stages:
1. **Landing Page:** A beautiful, mysterious entry point.
2. **Questionnaire:** A tactile, fluid, and exciting journey.
3. **Output Page:** A visually stunning and emotionally resonant payoff.

For user behavior, the trade-off of the 19 questions (necessary for the matrix accuracy) must be balanced so users don't get fed up and leave before the reveal. The quiz must feel exciting and responsive (like Pottermore). In terms of timeline, we will put serious, focused effort into the project without rushing the quality.

---

## SMART Success Criteria

### 1. Aesthetic & Experience Fidelity (Primary Metric)
* **Visual Appeal:** The visual presentation across all three pages must feel like a premium, interactive painting.
* **Performance:** UI rendering must maintain a consistent **60fps** on both desktop and mobile devices.
* **Transition Velocity:** Page turns and ink clearing animations must resolve in **under 4 seconds** to maintain momentum and match industry-best narrative standards.
* **Frictionless AI:** Zero perceived delay or errors during the multi-agent recommendation generation.

### 2. User Engagement & Retention (Secondary Metric)
* **Quiz Completion Rate:** Target a completion rate of **>80%** for users who click "Begin." This indicates that the tactile "bar-spoon" cursor and gestural transitions successfully offset the fatigue of a 19-question form.
* **Emotional Connection:** Measured by a high rate of PDF recipe card downloads, showing that users find the output valuable enough to save for real-world mixology.

### 3. Timeline Strategy
* **Quality-First Sprints:** Rather than building a quick Minimum Viable Product, success is defined by executing WDS phases (Trigger Mapping, UX Scenarios, Visual Design System, and Canvas Fluid Prototypes) sequentially with high fidelity.
