## Design Specificity Verdict

The pour writing is highly specific; the imagery is not. In a contact sheet, the set reads as a generated luxury-bar template with accessories swapped: 13 of 14 scenes repeat a near-identical brass dome lamp, almost every glass occupies the same right-of-centre position, and the same glossy walnut, orange-black grade, pristine tag, shallow focus and camera-facing prop cluster recur. “One room, different person” has collapsed into “one render, different nouns.”

The deterministic Impeccable scan returned zero code-pattern findings in `TheReading.tsx`, and the asset validator passed all 14 canonical pairs. Those are technical passes only; neither evaluates photographic credibility. Source inspection found one presentation-level amplifier: an orange screen-blended radial sits over the glass in `index.css`, warming already over-amber files.

No browser overlay was available because no mutable browser automation surface was exposed. Evidence came from labeled contact sheets, foreground-detail sheets, full-resolution inspection of all 14 wide assets, source inspection and cross-checking against the pour briefs.

## Overall Impression

The images are polished enough to impress one at a time and repetitive enough to lose trust as a system. The biggest opportunity is to replace “cinematic object arrangement” with observed cause and effect: a tool must be supported, wear must occur where hands touch, liquid must behave like the recipe, and personality evidence must look used rather than symbolically displayed.

## What’s Working

- All masters meet the 1920×1080 wide and 896×1200 portrait contracts, with usable copy space and protected drink/tag placement.
- The shared material world—dark wood, brass, paper, linen and restrained glassware—is coherent and fits Dionysus.
- The drink is usually the first read, and the blank physical-tag mechanism is consistent and usable.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] Cocktail physics are not reliable

Ramos foam reads as frosting, the Four Shares ice as resin, the Trickster sugar cube as a dry caramel block in red gel, and several still or shaken drinks carry decorative carbonation. This breaks trust in the bespoke recipe itself. Build a pour-truth checklist for every image and treat any mismatch in glassware, opacity, ice, bubbles, foam, garnish, rim or condensation as an automatic failure.

Suggested command: `$impeccable polish`

### [P1] Clone-template authorship defeats personalization

Thirteen repeated lamps, one camera height, one table, one grade and near-identical prop geometry make outcomes feel cosmetically reskinned. Keep the same world but vary the room zone, camera distance and height, practical source, negative-space shape, prop density and evidence gesture. A blurred silhouette contact sheet must no longer look like one shoot.

Suggested command: `$impeccable bolder`

### [P1] Personal evidence has become symbolic still-life dressing

Old books, keys, papers, bottles and linen are presented as archetype shorthand rather than traces of a person’s action. Pseudo-writing and generic antiques make this worse. Start each brief with two human habits, then add no more than three recipe traces. Every supporting object must answer “who used this, how, and why is it still here?”

Suggested command: `$impeccable distill`

### [P2] The global amber luxury-ad grade erases material truth

Wood, metal, paper, glass and shadows share the same orange gloss. The files already skew warm, and the runtime orange screen blend compounds it. Retain warm practicals but restore neutral shadows, dirty creams, dull metal and real ingredient colour; remove the direct orange screen blend over the glass.

Suggested command: `$impeccable colorize`

### [P2] The QA gate checks dimensions, not credibility

Malformed books, gemstone ice, cloned bottles, evenly sprayed condensation and glyph-like print survive because the automated validator only checks paths and dimensions. Add mandatory full-resolution physics/geometry review, blind personality recognition, and contact-sheet anti-clone review.

Suggested command: `$impeccable harden`

## Anti-Slop Acceptance Gate

1. **Pour truth:** independent check of glassware, fill, opacity, dilution, ice, foam, bubbles, rim, garnish and condensation. Any mismatch fails.
2. **100% physics and geometry:** inspect rims, stems, meniscus, contacts, book/paper construction, closures, reflections, shadows, fingers and all pseudo-text zones.
3. **Blind hierarchy read:** without copy, reviewers identify the drink first, one intended personality habit second and recipe evidence last.
4. **Contact-sheet anti-clone:** grayscale and blur the set. No non-required prop/composition signature may recur in more than 25% of images; tag and material world are exempt.
5. **Human irregularity:** at least one contact-specific use trace and one cropped, overlapped or partly occluded support object. Never present the entire prop set to camera.
6. **No-puzzle/no-symbol-army:** no magical rings, shadows, reflected drink armies, floating tools, generic antique keys or faux equations.
7. **Light and colour:** one explainable source, neutral shadows retained, distinct material response and no global amber wash.
8. **Runtime:** verify tag with short/long names, copy-safe area and the image in the actual result composition at desktop and portrait sizes.

## Persona Red Flags

- **Jordan, first-timer:** the drink is usually obvious, but the “person” is not. Jordan has to read the prose to understand why the image is theirs, while impossible foam, ice or sugar undermines confidence before the explanation begins.
- **Riley, deliberate stress tester:** method/image contradictions are immediate—peels left in Four Shares, multiple ice masses instead of one block, a dry Trickster cube and false carbonation. One failure makes the researched recipe feel fabricated.
- **Casey, distracted mobile user:** dense prop clusters collapse at phone scale into “amber drink beside old book and lamp.” The set loses quick recognition and shareability.
- **Cocktail enthusiast:** physically false hero details are not decorative errors; they invalidate the promised authority of the pour.

## Minor Observations

- Punch serving cups are a necessary exception to the generic one-glass rule and should be documented as such.
- Unreadable period print can be legitimate; glyph-like equations, repeated quasi-letters and diagram textures cannot.
- The full-bleed Ken Burns movement magnifies foreground artifacts, so assets must survive inspection beyond thumbnail size.
- The tag should feel handled, but the current tags are consistently pristine and similarly angled.
