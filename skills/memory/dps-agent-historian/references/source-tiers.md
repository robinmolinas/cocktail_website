**Indexes:** books with a `<file>.index.md` beside them in `_text/` (chapters, recipes, who's where) should be read before searching. Add an index when a new book arrives.

## The web (secondary)
Use it for what the library doesn't cover, and label it **secondary** in the dossier.
- Prefer the source closest to the original: the newspaper that got the confession, or a trade press piece quoting it, over a blog summarising a blog. (Seelbach: *The Spirits Business* quoting the NYT, over Difford's.)
- A paywalled primary source is noted as unread. The fact can still be used from a close secondary, marked so.
- Encyclopedias and recipe sites give leads, not facts, unless they cite a primary you can check.

## Labels (every claim gets one)
- **fact:** sourced, with page or URL. Can become an anchor.
- **legend:** the popular story, unsourced or disproved. Can be *told as a legend*, never as fact.
- **inference:** your own reasoning from facts ("decades of wine in one pour"). Never goes in the reading as fact.
- **our reading:** the bartender's interpretation, signposted in the text ("I like to think…").

## Conflicts
When sources disagree, record both in the dossier, and leave the contested detail out of the reading. (The Seelbach's invented year was 1912 in one source and about 1917 in another, so the reading says "from before Prohibition".)

## Copyright
Facts are free; wording isn't. Never carry a source's sentences into a pour. In the dossier, quote only as much as a claim needs (under 15 words). The Bartender manga: feeling and storytelling only, never its words.
