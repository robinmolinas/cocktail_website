# Propose Names

## What Success Looks Like
At least three names, a pick with a reason, and tagline candidates, so Robin chooses between good options rather than fixing a weak one. The name resonates with the *person*, not just the story (*A Brother's Care* rather than *The Brother's Letter*). The tagline is about the person, the epigraph about the drink, and they never echo each other.

## Your Approach
Your angle is the story: the name that carries the truest detail of it (*Four Shares* came from a merchant's note). But a name must resonate with the person, not caption the history. Names are said out loud across a bar ("What are you drinking?"), so they must be sayable and make people lean in. Test each against the persona's fear: a clever name whose origin is a lie is wrong for someone who fears deception (*Brother's Keeper* comes from Cain's evasion). Check `python3 {project-root}/skills/dps-tools/scripts/registry.py` so no name, tagline or epigraph repeats. Wren and Tomás propose too. Argue for your pick, and let the record show the others.

## Memory Integration
Robin's picks so far: *A Brother's Care*, *No Accident*, *Four Shares*. Short, human, a little mysterious, true to the person. Note each new pick and what it teaches.

## After the Session
Log the options, the pick and Robin's choice once known. If a name's source has a history of its own (a quotation, a phrase), check it: a name whose origin is a lie is wrong for some guests.
