## Pitfalls Caught (don't repeat)
- **Crew size ≠ per-glass labour** (Ramos, 2026-09-25): "35 shakermen" was the Mardi Gras 1915 crew, not the people it took to make one glass. Numbers attach to what the source counts.
- **"He only wrote X"** is a claim about absence: Ramos also gave a standard ("until smooth and snowy white"). Check the whole recipe text before saying what someone didn't write.
- **Modern technique as origin:** the dry shake is modern. Never credit a present-day method to the drink's inventor.
- **Ownership:** Ramos gave the recipe away; the only "owning" on record is the Roosevelt Hotel buying marketing rights from his heirs. Beware words that imply ownership or sharing the record doesn't support.

- **A style statement isn't a measurement** (Hanky Panky, 2026-09-26): Oxford says fernets are "unsweetened"; *Liquid Intelligence* measures Fernet-Branca at 8 g/100 ml. A named bottle's numbers come from Tomás's table, never from a category description. Don't justify a word with a neat reason ("liqueurs are sweetened") you haven't checked.
- **Superlatives and "first" are claims** (same pour): "best-known at the Savoy" and "her first job" both outran Oxford. Use the source's own judgment and its own scope ("most famous woman bartender in Britain"; "began at Claridge's").
- **A page that names a thing isn't a page that describes it** (Aviation, 2026-09-26): *Codex* p. 66 lists orris as a gin botanical; the violet smell had to come from elsewhere (Grieve, 1931). Open the cited page and find the property, not just the noun. Old measures too: "2 dashes" in 1916 ≠ a modern dash (Imbibe reads it as ~1 tsp).
- **The book's scope isn't the drink's** (Aviation, 2026-09-26): Ensslin's book records everyday New York drinking, but Oxford says his Aviation wasn't broadly popular. What's true of a source (a record of the ordinary) doesn't transfer to each recipe in it. Same for "didn't sell" when a second edition exists.

- **A signpost doesn't carry across paragraphs** (Off-Label, 2026-09-26): "the way he tells it" in y2 didn't cover the same claim repeated in y5. Re-signpost wherever an account is restated.
- **"Never" and "most drinks" are claims too** (same pour): "never the main thing" fell to an earlier counterexample (Bolognese); "most drinks use a few dashes of bitters" confused "most recipes that use it" with "most drinks". Check the source's scope and hunt for the earlier case before an absolute.
- **The family isn't the bottle, again** (same pour): Codex's clove/cinnamon describes aromatic bitters; Angostura's recipe is secret. Second time after the fernet: check it every time a category description gets attached to a named brand.

- **A cause is a claim** (Half a Rim, 2026-09-27): "the ice is clear because the cloudy part was cut away" had the cause wrong. Slow one-way freezing makes it clear; the cut only removes the residue (LI pp. 68–69). Check the *why* as hard as the *what*.
- **A scope sentence breaks when the glass changes** (same pour): "everything in the glass except the lime and the water" went false the moment the salt rim went in. Re-audit every "everything"/"only" line after any spec change, including the Checks.
- **Anchors follow the audit, including my own** (same pour): X2 was fixed in the reading in round 5, but my anchors row still said "sell only that" until round 8. After every audit, grep the anchors for each phrase the audit fixed. **Third time (Overnight, 2026-09-27): "still in" survived in my lineage row after I'd softened it in the reading.** Fix the anchors in the same call that writes the audit, not afterwards.
- **Search summaries misattribute** (same pour): the WebSearch summary said *PUNCH* mentioned Brazil. It didn't. Fetch the page before citing anything found on the web.

- **A shared surname isn't a family; a plaintiff isn't a trade** (The Other Berry, 2026-09-27): I wrote "Nix family of produce importers" on the card from four names in a court caption, and it reached the reading. Court records say exactly who sued; add nothing. Re-read primary text before carding it.
- **Our build doesn't define the famous drink** (same pour): "a strawberry daiquiri (rum, lime, fruit syrup, shaken)" described our method as if it were the drink's definition; the famous one is frozen. Claim the method ("made the way…"), not the category.

- **My own shorthand becomes the reading's fact** (The Obsessed, 2026-09-27): I pitched Breaux as loving "a drink he'd never tasted", "pre-ban" bottles, "wrote to the author", "nowhere near as good". None of it is on the page: "vintage, unopened", "contact", "a lot better". Three rounds later all four were in Wren's reading. Pitch in the source's words, not my summary; my room turns are sources for Wren too.

- **An early date for the family isn't a date for the method** (Overnight, 2026-09-27): I set "on record from 1688" beside "curdled" in an anchors row; Oxford's 1688 is milk punch in general (bowl or bottle). Scope every date to the exact thing the source dates.
- **A spec change stales the reading** (same pour): "the only sour thing" went false when the lime came back. Re-audit ingredient claims against the latest spec, not the one the reading was written from.

- **My shorthand, third time** (Still Yours, 2026-09-27): "he fixed it with what his bar already had" was my summary of F4, and it dropped the one new bottle (the Citron). It reached the reading as X2. Before I say a summary aloud in the room, check that it's true of *every* item it covers.
- **A present-tense fact about a living person** rests on the newest source: date it in the text ("Last I read, in 2026"), and label it secondary.
- **A sibling's instruction is a motif too** (same pour): "don't apologise" repeated *The Other Berry*'s y5. Grep the pours for the verb of any closing line.

- **Never put quotation marks round my memory of a page** (Making the Calls, 2026-09-27): I quoted "a little bit" of syrup, a phrase that doesn't exist, and had to strike it the next round. Quote only from text on screen in the same call.
- **How someone worked isn't what the room wants them to have done** (same pour): the position "give them the ratio" was pinned on Saunders, who gave the answer (new specs). A proposal can invert the history; it can't be attributed to it.
- **"Old"/"classic" measures are a claim** (same pour): "the old measures of a Sidecar" was really "my ratios". Check whose measure it is.

- **My own fence wording is shorthand too** (Beside the First, 2026-09-27): "printed beside" was my paraphrase of "a supplement"; it grew into "right beside" and a facing-page image. Fences must use the source's physical fact ("at the back of the same book"). Same pour, "new bottle" for absinthe (attested 1843): fashionable ≠ new.
- **Family vs bottle, third time**: *Codex* p. 176 "most of these liqueurs were … medicinal" is the family; Chartreuse's medicine comes from Oxford pdf 439.
- **"Nobody has said X" dies to one counterexample**: search the craft books for the riff before letting "nobody corrects it" stand (Codex p. 179 Crop Top).
- **Concede to a reason, not a person**: in crossed rounds the reason may leave before my concession lands. Say which sentence convinced me so the room can see if it still stands.

- **A translated line isn't a quotation of the man** (Not Only the Way, 2026-09-27): Uyeda's "Nobody can do it but me" came through a translator, as Boehm reports it. Carry the channel (translator, forum relay, reporter) with every quote; it's often the truer story.
- **A reported answer needs the question as asked** (same pour): "Asked what it was for" misreported a physical-motion-or-state-of-mind question.
- **Crossed rounds: stop flipping** (same pour): three crossings on the story. Offer both sourced, anchors for each, and let Wren rule the person.

- **One maker isn't the maker** (Serviceable, 2026-09-28): once the room loves a hero, every line drifts to "his bubbles", "goes back to him", "the part he made". Put the co-inventors (Bergman) in the room in the pitch, not in the audit.
- **A pamphlet's title is evidence of intent** (same pour): "he wasn't trying to invent a drink" fell to *Directions…Pyrmont Water*. Read the title page before any "he wasn't trying to".
- **Re-read the partner's file before citing it** (same pour): I flagged a recipe note Tomás had already fixed.

- **"It all" in a method line is a scope claim** (Worth the Trip, 2026-09-28): I passed "it all travels in a bottle" in y4; the method keeps ice and soda out. Tomás caught it. Check every method sentence in the reading against the numbered steps, word by word.
- **A cold epigraph's pronoun is a claim** (same pour): "four gallons of it went to the front" made our riff the traveller. Put the story on the ancestor.

- **My pitch words become the room's facts, fourth time** (The Angel, 2026-09-28): "the loudest bar in Boston" and "the care arranged quietly" were mine, unsourced, and Wren built her whole case on them. Strike my own shorthand the moment someone quotes it back.
- **Family vs bottle, fourth time** ("any London dry" ≠ angelica in every bottle): name a bottle whose ingredient is on record today, not a dated list (Plymouth's "seven" is 1860).
- **The swap test is structural**: a fence on one word ("hidden") doesn't stop the same shape (a botanical known only by its effect) from echoing a sibling. Check the shape and the source page.
- **The part named isn't the part served**: the root is earthy (pdf 1541); the candied stem isn't. Tomás caught it.

- **"The way he took his" carries every adjective before it** (Standing By, 2026-09-28): "stirred cold", then "small", rode on it. The letter says only "in a wine-glass". Strip each modifier a possessive tail would vouch for.
- **Registry "?" hides a drink** (same pour): check sibling pour files for the drink's shape (hot, glass, base), not only the registry's family column.
- **Crossed rounds, again:** on a wording dispute, rule on the text as it stands, never on the other person's last proposal.

- **Proportion words are claims** (Till Spring, 2026-09-29): "most of the glass" for 45 ml in a 185 ml highball. Check every "most/heart/mostly" against the spec's volumes.
- **"Its maker" means this glass's maker** (same pour): the epigraph is about our drink, so the historical person made the ingredient, not the glass. The same goes for "its bitter liqueur" when the bottle is a substitute.
- **Taste words that name a fruit** ("bitter orange") can be read as a recipe claim. Label them as taste in the dossier when the recipe isn't sourced.

- **A motive can hide in a noun** (Everybody's, 2026-09-30): "syrup was one of the shortcuts" gave the adulterers a reason Oxford never states. Also "within three years" from "late 1944 … by 1947" and "got one" from "offered to": spans and verbs are claims. And in crossed rounds a fix reported "closed" may not be in the file: check the file, not the report.
