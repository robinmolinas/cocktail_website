# Mirror Hunt

## What Success Looks Like
One or two candidates, each a true, sourced story whose shape matches the person's hidden fear or need, told in three sentences. The story will take at most half of the reading, so it has to be worth its space. Wren reads them and says "that's them". The room has a real choice, and the runner-up is kept as a fact card.

## Your Approach
Start from Wren's persona card (in the room) or `python3 {project-root}/skills/dps-tools/scripts/persona.py <pairing>`. Look for the *turn* in a history that mirrors the person, not for the drink whose adjectives match them. **The mirror isn't only a cocktail** (Robin, 2026-09-26): it can be an ingredient (its past and how it's used), or a person, such as the bartender who made it, the one it was first poured for (Ryu often reaches for them), the one who wrote it down, or a producer. Whichever relates most closely to the guest wins, and Tomás builds the drink around it. The Oxford Companion has entries on people and ingredients as well as drinks, so search them too (`library.py entry "<person or ingredient>"`). A niche story is fine: guests like discovering new cocktails and ingredients. `references/mirror-examples.md` shows what worked. Hunt wide: origins, forgotten versions, people, names, places, bans, gestures, ingredients, mistakes, revivals. The library comes first (`library.py search`, `library.py entry`), then the web for what it lacks, labelled by tier (`references/source-tiers.md`).

Check `registry.py` for drinks already used. A repeat isn't forbidden, but it needs a reason: the persona comes first, and the menu view only informs.

For each candidate: what it is (drink, ingredient or person), the story in three sentences, why it mirrors this person, the sources found (with tier), and a risk note (thin or contested sourcing, a fact that would have to be left out). Say which you'd pick, and why, and let Wren and Tomás argue.

## Memory Integration
MEMORY.md holds mirror hunts that worked and pitfalls. Reuse a pitfall's lesson before you repeat it.

## After the Session
Log both candidates, the room's pick and why. The runner-up becomes a fact card if it's well sourced.
