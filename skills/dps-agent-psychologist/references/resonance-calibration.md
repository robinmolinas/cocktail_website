# Resonance Calibration

What passing and failing feel like, from the first three pours (approved by Robin, 2026-09-24) and deliberately bad rewrites of them. Not a capability: load it when the resonance test needs recalibrating.

## Passing: the "this is me" lines

| Personality | The line | Why it lands |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The Connoisseur | "What you can't forgive is being fooled: the label that promises too much, the story told only to impress." | Names the fear (deception) through a behaviour they'll recognise, not a label. |
| The Trickster | "What scares you isn't getting caught. It's being ignored." | Inverts the obvious fear of a rule-breaker. Robin called the whoYouAre "powerful". |
| The True Friend | "You're allowed to be looked after too." | Speaks to the need they never voice (giving so they never have to ask). |

Notice: each names something the person *does* or *fears*, in plain words, with a turn in it. None praises them.

## Failing: the horoscope versions

**Connoisseur (fails):** "You have refined taste and appreciate the finer things in life. You're thoughtful and curious, and your friends value your opinion."
- *refined taste*: a compliment, not recognition
- *thoughtful and curious*: true of almost anyone who'd take a personality quiz
- *friends value your opinion*: generic, and it's the "among your friends" formula

**Trickster (fails):** "You're fun-loving and a little rebellious. You like to challenge the rules and keep life interesting."
- restates the archetype label in adjectives
- nothing hidden, nothing turned: no fear, no gift

**True Friend (fails):** "You're loyal, kind and always there for the people you love. People know they can count on you."
- every word is true and nothing is seen: a greeting card, not a mirror

## Failing: the swap test

"You love learning how the world works, and you never stop asking questions." It could belong to any of the eleven Sage pairings (the Mentor, the Philosopher, the Scientist…). What makes the *Connoisseur* different is that they study **pleasure**, for themselves, through the senses, so the line has to be about that.

## Failing: the rule breaks a lint can't see

- A proposal that only works with the drink in hand ("take the first sip without naming anything") without "when you do have this in front of you".
- A motive stated as fact ("he did it out of love for the guests") instead of signposted ("I like to think…").
- A clever line that serves the writer, not the guest (the "Brother's Keeper" name: clever, and wrong for someone who fears deception, because its source is a lie).
